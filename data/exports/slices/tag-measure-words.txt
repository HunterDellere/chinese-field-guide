//角落書屋/Slices/Measure words
本	běn	root, origin, this, a book — A tree with its root marked: the character for where a thing comes from. From root grows origin, the fundamental, one's own, the principal sum, and the bound volume you hold in your hand.
部	bù	part, section, ministry, measure word — The right-side city-mound radical marks an administrative division. From that grows the whole family of parts and wholes: a section of something, a government ministry, a military unit, and the measure word counted out for films, machines, and phones.
点	diǎn	dot, point, a little, o'clock — From a single dot of ink the character spreads into points and decimals, into the hours of the clock, into ordering food and lighting a flame, and into the small quantity that softens a sentence.
分	fēn	to divide, minute, point, cent — A knife splitting a thing in two. From the cut grows everything that comes in parts: the minute on the clock, the point on a test, the cent in your pocket, and the act of separating itself.
个	gè	the universal measure word, individual — The general-purpose measure word that counts almost anything, and the root of individual. From the bamboo-stalk tally grew the everyday classifier 一个 and the words for individual, personality, and build.
回	huí	to return, to turn back, a time — Two squares nested like a coiling eddy. The character for coming back: returning home, turning around, replying, and counting the times a thing happens.
间	jiān	space between, interval, room — Sunlight in the crack between two doors: the character for the space between things. From that gap grow time, the middle ground, the human world, and the room you count one by one.
件	jiàn	item, piece, measure word — The measure word for matters, garments, and gifts, and the character behind documents, parts, and cases.
量	liàng	quantity, capacity; to measure — 商量 shangliang, to discuss, is literally to measure something out together: the act of weighing it between two minds before anyone decides.
门	mén	door, gate, family, field — A picture of a two-leaf gate. From the swinging doors grow the gateway, the household behind them, the school or trade you enter, and the measure word for whole courses of study.
首	shǒu	head, first, chief, a measure word for songs and poems — A drawn head, with hair above an eye, and from it the senses of first, foremost, chief, leader, and the classifier you count songs and poems with. 首 is its own radical.
眼	yǎn	eye, glance — The everyday word for eye, and by extension any small opening, any moment of looking, any judgment made through sight. The body-part counterpart to literary 目.
按部就班	àn bù jiù bān	to follow the proper sequence: to do things step by step in due order — Lit: according-to-section, settle-into-rank. Originally a writer's image of arranging material into its right divisions and lines; now it means to proceed methodically by the established order, neither rushing nor skipping steps. Here 部 keeps its old sense of an ordered division.
本来面目	běn lái miàn mù	original face: a thing's true, unaltered nature — Lit: original-face-and-features. A Chan Buddhist phrase for one's true self before all conditioning, now used broadly for the real character of anything once the disguises are stripped away.
本末倒置	běn mò dào zhì	to put root and tip in the wrong order: to mistake the essential for the trivial — Lit: root-tip-upside-down. 本 (the root) and 末 (the branch-tips) are the two ends of the tree. To reverse them is to spend effort on the minor while neglecting what truly matters.
变本加厉	biàn běn jiā lì	to alter the base and pile on more: to grow worse than before, to intensify — Lit: change-base-add-severity. Said when behavior that was already bad escalates rather than improving, the original measure exceeded and then redoubled.
不计其数	bú jì qí shù	countless; too many to count — Literally "not to reckon their number," so many that counting is hopeless. Used for vast, uncountable quantities, the far end of the scale that 多少 stretches toward. The answer to 多少 when the true reply is "beyond counting."
不可估量	bù kě gū liàng	cannot be estimated: immeasurable, beyond reckoning — 不可 bù kě (cannot) + 估 gū (to estimate) + 量 liàng (to measure, to gauge). So vast or so consequential that no estimate can capture it. Used for losses, impact, or value too large to quantify: 损失不可估量 (the losses are immeasurable), 意义不可估量 (the significance is beyond reckoning).
部署周密	bù shǔ zhōu mì	deployed with careful thoroughness: arranged in close and complete order — 部署 bùshǔ is to lay out forces or tasks in their assigned positions, the military sense of 部; 周密 zhōumì means thorough and tightly planned. Said of a plan or operation set up with every detail in place.
大开眼界	dà kāi yǎn jiè	to greatly open one's horizons — to have one's eyes opened — Lit: greatly-open-eye-boundary. The standard phrase for the experience of suddenly seeing something new and broadening one's outlook. Said after a trip, a museum visit, a striking piece of work.
荡气回肠	dàng qì huí cháng	stirring the breath and coiling the gut: deeply moving, soul-stirring — Lit: surging-breath-coiling-innards. Said of music, writing, or a story so moving that it seems to turn one's very insides. 回 keeps its coiling sense here.
点石成金	diǎn shí chéng jīn	to touch stone and turn it to gold: to transform the ordinary into the priceless — Lit: touch-stone-become-gold. From Daoist immortal lore, the touch that turns base rock to gold. Now used of an editor, teacher, or craftsman who makes something dull suddenly shine.
独门绝技	dú mén ju é jì	a unique school's matchless skill: a signature technique known to no one else — Lit: sole-school-unmatched-skill. 门 here is a school or lineage of practice. A 独门绝技 is the secret art that belongs to one tradition or master alone.
多多益善	duō duō yì shàn	the more the better — Literally "more and more is the more beneficial," the more the merrier. From a remark attributed to the general Han Xin, who said that as a commander he could lead troops 多多益善 ("the more the better"). Built on the 多 ("many") of 多少.
分秒必争	fēn miǎo bì zhēng	to fight for every minute and second: to lose not a moment — Lit: minute-second-must-contend. Said when time is so pressing that each unit counts, in a race, an emergency, a deadline. 争分夺秒 is the common variant.
个个	gè gè	each and every one; all of them — Reduplicating the measure word makes it mean "every single one without exception." 他们个个都很努力 "every one of them works hard." A vivid, emphatic way to say "all."
个人主义	gèr én zhǔ yì	individualism — 个人 (the individual) + 主义 (-ism). The modern term for individualism, the doctrine that puts the single person ahead of the collective. Its standing contrast is 集体主义 jítǐ zhǔyì (collectivism).
个中滋味	gè zhōng zī wèi	the flavor within: the inner experience only the one who lived it can know — Lit: the-taste-within-this. 个中 means "within this matter," and 滋味 is flavor. The phrase points to feelings or hardships that an outsider cannot fully grasp, known only to the person who went through them.
海量	hǎi liàng	sea-sized capacity: a vast tolerance or a huge amount — 海 hǎi (sea) + 量 liàng (capacity). A capacity as large as the sea. Traditionally a generous compliment for someone of great tolerance, whether a magnanimous temperament (大人有海量, your honor is broad-minded) or a formidable drinking capacity at the table.
画龙点睛	huà lóng diǎn jīng	drawing a dragon and dotting the eye — the small finishing touch that brings the whole alive — From a Tang anecdote: the painter Zhang Sengyou painted dragons but refused to add eyes; when pressed, he dotted the eyes of two , and they immediately flew off the wall. The dot is what brings the form to life. Used today for the small but decisive move that makes a piece of work click.
回头是岸	huí tóu shì àn	turn your head and there is the shore: it is never too late to repent — Lit: turn-the-head-is-shore. From the Buddhist 苦海无边，回头是岸 ("the sea of suffering is boundless; turn back and the shore is there"). To stop a wrong course and come back is itself salvation.
回心转意	huí xīn zhuǎn yì	to turn the heart and shift the will: to change one's mind, to relent — Lit: turn-heart-shift-intent. Said of someone who softens, reconsiders, or comes back to a relationship or decision they had set aside.
间不容发	jiān bù róng fà	the gap admits not a hair: a situation of extreme danger or urgency — Lit: the-gap-does-not-allow-a-hair. The interval is so narrow not even a single hair could fit, so the margin for error is none at all. Here 间 takes the jiān reading for the gap itself.
量力而行	liàng lì ér xíng	measure your strength, then act: do only what you can manage — 量 liàng (to assess, here in the assessing sense) + 力 lì (strength) + 而 ér (and then) + 行 xíng (to act). Gauge how much you are capable of, and act within that limit. A counsel of prudence: take on what your resources, ability, and circumstances can actually support, rather than overreaching.
量入为出	liàng rù wéi chū	measure income to set spending: live within your means — 量 liàng (to gauge) + 入 rù (income, what comes in) + 为 wéi (to make, to determine) + 出 chū (expenditure, what goes out). Let the size of your income decide the size of your spending.
寥寥无几	liáo liáo wú jǐ	very few; scarcely any — Literally "sparse, sparse, not even a few," describing something so scarce it can almost be counted on one hand. Here 几 ("a few") takes its statement sense rather than its question sense. The mirror image of 多多益善.
门当户对	mén dāng hù duì	matched gates and doors: of equal social standing (for a marriage) — Lit: gate-matching-door-matching. The old principle that the two families in a marriage should be of comparable rank and wealth. Still used, sometimes critically, of pairing by status.
门庭若市	mén tíng ruò shì	the gateway like a marketplace: thronged with visitors — Lit: gate-courtyard-like-market. Said of a household or office so busy with callers that the entrance resembles a crowded market. Its opposite is 门可罗雀, a gate so deserted you could net sparrows in it.
妙手回春	miào shǒu huí chūn	miraculous hands bring back the spring; masterly healing — 妙手 (wondrous hands) + 回春 (return spring, restore life's season). The stock phrase of gratitude to a doctor, embroidered on the silk banners (锦旗 jǐnqí) that patients present to hospital departments, a living custom: the walls of a respected clinic hang red with them.
难分难解	nán fēn nán jiě	hard to divide and hard to untangle: locked together, evenly matched — Lit: hard-to-separate-hard-to-loosen. Of a contest too close to call, or of people so entwined, whether fighting or in love, that they cannot be pulled apart.
恰如其分	qià rú qí fèn	exactly to its measure: just right, neither too much nor too little — Lit: precisely-matching-its-portion. Praise for words or actions pitched at exactly the right degree. Here 分 takes the fèn reading, the proper allotted measure.
亲密无间	qīn mì wú jiàn	close with no gap between: utterly intimate, inseparable — Lit: intimate-close-without-gap. Of a friendship or bond so tight that no space, no estrangement, divides the two. Here 间 takes the jiàn reading, the gap as separation.
群龙无首	qún lóng wú shǒu	a flock of dragons with no head: a leaderless, directionless group — Lit: flock-dragons-without-head. A phrase from the Book of Changes: many capable members but no one to lead them, so the group drifts. Used of an organization left rudderless after its leader is gone.
是非分明	shì fēi fēn míng	right and wrong clearly divided: drawing a sharp line between them — Lit: right-wrong-divide-clear. Said in praise of someone with firm moral judgment who does not blur what is right with what is wrong.
首屈一指	shǒu qū yī zhǐ	the first finger you bend to count: second to none, the very best — Lit: first-bend-one-finger. When you count off the best on your fingers, this is the one you bend down first. Used to rank a person or thing as the foremost in its field. 他在这个行业首屈一指 "He is second to none in this industry."
挑拨离间	tiǎo bō lí jiàn	to stir up trouble and drive a wedge: to set people against each other — Lit: stir-up and sow-division. To deliberately create a gap between people who were close, by rumor or provocation. 离间 (jiàn) is to open a rift where there was none.
痛心疾首	tòng xīn jí shǒu	pain in the heart and ache in the head: bitterly grieved and resentful — Lit: pain-heart-ache-head. To feel grief and indignation so deep that heart and head both ache. Used for bitter regret or anger over a wrong or a loss. 对这种浪费，他感到痛心疾首 "He was bitterly grieved at such waste."
万紫千红	wàn zǐ qiān hóng	ten thousand purples, a thousand reds — a riot of color; abundant beauty — From the Song dynasty poet Zhu Xi 朱熹: 等闲识得东风面，万紫千红总是春 , "It is easy to recognize the face of the east wind: ten thousand purples and a thousand reds , it is all spring." The phrase moved from the poem into common use as the standard expression for spring's visual abundance.
眼花缭乱	yǎn huā liáo luàn	eyes blurring, vision tangling — dazzled, overwhelmed by abundance — Lit: eye-flower-tangle-disorder. Used for the sensory overload of a busy market, a fireworks display, a dazzling performance: too much beauty or activity to take in at once. Almost always positive in tone (the overload of richness, not chaos).
一点一滴	yì diǎn yì dī	a dot and a drop: bit by bit, little by little — Lit: one-dot-one-drop. The small increments by which patient effort accumulates. 一点一滴地积累 "to build up bit by bit."
一个个	yí ge gè	one by one; one after another — The triple form stresses that things happen or are dealt with individually, in sequence. 学生一个个走进教室 "the students filed into the classroom one by one."
一件不漏	yī jiàn bù lòu	not letting a single item slip: accounting for every piece, leaving nothing out — 漏 lòu = to leak; to omit. A colloquial set phrase built on 件, used when an inventory, a checklist, or a list of tasks is completed in full with no item missed. 他把行李一件不漏地清点了一遍。(He counted every piece of luggage without missing one.)
一件件	yī jiàn jiàn	one item at a time: piece by piece, one matter after another — A reduplicated measure-word phrase rather than a classical chengyu, but extremely common in speech. Doubling 件 turns it into an adverbial sense of taking things one at a time. 我们一件件地处理这些问题。(Let us handle these problems one by one.)
一举两得	yī jǔ liǎng dé	one move, two gains — kill two birds with one stone — Lit: one-raise/move-two-gains. 举 here is a verbal measure word (one lift/movement). A perfect analogue of "kill two birds with one stone." 这样做一举两得 "Doing it this way achieves two goals at once." Among the most common four-character idioms in business and daily speech.
一石二鸟	yī shí èr niǎo	one stone, two birds — accomplish two goals with a single action — The Chinese and English idioms are structurally identical. The image of a single stone bringing down two birds appears to have developed independently in both traditions, though the Chinese form likely circulated in literary Chinese before modern contact. In use: 这个方案真是一石二鸟。
一眼万年	yī yǎn wàn nián	a single glance, ten thousand years — a look that lasts forever — Lit: one-glance-ten-thousand-years. Modern romantic register, especially in song lyrics and pop fiction: the glance at first meeting that settles a lifetime. Builds on 眼 as the measure word for a single act of looking.
一字千金	yī zì qiān jīn	one character, a thousand gold pieces — words of immense value — 字 here is a classifier for characters/words. 金 (gold pieces) is the unit of price. From the story of Lü Buwei's 吕不韦 text 《吕氏春秋》: he challenged anyone to change a single character and offered a thousand gold pieces if they could improve it. Used to praise literary excellence.
有眼无珠	yǒu yǎn wú zhū	to have eyes but no pupils — to fail to recognize what is right in front of you — Lit: have-eyes-without-pearls (珠 = pearl, here pupil). Said of someone who has missed an obvious quality or opportunity: failed to spot a talent, dismissed a worthy person. Often self-deprecating: 我真是有眼无珠 "I had eyes but couldn't see."
原原本本	yuán yuán běn běn	from source and root: in full and exact detail, from start to finish — The doubled 原 (source) and 本 (root). To tell something 原原本本 is to give the whole account faithfully, leaving nothing out and adding nothing.
指指点点	zhǐ zhǐ diǎn diǎn	to point and gesture: to criticize or gossip about others behind their backs — The reduplicated 指点 zhǐdiǎn (to point out, to instruct) turns sour here: to keep pointing fingers and passing judgment on what is not one's business.
字里行间	zì lǐ háng jiān	within the words and between the lines: the meaning an author implies but does not state — Lit: inside-the-characters, between-the-lines. The unspoken sense that lives in the spaces of a text. 字里行间流露出真情 "true feeling shows between the lines."
罪魁祸首	zuì kuí huò shǒu	the ringleader of the crime and the chief of the disaster: the main culprit — Lit: crime-chief-disaster-head. The principal wrongdoer, the one most to blame for an offense or calamity. Here 首 is the head, the ringleader. 他是这场事故的罪魁祸首 "He is the chief culprit behind this accident."
多少 / 几	duō shao / jǐ	how many, how much — The two question words for quantity: 几 for small countable numbers and 多少 for larger or unknown amounts, why 几 needs a measure word and 多少 may skip it, and how to ask prices and ages.
量词	liàng cí	measure words — 个, 只, 条, 本, 张: the classifier taxonomy and the logic behind it.
案件	àn jiàn	a legal case; an incident under investigation — 案 àn (case; record; desk on which records were kept) + 件 jiàn (a piece). A discrete legal or police matter, treated as a single file of business. 案 alone meant the official desk and then the file of papers on it; with 件 it becomes one such case, counted and pursued on its own.
昂首	áng shǒu	to hold the head high; head raised — 昂 áng (to raise, to lift up) + 首 shǒu (head). To carry the head high, in pride or defiance. 昂首挺胸 "head high and chest out" is the stock image of someone walking with proud, confident bearing. Literary in tone; the spoken equivalent uses 头 tóu.
本来	běn lái	originally; at first; (it goes without saying that) — 本 běn (root; origin) + 来 lái (come). What was the case from the start, before things changed: 我本来想去 "I originally meant to go (but didn't)." It often carries a quiet sense that the original state was the natural or proper one.
本人	běn rén	oneself; the person in question; I (formal) — 本 běn (this; one's own) + 人 rén (person). The very person concerned, as opposed to a representative or a rumor. On forms and in formal speech it means "I myself": 本人同意 "I personally agree."
本事	běn shi	ability; skill; what one is capable of — 本 běn (root; one's own) + 事 shì (matter). The capacities a person carries in themselves, their real skill rather than their luck or connections: 有本事 "to have what it takes." A close synonym is 本领 běnlǐng.
本质	běn zhì	essence
本子	běn zi	notebook; exercise book; pad — 本 běn (volume) + 子 zi (suffix). A blank or ruled book to write in. Here 本 takes the toneless noun-suffix 子, exactly as in 桌子 and 房子.
闭	bì	to close, to shut (formal)
部队	bù duì	military unit; troops; armed forces — 部 bù (unit) + 队 duì (team; formation). A body of troops under one command, from a single unit up to the armed forces in general: 参军入部队 "to join the army." Soldiers say 在部队 "in the service."
部分	bù fen	part; section; portion — 部 bù (section) + 分 fen (a share). A part of something larger, the everyday word for a portion: 大部分 dàbùfen "the greater part, most of," 一部分 yí bùfen "a portion." Note the second syllable goes neutral.
部件	bù jiàn	component; assembly
部门	bù mén	department; division; branch — 部 bù (section) + 门 mén (gate; category). One demarcated wing of a company or institution: 销售部门 "the sales department," 政府部门 "government departments." The standard word for an organizational unit.
部首	bù shǒu	radical (the section head of a character) — 部 bù (section, division) + 首 shǒu (head). The radical of a character: the head-element under which a dictionary groups it. 首 itself is a 部首. To look a character up in a paper dictionary, you find its 部首 first, then count the remaining strokes.
部长	bù zhǎng	minister; head of a department — 部 bù (ministry) + 长 zhǎng (chief; head). The head of a ministry or department: 外交部长 "the Foreign Minister." The 长 here is the leader-sense, read zhǎng, not the cháng of "long."
测量	cè liáng	to measure: to survey, to gauge with an instrument — 测 cè (to gauge, to fathom) + 量 liáng (to measure). The formal verb for taking precise measurements: surveying land, gauging distance, recording temperature or pressure with an instrument. 测量土地 (to survey land), 测量距离 (to measure distance).
差不多	chà bu duō	almost; about the same
充分	chōng fèn	ample; full; sufficient — 充 chōng (to fill) + 分 fèn (measure; share). Filled to the proper measure, fully sufficient: 充分的准备 "ample preparation," 充分利用 "to make full use of." Note the fèn reading.
出门	chū mén	to go out; to leave home; to set off on a trip — 出 chū (to exit) + 门 mén (door). To step out the door, whether for the day or for a journey: 我出门了 "I'm heading out." Its opposite is 回家 huí jiā or 进门 jìnmén.
窗	chuāng	window
次	cì	time; occurrence
大量	dà liàng	a large amount: a great quantity of — 大 dà (big) + 量 liàng (amount). Functions as a quantifier before mass and plural nouns: 大量资金 (a large amount of capital), 大量时间 (a great deal of time), 大量生产 (to mass-produce).
地点	dì diǎn	place; location; site; venue — 地 dì (ground) + 点 diǎn (point). A specific point on the ground, the precise spot where something happens: a meeting place, an event venue, the scene of an incident. More exact than 地方 dìfang.
点菜	diǎn cài	to order dishes (at a restaurant) — 点 diǎn (to choose; to dot) + 菜 cài (dish). To pick dishes off a menu and order them. The host usually does the 点菜 for the table. For the many senses of 点 see the 点 entry.
点头	diǎn tóu	to nod (the head) — 点 diǎn (to touch lightly, to dip) + 头 tóu (head). To dip the head: to nod in agreement or greeting. 点头同意 "to nod in assent." Its opposite gesture is 摇头 yáotóu, to shake the head.
点心	diǎn xīn	Dim sum — Dim sum: small Cantonese dishes served with tea in the teahouse tradition. The term literally means "touch the heart." Dim sum culture grew from the Cantonese tea-drinking and commercial meeting tradition of Guangdong and Hong Kong and is now the most internationally recognized format of Chinese com…
动量词	dòng liàng cí	verbal measure word (次/遍/回)
多大	duō dà	how old; how big
多少钱	duō shao qián	how much (money); what's the price — The universal way to ask a price. Because cost can be any amount, you use 多少, never 几: 这件衣服多少钱？("how much is this piece of clothing?"). The answer uses money measure words: 块 kuài (yuan, colloquial), 毛 máo (ten cents), 分 fēn (cent). 一共多少钱 ("how much altogether") totals a bill.
返回	fǎn huí	to return; go back
房间	fáng jiān	room (inside a building) — 房 fáng (room; building-section) + 间 jiān (space between; a measure word for rooms). The standard neutral word for a room in a house, hotel, or office. 三室两厅 = three bedrooms two living rooms (standard real-estate description).
分开	fēn kāi	to separate; to part; to divide up — 分 fēn (divide) + 开 kāi (open; apart). To pull two things apart or keep them distinct: 把垃圾分开 "sort the rubbish," 我们分开走 "let's go separately."
分类	fēn lèi	to classify; to sort into categories — 分 fēn (divide) + 类 lèi (kind; category). To divide things into kinds: 垃圾分类 "waste sorting," 图书分类 "cataloguing books."
分配	fēn pèi	to allot; distribute
分手	fēn shǒu	to part ways; to break up (a couple) — 分 fēn (separate) + 手 shǒu (hand). To let go of one another's hand. In everyday use it most often means a romantic break-up, but it can simply mean two people parting at a crossroads.
分数	fēn shù	score; mark; (math) fraction — 分 fēn (point) + 数 shù (number). A test score, and in mathematics a fraction. Both senses share the idea of a number standing below or against a whole.
分钟	fēn zhōng	minute (of time) — 分 fēn (a divided unit) + 钟 zhōng (clock). A minute as a span of time: 等五分钟 "wait five minutes." To tell the time on the clock face, 分 alone is enough: 两点十五分 "two fifteen."
份	fèn	measure word for copies and portions
俯首	fǔ shǒu	to bow the head; to submit — 俯 fǔ (to bend down, to lower) + 首 shǒu (head). To lower the head, and by extension to yield or submit. 俯首听命 "to bow the head and take orders" describes obedient submission. The direct opposite of 昂首.
干部	gàn bù	cadre; official
个别	gè bié	individual; a small number of; exceptional — 个 gè (single) + 别 bié (separate; other). Either "one by one, individually" (个别谈话 "to talk to people one at a time") or "only a few, exceptional" (个别情况 "a few isolated cases").
个人	gèr én	individual; personal; in one's own view — 个 gè (single) + 人 rén (person). The single person set against the group, and by extension "personal, private": 个人观点 "a personal opinion," 个人信息 "personal information." Its foil is 集体 jítǐ (the collective).
个体	gè tǐ	an individual (entity); a single member — 个 gè (single) + 体 tǐ (body; entity). A single unit within a larger whole, used in biology and economics alike: 个体差异 "individual differences," 个体户 "a self-employed business owner."
个性	gè xìng	individuality; individual character; distinctiveness — 个 gè (individual) + 性 xìng (nature). The traits that distinguish one person from another, the stamp of the individual: 有个性 "to have a strong personality, to be distinctive," 个性化 "personalized, customized." Often praised as a mark of a person who is not interchangeable with the crowd.
个子	gè zi	height; stature; build (of a person) — 个 gè + 子 zi (noun-suffix). A person's height or physical build: 高个子 "tall build," 矮个子 "short build," 个子很高 "very tall." Used of the whole frame of a person, not a measured number.
各	gè	each; every (distributive)
根本	gēn běn	fundamental; basic; (with a negative) at all — 根 gēn (root) + 本 běn (root). The two words for "root" doubled. As an adjective, fundamental or essential: 根本问题 "the fundamental issue." As an adverb with a negative, it means "not at all": 我根本不知道 "I had no idea whatsoever."
关门	guān mén	to close the door; (of a shop) to close for the day or for good
观察	guān chá	to observe; to watch closely; observation — 观 guān (to look) + 察 chá (to examine). To watch with attention in order to learn something: a scientist, a doctor, or a detective 观察 their subject. Stronger and more deliberate than simply watching.
很多	hěn duō	a lot; many
回答	huí dá	to answer; to reply; an answer — 回 huí (back) + 答 dá (to respond). To send a response back to a question: 回答问题 "to answer a question." As a noun, the answer itself: 一个满意的回答 "a satisfactory answer."
回家	huí jiā	to go home; to return home — 回 huí (to return) + 家 jiā (home). One of the most common V+location phrases in the language. The directional verb 回 indicates movement back toward an origin point: home as the natural destination of return, not merely a place you happen to go.
回来	huí lái	to come back; to return — 回 huí (to return; to turn back) + 来 lái (toward here). To turn and come back toward the speaker's reference point, usually home, a shared location, or where you were before leaving. The counterpart is 回去 huíqù (to go back, returning away from the speaker).
回去	huí qù	to go back
回头	huí tóu	to turn one's head; later; to repent — 回 huí (turn back) + 头 tóu (head). To turn the head around, and by extension "in a little while" (回头见 "see you later") or to turn back from a wrong path (回头是岸 "to repent is to reach the shore").
回忆	huí yì	to recall; to look back on; a memory — 回 huí (turn back) + 忆 yì (to remember). To turn the mind back to the past. As a noun, a remembered scene: 美好的回忆 "fond memories." The eddy here loops through time rather than space.
基本	jī běn	basic; fundamental; (基本上) basically, on the whole — 基 jī (foundation) + 本 běn (root). The base layer of something: 基本功 jīběngōng (basic skills), 基本工资 (base salary). The adverb 基本上 jīběnshàng means "basically, by and large."
集体	jí tǐ	collective; group; the community — 集 jí (to gather) + 体 tǐ (body). A gathered body of people acting as one: 集体活动 "a group activity," 集体主义 "collectivism." Set against the individual, 个人 gèrén.
几	jǐ	how many (small number); a few — Asks about a small, countable number, typically under ten, and must be followed by a measure word: 几个 ("how many"), 几本 ("how many volumes"), 几位 ("how many people," polite). It assumes the answer is small, so it is the word for guests, family members, hours, or days.
几个	jǐ ge	a few; several; how many — 几 jǐ (how many; a few) + 个 ge. Either a question ("how many?") or a vague small number ("a few"), depending on context: 几个人 "how many people?" or "a few people."
节骨眼	jié gǔ yǎn	critical juncture; the pivot point of a situation — 节 jié (joint) + 骨 gǔ (bone) + 眼. The eye at the joint of the bone , the precise small spot at which a thing turns. Colloquial; almost always paired with 上 in 节骨眼上 "at the critical juncture."
酒量	jiǔ liàng	drinking capacity: how much alcohol one can hold — 酒 jiǔ (alcohol) + 量 liàng (capacity). A person's tolerance for drink, the amount they can take before they are affected. A culturally loaded word at the banquet table, where 酒量好 (a good capacity) is treated as a social asset: 他酒量很大 (he can really hold his drink).
局部	jú bù	a local part; partial
开门	kāi mén	to open the door; (of a shop) to open for business — 开 kāi (to open) + 门 mén (door). To open a door, and idiomatically for a shop to open: 商店几点开门？ "What time does the shop open?" Its pair is 关门 guānmén, to close.
课本	kè běn	textbook — 课 kè (lesson) + 本 běn (volume). The book for a course of study. A close synonym is 教科书 jiàokēshū, slightly more formal.
空间	kōng jiān	space; room (abstract); scope — 空 kōng (empty) + 间 jiān (space). Empty space, both physical and figurative: 没有空间 "no room," 发展空间 "room to grow." Paired with 时间, the two name space and time together.
筷子	kuài zi	chopsticks
礼物	lǐ wù	gift; present — 礼 lǐ (ritual offering) + 物 wù (thing; object; matter). Lit. "a ritual-offering made material." A gift in Chinese culture is always, at some level, a ritual object that carries social meaning exceeding its material value.
力量	lì liàng	strength: force, power, capability — 力 lì (force, strength) + 量 liàng (measure, amount). The measurable quantity of force, whether physical, social, or moral: 体力的力量 (bodily strength), 团结的力量 (the power of unity), 知识就是力量 (knowledge is power).
量体温	liáng tǐ wēn	to take a temperature: to measure body heat — 量 liáng (to measure) + 体温 tǐwēn (body temperature). A high-frequency everyday verb-object phrase: to take someone's temperature with a thermometer. The same 量 measures other body data in parallel phrases, 量血压 (liáng xuèyā, to take blood pressure), 量身高 (liáng shēngāo, to measure height).
零件	líng jiàn	spare part; component part — 零 líng (fragmentary; odd; loose) + 件 jiàn (a piece). The small individual parts that make up a machine, especially replaceable ones. 零 carries the sense of scattered, loose pieces, so 零件 is the loose hardware: screws, gears, the bits kept in a parts drawer.
每	měi	every, each one of an identical series — 每 walks through a set member by member: 每个人 (every person), 每本书 (every book). It requires a measure word before the noun, with a short list of exceptions that are themselves measure-like: 每天, 每年, 每次 (day, year, time).
门口	mén kǒu	doorway; entrance — 门 mén (door) + 口 kǒu (mouth; opening). The mouth of the door, the spot just at the entrance: 在门口等 "to wait at the door," 学校门口 "the school gate."
门票	mén piào	entrance ticket: an admission ticket; the entry fee — 门 mén (door; gate; entrance) + 票 piào (slip; ticket). The ticket that admits you through the gate: into a park, a museum, a temple, a concert. By extension it also names the price of admission, what you pay at the door.
末	mò	secondary older male roles — The 末 was originally a distinct category for secondary older male characters -- supporting figures who are not the central hero (that is 老生) but are male, older, and positive or neutral in moral valence. In modern Peking Opera practice, 末 has merged almost entirely into 老生.
那个	nà ge	that one — "Uh…" or "that…" The single most common verbal filler in Mandarin, used to stall for half a second while the next word arrives. Stretched out as 那个… 那个… it functions almost identically to English "um." Overusing it sounds hesitant; sprinkling it in sounds native.
内部	nèi bù	internal; the interior; inside (of an organization) — 内 nèi (inside) + 部 bù (section). The inner part of a body, machine, or group, often the confidential inside of an organization: 内部消息 "inside information," 内部会议 "an internal meeting." Its pair is 外部 wàibù (external).
期间	qī jiān	period; during the time of — 期 qī (a fixed period) + 间 jiān (interval). A defined stretch of time, often used after an event to mean "during": 假期期间 "during the holidays," 会议期间 "in the course of the meeting."
起初	qǐ chū	at first; in the beginning
全部	quán bù	all; the whole; entire — 全 quán (whole) + 部 bù (part; section). The complete set of parts, every one included: 全部作品 "the complete works," 全部完成 "all finished." It points at the totality of a definite group of things.
泉眼	quán yǎn	the eye of a spring; the place where spring water emerges — 泉 quán (spring) + 眼. The mouth of a natural spring , the small opening from which water bubbles up. Common in classical Chinese poetry and travel writing.
缺点	qu ē diǎn	shortcoming
容量	róng liàng	capacity: how much a container or system can hold — 容 róng (to contain, to hold) + 量 liàng (amount). The maximum a vessel, device, or system can take in: a bottle's volume, a battery's charge, a hard drive's storage, a hall's seating. 电池容量 (battery capacity), 硬盘容量 (disk capacity), 存储容量 (storage capacity).
入门	rù mén	to get started; to cross the threshold of a subject; a primer — 入 rù (to enter) + 门 mén (door). To step across the doorway of a field, to grasp its basics. A book titled 《汉语入门》 is "Chinese: A Primer," a doorway into the language.
软件	ruǎn jiàn	software — 软 ruǎn (soft) + 件 jiàn (a piece). The intangible programs that run on a computer, paired against 硬件 yìngjiàn (hardware), 硬 yìng meaning hard. The soft and hard contrast is a direct calque of the English pair, with 件 supplying the shared unit-of-a-system sense.
商量	shāng liang	to discuss: to talk something over, to consult — 商 shāng (to consult, to confer) + 量 liang (measure, toneless here). To talk a matter over with someone before deciding: to weigh it between two minds.
少量	shǎo liàng	a small amount
身份	shēn fèn	identity; status — 身 (self) + 份 (share, allotment): one's allotted position. 身份证 (shēnfènzhèng) is the national ID card, the document requested at every train station, hotel, and SIM-card counter, which makes this compound survival vocabulary rather than abstraction.
事件	shì jiàn	incident; event
视线	shì xiàn	line of sight; gaze
首次	shǒu cì	the first time; for the first time — 首 shǒu (first) + 次 cì (time, occurrence). The first occasion on which something happens, common in news and formal writing. 首次访问 "a first visit," 首次公开 "made public for the first time." More formal than the spoken 第一次 dì-yī cì.
首都	shǒu dū	capital city — 首 shǒu (head, chief) + 都 dū (metropolis, city). The head-city of a country, its seat of government. 中国的首都是北京 "China's capital is Beijing." Note the reading: here 都 takes its first tone dū (city), not the toneless dōu (all).
首领	shǒu lǐng	chieftain; leader; head (of a group) — 首 shǒu (head, chief) + 领 lǐng (to lead; the neck, the collar). The leader of a tribe, band, or organization. Carries a slightly traditional or martial flavor: the chief of a clan, the head of an outfit. 部落首领 "a tribal chieftain."
首先	shǒu xiān	first of all; firstly; in the first place — 首 shǒu (first) + 先 xiān (before, prior). The standard opener for an ordered list of points, often paired later with 其次 qícì (next) and 最后 zuìhòu (finally). Marks the very first item in a sequence of reasons, steps, or arguments.
首要	shǒu yào	of prime importance; the foremost — 首 shǒu (first) + 要 yào (important). Marking the single most important matter, ranking above all others. 首要任务 "the primary task," 首要问题 "the chief problem." Stronger and more formal than 重要 zhòngyào (important), since it claims the top spot, not just high rank.
数词	shù cí	numeral
数量	shù liàng	quantity: the amount or number of something — 数 shù (number) + 量 liàng (amount). The neutral, countable "how many / how much" of a thing. 数量 answers questions of scale and stock: how many units, how large a supply.
瞬间	shùn jiān	an instant; the blink of an eye — 瞬 shùn (a blink) + 间 jiān (interval). The smallest interval of time, the moment it takes to blink: 一瞬间 "in a flash." The interval shrunk almost to nothing.
所有	suǒ yǒu	all; every; everything one has — 所 suǒ (that which; a place; nominalizing particle) + 有 (have/exist). 所有 as a determiner: 所有的学生 (all the students). As a noun: 他失去了所有 (He lost everything he had). As a formal possession compound in legal/written Chinese: 所有权 suǒyǒuquán = ownership rights, title.
条	tiáo	measure word for long thin items
外部	wài bù	external; the outside
卫生间	wèi shēng jiān	bathroom; toilet; restroom — 卫生 wèishēng (hygiene) + 间 jiān (room). The polite, standard word for a bathroom in homes and public buildings. A common synonym is 洗手间 xǐshǒujiān.
文件	wén jiàn	document; file; paperwork — 文 wén (writing; text) + 件 jiàn (a piece). A written document, an official paper, or a computer file. The word covers both physical paperwork and digital files: on a screen, 文件 is exactly the word for a file, and 文件夹 wénjiànjiā is a folder.
瞎	xiā	blind
心眼	xīn yǎn ( r )	wits; smarts; (also) intent, motive of the heart — 心 xīn (heart) + 眼. The eye of the heart , the inner faculty of judgment and quick-wittedness. 心眼多 xīnyǎn duō "to have many wits" (clever, calculating, or scheming, depending on tone). 心眼好 xīnyǎn hǎo "to have a good heart-eye" (kind-hearted, well-intentioned).
信件	xìn jiàn	letters; correspondence (as a category) — 信 xìn (letter; trust) + 件 jiàn (a piece). A collective, somewhat formal term for letters and written correspondence. A single letter is 一封信 (using 封 fēng); 信件 names the whole category of correspondence, the kind of word a post office or office form would use.
眼光	yǎng uāng	vision; taste; judgment; the discerning eye — 眼 yǎn + 光 guāng (light). Literally eye-light. The kind of seeing a person has , their taste in art, their judgment in business, their ability to spot a good investment or a good partner. 有眼光 yǒu yǎnguāng "to have a good eye"; 没眼光 "to have poor judgment"; 长远的眼光 "long-term vision."
眼界	yǎn jiè	horizon (of experience); breadth of one's outlook — 眼 yǎn + 界 jiè (boundary; realm). The boundary of what one's eye has taken in , how widely one has seen, traveled, read, lived. 开阔眼界 kāikuò yǎnjiè "to broaden one's horizons" is one of the standard reasons given in Chinese for travel, study abroad, and reading widely.
眼睛	yǎn jing	eye(s); the eyes as organ — 眼 yǎn + 睛 jīng (eyeball; pupil). The standard everyday word for "eyes" in spoken Chinese. Single 眼 alone almost never serves this role; for "her eyes are beautiful" you say 她的眼睛很美 not 她的眼很美. The 睛 is light and unstressed in pronunciation.
眼泪	yǎn lèi	tears — 眼 yǎn + 泪 lèi (tear). Standard word for tears. 流眼泪 liú yǎnlèi "to shed tears"; 擦眼泪 cā yǎnlèi "to wipe away tears." The classical/literary equivalent is just 泪 alone or 泪水 lèishuǐ.
眼力	yǎn lì	perceptiveness; discerning power; visual acuity — 眼 yǎn + 力 lì (strength). The strength of one's seeing , both literally (sharpness of vision) and figuratively (judgment, ability to spot quality or fakery). The skilled antique-dealer is praised for 眼力 in detecting forgeries.
眼皮	yǎn pí	eyelid — 眼 yǎn + 皮 pí (skin). Eyelid. The colloquial idiom 眼皮底下 yǎnpí dǐxià "right under one's eyelids" means "right in front of one, in plain sight" , usually used to describe something missed or stolen despite being right there.
眼神	yǎn shén	the look in one's eyes; gaze; expression — 眼 yǎn + 神 shén (spirit; expression). Not the eyes themselves but the spirit visible in them , the look one gives, the expression of feeling that the eyes carry. A high-frequency word in fiction and dialogue: 他的眼神很温柔 "the look in his eyes was very gentle"; 用眼神告诉我 "tell me with your eyes."
摇头	yáo tóu	to shake the head
钥匙	yào shi	key
一杯水	yī bēi shuǐ	a cup / glass of water — 杯 bēi — 杯 bēi (cup, glass) counts liquids held in cups or glasses. The container becomes the classifier. This is the most transparent type: the classifier is the vessel. A large class of classifiers work this way , 碗, 盘, 瓶, 桶, 壶 , each named for the physical container holding the substance.
一部电影	yí bù diàn yǐng	a film; one movie — 一 yī (one) + 部 bù (measure word) + 电影 diànyǐng (film). The standard way to count a film, treating it as a single complete work. The same 部 counts a 小说 (novel) or a 电视剧 (TV series).
一部手机	yí bù shǒu jī	a mobile phone; one cellphone — 一 yī (one) + 部 bù (measure word) + 手机 shǒujī (phone). 部 counts a phone as a self-contained device. You will also hear 一个手机 in casual speech, but 一部手机 is the proper classifier.
一部小说	yí bù xiǎo shuō	a novel; one work of fiction — 一 yī (one) + 部 bù (measure word) + 小说 xiǎoshuō (novel). 部 counts a full-length novel as one finished work. For a short story you would more likely use 篇 piān, the classifier for shorter writings.
一点儿	yì diǎn r	a little; a bit (neutral, often comparative) — After an adjective, 一点儿 makes a neutral comparison, "a little more X": 大一点儿 (a bit bigger), 快一点儿 (a bit faster), 便宜一点儿 (a little cheaper). After a verb, it means "a little / some": 喝一点儿水 (drink a little water), 说一点儿 (say a bit).
一个	yí gè	one (with general measure word) — 一 + 个 (general measure word). 一 before tone 4 (个 gè) → yí. The most frequent combination of 一 in everyday speech: 一个人 yí gè rén (one person), 一个问题 yí gè wèntí (one question), 一个地方 yí gè dìfang (one place). 个 gè is the default measure word when no specific one applies.
一件礼物	yī jiàn lǐ wù	a gift: one present — 一 yī (one) + 件 jiàn (measure word) + 礼物 lǐwù (gift; present). A present counts as a discrete object, so it takes 件. The same classifier extends to other countable belongings and pieces of luggage: 一件行李 (one piece of luggage), 一件作品 (one work of art).
一件事	yī jiàn shì	a matter: one affair; one thing to deal with — 一 yī (one) + 件 jiàn (measure word) + 事 shì (matter; affair). 件 is the standard classifier for an affair, an errand, or a piece of business, anything that can be treated as a single, separable concern. You do not say 一个事 in careful speech; the matter takes 件.
一件衣服	yī jiàn yī fu	an item of clothing: one garment — 一 yī (one) + 件 jiàn (measure word) + 衣服 yīfu (clothing). 件 counts upper-body and one-piece garments: shirts, coats, dresses, sweaters. Lower-body garments take 条 tiáo instead (一条裤子 a pair of trousers, 一条裙子 a skirt), so the measure word itself signals which part of the body is meant.
一首歌	yī shǒu gē	one song; a song — 一 yī (one) + 首 shǒu (measure word) + 歌 gē (song). 首 is the classifier for songs and poems: 一首歌 a song, 一首诗 a poem, 一首词 a lyric. Each piece is counted as one self-contained "head." 唱一首歌 "to sing a song."
一首诗	yī shǒu shī	one poem; a poem — 一 yī (one) + 首 shǒu (measure word) + 诗 shī (poem). The same classifier counts poems. 写一首诗 "to write a poem," 背一首诗 "to recite a poem from memory." A classical anthology is described as so many 首 of verse.
一些	yī xiē	some (partitive)
衣服	yī fu	clothes
硬件	yìng jiàn	hardware
优点	yōu diǎn	strong point; merit; advantage — 优 yōu (excellent) + 点 diǎn (point). A good point, a strength. Its exact opposite is 缺点 quēdiǎn, a shortcoming. Together they are the standard pair for weighing a person, plan, or product.
邮件	yóu jiàn	mail; email; a postal item — 邮 yóu (post; mail) + 件 jiàn (a piece). Originally any item sent through the post. In everyday modern use it most often means email: 电子邮件 diànzǐ yóujiàn (electronic mail) is regularly shortened to 邮件. To send one is 发邮件 fā yóujiàn.
有点儿	yǒu diǎn r	a bit; somewhat (often "a bit too…") — Placed before an adjective (or a feeling-verb) to say something is "a bit / somewhat" that way, usually with a tinge of complaint or an unwanted state. 有点儿 + 累 / 冷 / 贵 / 难 = a bit tired / cold / expensive / hard. It describes a current condition the speaker finds slightly undesirable.
元首	yuán shǒu	head of state
原来	yuán lái	originally; as it turns out; so that's how it is — 原 yuán (original; the root; where something came from) + 来 lái (came from; arrived from). "From the original state," used in two senses. Temporal: originally, at first (他原来住在北京 = He originally lived in Beijing). Revelatory: "so that's how it is / now I understand" (原来如此！= So that's the reason!).
原因	yuán yīn	cause; reason — 原 yuán (origin; source) + 因 yīn (cause). The standard noun for the cause or reason behind something: 失败的原因 "the reason for the failure." Its pair is 结果 jiéguǒ, the result.
张	zhāng	measure word for flat things
这个	zhè ge	this; this one — 这 zhè (this) + 个 ge. The demonstrative "this one," with 个 supplying the classifier slot. Its pair is 那个 nàge (that one). Often heard as a filler too, like an English "um."
这回	zhè huí	this time; on this occasion — 这 zhè (this) + 回 huí (time). The colloquial "this time," interchangeable with 这次 zhè cì but a touch more casual: 这回我一定赢 "this time I'll win for sure."
针眼	zhēn yǎn	the eye of a needle; (medical) sty — 针 zhēn (needle) + 眼. The hole in a sewing needle , the same metaphor English uses. Also a colloquial term for a sty on the eyelid (the small swelling looking like a tiny eye).
睁	zhēng	to open (the eyes)
整个	zhěng gè	the whole; entire — 整 zhěng (whole) + 个 gè. "The whole of" something taken as one piece: 整个城市 "the whole city," 整个晚上 "the entire evening." Here 个 keeps its sense of a single complete unit.
整体	zhěng tǐ	the whole; the entirety; as a whole — 整 zhěng (whole, complete) + 体 tǐ (body). A thing taken as one integrated body rather than as separate parts. Its standing contrast is 部分 bùfen, a part: 整体利益 "the interests of the whole."
之间	zhī jiān	between; among (more formal) — 之 zhī (classical possessive) + 间 jiān (space between). The bookish, frequent way to say "between": 朋友之间 "between friends," 国与国之间 "between nations." It frames the space within a relationship or a set.
只	zhī	measure word for animals
指示代词	zhǐ shì dài cí	demonstrative pronoun (这/那/哪)
质量	zhì liàng	quality: how good something is (also physical mass) — 质 zhì (substance, nature) + 量 liàng (measure). In everyday speech 质量 is quality, the standard or grade of a product or piece of work: 质量好 (good quality), 提高质量 (to raise quality). In physics the same word is the technical term for mass. Context separates the two cleanly.
中间	zhōng jiān	middle; center; in between; among — 中 zhōng (center) + 间 jiān (space). The space in the middle, whether of a row, a room, or a stretch of time: 坐在中间 "to sit in the middle," 在两座山中间 "between two mountains."
钟	zhōng	clock; o'clock
重点	zhòng diǎn	key point; focus; emphasis; priority — 重 zhòng (heavy; important) + 点 diǎn (point). The heavy point, the part that matters most. 重点大学 "key universities," 划重点 "to mark the key points," the things to underline.
重量	zhòng liàng	weight (the physical quantity) — 重 zhòng (heavy) + 量 liàng (quantity; measure). The amount of heaviness, weight as measured: 重量很轻 "the weight is light." The bodily version is 体重 tǐzhòng, one's body weight.
注意	zhù yì	to pay attention; to take note; to watch out — 注 zhù (to pour; to fix) + 意 yì (notion; attention). To pour the mind onto something, that is, to pay attention: 注意安全 "mind your safety," 请注意 "please take note."
专门	zhuān mén	specialized; specially; on purpose — 专 zhuān (focused; sole) + 门 mén (field). Concentrated on one doorway. As an adjective, specialized: 专门知识 "specialized knowledge." As an adverb, specially or on purpose: 我专门来看你 "I came specially to see you."
字眼	zì yǎn	the key word; the word one fastens on — 字 zì (character; word) + 眼. The eye of the phrase , the single word that carries the weight, that you pivot a sentence on, that an opponent picks at. 抠字眼 kōu zìyǎn "to pick at the wording" (often pejoratively, like English "splitting hairs over phrasing").
自首	zì shǒu	to turn oneself in; to confess to the authorities — 自 zì (oneself) + 首 shǒu (to bow the head, to confess). To give oneself up to the law of one's own accord, before being caught. A legal term: voluntary surrender that often earns a lighter sentence. The head bows itself before justice.
