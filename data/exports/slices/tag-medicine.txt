//角落書屋/Slices/Medicine
医院	yī yuàn	hospital — The healing courtyard: how an arrow wound, a wine jar, and the institutional suffix 院 assembled the word for hospital.
病急乱投医	bìng jí luàn tóu yī	when the illness is desperate, one runs to any doctor — A proverb rather than a classical chengyu: desperation abandons judgment. Used far beyond medicine, for panicked job hunts, failing companies grasping at consultants, or students trying every cram school at once. The tone is sympathetic but critical; the flailing rarely works.
对症下药	duì zhèng xià yào	prescribe according to the symptoms; tailor the remedy to the problem — 对 (match) + 症 (symptoms) + 下药 (administer medicine). From the case lore of the Han physician Hua Tuo, who gave two patients with identical headaches different prescriptions because the causes differed.
久病成医	jiǔ bìng chéng yī	long illness makes the patient a doctor — Chronic experience becomes expertise. Said of patients who know their condition better than junior doctors, and by extension of anyone whose long exposure to a problem has made them its reluctant expert: the veteran user who knows the software's bugs better than the developers.
妙手回春	miào shǒu huí chūn	miraculous hands bring back the spring; masterly healing — 妙手 (wondrous hands) + 回春 (return spring, restore life's season). The stock phrase of gratitude to a doctor, embroidered on the silk banners (锦旗 jǐnqí) that patients present to hospital departments, a living custom: the walls of a respected clinic hang red with them.
阿是穴	ā shì xu é	Ashi points — Ashi points: tender spots with no fixed location, needled wherever the patient says "Ah, yes, that is it!" when pressed. Not on any meridian map, but recognized in classical texts as clinically valid.
艾灸	ài jiǔ	Moxibustion , burning dried mugwort at or near acupoints to warm and tonify. Paired with needling in the term 针灸 (zhēnjiǔ), which names the two techniques together. Used specifically for cold-type and deficiency-type conditions where warming stimulation is indicated.
本草	běn cǎo	Materia medica — Materia medica: the traditional term for the pharmacological literature and its subject matter.
本草纲目	běn cǎo gāng mù	the materia medica
不舒服	bù shū fu	Unwell — Unwell: "not comfortable." The all-purpose symptom, the polite sick-day excuse, and the doctor's opening question in negative form: 哪里不舒服?
得气	dé qì	Arrival of qi, the sensation confirming correct needling — Arrival of qi, the sensation confirming correct needling: heaviness, distension, or a spreading warmth at the needled point. Classical and modern acupuncturists both treat déqì as the essential marker of effective treatment.
毒性	dú xìng	Toxicity, one of the key properties documented for each drug in the Bencao Gangmu. Li distinguished between non-toxic (无毒), slightly toxic (小毒), moderately toxic (有毒), and highly toxic (大毒) substances, with corresponding cautions about dosage, preparation, and contraindications.
发烧	fā shāo	To run a fever — To run a fever: emit burn. 量体温 is to take a temperature; 退烧 is the fever breaking, and 退烧药 the medicine that breaks it.
感冒	gǎn mào	cold; flu; to catch a cold — 感 gǎn + 冒 mào (to emit; to brave recklessly). In traditional Chinese medicine, illness = pathogenic qì 气 entering the body. 感冒 = receiving and being overcome by wind-cold 风寒.
挂号	guà hào	To register at a hospital — To register at a hospital: hang a number. 挂个专家号 is booking the senior specialist; 挂不上号 is the national complaint about famous hospitals.
护士	hù shi	nurse — 护 (protect, the 护 of 保护) + 士 (officer, the scholar-gentleman suffix of 战士 soldier and 博士 PhD). Coined when modern nursing arrived in the late Qing; the profession's day, 护士节, falls on Florence Nightingale's birthday, May 12.
急诊	jí zhěn	emergency medical treatment, the ER — 急 (urgent) + 诊 (zhěn, to diagnose). Emergency consultation. 急诊 is the hospital department you go to when something cannot wait, the Chinese counterpart of the emergency room. 看急诊 (kàn jízhěn) means to go to the ER.
经络	jīng luò	meridians; the qi channel system — 经 jīng (warp thread; main channel) + 络 luò (network thread; connecting channel). Together they describe the body's energy network: 12 primary 经 channels + 8 extraordinary 奇经八脉 vessels + an extensive network of 络 branch channels. The basis of acupuncture, acupressure, and qigong.
君臣佐使	jūn chén zuǒ shǐ	Ruler, minister, assistant, envoy — Ruler, minister, assistant, envoy: the four functional roles within a compound herbal formula.
看病	kàn bìng	to see a doctor; to seek medical treatment — 看 (to look at, to examine) + 病 (illness). The verb covers both sides of the clinical encounter: the patient "looks at" the illness by going to be treated, and the doctor "looks at" the illness to diagnose it. Context distinguishes who is doing the looking. 我去看病 (I'm going to see a doctor).
上火	shàng huǒ	to get internal heat; to be inflamed; to get irritable — 上 shàng (to rise; to ascend) + 火 huǒ (fire). A TCM concept: excess heat (fire) in the body rising upward, causing sore throat, mouth ulcers, red eyes, or irritability. Used medically and colloquially. 吃辣的容易上火 "Eating spicy food easily causes 上火."
四气五味	sì qì wǔ wèi	Four temperatures and five flavors — Four temperatures and five flavors: the two-axis system for classifying drug properties. The four temperatures: cold (寒), cool (凉), warm (温), hot (热). The five flavors: sour (酸), bitter (苦), sweet (甘), pungent (辛), salty (咸). Each flavor has specific organ affinities and physiological effects.
汤药	tāng yào	Decocted herbal medicine — Decocted herbal medicine: herbs simmered in water to produce a strong tea. The traditional and most common form of herbal administration, now competing with granules and capsules for convenience.
体质	tǐ zhì	Constitution — Constitution: one's inherent physical and energetic type, which determines predispositions to certain diseases and guides appropriate diet, lifestyle, and treatment choices. Nine constitutional types are defined in modern TCM standardization.
铜人	tóng rén	Bronze man — Bronze man: the life-sized bronze figure commissioned in 1026 CE with channel lines and point locations engraved on the surface and a hollow interior. Filled with water and wax for examination purposes, it was the world's first standardized anatomical teaching model for acupuncture instruction.
望闻问切	wàng wén wèn qiē	The four examinations , looking (observation of complexion, tongue, posture), listening/smelling (voice, breath), asking (history, symptoms), and touching (pulse diagnosis). The complete TCM diagnostic protocol compressed into four characters.
穴位	xu é wèi	acupoint — Acupoint , a specific location on a channel where qi gathers close to the surface and is most accessible to needling or moxa stimulation. Also called 腧穴 shùxué in classical texts. The body has 361 standard points on the 14 main channels.
学院	xu é yuàn	college, institute
药	yào	Medicine — Medicine. 吃药 is taking it, 开药 is prescribing it, 中药 is the herbal kind, and 药店 the pharmacy. The grass radical 艹 on top remembers that medicine began as herbs.
医生	yī shēng	doctor; physician — 医 yī (medicine; to heal) + 生 shēng (practitioner). The standard word for doctor in mainland China. Note the 生 suffix , it originally meant "one who practices/studies" (compare 学生, 先生). 看医生 = "to see a doctor."
针灸	zhēn jiǔ	acupuncture and moxibustion — 针 zhēn (needle) + 灸 jiǔ (moxibustion, burning dried mugwort at acupoints to warm qi). The compound covers both therapies, which are always taught together. Acupuncture inserts fine needles at specific points (穴位 xuéwèi, acupoints) along the meridians to regulate the flow of qi.
住院	zhù yuàn	to be hospitalized — 住 (live, stay) + 院 (the hospital courtyard). Separable: 住了三天院 is three days in the hospital. Its exit is 出院 (be discharged), the phone call every family waits for.
