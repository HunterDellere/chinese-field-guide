//角落書屋/Slices/Negation
不	bù	no, not, negation — The most used character in Mandarin, and the foundation of every denial, refusal, and negation.
非	fēi	not, wrong, un- — Two wings spreading in opposite directions: from that image of opposition grows the great negator. Not and non-, the line between right and wrong, and the un- prefix that turns a word against itself.
无	wú	nothing, without, non-being — A dancer with arms outstretched, holding nothing. The character behind every Chinese negation of having, every Daoist non-doing, and the philosophical pole that pairs with 有 to frame the whole cosmos.
半途而废	bàn tú ér fèi	to give up halfway: to abandon a task before it is finished — Lit: half-road-and-quit. 而 links the halfway point to the giving-up that follows. Said in reproach of someone who starts strong but lacks the resolve to see a thing through.
不可思议	bù kě sī yì	inconceivable; unimaginable — Literally "cannot be thought or discussed," beyond comprehension, originally a Buddhist term for the ineffable. Here 可 ("can / possible"), the first character of 可能, takes its sense of "able to be": what cannot possibly be grasped by thought.
不三不四	bù sān bù sì	neither one thing nor another — shady, dubious, not upright — Lit: not-three not-four. Three and four are "middle" numbers: something that is neither here nor there, neither proper nor improper. Used for shady people or behavior that doesn't conform to standards.
不言而喻	bù yán ér yù	without words, it is understood — self-evident; goes without saying — Lit: not-speak-yet-understand. What goes without saying: a truth so obvious that to speak it would be redundant. Contrasts with the power attributed to 语 elsewhere: here, the deepest understanding transcends the need for words.
非同小可	fēi tóng xiǎo kě	no small matter: serious, of great importance — Lit: not-the-same-as-trivial. 小可 means a small or trivial thing, and 非同 negates it: this is not on a par with the trivial, so treat it with weight. 这件事非同小可 "this is no small matter."
是非曲直	shì fēi qū zhí	right and wrong, crooked and straight: the true merits of a case — Lit: right-wrong-crooked-straight. The four poles by which a dispute is judged. To 分清是非曲直 is to sort out who is truly in the right, free of bias.
啼笑皆非	tí xiào jiē fēi	unable to laugh or cry: caught between amusement and dismay — Lit: crying-laughing-both-wrong. Neither weeping nor laughing fits the situation, the awkward state of finding something at once absurd and exasperating. 令人啼笑皆非 "leaves one not knowing whether to laugh or cry."
为非作歹	wéi fēi zuò dǎi	to do evil; to commit crimes and wrongdoing — Lit: do-wrong-make-evil. 非 (wrong) and 歹 dǎi (bad, evil) pair up to name flagrant wrongdoing. Said of those who run riot doing harm: 为非作歹的歹徒 "evildoers who run amok."
都	dōu	all, both, even: the universal scope adverb — The adverb that quantifies everything before it, not after, and whose position in a sentence determines whether you mean "none" or "not all."
没有	méi yǒu	negation of possession and past action — The two most important negation characters, 不 and 没, and why they cannot be swapped.
别	bié	don't (imperative negation)
并非	bìng fēi	is not in fact; really is not — 并 bìng (actually; emphatic) + 非 fēi (not). A firm, slightly formal "is not at all," used to push back against an assumption: 并非如此 "that is not the case," 并非不可能 "not in fact impossible."
不错	bú cuò	pretty good, not bad; correct, right — 不 bù (not) + 错 cuò (wrong). Two readings from one shape: as praise, "quite good, not bad" (成绩不错 "good grades"); as confirmation, "right, correct" (一点不错 "exactly right"). Note the tone sandhi: 不 shifts to bú before the falling 错.
不得不	bù dé bù	cannot but; have no choice but to — 不得不 (not-obtain-not) = double negation creates strong obligation: "cannot not do" = must do, compelled by circumstances. Stronger than 必须 bìxū in the sense of reluctant necessity. The speaker has no choice.
不得了	bù dé liǎo	extremely; terrible; amazing (intensifier) — 不 + 得了 déliǎo (can be concluded; can be over). "Cannot be concluded" → extreme beyond measure. Used after adjectives as a strong intensifier: 好得不得了 = incredibly good. Also standalone: 不得了！= This is unbelievable / It's a disaster! (depending on context).
不都	bù dōu	not all; not every one (partial negation) — 不 precedes 都. The negation falls on the universal claim: "it's not the case that all of them do X." Some do, some don't. This is logically weaker than 都不 and much more common in careful, qualified speech.
不好意思	bù hǎo yì si	embarrassed; sorry to trouble you; excuse me — Lit: not-good-meaning/face. The phrase used when imposing on someone, asking a favor, or causing mild embarrassment. One of the most important social lubricants in Mandarin. Not a strong apology, more like "I feel awkward about this" or "pardon the imposition."
除非	chú fēi	unless; only if — 除 chú (except) + 非 fēi (not). Literally "except if not," hence "unless." Often paired with 否则 fǒuzé or 才 cái: 除非下雨，否则我们就去 "we'll go unless it rains."
错误	cuò wù	mistake, error; wrong, mistaken — 错 cuò (wrong) + 误 wù (to err, mistake). Both a noun, the mistake itself, and an adjective, mistaken or incorrect: 犯了一个错误 "made a mistake," 错误的决定 "a wrong decision." The standard, slightly formal word for an error of fact or judgment.
大家	dà jiā	everyone; everybody (warm, inclusive) — 大 dà (big) + 家 jiā (family; home). Literally "the big family," used for "everyone present" or "all of us." Much warmer and more conversational than 人们. The go-to word for addressing or including a present group: 大家好！(Hello everyone!).
都 , 全体	dōu , quán tǐ	都: every member of a group — simultaneous coverage — 都 applies a single predicate to all members of a group at the same time. The group is fixed; 都 says no member is excluded from the statement. Works with pronouns (我们, 他们, 大家), explicit quantifier phrases (三个人, 所有人), and fronted question words (什么, 谁, 哪儿).
非常	fēi cháng	extremely; very; unusual; extraordinary — 非 fēi (not) + 常 cháng (ordinary). As an adverb before an adjective it means "extremely" (非常高兴 "extremely happy"); as an adjective before a noun it keeps the older sense "extraordinary, exceptional" (非常时期 "extraordinary times").
非法	fēi fǎ	illegal; unlawful — 非 fēi (not) + 法 fǎ (law). Outside the law, not lawful: 非法行为 "illegal conduct," 非法移民 "illegal immigration." Its opposite is 合法 héfǎ (lawful).
非议	fēi yì	to criticize; to reproach; censure — 非 fēi (to fault) + 议 yì (to discuss; opinion). To talk of something as wrong, to find fault with it: 无可非议 "beyond reproach, nothing to criticize." Here 非 acts as a verb, "to deem wrong."
否则	fǒu zé	otherwise; if not
毫无	háo wú	utterly without; not the slightest
合法	hé fǎ	lawful; legal; legitimate — 合 hé (to fit; to accord with) + 法 fǎ (law). In accordance with the law, on the right side of the line: 合法权利 "lawful rights." Its opposite is 非法 fēifǎ (illegal) or 违法 wéifǎ (to break the law).
没	méi	not; have not
没关系	méi guān xi	it doesn't matter; never mind; that's okay — One of the most indispensable phrases in Mandarin. The standard response to 对不起 (sorry). Literally "no relationship" , meaning "it has no effect on us; it carries no weight." Learn this before your first week of speaking Chinese.
没什么	méi shén me	nothing / it's nothing / no big deal — 没 + 什么 (what; anything). 没什么 = "nothing; it's nothing." As a response to thanks or apologies: 没什么！= "No worries! / It's nothing!" Also as a statement: 冰箱里没什么吃的 "There's nothing much to eat in the fridge."
哪儿	nǎ r	where (fronted + 都 = everywhere)
哪儿都	nǎ r dōu	everywhere; anywhere — 哪儿 (where, which place) fronted before 都 covers all locations. 哪里都 is the less colloquial variant; 哪儿都 is the Beijing/northern colloquial form. The construction makes a place-universal claim: no location is excluded.
谁	shéi	who (fronted + 都 = everyone)
谁都	shéi dōu	everyone; anyone (no person excluded) — 谁 (who) fronted before 都 universalizes over all persons. 谁都知道 = "everyone knows," with the implication that no person could be ignorant of this fact. Often carries a mild rhetorical force: the speaker is emphasizing how obvious or widespread something is.
什么	shén me	what (fronted + 都 = everything)
什么都	shén me dōu	everything; anything (without exception) — 什么 fronted before 都 universalizes over all things or all options. 什么都行 literally "anything goes" , no restriction exists. Extremely common in everyday speech for expressing total flexibility or total acceptance.
是非	shì fēi	right and wrong; the rights and wrongs; trouble; gossip — 是 shì (right; this; is) + 非 fēi (wrong). The pairing of right and wrong as a single notion: 明辨是非 "to clearly distinguish right from wrong." By a common slide it also means the quarrels and gossip that arise from disputes: 惹是非 "to stir up trouble."
所有	suǒ yǒu	all; every; everything one has — 所 suǒ (that which; a place; nominalizing particle) + 有 (have/exist). 所有 as a determiner: 所有的学生 (all the students). As a noun: 他失去了所有 (He lost everything he had). As a formal possession compound in legal/written Chinese: 所有权 suǒyǒuquán = ownership rights, title.
无聊	wú liáo	bored; boring; pointless
无名	wú míng	nameless; unknown; unnamed
无视	wú shì	to ignore, to disregard
无私	wú sī	selfless, without private interest
无所谓	wú suǒ wèi	it doesn't matter; whatever
也 , 追加	yě , zhuī jiā	也: one more added to an existing set — 也 always implies that the predicate has already been established for another entity. The sentence using 也 adds one more item. This requires a prior context (explicit or implicit) in which the predicate was already true of someone or something else. 也 cannot start a discourse out of nowhere.
怎么都	zěn me dōu	no matter how; by any means; however you try — 怎么 (how, in what way) fronted before 都 universalizes over all methods or manners. Often used in negative or frustrated contexts: no matter what approach is tried, the result is the same. The construction can also express total permissiveness: "any way is fine."
只	zhī	measure word for animals
