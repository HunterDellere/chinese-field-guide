//角落書屋/Slices/Tense
不了了之	bù liǎo liǎo zhī	to let a matter fade away unresolved; to end without proper conclusion — Lit: not-conclude-conclude-it. Uses 了 liǎo as a verb twice , "to not conclude [a matter], to conclude [it anyway]." A set phrase for when a problem or investigation is allowed to dissolve without resolution , bureaucratic negligence, diplomatic evasion, quiet abandonment. 这件事就这样不了了之了。
尘埃落定	chén āi luò dìng	the dust has settled; a situation has finally reached resolution — Lit: dust-settle-fixed. Evokes the moment after turmoil when everything finally comes to rest and the outcome is clear. Title of Alai's celebrated Tibetan novel.
大功告成	dà gōng gào chéng	the great work is accomplished — mission complete — Lit: great-achievement-announce-accomplished. A formal declaration that a major undertaking (a construction project, a campaign, a long creative work) has been completed. Often used with a tone of proud announcement: 大功告成！(We did it!).
光阴似箭	guāng yīn sì jiàn	time flies like an arrow — the days pass with frightening speed — 光阴 guāngyīn (time, light-and-shadow) + 似 sì (resembles) + 箭 jiàn (arrow). Time shoots past like a loosed arrow, gone before you can grasp it. Usually paired with its twin 日月如梭 (the sun and moon shuttle by like a weaver's shuttle). 光阴似箭，日月如梭，一晃十年就过去了。
来日方长	lái rì fāng cháng	the days to come are still long — there is still much time ahead — 来日 = the coming days, the future (days that come toward us). 方长 = just beginning to be long. A comfort phrase: things not accomplished now can still be done; relationships not deepened yet have time. 我们来日方长，不必着急 "We still have plenty of time ahead , no need to rush."
日新月异	rì xīn yu è yì	changing by the day and the month — rapid, ceaseless progress — Literally "new each day, different each month," for change so fast it is visible from one stretch of time to the next. The standard phrase for the breakneck pace of modern development, especially technology and the transformation of cities, the felt texture of living in the 现代 age.
善始善终	shàn shǐ shàn zhōng	good beginning, good ending; to see something through properly from start to finish — Lit: good-start-good-end. The virtue of completing what one begins, and completing it well. 终 zhōng (end, conclusion) is the Classical Chinese counterpart to the grammatical 了 , both mark the reaching of an end. This idiom captures the moral weight of completion.
事过境迁	shì guò jìng qiān	the affair has passed and the circumstances have changed — 事 (the matter) 过 (has passed) 境 (circumstances) 迁 (have shifted). Note 过 in its full verbal sense, the same morpheme that grammaticalized into the experiential particle. Used to wave off old grievances: 事过境迁，别再提了 "It's all in the past, don't bring it up again."
未雨绸缪	wèi yǔ chóu móu	prepare before the rain comes — From the Book of Songs (诗经, Shījīng): the bird weaves its nest before the rains arrive. The counterpart to 亡羊补牢 , prevention before the fact. Together the two idioms cover the complete cycle of crisis management: prepare if you can, and repair decisively if prevention fails.
一而再，再而三	yī ér zài , zài ér sān	once, then again, then a third time — time after time; repeatedly — The fullest expression of Chinese repetition idiomatics. 一而再 (once to twice), 再而三 (twice to three times): the escalating structure conveys frustration or persistence. Almost always used with a negative coloring: someone has done something they shouldn't have, repeatedly. 他一而再，再而三地失约，让人很失望。
了	le	the perfective particle — Post-verb 了 marks a completed action; sentence-final 了 announces a new situation, and the two can stack in one sentence.
时态	shí tài	past, present, future — Mandarin verbs never change form: time words, 了, 过, 着, 在, and 会 do the work English does with conjugation.
又	yòu / zài	再 · again, twice over — Chinese has two agains: 又 looks backward at a repetition that happened, 再 looks forward to one still to come. 再见 is the proof.
不来了	bù lái le	not coming anymore; decided not to come — 不…了 is the negation of sentence-final 了 , it signals a reversal of an expected or prior state. The 了 here paradoxically signals a new situation: the new situation is "not doing X anymore." Extremely common for announcing changed plans, habits that have been given up, or states that have ended.
曾经	céng jīng	once, formerly
吃了	chī le	ate; has eaten (the action is complete) — The most basic post-verb 了 construction. The 了 immediately follows the verb and signals that the action was completed. With a bare monosyllabic object, the 了 sits between verb and object. With a quantified object, this placement is even clearer and more natural.
春天了	chūn tiān le	it's spring now; spring has arrived (no verb needed) — One of the most elegant features of sentence-final 了: it can attach directly to a noun phrase, without any verb, to announce that the situation has become that thing. This works because 了 marks change of state , and "it has become spring" is a state change even with no overt verb present.
动词 + 过	guo	past experience, "have ever done" — 过 marks that the subject has undergone the experience at least once, at some indefinite point. 我去过中国 (I have been to China) says nothing about when; it registers the experience itself. Contrast 我去了中国, a specific completed trip. Negation keeps 过 but uses 没: 我没去过中国 (I have never been to China).
动词 + 了	le	completed action, the closest thing to a past tense — 了 after the verb marks the action as completed. With a past time word it produces what English renders as simple past: 我昨天买了一本书 (I bought a book yesterday). But 了 marks completion, not pastness: 明天吃了饭再走 (leave tomorrow after finishing dinner) puts 了 in a future clause.
动词 + 着	zhe	sustained state: the door stands open — 着 marks a state that holds: 门开着 (the door is open), 墙上挂着一幅画 (a painting hangs on the wall), 他穿着一件红衣服 (he's wearing a red shirt). Where 在 tracks an activity unfolding, 着 freezes the result and holds it. The contrast is sharpest with verbs like 穿: 他在穿衣服 (he is putting clothes on) vs.
刚 · 已经 · 以前	gāng yǐ jīng yǐ qián	just now · already · formerly, the past-pointing adverbs — 刚 gāng (just now) places an action in the immediate past: 他刚走 (he just left). 已经 yǐjīng (already) pairs with 了 to mark a completed state: 我已经吃了 (I've already eaten). 以前 yǐqián (before, formerly) frames a whole clause in the past: 我以前住在上海 (I used to live in Shanghai).
刚才	gāng cái	just now; a moment ago — 刚 gāng (just; barely) + 才 cái. A time word for the very recent past, the moment that has only just passed: 刚才他还在这儿 "he was still here just now." Here 才 keeps its sense of a threshold only just crossed.
光杆动词	guāng gǎn dòng cí	the bare verb: habits, facts, general truths — The unmarked verb is the default for habitual actions and standing facts, usually anchored by a frequency word: 我每天喝咖啡 (I drink coffee every day), 他住在北京 (he lives in Beijing), 地球绕太阳转 (the earth orbits the sun). No particle needed; adding 了 here would wrongly signal a completed event.
将来	jiāng lái	the future; in the future; what is coming — 将 jiāng (about to; will; the near future) + 来 lái (coming; arriving). What is about to arrive, the future approaching. 将来 refers to the general future (not an immediate event). Slightly more formal than 以后 yǐhòu. Contrast: 未来 wèilái (the future, lit.
就要	jiù yào	about to; soon (imminent)
看了两遍	kàn le liǎng biàn	watched twice; read it through twice — 了 with a frequency complement (遍 biàn = complete passes through, 次 cì = times, 回 huí = times in colloquial speech). Marks the action as having been performed a counted number of complete cycles. The quantification of repetition implies completion of each cycle.
了不起	liǎo bu qǐ	remarkable; extraordinary; amazing — 了 liǎo (to conclude; to surpass) + 不 + 起 (unable to rise above; potential complement marker). Literally "cannot be surpassed or concluded" , what cannot be surpassed is extraordinary. A high-praise adjective in everyday speech.
了结	liǎo jié	to conclude; to settle; to bring to a definitive end — 了 liǎo (to conclude) + 结 jié (knot; tied up; settled). To tie off and close a matter definitively. Used for settling disputes, concluding legal cases, or resolving something that has dragged on without resolution. Has a formal or literary flavor , matters of weight are 了结d.
了解	liǎo jiě	to understand thoroughly; to have rounded knowledge of — 了 liǎo (clear-sighted; complete) + 解 jiě (to unravel; to untie). The full unraveling of something until it is entirely clear , understanding that goes all the way through, not just surface knowledge. More thorough than 知道 zhīdao (to know a fact). Common across casual speech and formal registers.
买了	mǎi le	bought; purchased (transaction complete) — Transactional and decisional verbs with 了 are extremely common in everyday conversation. The completion is commercially and practically significant , the item has been acquired, the deal is done, the decision has been executed.
没吃	méi chī	didn't eat; have not eaten — 没(有) is the standard negator for completed actions. It replaces 了 entirely , you never say 没吃了, because 没 already carries the information that no completion occurred. The 了 is superfluous and wrong. This is a near-universal rule: 没 and post-verb 了 are mutually exclusive.
明天	míng tiān	tomorrow — 明 míng (bright; dawn; the next) + 天 tiān (day). The "bright day," the day the sun will rise again. 明 also means "next" in 明年 míngnián (next year) and 明月 míngyuè (the bright moon, a poetic image for longing). Note: in classical Chinese, 明日 míngrì is the literary form; 明天 is modern spoken Mandarin.
时间词 + 光杆动词	shí jiān cí + ve r b	the plain future: tomorrow I go — A future time word with a bare verb is complete and idiomatic: 我明天去上海 (I'm going to Shanghai tomorrow), 下个月放假 (vacation starts next month). No marker is required at all.
受不了	shòu bu liǎo	can't stand it; unbearable; past one's limit — 受 shòu (to endure; to receive) + 不了 liǎo (cannot complete the action of enduring). The potential complement with 不了 means the action cannot reach completion , endurance hits its limit. One of the most frequently used expressions in daily speech for frustration, exhaustion, or overwhelm.
他结婚了	tā jié hūn le	he got married; he is now a married man — Major life transitions , marriage, graduation, birth, death, career changes , are natural territory for sentence-final 了. The 了 frames the event as a change that affects the current situation: the relevant new fact is that he is now married. It answers an implicit question about the present.
她来了两个星期了	tā lái le liǎng gè xīng qī le	she has been here for two weeks (and is still here) — The double-了 construction with a duration complement is one of its most important uses. The first 了 marks the initial arrival (completed action of coming); the second 了 marks the current relevance , she is still here now. Without the second 了, the sentence implies the visit is concluded.
我明白了	wǒ míng bai le	I understand now; I get it (I have entered a state of understanding) — State-change verbs like 明白 (understand), 知道 (know), 懂 (grasp), 累 (tired), 饿 (hungry), 高兴 (happy) pair powerfully with sentence-final 了. The 了 marks the entry into that state , the moment the change occurs. Before: didn't understand. After: understands.
下雨了	xià yǔ le	it's raining now; it has started to rain — The canonical example of sentence-final 了 announcing a new situation. There is no past tense here , the rain is starting now, and the 了 signals a change of state has occurred or is occurring. This 了 carries the meaning: "now things are different from before."
学了三年	xu é le sān nián	studied for three years (bounded period) — 了 with a duration complement signals an action sustained for a period of time and treated as a completed, bounded episode. This construction is essential for talking about study, work, residence, and sustained experience. The duration frames the action as a finished chunk of time.
要 · 打算 · 准备	yào dǎ suàn zhǔn bèi	intention and plan: what someone is going to do — 要 before a verb marks intention or imminence: 我要回家 (I'm going home / I want to go home; context decides). 打算 dǎsuàn (to plan) and 准备 zhǔnbèi (to prepare, to be about to) make the plan explicit: 我打算明年去中国 (I plan to go to China next year).
以前 · 现在 · 以后	yǐ qián xiàn zài yǐ hòu	before · now · after: the relative frame — 以前 and 以后 attach to any reference point: 三年以前 (three years ago), 吃饭以后 (after eating), or stand alone to mean "formerly" and "from now on". 的时候 de shíhou (when …) frames simultaneity: 我小的时候 (when I was little).
又来了	yòu lái le	here we go again — Deployed at the first bars of a familiar tune: the uncle's stock speech, the app's renewal popup, the roommate's diet announcement. 你又来了 aims it at a person, affection and exasperation mixed to taste.
再见	zài jiàn	goodbye (see you again) — The forward-looking again fused to the meet verb. Internet speech numbers it 88 (bābā, "bye-bye"), and the dramatic register upgrades to 永别 for farewells meant to be final.
再说	zài shuō	we'll talk about it later; let's deal with it when the time comes; moreover (discourse marker) — 再 (later; further) + 说 (to speak; to deal with). Multiple uses: (1) Deferral: 这件事以后再说 (We'll deal with this later , let's not now). Very common for postponing a conversation or decision. (2) Discourse marker meaning "moreover; besides": 再说，我也没空 (Besides, I'm not free either).
昨天	zuó tiān	yesterday — 昨 zuó (yesterday; past) + 天 tiān (day). The 昨 character is rarely used outside this compound and 昨晚 zuówǎn (last night) in modern Chinese. Note the tonal transition: 昨 zuó is tone 2, 天 tiān is tone 1, two rising pitches in sequence, which requires attention in spoken practice.
昨天 · 今天 · 明天	zuó tiān jīn tiān míng tiān	the day series, and its symmetric siblings — The day series runs 前天 (day before yesterday) · 昨天 · 今天 · 明天 · 后天 (day after tomorrow). Years mirror it exactly: 前年 · 去年 · 今年 · 明年 · 后年. Weeks and months use 上/下 (above/below) instead: 上个星期 (last week), 下个月 (next month).
