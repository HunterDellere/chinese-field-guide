//角落書屋/Slices/Emotion
爱	ài	love, to love, to cherish — Love in all its forms: romantic, familial, and the Confucian ethic of universal benevolence.
春	chūn	spring, springtime, vitality, the stir of desire — Sun pressing up through young shoots: the season of growth, and the oldest Chinese metaphor for life-force and erotic longing.
感	gǎn	to feel, to resonate — Feeling, perception, gratitude, and the cosmology of sympathetic resonance.
害	hài	to harm, injury, calamity, to fear — Under the roof a mouth and a blade build the sense of injury done; the same glyph also means to fear, as in 害怕.
欢	huān	joy, merry, to be pleased — A breath of open delight: the hand and the open mouth that make laughter. From this glyph come joy and merriment, to like and to welcome, and the lively, high-spirited mood.
急	jí	urgent, anxious, hasty, rapid — A heart pressed from above: 急 stacks a phonetic element over 心, so the glyph itself feels the squeeze of urgency.
紧	jǐn	tight, taut, tense, urgent — Thread drawn taut over the silk radical; the same word carries a tight knot, a tense nerve, an urgent deadline, and a tight wallet.
苦	kǔ	bitter, hardship, suffering, painstaking — The grass radical over 古 names a bitter herb on the tongue, then stretches to every flavor of hardship and effort.
快	kuài	fast, happy — 快 joins 忄 (heart) to 夬 (a decisive cut): speed and joy are the same image, a heart running without drag.
乐	lè	happy, joyful (lè); music (yuè) — A picture of a wooden frame strung with silk and a drum, an early musical instrument. The simplified 乐 carries two readings: yuè for music, the original sense, and lè for joy, the happiness that music gives.
慢	màn	slow, unhurried — Pace becomes a moral register: 慢 màn carries the same heart radical as 快, and extends from physical slowness into social neglect and arrogance.
情	qíng	feeling, affection, situation — The heart radical beside the phonetic 青: the movements of the heart. From that one image grows feeling and mood, affection and love, and the wider sense of a situation or state of affairs.
思	sī	to think, to miss, thought — The field of the mind above the heart: thinking as cultivation, longing as thought turned inward.
喜	xǐ	joy, to like, happy events — A festive drum sounding above an open mouth: the sound of joy and the smiling mouth that answers it. From this image grow the words for liking, delight, and every happy occasion, above all the wedding.
想	xiǎng	to think, to want, to miss — 想 = 相 (mutual, facing) + 心 (heart): the heart turned toward something, carrying the full range from thought to desire to longing in a single gesture.
笑	xiào	to laugh, to smile, to mock — Bamboo over a bending person: a form that has split scholars since the Song over whether laughter or rustling reeds came first.
心	xīn	heart, mind — The radical at the root of all feeling, and the Chinese conception of thought itself.
意	yì	meaning, intention, the wish of the mind — Sound over heart: 意 is what the heart means before it is spoken. From that image grows meaning and significance, intention and willingness, and the notions of attention, satisfaction, and the unexpected.
高兴	gāo xìng	happy, glad — Happiness as elevation: spirits that rise. The everyday glad word, and how it divides the territory with 开心, 快乐, and 幸福.
喜欢	xǐ huan	to like — The everyday verb for liking, and how it differs from loving (爱), enjoying (喜爱), and being willing (愿意).
爱屋及乌	ài wū jí wū	love me, love my dog — love extends to all connected with the beloved — Lit: love-house reach-crow. If you love the house, you even love the crow on the roof. Love spreads to everything associated with the beloved, even people or things that would otherwise seem unremarkable.
安居乐业	ān jū lè yè	to live in peace and be content in one's work: a settled, secure life — Lit: peaceful-dwelling, joyful-work. 安居 is to live securely, 乐业 to take pleasure in one's occupation. The classic image of social well-being: a people housed, employed, and at ease. Often invoked as the aim of good governance.
傲慢无礼	ào màn wú lǐ	arrogant and rude; haughty and without manners — Lit: arrogant-rude-without-courtesy. The social-failure sense of 慢 crystallized into a four-character indictment.
百感交集	bǎi gǎn jiāo jí	a hundred feelings converging — a complex rush of mixed emotions all at once — Lit: hundred-feelings-intersect-gather. For when emotions are too tangled and complex to name individually. 百 = figurative "many."
博爱	bó ài	universal love; philanthropy — broad, impartial love for all — 博 bó = broad, extensive. Sun Yat-sen's Three Principles include 博爱 as a founding ideal. Also the Chinese word for philanthropy and humanitarian love. Related to the Mohist concept of 兼爱.
不欢而散	bù huān ér sàn	to part on bad terms: to break up unhappily, the gathering souring — Lit: not-joyful-and-scatter. The mirror image of the joyful idioms: a meeting or meal that ends in discord, everyone leaving displeased. 两人话不投机，不欢而散 "the two could not agree, and parted on bad terms."
不慌不忙	bù huāng bù máng	neither panicked nor rushed — composed and unhurried — Lit: not-panic-not-hurry. Does not contain 慢 but is its most important semantic companion. Where 慢 can carry a negative charge (negligence, arrogance), 不慌不忙 frames the same unhurriedness as mastery: the person who is slow because they are in control, not because they are lazy or contemptuous.
不紧不慢	bù jǐn bù màn	neither tight nor slow: unhurried, even-paced — A steady, measured tempo with no rush and no dawdling. Often admiring, describing someone who keeps composure under pressure.
操之过急	cāo zhī guò jí	to act on it too hastily: to rush a matter — A gentle warning that someone moved too fast and forced a result before its time. Often used to advise patience: 这件事不能操之过急 (don't rush this).
朝思暮想	zhāo sī mù xiǎng	to think of morning and night; to long for ceaselessly — 朝 zhāo (morning; dawn) + 思 sī (to think of; to long for) + 暮 mù (dusk; evening) + 想 xiǎng (to think of; to miss). Thinking at dawn and thinking at dusk: longing that fills the entire arc of a day. Used for intense, sustained yearning: romantic longing, homesickness, or grief for the absent.
出乎意料	chū hū yì liào	to come out beyond expectation: contrary to what anyone anticipated — Lit: come-out-of-reckoning. 意料 is what the mind reckons in advance; to fall 出乎意料 is to land outside it. Its mirror is 不出所料, "just as expected."
春风得意	chūn fēng dé yì	riding the spring wind, full of pride: flush with success — From Mèng Jiāo's Tang poem on passing the imperial exam. Describes someone on a winning streak, often with a hint that the pride may be premature.
春暖花开	chūn nuǎn huā kāi	spring is warm and flowers bloom: the good season has come — The stock image of spring at its best. Used literally for the season and figuratively for the right moment, a turn for the better after hardship.
大快人心	dà kuài rén xīn	greatly satisfying to the people's hearts — a righteous outcome that pleases everyone — Lit: greatly-fast/free-people's-hearts. Used when a wrongdoer receives punishment, a long-overdue verdict is handed down, or a corrupt official is finally removed. The 快 here is squarely in the "heart released from obstruction" sense: justice has cleared away what was blocking everyone.
感激涕零	gǎn jī tì líng	"so grateful the tears fall" — moved to tears by gratitude; deeply thankful — 感激 (deep gratitude) + 涕 (tears) + 零 (to fall in drops). Here 零 returns to its literal verb sense, "to fall in drops," applied not to rain but to tears: gratitude so overwhelming that the tears come down. The phrase describes being moved to weeping by another's kindness or favor.
感慨万千	gǎn kǎi wàn qiān	ten thousand emotions surging — overwhelmed by bittersweet feeling at life's passage — Go-to phrase for homecomings, reunions, and witnessing great change. Appears constantly in memoirs and personal essays.
感同身受	gǎn tóng shēn shòu	to feel it as if one's own body received it; deep empathy — Originally a formula of thanks on another's behalf (I feel your kindness to them as if I received it myself); modern usage has shifted it to empathy generally, feeling someone's pain as one's own.
哈哈大笑	hā hā dà xiào	to roar with laughter: a full belly laugh — 哈哈 is the sound of laughter itself, 大笑 a big laugh. The everyday, vivid way to say someone burst out laughing. Common in narration and casual speech alike.
害群之马	hài qún zhī mǎ	a horse that harms the herd: one bad apple — From the Zhuāngzǐ, where the Yellow Emperor is told to govern by removing whatever harms the herd. Now the person who spoils a group or organization.
含辛茹苦	hán xīn rú kǔ	to hold pungency and swallow bitterness: to endure every hardship — 茹 (rú) means to eat. Almost always said of parents, especially mothers, who suffer silently for years to raise their children.
欢天喜地	huān tiān xǐ dì	wild with joy: overjoyed, beside oneself with delight — Lit: joy-Heaven-glad-Earth, joy that fills the whole sky and ground. Said of someone bursting with happiness at good news, a reunion, or a festival. 一家人欢天喜地过新年 "the family rang in the New Year overjoyed."
欢欣鼓舞	huān xīn gǔ wǔ	elated and inspired: filled with joy and high spirits — Lit: joyful-glad-drum-dance, so cheered that one beats the drum and dances. Used of a group lifted and energized by good news or a great event, joy that rouses people to action.
急功近利	jí gōng jìn lì	eager for quick merit and near-term gain: shortsighted opportunism — Lit: anxious-merit-near-profit. A criticism of those who chase immediate results and quick profit at the expense of long-term, sustainable success. The opposite of the 功夫 ethic of patient, foundational effort.
急中生智	jí zhōng shēng zhì	wisdom born from urgency — a bright idea in a crisis; thinking fast under pressure — 急 jí (urgent; pressed; crisis) + 中 zhōng (within; in the midst of) + 生 shēng (to be born; to arise) + 智 zhì (wisdom; clever idea). The intelligence that surfaces specifically when there is no time to think: the flash of insight that saves a situation.
见笑	jiàn xiào	to be laughed at: forgive my poor effort — Lit: be-seen laughed-at. A set humble phrase: 让您见笑了 ("I've let you laugh at me") deflects praise for one's own work or skill. Polite, slightly formal register.
皆大欢喜	jiē dà huān xǐ	all are greatly delighted — everyone ends up happy — Originally the closing formula of Buddhist sutras, where the assembly rejoices at the teaching and departs; now the phrase for win-win endings, amicable settlements, and comedies. It is also the standard Chinese title of Shakespeare's As You Like It, a translation with better provenance than most.
紧锣密鼓	jǐn luó mì gǔ	tight gongs and dense drums: intense, busy preparation — From the loud, crowded percussion that builds before an opera's climax. Modern usage describes a project being driven forward at full, urgent pace behind the scenes.
紧要关头	jǐn yào guān tóu	a crucial, critical juncture — The pivotal moment when everything hangs in the balance. Common in news and narrative for the turning point of a crisis or contest.
苦尽甘来	kǔ jìn gān lái	bitterness ends, sweetness comes: hardship gives way to better days — 苦 (bitter) and 甘 (sweet) frame a full turn of fortune. The standard consolation offered to anyone enduring a long hard stretch; the reward is built into the saying.
苦口婆心	kǔ kǒu pó xīn	bitter mouth, grandmotherly heart: to urge earnestly and patiently — Describes someone who repeats hard advice tirelessly out of genuine care, the way an old woman nags her grandchild for their own good.
快马加鞭	kuài mǎ jiā biān	whip a fast horse — press on faster when already moving well — Lit: fast-horse-add-whip. The image is of a courier or cavalry rider who is already galloping but spurs the horse harder anyway. Metaphorically: when momentum is good, don't coast. Used to encourage continued effort or accelerated progress, often in workplace or project contexts.
乐不思蜀	lè bù sī shǔ	so happy as to forget Shu: too content to think of home — Lit: happy-not-miss-Shu. From the deposed ruler of the kingdom of Shu, who, kept in comfort by his conquerors, said he no longer missed his homeland. Now said of anyone so absorbed in present pleasure that they forget where they came from.
乐极生悲	lè jí shēng bēi	joy at its extreme gives birth to sorrow — From the Records of the Grand Historian: the wit Chunyu Kun warned King Wei of Qi, over wine, that drinking to the limit ends in disorder and joy at its limit turns to grief (酒极则乱，乐极则悲).
良药苦口	liáng yào kǔ kǒu	good medicine is bitter to the mouth: honest advice is hard to hear — From the Kǒngzǐ Jiāyǔ. Often completed by 忠言逆耳 (loyal words grate on the ear). Used to soften unwelcome but useful criticism.
两情相悦	liǎng qíng xiāng yu è	two hearts delighting in each other — mutual affection — A classical phrase for mutual romantic fondness, where 悦 yuè (to delight in, akin to 喜欢) is shared by both sides. It names the ideal of love that is returned, two people who like and are gladdened by each other, and is often quoted in the context of relationships and marriage.
慢工出细活	màn gōng chū xì huó	slow work produces fine goods — Lit: slow-work-produce-fine-goods. The Chinese counterpart to "haste makes waste." A proverb rather than a classical four-character 成语 in the strict sense, but widely used and culturally important. Invoked to defend careful, unhurried craftsmanship against pressure for speed.
慢条斯理	màn tiáo sī lǐ	slow and methodical; unhurried and in proper order — Lit: slow-stitch-this-manage. Describes someone who does everything at an unhurried, measured pace and in proper sequence. Can be neutral (a composed, methodical person) or slightly pejorative (someone frustratingly slow to act).
妙手回春	miào shǒu huí chūn	miraculous hands bring back the spring; masterly healing — 妙手 (wondrous hands) + 回春 (return spring, restore life's season). The stock phrase of gratitude to a doctor, embroidered on the silk banners (锦旗 jǐnqí) that patients present to hospital departments, a living custom: the walls of a respected clinic hang red with them.
情不自禁	qíng bù zì jīn	unable to restrain one's feelings: cannot help oneself — Lit: feeling-not-self-restrain. When emotion wells up too strongly to hold back: 她情不自禁地哭了 "she could not help crying." 禁 here is jīn, "to bear; to restrain."
全心全意	quán xīn quán yì	with whole heart and whole mind: wholeheartedly, with total devotion — Lit: whole-heart-whole-intent. The 心 (heart) and 意 (intent) doubled to mean nothing held back: 全心全意为人民服务 "to serve the people wholeheartedly." The strongest everyday way to say one is fully committed.
三心二意	sān xīn èr yì	"three hearts, two intentions" — indecisive; half-hearted; wavering — Three hearts pulling in different directions, two sets of intentions competing: the image is of someone who cannot commit. Used to criticize a lack of decisiveness or sincerity in a relationship, a task, or a position. 做事不能三心二意 (You can't do things half-heartedly).
伤天害理	shāng tiān hài lǐ	to wound heaven and harm reason: utterly immoral — An act so cruel it offends both heaven and natural law. Strong condemnation of conscienceless deeds.
深思熟虑	shēn sī shú lǜ	deep thought, mature deliberation — to think long and carefully before acting — 深 shēn (deep) + 思 sī (think) + 熟 shú (mature; thoroughly cooked) + 虑 lǜ (to deliberate; to consider). A four-character description of the deliberative ideal: thought that has gone deep and has been given time to mature, like food that has cooked through completely.
手忙脚乱	shǒu máng jiǎo luàn	hands busy, feet disordered — flustered and disorganized — Lit: hands-busy-feet-chaotic. The image of someone scrambling in a panic, hands and feet moving in disordered haste. Used to describe being overwhelmed and disorganized: the opposite of calm competence. 他一到过年就手忙脚乱 "Around New Year, he's always running around in a flustered state."
手足之情	shǒu zú zhī qíng	the affection of hands and feet: the bond between brothers and sisters — Lit: hand-foot's-feeling. 手足 (hands and feet) is a classical image for siblings, who are as inseparable as one's own limbs. The phrase names the deep tie between brothers and sisters.
谈笑风生	tán xiào fēng shēng	to chat and laugh with verve: lively, easy conversation — Lit: talk-laugh wind-rises, as if a fresh breeze sprang up from the talk. Describes a person who holds a room with relaxed, witty conversation. A warm compliment to a host or a speaker.
通情达理	tōng qíng dá lǐ	"through to feeling, reaching to reason": sensible, reasonable, understanding — 通 (to be through to) + 情 qíng (human feeling; the way people are) + 达 dá (to reach) + 理 lǐ (reason; principle). A person who is 通情达理 understands both how people feel and what reason requires, and weighs the two fairly.
为民除害	wèi mín chú hài	to rid the people of a scourge — To eliminate a menace for the public good, as a hero kills a man-eating beast. Praising tone, used for someone who removes a real threat.
喜出望外	xǐ chū wàng wài	joy beyond what one hoped — overjoyed by surprise — 喜 (delight) arriving from outside (外) the range of expectation (望). The precise word for good news you did not dare hope for: the scholarship, the recovered wallet, the friend at the airport gate. Stronger and more specific than 很高兴.
喜欢就好	xǐ huan jiù hǎo	as long as you like it, that's enough — A warm everyday phrase, not a classical chengyu, used when giving a gift or making a choice for someone: if you like it, that's all that matters. It captures a generous, easygoing attitude and is one of the most common natural uses of 喜欢 in spoken Chinese.
喜气洋洋	xǐ qì yáng yáng	brimming with joy: radiant with a festive, happy spirit — Lit: festive air spilling over. 洋洋 paints joy overflowing in every direction. Said of a face, a household, or a whole town lit up with celebration, a stock phrase at weddings and the New Year.
喜闻乐见	xǐ wén lè jiàn	glad to hear and pleased to see — popular and well-liked — Describes something the public genuinely enjoys and welcomes, literally "glad to hear it and happy to see it." 喜 (glad) and 乐 lè (happy) pair with 闻 (hear) and 见 (see). Often used of art, entertainment, or activities that are widely and warmly received.
相亲相爱	xiāng qīn xiāng ài	to be close and loving — mutual affection between people — Lit: mutually-close mutually-love. The standard phrase for loving harmony in a family or couple. Often used in toasts and wedding speeches.
想入非非	xiǎng rù fēi fēi	to let the mind wander into improper territory; lost in fantasy — 想 xiǎng (to think; to imagine) + 入 rù (to enter; to go into) + 非非 fēifēi (beyond right and wrong; in Buddhist usage, the realm beyond ordinary thought).
笑里藏刀	xiào lǐ cáng dāo	a dagger hidden in a smile: friendly on the surface, treacherous beneath — Lit: laugh-inside hide-knife. Traced to the Tang minister Lǐ Yìfǔ, whose mild smile masked a ruthless heart. Names a person whose warmth is a weapon, the dark twin of 微笑.
心急如焚	xīn jí rú fén	heart anxious as if burning: consumed with worry — 焚 (fén) is to set ablaze. The heart so 急 it feels on fire, used for someone waiting in agony for urgent news. Literary and intense.
心快口直	xīn kuài kǒu zhí	quick heart, straight mouth — frank and direct by nature — Lit: heart-fast-mouth-straight. A description of someone whose inner state and outer speech are perfectly aligned: no strategic filtering, no diplomatic softening. Used as a compliment among people who value directness, and occasionally as an excuse after saying something pointed.
心旷神怡	xīn kuàng shén yí	heart vast, spirit at ease — expansive peace and contentment in beautiful surroundings — 旷 kuàng = open; vast (like an open plain). 怡 yí = pleased; comfortable. The specific feeling of contentment and openness experienced in a beautiful landscape, after a burden lifts, or at a moment of rare clarity. Common at scenic spots: 站在山顶，令人心旷神怡。
心满意足	xīn mǎn yì zú	heart full and wishes fulfilled: perfectly content and satisfied — Lit: heart-full-intent-satisfied. Pairs 心 and 意 again, both filled to the brim. Said of someone wanting for nothing, the calm fullness of having one's desires met.
心想事成	xīn xiǎng shì chéng	may what the heart wishes come true — 心 xīn (heart) + 想 xiǎng (think; wish) + 事 shì (matter; affair) + 成 chéng (to succeed; to be accomplished). A blessing structure: heart-wishes turn into accomplished realities. Among the most common felicitations in Chinese , standard at New Year (新年快乐，心想事成！), birthdays, and before examinations.
心心相印	xīn xīn xiāng yìn	heart imprinting on heart — deep mutual understanding; soul connection — 印 yìn = seal; to stamp. Like two wax seals pressed together making a perfect impression , silent, complete correspondence between two people's inner worlds. 他们心心相印，不需要多说话就能理解彼此。
心有灵犀	xīn yǒu líng xī	hearts share the rhinoceros horn — telepathic understanding; being on the same wavelength — From a Tang poem by Li Shangyin (李商隐): 身无彩凤双飞翼，心有灵犀一点通. The rhinoceros horn was believed in classical natural philosophy to have a channel running through its center , an image of two hearts with a shared passage between them, allowing understanding to flow without words.
兴高采烈	xìng gāo cǎi liè	spirits high, expression ablaze — in jubilant high spirits — Originally literary criticism: Liu Xie's Wenxin Diaolong used 兴高而采烈 to praise the poet Ji Kang's lofty inspiration and brilliant style. The phrase migrated from describing writing to describing faces, and now marks any scene of visible collective joy: children on the last day of school arrive 兴高采烈.
一见钟情	yí jiàn zhōng qíng	to fall in love at first sight — Lit: one-look-concentrate-feeling. 钟 zhōng here means to gather or focus one's feeling on someone. The standard phrase for instant romantic attraction on a first meeting.
一心一意	yī xīn yī yì	single-minded; wholeheartedly devoted — one heart, one mind — Lit: one-heart-one-intention. Used for unwavering focus or devotion , to a person, a task, or a cause. 他一心一意地爱着她。(He loves her with his whole heart and mind, without wavering.) The antonym is 三心二意 sān xīn èr yì , "three hearts, two intentions" , indecisive and halfhearted.
贻害无穷	yí hài wú qióng	to leave behind endless harm — 贻 (to bequeath) + 害 + 无穷 (endless): a mistake or evil whose damage runs on without limit. Compare the proverb 害人之心不可有，防人之心不可无 (never hold a heart that harms others, never drop the guard against being harmed), which frames the moral middle ground 害 sits on.
异想天开	yì xiǎng tiān kāi	wildly fanciful ideas; to have one's head in the clouds — 异 yì (strange; different; fantastical) + 想 xiǎng (thought; imagine) + 天 tiān (sky; heaven) + 开 kāi (to open). Fantastical thoughts that open the sky itself: thinking so far outside reality that heaven itself cracks open.
雨后春笋	yǔ hòu chūn sǔn	spring bamboo shoots after rain: springing up everywhere at once — The image of bamboo shoots erupting after spring rain. Modern usage describes new businesses, buildings, or trends multiplying rapidly.
助人为乐	zhù rén wéi lè	to take joy in helping others — Literally "to help others as one's joy," to find genuine pleasure in aiding people. A prized social virtue, often used to praise the selflessly helpful. Built on 助 (to aid), the same character at the heart of 帮助.
自得其乐	zì dé qí lè	to find one's own pleasure: content in one's own way — Lit: self-obtain-its-joy. Said of someone happily absorbed in a quiet pursuit of their own, needing no audience or approval, perfectly content alone.
左思右想	zuǒ sī yòu xiǎng	thinking left and right — to turn something over and over in the mind; to deliberate from all angles — 左 zuǒ (left) + 思 sī (to think) + 右 yòu (right) + 想 xiǎng (to think). The spatial metaphor makes tangible what overthinking feels like: the mind moving left, then right, finding no ground to rest on.
爱国	ài guó	patriotic; to love one's country — 爱 ài (love) + 国 guó (country). Patriotism. A deeply freighted term in Chinese political culture: 爱国主义 àiguó zhǔyì is patriotism as ideology, 爱国人士 patriotic personages. In everyday use, simply "to love one's country."
爱好	ài hào	hobby; interest; pastime — 爱 ài (to love) + 好 hào (to be fond of). A thing one loves doing , a hobby or passion. One of the first personal vocabulary items learners need: 你有什么爱好？(What are your hobbies?)
爱护	ài hù	to cherish and protect; to take loving care of — 爱 ài + 护 hù (to protect; to guard). To love something by protecting it. Used for public property (爱护环境), younger people (爱护学生), and cultural heritage. A civic and ethical register.
爱情	ài qíng	romantic love; love as a feeling — 爱 ài + 情 qíng (feeling; sentiment; romance). The noun for romantic love as an abstract force or state. 爱情故事 = love story; 爱情电影 = romance film; 谈恋爱 is the verb for being in a relationship, but 爱情 names the thing itself.
傲慢	ào màn	arrogant; haughty; condescending — 傲 ào (proud, lofty, unbending) + 慢 màn (slow to respond, dismissive of others). The most common Chinese word for arrogance: deliberate condescension, the attitude of someone who considers themselves above the normal courtesies. Clearly pejorative.
悲	bēi	sorrow; grief
悲观	bēi guān	pessimistic
悲伤	bēi shāng	sad; grieved
不快	bù kuài	unhappy; displeased; feeling off — 不 bù (negation) + 快 kuài. The heart is not flowing freely , something is blocking it. Milder and more internal than 不高兴 (visibly in a bad mood); 不快 often describes a quiet dissatisfaction or sense of offense that hasn't been expressed.
不喜欢	bù xǐ huan	to not like; to dislike — Negate with 不 before the verb. To soften, insert 太: 不太喜欢 ("don't really like") is gentler and more polite than a flat 不喜欢. For a stronger feeling, 讨厌 tǎoyàn means "to find annoying, to dislike intensely."
嘲笑	cháo xiào	to mock; to ridicule; to jeer at — 嘲 cháo (to taunt; to deride) + 笑 xiào (laugh). To laugh at someone with scorn. This is the cruel edge of 笑: the same sound aimed downward at a target. Takes a direct object, the person mocked.
吃苦	chī kǔ	to endure hardship; to eat bitterness — 吃 chī (eat) + 苦 kǔ (bitter; hardship). To eat bitterness , to endure difficulty without complaint. One of the most culturally loaded compounds in Chinese. 能吃苦 (able to endure hardship) is a major virtue. 吃苦耐劳 = endure hardship and labor without complaint.
迟缓	chí huǎn	sluggish, tardy
触动	chù dòng	to touch; to stir
春风	chūn fēng	spring breeze; warmth, favor — 春 (spring) + 风 (fēng, wind). The mild wind of spring, and by extension any warm, reviving influence, a kind teacher, good news, a stroke of fortune. The phrase 春风得意 (chūnfēng déyì) describes riding high on success.
春季	chūn jì	spring quarter
春联	chūn lián	new-year couplets
春天	chūn tiān	spring; springtime — 春 (spring) + 天 (tiān, day/season). The everyday word for the season, the one you reach for in conversation. 天 here marks a stretch of time rather than a single day, the same suffix in 夏天 (summer) and 冬天 (winter).
春意	chūn yì	the feel of spring; signs of spring — 春 (spring) + 意 (yì, sense/intent). The atmosphere of spring in the air, the green budding and softening light that signal the season has arrived. 春意盎然 (chūnyì àngrán) describes a scene brimming with spring.
春游	chūn yóu	spring outing
春雨	chūn yǔ	spring rain
从容	cóng róng	calm and unhurried; composed
大笑	dà xiào	to laugh loudly
怠慢	dài màn	to neglect; to slight a guest through inattention — 怠 dài (lazy, inattentive, slack) + 慢 màn (slow). To fail to attend to someone with the care and promptness they deserve. The standard word for hosting someone poorly or neglecting a guest's needs. Often appears in apologies: 怠慢了您, please forgive my poor hospitality.
担心	dān xīn	to worry; to be concerned about — 担 dān (to carry on a shoulder pole; to bear a load) + 心 xīn (heart). Lit. "to carry the weight of the heart." Worry as a physical burden: the heart becomes heavy, something you haul around all day. One of the most common emotional verbs in everyday Mandarin.
第六感	dì liù gǎn	sixth sense
冬天	dōng tiān	winter
逗	dòu	to tease; to amuse
毒害	dú hài	to poison, harm
放慢	fàng màn	to slow down; to ease up the pace — 放 fàng (to release, to let go) + 慢 màn (slow). To deliberately release speed. Used for vehicles, speech, breathing, economic growth. The verb of conscious deceleration.
放松	fàng sōng	to relax; to loosen
放心	fàng xīn	to relax; to stop worrying; to feel at ease — 放 fàng (to put down; to release; to set free) + 心 xīn (heart). Lit. "put the heart down." The direct antonym of 担心. If worry is carrying the heart, 放心 is setting it back on the ground: the relief of knowing that what you feared is safe or resolved. Extremely frequent in reassurances and farewells.
飞快	fēi kuài	lightning fast; at a flying pace — 飞 fēi (to fly) + 快 kuài (fast). Vivid intensifier: the speed of something that seems to fly. It modifies a verb as an adverb or stands alone as a predicate adjective. Also used for a blade that is razor-sharp , fast at cutting.
甘	gān	sweet, willing
赶	gǎn	to rush, to catch
赶紧	gǎn jǐn	hurry up; quickly; right away — 赶 (gǎn, to rush, to drive forward) + 紧 (tight). To move with no slack, to act before the moment slips. A spoken, everyday urging to do something now.
赶快	gǎn kuài	hurry up; do it right now — 赶 gǎn (to catch up; to rush) + 快 kuài (fast). Used when something needs to happen without delay. Always an adverb modifying a following verb; it conveys urgency rather than habitual speed, with an implicit time pressure in the context.
感到	gǎn dào	to feel; to register a state — 感 gǎn (feel) + 到 dào (arrive at · result marker). The 到 is a resultative complement signaling that the feeling has landed, been registered as a completed state. The most neutral, all-purpose feeling verb. Almost always takes an adjectival or clausal complement.
感动	gǎn dòng	to be moved; to touch someone's heart — 感 gǎn + 动 dòng (to move). The heart set in motion by something beautiful, kind, or touching. One of the most emotionally central verbs in Chinese, high frequency in literature, film, and daily speech.
感恩	gǎn ēn	to honor all grace received; gratitude as virtue — 感 gǎn + 恩 ēn (grace; benevolence: the kindness of parents, teachers, heaven). A Confucian virtue, the act of recognizing and honoring all goodness received throughout one's life. The deepest gratitude word.
感化	gǎn huà	to reform through moral influence; to transform with virtue — 感 gǎn + 化 huà (to transform; to convert). To touch someone's heart so deeply they are morally transformed. A key Confucian concept: the ideal ruler governs by exemplary virtue; his presence alone 感化s those around him.
感激	gǎn jī	deeply grateful; moved by another's kindness — 感 gǎn + 激 jī (to stir; to agitate; rapids, turbulent water). The heart stirred up by a significant kindness. Implies emotional debt that moves you. Stronger than 感谢; use for meaningful help, not everyday thanks.
感觉	gǎn ju é	feeling; impression; to feel (V &amp; N) — 感 gǎn + 觉 jué (to awaken; become aware; originally: waking from sleep, the first moment of consciousness). A dual-category word that functions as both verb and noun. The most flexible word in the 感 family. Covers gut feeling, subjective impression, intuition.
感慨	gǎn kǎi	bittersweet swell of emotion; to sigh with feeling — 感 gǎn + 慨 kǎi (to sigh; to lament; a person exhaling with grief). The bittersweet wave felt when contemplating time, change, and transience: the specific ache of things passing. Quintessentially literary. Common in memoirs, essays, and poetry.
感冒	gǎn mào	cold; flu; to catch a cold — 感 gǎn + 冒 mào (to emit; to brave recklessly). In traditional Chinese medicine, illness = pathogenic qì 气 entering the body. 感冒 = receiving and being overcome by wind-cold 风寒.
感情	gǎn qíng	feelings; affection; emotional bond — 感 gǎn (to feel) + 情 qíng (feeling). The broad word for emotional feeling, and especially the affection that builds up between people: 他们感情很好 "they have a close bond." Also "to develop feelings": 日久生情 "feelings grow with time."
感染	gǎn rǎn	to infect; to inspire; to spread into — 感 gǎn + 染 rǎn (to dye; to stain; to spread, as dye spreads through cloth). The same word covers pathogen transmission and emotional contagion, both understood as resonance spreading from one body to another, rooted in the classical cosmology of qì 气.
感人	gǎn rén	touching; moving (adj)
感伤	gǎn shāng	melancholy; tender sadness — 感 gǎn + 伤 shāng (wound; grief). The heart wounded by what it feels. Tender, autumnal. The feeling of parting, of beauty that cannot last. A key register in classical Chinese poetry and the ci 词 lyric tradition.
感受	gǎn shòu	lived experience; to experience deeply — 感 gǎn + 受 shòu (to receive; to undergo; to be a recipient). Something comes to you and is taken in. Emphasizes the experiential process rather than the end-state, richer and more embodied than 感觉. Common in art criticism, travel writing, and introspective prose.
感叹	gǎn tàn	to exclaim with feeling; to marvel — 感 gǎn + 叹 tàn (to sigh; to exclaim). When emotion erupts as an exclamation. Memorable fact: 感叹号 gǎntànhào = the exclamation mark (!). The character literally encodes the grammar of feeling.
感悟	gǎn wù	felt insight; wisdom from lived experience — 感 gǎn + 悟 wù (to awaken; enlightenment; the word for Buddhist satori). Insight that comes from experience touching the heart. Deeper than knowledge.
感谢	gǎn xiè	to thank sincerely; gratitude (formal) — 感 gǎn + 谢 xiè (to thank; originally: water overflowing, pouring out one's feelings). Warmer and more sincere than 谢谢. Standard in writing, speeches, and formal situations. Also functions as a noun in 表示感谢.
感性	gǎn xìng	emotional; sensory; intuitive
感应	gǎn yìng	resonance; sympathetic response; induction — 感 gǎn + 应 yìng (to respond; to echo). Used in both physics (电磁感应 diàncí gǎnyìng, electromagnetic induction) and classical cosmology. The doctrine of 天人感应 tiān rén gǎnyìng, Heaven and humanity in constant resonant dialogue, is a pillar of Han Confucian thought.
感知	gǎn zhī	perceptual cognition; to perceive — 感 gǎn + 知 zhī (to know; cognition). Perception as an act of knowing: the full cycle from sensation to understanding. The most formal and academic of the four.
搞笑	gǎo xiào	funny; hilarious
恭喜	gōng xǐ	to congratulate; congratulations — 恭 gōng (respectful) + 喜 xǐ (joy). To respectfully share in someone's happiness. Doubled as 恭喜恭喜 it is the warm spoken "congratulations," and 恭喜发财 gōngxǐ fācái is the standard New Year greeting wishing wealth.
共鸣	gòng míng	resonance; to resonate
故意	gù yì	deliberately; on purpose; intentionally — 故 (deliberate) + 意 (yì, intention, will, mind). To do something on purpose, with conscious intent. Used adverbially: 他故意迟到 (he was late on purpose), 不是故意的 (didn't mean to, it wasn't intentional).
害病	hài bìng	to fall ill, to be sick — 害 (to be afflicted by) + 病 (illness). The causative branch: to be struck down by sickness. Colloquial and slightly regional; 生病 (shēngbìng) is the neutral standard.
害虫	hài chóng	pest, harmful insect — 害 (harmful) + 虫 (insect). An insect that damages crops or carries disease. Its counterpart is 益虫 (yìchóng), a beneficial insect.
害怕	hài pà	to be afraid, to fear — 害 (dread) reinforced by 怕 (to fear). The default spoken verb for fear of a concrete thing or outcome. Takes an object directly or stands alone.
害羞	hài xiū	shy, bashful — 害 (to suffer a feeling) + 羞 (shame). To be visited by shyness, the blush of being seen. Describes a person's manner, not a one-off act.
核心	hé xīn	core; nucleus; the essential center — 核 hé (nucleus; kernel; seed, as in 核武器 nuclear weapons) + 心 xīn (heart). The innermost, hardest, most essential center. Where 中心 is the geometric center, 核心 is the conceptual kernel, what everything else wraps around. Heavily used in political discourse, business strategy, and academic writing.
花开	huā kāi	flowers bloom
怀春	huái chūn	to long for love
欢呼	huān hū	to cheer; to hail with joy — 欢 huān (joy) + 呼 hū (to shout; to call). To shout for joy, the sound a crowd makes in celebration: 欢呼声 "cheers," 人们欢呼雀跃 "people cheered and jumped for joy."
欢快	huān kuài	lively; cheerful; brisk and light-hearted — 欢 huān (joy) + 快 kuài (fast; quick). Joy with a quick, light tempo: a brisk and cheerful mood. Often said of music, rhythm, and movement: 欢快的旋律 "a lively melody," 欢快的舞步 "brisk, cheerful dance steps."
欢乐	huān lè	joy; merriment; happy and festive — 欢 huān (joy) + 乐 lè (happy; glad). Joy of the shared, festive kind: a celebration, a holiday, a crowd in good spirits. 欢乐的气氛 "a joyful atmosphere." More outward and communal than the quiet 高兴.
欢送	huān sòng	to give a warm send-off — 欢 (joyful) plus 送 (see off). To send someone off with celebration: a farewell party for a retiring colleague or a graduating class. The opposite number of 欢迎 (huānyíng), to welcome.
欢腾	huān téng	to be jubilant; to seethe with joy — 欢 huān (joy) + 腾 téng (to leap; to surge). A crowd boiling over with joy, leaping and shouting. Said of stadiums, squares, and whole towns at a great moment: 全场欢腾 "the whole place erupted in jubilation."
欢喜	huān xǐ	happy; joyful; delighted — 欢 huān (merry) + 喜 xǐ (glad). A warm, somewhat colloquial word for being happy and pleased, common in the south and in the phrase 欢欢喜喜 "all merry and glad," and in 皆大欢喜 "everyone happy."
欢迎	huān yíng	to welcome; welcome! — 欢 huān (joy) + 迎 yíng (to greet; to meet). To receive someone gladly. Also the set greeting 欢迎 "welcome!" and the shop sign 欢迎光临 "welcome, please come in." 受欢迎 shòu huānyíng means "popular; well-liked."
缓慢	huǎn màn	slow, gradual (formal)
幻想	huàn xiǎng	fantasy; illusion; to fantasize — 幻 huàn (illusion; phantom; to change; to vanish) + 想 xiǎng (thought; imagination). Thinking directed at what is illusory or impossible: fantasy, wishful thinking, daydreaming. Carries a mildly critical register , implies the thought has decoupled from reality.
急救	jí jiù	first aid, emergency rescue — 急 (urgent) + 救 (jiù, to save). Saving in haste. The set term for first aid and emergency rescue: 急救箱 (jíjiùxiāng) is a first-aid kit, 急救车 (jíjiùchē) an ambulance.
急忙	jí máng	hurriedly, in a rush — 急 (urgent) + 忙 (máng, busy). An adverb of manner: doing something fast because time is short. It sits right before the verb to color the action with haste.
急性子	jí xìng zi	an impatient person, hot-tempered type — 急 (impatient) + 性子 (xìngzi, temperament). Someone whose nature is to rush, who cannot stand waiting. The opposite is 慢性子 (mànxìngzi), an unhurried, easygoing type.
急诊	jí zhěn	emergency medical treatment, the ER — 急 (urgent) + 诊 (zhěn, to diagnose). Emergency consultation. 急诊 is the hospital department you go to when something cannot wait, the Chinese counterpart of the emergency room. 看急诊 (kàn jízhěn) means to go to the ER.
记忆	jì yì	memory; to remember (formal) — 记 jì (to remember) + 忆 yì (to recall, recollect). The faculty of memory and its contents, more formal and abstract than 记得: 童年的记忆 "childhood memories," 记忆力 jìyìlì "the power of memory."
加快	jiā kuài	to accelerate; to speed up — 加 jiā (to add; to increase) + 快 kuài (fast). Adding more speed to something already in motion. Common in official and news language for policy goals, construction schedules, and economic development; equally at home in everyday speech.
艰苦	jiān kǔ	arduous, grim
节奏	jié zòu	rhythm, pace (of life)
紧跟	jǐng ēn	follow closely; keep up with — 紧 (close, tight) + 跟 (gēn, to follow). To follow with no gap. Physical (stay right behind) or figurative (紧跟时代, keep pace with the times).
紧急	jǐn jí	urgent; emergency — 紧 (pressing) + 急 (jí, hurried, anxious). Two words for urgency stacked together, intensifying each other. The register of sirens, evacuations, and red-banner notices: 紧急出口 (emergency exit), 紧急情况 (emergency situation).
紧紧	jǐn jǐn	tightly; closely; firmly — The doubled form intensifies the grip. 紧紧 describes an action done with full, unrelenting tightness, often a hold or an embrace that won't let go.
紧张	jǐn zhāng	nervous; tense; in short supply — 紧 (taut) + 张 (zhāng, to stretch open). A surface pulled tight from both ends. Used for nerves before an exam, the tension in a thriller, and resources stretched thin (时间紧张, time is short).
惊喜	jīng xǐ	a pleasant surprise; to be pleasantly surprised — 惊 jīng (startled) + 喜 xǐ (joy). The happy jolt of an unexpected good thing: 一个惊喜 "a surprise (gift or event)," 又惊又喜 "both startled and delighted."
精神	jīng shén	spirit; vitality; morale; mental state
开心	kāi xīn	happy; glad; to be happy — 开 kāi (open) + 心 xīn (heart). Lit. "open heart." The heart is opened, unblocked, free. One of the most common everyday happiness words in Mandarin, lighter and more colloquial than 高兴 gāoxìng (pleased, glad) and more immediate than 幸福 xìngfú (lasting happiness; bliss).
考	kǎo	to examine; to investigate
可爱	kě ài	cute; adorable; lovable — 可 kě (can; worthy of) + 爱 ài (love). Worthy of being loved: cute, adorable. One of the most-used adjectives in everyday Mandarin. Used for children, animals, cartoon characters, and endearing people.
可笑	kě xiào	laughable; ridiculous; ludicrous — 可 kě (worthy of; -able) + 笑 xiào (laugh). Worthy of being laughed at: ridiculous, absurd. Built on the same 可 + verb pattern as 可爱 (worthy of love → cute) and 可怕 (worthy of fear → frightening). Often carries a sting.
渴望	kě wàng	to crave; to yearn for
刻苦	kè kǔ	hardworking, painstaking, assiduous — 刻 (kè), to carve or engrave, joins 苦 (kǔ) to describe effort that bites in: study or training pursued through self-denial. A 刻苦 student drills past comfort, the way a blade is sharpened against stone.
恐惧	kǒng jù	fear, dread
哭	kū	to cry; to weep
苦瓜	kǔ guā	bitter melon
苦力	kǔ lì	hard manual labor; a coolie — 苦 (bitter, hard) plus 力 (lì, strength) names grueling physical labor and, historically, the laborer himself. The English word "coolie" carries this same syllable. In modern speech 干苦力 means to do back-breaking work.
苦练	kǔ liàn	to train hard, to drill relentlessly — Here 苦 works as an adverb on 练 (liàn, to train): to practice bitterly, to grind. The word frames mastery as something earned through painful repetition rather than talent.
苦难	kǔn àn	suffering, tribulation, misery — 苦 (bitter) joins 难 (nàn, calamity, disaster) to name suffering on a large, often historical scale: famine, war, oppression. Weightier than everyday hardship, 苦难 belongs to the language of nations and generations.
苦恼	kǔn ǎo	vexed, distressed
苦心	kǔ xīn	painstaking effort; careful pains — 苦 (bitter) plus 心 (xīn, heart, mind) names the trouble one's heart goes through for someone else's sake. 一片苦心 (yí piàn kǔxīn) is a heartful of well-meant pains, often unrecognized.
苦衷	kǔ zhōng	hidden hardship; unspoken difficulty — 苦 (bitter) plus 衷 (zhōng, inner feelings) names the private difficulty behind a person's puzzling choice, the bitterness they cannot say aloud. To have a 苦衷 is to act badly for a reason you keep hidden.
快餐	kuài cān	fast food
快车	kuài chē	express train or bus
快递	kuài dì	express delivery; courier
快点	kuài diǎn	be quick; a bit faster; hurry — 快 kuài (fast) + 点 diǎn (a little; somewhat , the softening particle). The most colloquial way to tell someone to speed up. Tonal in its delivery: said gently it is a nudge, said sharply it is impatient.
快活	kuài huó	carefree; cheerful; lively and at ease — 快 kuài (unobstructed) + 活 huó (alive; living; moving freely). The image is life flowing without constraint. Carries a slightly old-fashioned or literary flavor in modern Mandarin, but remains warm and vivid in fiction and colloquial northern speech. Describes people who seem naturally unburdened.
快乐	kuài lè	happy; joyful; happiness (as a state) — 快 kuài (released; unobstructed) + 乐 lè (music; joy; ease). The standard word for happiness in modern Mandarin: it is an adjective predicate, a pre-noun modifier, and a noun. Appears in greetings and fixed phrases far more than the simple 快 alone.
快速	kuài sù	fast; rapid; at high speed — 快 kuài (fast) + 速 sù (speed; velocity). The compound reinforces both components, producing the most neutral, all-purpose word for fast in formal or written Chinese. Works as an adjective before nouns or as an adverb before verbs.
狂欢	kuáng huān	to revel; carnival; wild celebration — 狂 kuáng (wild; unrestrained) + 欢 huān (joy). Joy turned up to its loudest: unrestrained revelry. 狂欢节 kuánghuānjié is the standard word for Carnival, and 狂欢 names any wild, all-out celebration.
懒	lǎn	lazy
乐观	lè guān	optimistic; hopeful — 乐 lè (happy) + 观 guān (to view). To view things on the bright side. Its mirror is 悲观 bēiguān (pessimistic), 悲 bēi being sorrow: the two share 观 and differ only in the mood that colors the seeing eye.
乐器	yu è qì	musical instrument — 乐 (music, yuè) + 器 (implement). Each instrument takes its own playing verb: 弹 (pluck) for piano and guitar, 拉 (draw) for bowed strings, 吹 (blow) for winds, 打 (strike) for percussion. Asking 你会什么乐器? is standard small talk among students.
乐曲	yu è qǔ	a musical composition; a piece of music — 乐 yuè (music) + 曲 qǔ (tune; melody). A composed piece of music: 一首乐曲 "a piece of music." The 曲 names the melody, the 乐 the musical art it belongs to.
乐趣	lè qù	delight; pleasure; the fun of a thing — 乐 lè (joy) + 趣 qù (interest; fun). The pleasure or enjoyment found in an activity: 读书的乐趣 "the pleasure of reading."
乐意	lè yì	to be glad to; willing — 乐 lè (glad) + 意 yì (intention; will). To be happy to do something, with the nuance of genuine willingness: 我很乐意帮忙 "I'd be glad to help."
累	lèi	tired, weary
理想	lǐ xiǎng	ideal; aspiration; ideal (adjective) — 理 lǐ (principle; pattern; the inherent structure of things) + 想 xiǎng (thought; thinking). Principled thinking: the vision of how things ought to be, arrived at by sustained reflection on value and order. Functions as a noun (an ideal, a dream) and as an adjective (ideal conditions, ideal outcome).
理性	lǐ xìng	rational (contrast with 感性)
厉害	lì hai	fierce, formidable; impressive — 厉 (severe, sharp) + 害 (harm), here neutral-toned. Literally cutting and harmful, the word swung into admiration: someone or something so strong it could hurt you. Praise and warning at once.
立春	lì chūn	Start of Spring (solar term) — 立 (lì, to stand/establish) + 春 (spring). The first of the twenty-four solar terms (节气 jiéqì), falling around February 4, when the year's farming cycle is reckoned to begin. 立 marks a season being set up, the same verb in 立夏, 立秋, 立冬.
利益	lì yì	benefit, interest
灵魂	líng hún	soul
领悟	lǐng wù	to grasp deeply
虑	lǜ	to deliberate; to consider; worry
满意	mǎn yì	satisfied; pleased; content — 满 mǎn (full) + 意 yì (heart; wish). The heart filled to its wish, hence satisfied: 对结果很满意 "very pleased with the result." Its opposite is 不满 bùmǎn, dissatisfied.
慢慢	màn màn	slowly; gradually; little by little — Reduplication of 慢 to form an adverb of manner. 慢慢 implies an unhurried pace that unfolds naturally over time, without pressure. Warmer in register than 慢慢地 (the version with the adverbial particle 地), though both are standard.
慢性	màn xìng	chronic; slow-developing (medical) — 慢 màn (slow) + 性 xìng (nature, character, disposition). "Of a slow nature." The medical term for chronic conditions, contrasted with 急性 jíxìng (acute, sudden-onset). 慢性病 mànxìngbìng = chronic illness. 慢性子 mànxíngzi = a person with a slow, unhurried temperament (slightly pejorative; see note).
慢走	màn zǒu	walk slowly — polite farewell to a departing guest — 慢 màn (slow) + 走 zǒu (to walk; to leave). Literally "walk slowly" , said by a host as a guest departs, equivalent to "take care" or "safe travels." The Chinese farewell phrase, so common it has become almost automatic. The literal meaning (don't rush away) has calcified into pure social courtesy.
忙	máng	busy
梦	mèng	dream
梦想	mèng xiǎng	dream; aspiration; to dream of — 梦 mèng (dream; to dream) + 想 xiǎng (thought; aspiration). Dream-thought: a vision held in the heart that exceeds the present reality. Warmer and more personal than 理想 (which has a principled, philosophical register); closer to the English "dream" in the sense of a cherished personal ambition.
敏感	mǐng ǎn	sensitive; highly responsive — 敏 mǐn (quick; alert; agile) + 感 gǎn (feel). Quick to feel. Used across registers: personality traits, skin types, and politically sensitive topics.
耐心	nài xīn	patience
难	nán	difficult, hard
难过	náng uò	sad
脑	nǎo	brain
内心	nèi xīn	inner heart; deepest feelings; the inner self — 内 nèi (inside; inner) + 心 xīn (heart). The deepest, most private layer of a person's interior life, what they feel before they decide how to present it to the world. Common in introspective prose, therapeutic contexts, and literary characterization. 内心世界 = the inner world.
亲情	qīn qíng	family affection; the bond of kinship — 亲 qīn (kin; close) + 情 qíng (feeling). The affection that runs between family members, the love of parent and child or among siblings. It completes the trio with 爱情 (love) and 友情 (friendship).
青春	qīng chūn	youth; the prime of life — 青 (qīng, green/blue, the color of new growth) + 春 (spring). "Green spring," the years of youth at their freshest. Used for the whole season of being young, often with a note of how briefly it lasts.
轻慢	qīng màn	to treat lightly; to show disrespect through dismissiveness — 轻 qīng (light, slight, to make little of) + 慢 màn (dismissive). To treat someone or something as if it barely deserves attention. More literary than 怠慢 or 傲慢. Often appears in classical texts and formal written criticism.
情感	qíng gǎn	emotion (literary)
情况	qíng kuàng	situation; circumstances; condition — 情 qíng (circumstance) + 况 kuàng (condition). The standard word for a situation or set of circumstances: 了解情况 "to understand the situation," 紧急情况 "an emergency."
情人	qíng rén	lover; sweetheart
情形	qíng xíng	situation; circumstances; state of affairs — 情 qíng (circumstance; feeling) + 形 xíng. The plain, everyday word for a situation or set of circumstances. 实际情形 = the actual situation; 这种情形 = this kind of case. Close to 情况 qíngkuàng and often interchangeable, with 情形 leaning slightly more descriptive.
情绪	qíng xù	emotion; emotional state; mood (often the volatile side) — 情 qíng (feeling) + 绪 xù (threads; strands). The tangled threads of feeling, an emotional state that can run high or low: 控制情绪 "to control one's emotions," 闹情绪 "to be in a mood; to sulk."
秋天	qiū tiān	autumn
热情	rè qíng	enthusiasm; warmth; warmly hospitable — 热 rè (hot) + 情 qíng (feeling). Warm feeling, used both as a noun (enthusiasm, passion) and as an adjective for a warm, welcoming person: 他很热情 "he is very warm and friendly."
仁爱	rén ài	benevolence and love; humanitarian care; genuine concern for others — 仁 rén (humaneness) + 爱 ài (love; affection). The compound that captures 仁's essential motion: active, outward-reaching care for others. 仁 without 爱 is a concept; 仁爱 is what it looks like in practice.
忍	rěn	to endure
伤害	shāng hài	to hurt, injure
伤心	shāng xīn	heartbroken; deeply sad; grieving — 伤 shāng (wound; injury) + 心 xīn (heart). Lit. "wounded heart." The heart as a physical organ that can be injured. Used for real losses: a pet dying, a relationship ending, a deeply disappointing outcome. Stronger and more pointed than 难过 nánguò (distressed, upset).
生机	shēng jī	vitality, life-force
事情	shì qing	matter; affair; thing (to be dealt with) — 事 shì + 情 qíng (situation; circumstance). The everyday noun for a matter or affair, anything happening that you talk about or handle. The most common way to say "thing" when you mean a situation rather than an object.
手头紧	shǒu tóu jǐn	short of money
爽	shuǎng	refreshing; great; on form (colloquial)
思考	sī kǎo	to think carefully; to reflect; to consider — 思 sī (to think; thought) + 考 kǎo (to examine; to investigate; to test). Deliberate, directed thinking. 思考 is the most common everyday word for careful, intentional mental activity , the kind of thinking you do when you have a problem in front of you and are genuinely working through it.
思路	sī lù	train of thought; line of reasoning; thinking process — 思 sī (thought) + 路 lù (road; path; route). Lit. "the road of thought." The direction and movement of how thinking unfolds , not the conclusion but the path that leads there. 思路清晰 (a clear line of reasoning) is high praise in Chinese academic and professional contexts.
思念	sīn iàn	to miss; to think of with longing; to yearn for — 思 sī (to think; to miss) + 念 niàn (to think of; to read aloud; to remember , itself containing 心 in its lower half). The compound that doubles the weight. 思念 is used specifically for people , loved ones who are far away, people who have died, a home one has left.
思乡	sī xiāng	homesickness; longing for one's hometown — 思 sī (to miss; to long for) + 乡 xiāng (hometown; native place; countryside). Lit. "to miss the home place." Homesickness as a deliberate, named condition , not vague discomfort but a specific directional longing, pointing back to the place of origin.
思想	sī xiǎng	thought; ideology; way of thinking — 思 sī (to think; thought) + 想 xiǎng (to think; to want; idea). Thought as a formed body , both personal and systematic. 思想 operates on two levels. At the personal level it describes someone's habitual way of thinking, their mental orientation or values: 思想开放 (open-minded , lit. "open thought").
松	sōng	released; unbound — the key quality sought in practice
速度	sù dù	speed; velocity
损害	sǔn hài	to damage, impair
讨厌	tǎo yàn	to dislike; to find annoying
体验	tǐ yàn	to experience; to live through; an experience — 体 tǐ (body) + 验 yàn (to test, to verify). To know something by passing through it in person: 体验生活 "to experience real life." As a noun, the experience itself: 难忘的体验 "an unforgettable experience."
甜	tián	sweet
同意	tóng yì	to agree; to consent; to approve — 同 tóng (same) + 意 yì (idea; intention). To hold the same idea, to give one's assent: 我同意你的看法 "I agree with your view." The opposite is 反对 fǎnduì, to oppose.
痛	tòng	pain, to ache
痛苦	tòng kǔ	pain, agony, suffering — 痛 (tòng), to ache, joins 苦 (kǔ), bitter, to name suffering felt in body or mind. The most direct word for anguish, it covers physical pain and emotional torment alike.
痛快	tòng kuài	frank and direct; thoroughly enjoyable — 痛 tòng (pain; with full intensity) + 快 kuài (free-flowing; pleasurable). Two distinct but related uses. As a personality or speech descriptor: so direct and honest it cuts , blunt, refreshingly frank. As an experience descriptor: so wholly satisfying that nothing was held back.
玩笑	wán xiào	a joke; a prank; banter — 玩 wán (to play) + 笑 xiào (laugh). Play-laughter: a joke made between people who are at ease together. 开玩笑 kāi wánxiào (to crack a joke, to be kidding) is one of the highest-frequency phrases in spoken Mandarin.
玩笑话	wán xiào huà	words said in jest
危害	wēi hài	to harm; harm, hazard — 危 (endanger) plus 害 (harm). The danger turned active: to damage or jeopardize something, and the resulting harm itself. Common with health, society, environment.
微笑	wēi xiào	to smile; a smile — 微 wēi (faint; slight; minute) + 笑 xiào (laugh/smile). A small, soundless laugh: the smile. The word that names the warm, closed-lipped expression, distinct from the open-mouthed 大笑.
温暖	wēn nuǎn	warm
无害	wú hài	harmless
无意	wú yì	unintentional
喜爱	xǐ ài	to love; to be fond of; to favor — 喜 xǐ (to delight in) + 爱 ài (to love). A warmer, more literary cousin of 喜欢, often used of things admired or cherished: 深受喜爱 "deeply loved (by the public)," 喜爱的作家 "a favorite author."
喜好	xǐ hào	a liking; a preference; to be keen on — 喜 xǐ (to like) + 好 hào (to be fond of, 4th tone). Both a verb and a noun for a settled preference or taste: 个人喜好 "personal preferences," 喜好读书 "fond of reading." Note 好 here is hào, not hǎo.
喜气	xǐ qì	a festive air; an atmosphere of joy — 喜 xǐ (joy) + 气 qì (air, mood). The bright, celebratory atmosphere of a happy occasion, the feeling in a room hung with red: 满屋喜气 "the whole house full of festive joy."
喜事	xǐ shì	a happy occasion; a joyous event; (esp.) a wedding — 喜 xǐ (joy) + 事 shì (matter, affair). Any auspicious event, and by default a wedding. Its standing opposite is 丧事 sāngshì, a funeral, the unhappy event.
喜悦	xǐ yu è	joy; delight; gladness — 喜 xǐ (joy) + 悦 yuè (pleased). A literary, slightly elevated word for inner gladness, the quiet glow of a good thing: 丰收的喜悦 "the joy of a good harvest," 内心的喜悦 "the delight within."
夏天	xià tiān	summer
陷害	xiàn hài	to frame, entrap
相思	xiāng sī	mutual longing; lovesickness; romantic yearning — 相 xiāng (mutually; each other) + 思 sī (to miss; to long for). The classical word for romantic yearning. 相思 is not a modern word , it carries centuries of weight from classical poetry.
想法	xiǎng fǎ	idea; opinion; what one thinks — 想 xiǎng (to think) + 法 fǎ (method; approach; way). The product of thinking: what thinking produces when it runs long enough. Used freely for ideas, opinions, views, and intentions. 你有什么想法？ is one of the most common ways to ask for someone's opinion in meetings and conversations.
想家	xiǎng jiā	to be homesick; to miss home — 想 xiǎng (to miss) + 家 jiā (home; family; household). The heart facing home from a distance. A verb in Chinese where English uses an adjective ("homesick").
想念	xiǎng niàn	to miss someone; to long for; to think of fondly — 想 xiǎng (to think of; to miss) + 念 niàn (to think of; to recite; to hold in mind). Two overlapping words for mental attending-to, doubled for emphasis: 念 carries the connotation of something repeated, recurring, kept alive by repetition, the way a prayer or a name is recited.
想象	xiǎng xiàng	to imagine; imagination — 想 xiǎng (to think) + 象 xiàng (image; likeness; elephant , 象 was borrowed for "image" because of its visual suggestiveness: the elephant is the largest thing you picture). Think-image: to form mental images of things not present. Used as both a verb (to imagine) and a noun (imagination).
想要	xiǎng yào	to want; to desire (explicit) — 想 xiǎng (want; think) + 要 yào (want; need; will). The two wanting words stacked for explicitness or emphasis. Where 想 alone can be tentative (I'd like to; I'm thinking of), 想要 is more direct and pointed. Used when desire is clear and conscious rather than a vague inclination.
小心	xiǎo xīn	be careful; take care; cautious — 小 xiǎo (small) + 心 xīn (heart; mind). A small heart is one drawn in, watchful, attentive to fine detail. The literal image captures the bodily experience of caution: the heart pulling back, contracting, alert to what is delicate or dangerous.
笑话	xiào hua	a joke; to laugh at (someone) — 笑 xiào (laugh) + 话 huà (speech; words). Laugh-words: a joke, a funny story. As a verb it flips to "to laugh at, to make a fool of." 讲笑话 = to tell a joke; 闹笑话 = to make an embarrassing blunder that others laugh at.
笑脸	xiào liǎn	smiling face; a smile worn for others — 笑 (laugh, smile) + 脸 (face). A face shaped into a smile, especially the welcoming one you put on for guests or customers. The opposite expression is 板着脸 (bǎnzhe liǎn), to hold the face stiff and stern.
笑容	xiào róng	a smile; a smiling expression — 笑 xiào (to smile; to laugh) + 容 róng (facial expression). The smile as a look worn on the face, treated as a noun: something one has, shows, or loses. Where 笑 is the act of smiling, 笑容 is the expression itself, the way the face holds a smile.
心动	xīn dòng	heart moved; tempted
心理	xīn lǐ	psychology; mental state; mindset — 心 xīn (heart) + 理 lǐ (logic; principle; grain of wood). "The logic of the heart." Used both as the general term for one's psychological state and as the root of 心理学 (psychology as a discipline).
心灵	xīn líng	soul; inner spirit
心情	xīn qíng	mood; state of mind — 心 xīn (heart) + 情 qíng (emotion; situation). The weather of the inner world. Where 情 alone is broad, covering emotion, circumstance, and romantic feeling, 心情 focuses it: the specific emotional atmosphere a person inhabits right now. It fluctuates by the hour.
心思	xīn sī	thoughts; mental energy; what the mind is occupied with — 心 xīn (heart) + 思 sī (to think; thought). What the heart is currently thinking about, or whether it has the mental space to think about something at all.
心意	xīn yì	intention; goodwill; the feeling behind a gesture — 心 xīn (heart) + 意 yì (intention; meaning). The feeling or thought behind an action: the sincerity and warmth that a gift, gesture, or visit carries. Often used when downplaying the material value of what you have brought: 这只是一点心意 ("this is just a small token of my regard").
心脏	xīn zàng	the heart (anatomical organ) — 心 xīn + 脏 zàng (internal organ, the biological sense; same character used in 器官, organs of the body). In modern Chinese, 心 alone almost never refers to the biological organ; that role belongs entirely to 心脏. The standalone 心 is always the conceptual, emotional, and cognitive heart.
辛	xīn	pungent; laborious
辛苦	xīn kǔ	hard, toilsome; thanks for your trouble — 辛 (xīn), pungent or laborious, pairs with 苦 (kǔ), bitter, to name effort that wears you down. As a set phrase it doubles as warm thanks: telling someone 辛苦了 acknowledges the toil they just put in for you.
兴趣	xìng qù	interest, hobby — 兴 (aroused spirit, the xìng reading) + 趣 (inclination, delight). 对…感兴趣 ("to feel interest toward") is the essential pattern: 我对中国历史很感兴趣. 兴趣班 are the after-school enrichment classes filling Chinese childhood weekends.
幸福	xìng fú	happiness; well-being; blessed fortune — 幸 xìng (fortunate; lucky; to survive) + 福 fú (blessing). The compound for subjective happiness and well-being , what you feel when life is going right. 幸福感 = sense of well-being. 幸福生活 = a happy life. One of the most searched Chinese words globally.
性格	xìng gé	character; personality; disposition — 性 xìng (nature) + 格 gé (standard, frame). The settled set of traits that make up who a person is: 性格开朗 "an open, cheerful character," 性格内向 "an introverted personality." The standard word for someone's overall character, often the answer to "what kind of person are they."
要紧	yào jǐn	important; serious; mattering — 要 (yào, to want, to need) + 紧 (tight). What is held tight is what matters. Most common in the negative 不要紧 (bùyàojǐn), meaning never mind, it doesn't matter, often said to reassure.
要紧关头	yào jǐn guān tóu	critical moment; crucial juncture — 要紧 (crucial) + 关头 (guāntóu, the pivotal point). The hinge of a situation, where the outcome is decided and there is no room to hesitate.
意见	yì jiàn	opinion; view; objection — 意 yì (notion) + 见 jiàn (to see; view). What the mind sees on a matter, that is, a considered opinion: 提意见 "to offer a view." It can lean negative, where 有意见 means to have a complaint or grievance.
意识	yì shí	consciousness; awareness
意外	yì wài	unexpected; an accident; a mishap — 意 yì (notion; expectation) + 外 wài (outside). Outside what the heart anticipated, hence unexpected: 意外的消息 "surprising news." As a noun it names an accident or mishap that fell beyond anyone's reckoning.
意义	yì yì	significance; deeper meaning; meaningfulness — 意 (intention) + 义 (righteousness; principle). More serious and weighty than 意思. 意义 refers to significance in the deeper sense , the kind that requires reflection. Used for historical significance, life meaning, moral weight. 人生的意义 (the meaning of life) uses 意义, never 意思.
用心	yòng xīn	attentively; with care
忧	yōu	worry; anxiety
悠闲	yōu xián	leisurely, at ease
友情	yǒu qíng	friendship; the feeling between friends — 友 yǒu (friend) + 情 qíng (feeling). The bond of friendship. Close to 友谊 yǒuyì in meaning, but 友情 leans on the felt warmth, while 友谊 leans on the formal relationship.
有害	yǒu hài	harmful, noxious — 有 (to have) + 害 (harm): possessing the capacity to wound. Its antonym 无害 (wúhài) marks a thing as harmless. Frequent on labels and in science writing.
娱乐	yú lè	entertainment; recreation; to amuse oneself — 娱 yú (to amuse) + 乐 lè (joy). Entertainment or recreation, both the activities and the having of fun: 娱乐活动 "recreational activities," 娱乐圈 "the entertainment world."
愉快	yú kuài	pleasant; cheerful; agreeable — 愉 yú (pleased; at ease; the heart-radical with a sound component suggesting relaxed breathing) + 快 kuài (unobstructed joy). More refined and slightly more formal than 快乐. The word you reach for when describing an experience, a meeting, or an atmosphere as genuinely pleasant.
愉悦	yú yu è	joyful; delighted (literary)
愿意	yuàn yì	to be willing; to want to — 愿 yuàn (to wish) + 意 yì (intent). The willingness of the heart to do something: 我愿意帮你 "I'm willing to help you." It expresses consent and readiness, not mere ability.
灾害	zāi hài	disaster, calamity
着急	zháo jí	to worry, to feel anxious — 着 (zháo, to be caught by) + 急 (urgency). To be seized by anxiety. This is the inward state of 急: a fretting heart, not a fast body. It is the most common everyday word for feeling worried over something time-sensitive.
着急上火	zháo jí shàng huǒ	frantic with worry
真心	zhēn xīn	a sincere heart; from the heart (adv.) — 真 zhēn + 心 xīn (heart). Sincerity located at the heart-level: not just attitude (真诚) but the felt inner orientation. As a noun: 一片真心 "a heart full of sincerity." As an adverb: 真心爱你 "I love you from the heart."
直觉	zhí ju é	intuition
中心	zhōng xīn	center; central point; hub — 中 zhōng (middle) + 心 xīn (heart). Lit. "middle heart." The conceptual and physical center of something: a city, an argument, an organization. Used freely in both spatial and abstract senses. Extremely productive in compound formation: any institution can become a 中心 by adding its domain.
重心	zhòng xīn	center of gravity; focus; emphasis — 重 zhòng (heavy; weight) + 心 xīn (heart; center). A physics term that has crossed fully into metaphorical use: where the weight of attention, effort, or strategic priority lies. Used in business planning, policy discussions, and personal goal-setting.
主意	zhǔ yi	idea; plan; decision — 主 zhǔ (governing) + 意 yì (intention). The plan one settles on, the idea one acts by: 好主意 "a good idea," 拿主意 "to make up one's mind." Note the neutral tone on yi.
注意	zhù yì	to pay attention; to take note; to watch out — 注 zhù (to pour; to fix) + 意 yì (notion; attention). To pour the mind onto something, that is, to pay attention: 注意安全 "mind your safety," 请注意 "please take note."
抓紧	zhuā jǐn	grasp firmly; make the most of (time) — 抓 (zhuā, to grab) + 紧 (tight). To grab and hold with no slack. Literally to grip something hard, figuratively to seize time and not waste it: 抓紧时间 (zhuājǐn shíjiān), make the most of the time.
最喜欢	zuì xǐ huan	to like the most; favourite — With 最 zuì (most), 喜欢 expresses a favourite. The attributive pattern 最喜欢的 + Noun gives "favourite X": 我最喜欢的电影 (my favourite film). This is the standard way to say "favourite," since Chinese has no single adjective for it.
