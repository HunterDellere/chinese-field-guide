//角落書屋/Slices/Comparison
更	gèng	even more; to change; night watch — A character that split across two readings: the comparative pulse of spoken Mandarin and the sonic clock of premodern China.
如	rú	like, as, if, according to — A woman speaking and a mouth complying: from following a command grew the great comparison-and-condition word. 如 is how Chinese says like, as, for example, and if.
越	yu è	to cross over, to surpass — The character of crossing-and-exceeding: a foot-radical verb that travels from "stride over a barrier" to "the more, the more" to a Chinese state name and a national poetic gesture.
比较	bǐ jiào	to compare; relatively, fairly — Compare and qualify: the word that measures differences and hedges claims in everyday Mandarin.
不到长城非好汉	bú dào cháng chéng fēi hǎo hàn	"he who has not reached the Great Wall is not a true hero" — From Mao Zedong's 1935 poem 清平乐·六盘山. Not technically a 成语 (it is seven characters), but functions as a fixed quotation. Often paired in modern usage with 越长城: to "cross the wall" is to prove oneself. The verb implied is 越 (to cross over) or 到 (to reach).
称心如意	chèn xīn rú yì	to suit the heart and match the wish: perfectly satisfactory — Lit: fitting-the-heart-as-the-wish. Everything turning out exactly as one hoped, in feeling and in fact alike. A warm phrase for contentment, and a common blessing for someone's plans or new beginnings.
翻山越岭	fān shān yu è lǐng	"to cross mountain ridge after mountain ridge" — to traverse difficult terrain, by extension, to overcome great obstacles — A four-character compound combining 翻越 (to climb over) split across 山 (mountain) and 岭 (ridge). Used both literally (an arduous mountain journey) and figuratively (overcoming a series of difficulties to reach a goal). Frequent in war-history and biographical writing. 他翻山越岭，终于来到了家乡。
改弦更张	gǎi xián gēng zhāng	change the string and retune — fundamental reform, not adjustment — 改弦 = to change the string (of a stringed instrument); 更张 = to restring and retune. The 更 is gēng, first tone. A musician who only retunes (改弦) is making incremental adjustments; one who also rerestrings (更张) is starting fresh.
如火如荼	rú huǒ rú tú	like fire and like white reeds: in full, blazing swing — Lit: like-fire-like-white-flowers. 荼 is the white flower of a reed. From an account of an army arrayed in red and white, the phrase now describes a movement or campaign surging ahead at full intensity.
如释重负	rú shì zhòng fù	as if a heavy load were set down: deeply relieved — Lit: as-if-releasing-a-heavy-burden. The feeling of relief once a worry or duty is finally over, the body lightening as the weight comes off. 长舒一口气 "to let out a long breath" is its companion image.
如鱼得水	rú yú dé shuǐ	like a fish that has found water — to be in one's element; perfectly suited to one's environment — A fish out of water dies; a fish in water is entirely itself. This chengyu describes the state of someone who has found the exact environment where they thrive , a new job that suits them perfectly, a community where they feel at home. 他在新公司如鱼得水，如虎添翼。
三更半夜	sāng ēng bàn yè	the dead of night — the third watch, past midnight — 三更 (third night watch, 11 pm to 1 am) + 半夜 (middle of the night). The 更 is gēng, first tone. A fixed phrase for any unreasonably late hour. 你三更半夜打电话干什么? "What are you calling at this hour for?" Still in active everyday use; most speakers do not connect it to the premodern watch system.
万象更新	wàn xiàng gēng xīn	all things renewed — the entire world refreshed and reborn — 万 (ten thousand; all) + 象 (phenomena; appearances) + 更新 (to renew). The 更 is gēng, first tone. A classic New Year phrase; appears on Spring Festival banners alongside 春回大地 (spring returns to the earth).
越来越好	yu è lái yu è hǎo	"better and better" — a wish or observation of continuous improvement — Not classical 成语 but a near-fixed conversational formula and a cultural mantra of optimism. The opening line of a famous song by Sun Yue (孙悦) used at the 2000 Lunar New Year gala, 越来越好 ("Better and Better"), fixed it in popular memory.
越陷越深	yu è xiàn yu è shēn	"the more one sinks, the deeper one falls" — to become increasingly trapped — A 越…越… frame applied to 陷 (xiàn, to sink, to become trapped). Used for any situation where each step makes escape harder: debt, addiction, scandal, a bad relationship.
越俎代庖	yu è zǔ dài páo	"to overstep the sacrificial vessel and take the cook's place" — to do someone else's job, to overstep one's role — From the Zhuangzi (庄子). 俎 zǔ is the sacrificial chopping block , the priest's tool , and 庖 páo is the kitchen's domain. The original image is of a priest abandoning the altar to do the cook's work.
还	hái	still, also, even: the continuative adverb — One adverb, four distinct jobs, and a single thread connecting all of them: something continues past the expected boundary.
一点儿 / 有点儿	yì diǎn r / yǒu diǎn r	a little — The classic confusion pair: 有点儿 comes before an adjective and leans negative ("a bit too…"), while 一点儿 comes after and is neutral ("a little more").
一样 / 像	yí yàng / xiàng	same and similar — How to say two things are the same (A 跟 B 一样) or alike (A 像 B), and how this differs from the unequal comparison with 比.
与其…不如	yǔ qí bù rú	rather than … it would be better to — Comparative preference construction: the most elegant way to argue for one choice over another.
越来越	yu è lái yu è	more and more; increasingly — The degree escalation pattern: expressing a quality that grows continuously over time.
比如	bǐ rú	for instance
不断	bú duàn	continuously; without stopping
不妨	bù fáng	might as well; no harm in
不如	bù rú	not as good as; would be better to — 不 bù (not) + 如 rú (as good as). Either ranks one thing below another (A 不如 B, "A is not as good as B") or proposes a better course ("we'd be better off..."). Context tells the two apart.
不一样	bù yí yàng	different
差不多	chà bu duō	almost; about the same
超越	chāo yu è	to surpass; to exceed; to transcend — 超 (chāo, to overtake; to exceed) + 越. The figurative summit of the family: surpassing a competitor, exceeding a limit, transcending a category. Used in business, sport, philosophy, and self-development contexts.
穿越	chuān yu è	to pass through; to cut across; (popular fiction) to time-travel — Cutting through a region. Also the standard verb for popular time-travel fiction (穿越剧, 穿越小说) where a modern character is thrown into a past dynasty.
倒不如	dào bù rú	it would actually be better to (colloquial)
翻越	fān yu è	to climb over (especially a mountain or wall) — 翻 (fān, to flip; to turn over) + 越. The physical act of climbing up and over an obstacle. Strongly associated with crossing mountain ranges; the verb of choice for descriptions of mountain expeditions, escape across hostile terrain, and the Long March.
反而	fǎn ér	on the contrary; instead; the other way around — 反 fǎn (reverse) + 而 ér (and so). Marks an outcome that runs opposite to expectation: you would predict one thing, but the reverse happens. 不但没…反而… "not only did not... but on the contrary...".
更多	gèng duō	even more (quantity) — 更 gèng (even more) + 多 duō (many; much). The most frequent collocation for comparative quantity. Appears in both spoken and written registers without restriction.
更改	gēng gǎi	to alter; to change (formal) — 更 gēng (to change) + 改 gǎi (to alter; to revise). A formal compound for changing plans, decisions, or documents. More official in tone than 改变 gǎibiàn and preferred in administrative contexts.
更好	gèng hǎo	even better — 更 gèng + 好 hǎo (good). The quintessential comparison pair. Appears in everyday speech, advertising, and formal writing with equal frequency.
更何况	gèng hé kuàng	let alone; much less; to say nothing of — 更 gèng + 何况 hékuàng (how much more; not to mention). A rhetorical connective that escalates from a difficult or surprising premise to an even more extreme conclusion. Introduces the harder case: if X is already the case, 更何况 Y is the clincher.
更换	gēng huàn	to replace; to switch out — 更 gēng (to change) + 换 huàn (to swap; to exchange). To replace one thing with another of the same kind: a part, a component, a person in a role. Emphasizes substitution more than 更改, which emphasizes revision.
更加	gèng jiā	even more; still more (emphatic) — 更 gèng + 加 jiā (to add; to increase). A compound adverb that amplifies the degree of 更 further. Preferred in formal writing, speeches, and academic prose. In speech, bare 更 is more natural; 更加 in conversation can sound slightly elevated.
更新	gēng xīn	to update; to renew; to replace with something new — 更 gēng (to change; to replace) + 新 (new). To make new again: to update software (更新系统), to renew a contract (更新合同), to replace aging infrastructure (更新设备). A standard verb in technology, administration, and policy. 持续更新 (continuous updating) is a phrase familiar to every software user.
更衣	gēng yī	to change clothes; dressing room — 更 gēng (to change) + 衣 yī (clothes; garment). To change one's clothing. Formal and classical in register; in modern spoken Chinese 换衣服 huàn yīfu is more natural. Survives today primarily in 更衣室 gēngyīshì (changing room; locker room).
还不如	hái bù rú	would still be better to (spoken)
还好	hái hǎo	not bad; passable; fortunately (context-dependent) — The most versatile of the three hedges. As a standalone response to "how was it?" or "how are you?", 还好 reads as "fine, okay, not bad." As an opener to a clause, it shifts to "fortunately, luckily" , the speaker is noting that things didn't go as badly as they might have.
还是算了	hái shì suàn le	forget it; never mind; on second thought, no
好像	hǎo xiàng	seems like; as if
何必	hé bì	why must; there is no need to; why go so far? — 何 hé (why; what; how , the classical interrogative) + 必 bì (must; certainly). A rhetorical question that questions the necessity of an action, usually suggesting that the action is excessive, unnecessary, or misguided.
何况	hé kuàng	let alone; not to mention
或者	huò zhě	or; either...or (written and spoken) — 或 (perhaps; one possibility) + 者 (the said one; that case). Originally meaning "one possibility or the other," 或者 grammaticalized into a straightforward conjunction meaning "or." It works across both spoken and written registers, unlike 还是 hái shì (which is restricted to questions) or 抑或 yìhuò (whic…
极	jí	extremely
假如	jiǎ rú	supposing; if; in case — 假 jiǎ (false; supposed) + 如 rú (suppose). A slightly more formal "if," at home with imagined or hypothetical cases: 假如我是你 "supposing I were you." Interchangeable with 如果 in most sentences.
就	jiù	then; as soon as
例如	lì rú	for example; for instance; such as — 例 lì (example; case) + 如 rú (like). The standard phrase for introducing an instance: 例如这本书 "for example, this book." Its close cousin is 比如 bǐrú, which is more colloquial.
宁可	nìng kě	would rather (at a cost)
宁缺毋滥	nìng qu ē wú làn	better to lack than to have inferior
宁愿	nìng yuàn	would willingly prefer
仍然	réng rán	still; as before; yet remaining — 仍 réng (still, as before) + 然 rán (so). Marks that a state holds unchanged despite what might have altered it: 仍然 here, the situation is still thus. Close to 还是 háishì but more written.
日益	rì yì	increasingly; day by day (formal)
如此	rú cǐ	like this; so; in this way — 如 rú (like) + 此 cǐ (this). A literary "like this, so," used both to point back at what was said and to intensify: 如此美丽 "so beautiful." The set phrase 原来如此 means "ah, so that's how it is."
如果	rú guǒ	if; in the event that — 如 rú (suppose) + 果 guǒ (outcome). The default "if," opening the condition clause and usually answered by 就 jiù in the result: 如果你来，我就很高兴 "if you come, I'll be glad."
如何	rú hé	how; in what way; what about — 如 rú (according to) + 何 hé (what). The literary "how," asking by what means or in what manner: 如何解决 "how to solve it." The spoken everyday word is 怎么 zěnme; 如何 belongs to writing and formal speech.
如同	rú tóng	just like; the same as — 如 rú (like) + 同 tóng (same). A slightly literary way to say one thing is just like another, common in description and writing: 如同白昼 "as bright as day." In speech 好像 hǎoxiàng is the everyday equivalent.
如意	rú yì	as one wishes; satisfactory — 如 rú (according to) + 意 yì (wish; intention). Turning out the way one hoped: 称心如意 "perfectly content." A staple of festive blessings, where 万事如意 wishes that every affair go as desired.
稍微	shāo wēi	slightly; a touch
太了	tài le	too… (excessive)
相比	xiāng bǐ	in comparison; compared to
像	xiàng	to resemble; to be like; such as — A verb meaning "to resemble, to be like." The basic frame is A 像 B ("A is like B"), asserting likeness rather than identity. It powers similes in the frame A 像 B 一样 + Adj/Verb ("A is just like B"). 像 also introduces examples, "such as / like" (像…这样的, "things like…").
要是	yào shi	if; supposing (colloquial conditional) — 要 (want/will) + 是 shì (be/this). A colloquial conditional meaning "if; supposing that." Slightly warmer and more personally invested than the neutral 如果 rúguǒ. Implies the speaker has some stake in the condition. Typically paired with 就 jiù in the consequence clause.
一点儿	yì diǎn r	a little; a bit (neutral, often comparative) — After an adjective, 一点儿 makes a neutral comparison, "a little more X": 大一点儿 (a bit bigger), 快一点儿 (a bit faster), 便宜一点儿 (a little cheaper). After a verb, it means "a little / some": 喝一点儿水 (drink a little water), 说一点儿 (say a bit).
一样	yí yàng	the same; alike; equally — Marks two things as the same. The frame is A 跟 B 一样 (跟 gēn or 和 hé link the two). Add an adjective to compare a specific quality as equal: A 跟 B 一样 + Adj. Negate as A 跟 B 不一样 ("different") or A 跟 B 不一样 + Adj. 一样 alone can also modify a noun: 一样的衣服 ("the same clothes").
依然	yī rán	still (literary, often emotional)
犹如	yóu rú	just as; like
有点儿	yǒu diǎn r	a bit; somewhat (often "a bit too…") — Placed before an adjective (or a feeling-verb) to say something is "a bit / somewhat" that way, usually with a tinge of complaint or an unwanted state. 有点儿 + 累 / 冷 / 贵 / 难 = a bit tired / cold / expensive / hard. It describes a current condition the speaker finds slightly undesirable.
与其等待，不如行动	yǔ qí děng dài , bù rú xíng dòng	rather than waiting, it's better to act — The simplest pattern: verb phrases on both sides. 与其 + action to reject, 不如 + action to recommend. The rhetorical punch: waiting is rejected as the inferior choice; action is endorsed.
愈来愈	yù lái yù	more and more (Taiwan variant)
越发	yu è fā	increasingly (literary)
越过	yu è guò	to cross over; to get past — 越 + 过 (guò, to pass). The most basic verb of crossing: a fence, a stream, a hurdle, a difficulty. Highly productive in both literal and figurative senses.
越界	yu è jiè	to cross a boundary; to overstep
越南	Yu èn án	Vietnam (lit. "south of Yue")
越权	yu è quán	to overstep one's authority — 越 + 权 (quán, authority; power). Acting beyond what your position permits , a serious accusation in any organization, especially in the bureaucratic hierarchies that have shaped Chinese institutional life since imperial times.
逐渐	zhú jiàn	gradually; step by step
