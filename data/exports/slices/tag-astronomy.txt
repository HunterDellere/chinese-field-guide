//角落書屋/Slices/Astronomy
星	xīng	star; celestial body; spark — Born of the sun radical and a sprout, 星 names the stars, the planets, the week, movie fame, and the spark that starts prairie fires.
披星戴月	pī xīng dài yu è	cloaked in stars, capped with the moon — toiling from before dawn until after dark — To wear the stars as a cape and the moon as a hat is to be outdoors working while they are up. Said admiringly of farmers, delivery riders, and anyone on the early-and-late grind: 他披星戴月地工作，就为了孩子上大学。He works from starlight to moonlight, all so his child can attend university.
物换星移	wù huàn xīng yí	things change, stars shift — time passes and the world turns over — From Wang Bo's Tang-dynasty Pavilion of Prince Teng poem: 物换星移几度秋, "things change, stars shift, how many autumns now?" The stars' slow precession as the clock of dynasties. Used for nostalgia at scale: returning to a transformed hometown, rereading an old diary.
星罗棋布	xīng luó qí bù	arrayed like stars, spread like chess pieces — dotted everywhere — 星罗 (netted like stars) + 棋布 (laid out like go stones). The standard descriptor for anything scattered densely across a landscape: lakes on a delta, islands in an archipelago, convenience stores in Shanghai. The two images, night sky and go board, are both grids the classical eye knew intimately.
星星之火，可以燎原	xīng xing zhī huǒ , kě yǐ liáo yuán	a single spark can set the prairie ablaze — 星星 here is the spark sense of 星. The image of fire racing across a plain is ancient (the Book of Documents already warns of fire on the grassland), but the phrase's modern fame comes from Mao Zedong's 1930 essay taking it as a title, arguing a small revolutionary force could ignite the country.
航天	háng tiān	Spaceflight — Spaceflight: "sailing the heavens," on the model of 航海 (seafaring). 航天员 is the official word for China's astronauts; the industry is 航天工程, space engineering.
火星	huǒ xīng	Mars; a spark — The fire star twice over: the red planet of the five-phase planet names, and the everyday spark from a fire or a grinder's wheel. Internet slang added a third life: a 火星人 (Martian) is someone hopelessly behind on the news.
历法	lì fǎ	lunisolar calendar
明星	míng xīng	star (celebrity) — 明 (bright) + 星: the bright star, classical Chinese for Venus before it became the word for screen and stage fame. 追星 ("chasing stars") is fandom, and a 粉丝 (fěnsī, fan, a sound-loan that also means glass noodles) does the chasing.
太阳	tài yáng	the sun — 太 tài (supreme; ultimate; the greatest) + 阳 yáng (yang force; light). The Supreme Yang , the ultimate source of yang energy in the cosmos. In the 五行 framework, the sun is the source of Fire phase energy. 太阳能 (solar energy) uses exactly this logic.
天文	tiān wén	Astronomy — Astronomy: heaven's patterns. 天文数字 ("an astronomical figure") is the standard idiom for an unthinkably large sum, same metaphor as English.
卫星	wèi xīng	satellite (natural or artificial) — 卫 (guard) + 星: the guard star that attends a planet. Covers the moon, the GPS constellation, and the dish antenna (卫星天线). The Great Leap Forward idiom 放卫星 ("launching a satellite") still means announcing an inflated achievement.
五行	wǔ xíng	the five phases; the five agents — 五 wǔ + 行 xíng (to move; phase; agent). Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water, understood as five phases of cyclical change rather than fixed substances. The generating cycle (相生) runs Wood feeds Fire, Fire makes Earth (ash), Earth bears Metal, Metal carries Water, Water nourishes Wood.
物理	wù lǐ	physics; the principles of matter — 物 wù (thing; matter) + 理 lǐ (principle). Literally "the principles of things," the modern name for physics. A clear case of 理 meaning the lawful pattern running through the physical world.
星期	xīng qī	week; day of the week — 星 (star) + 期 (period): the star period, from the seven-luminary week. Days attach numbers: 星期三 is Wednesday. Asking the day is 今天星期几? , one of the few questions where 几 needs no measure word.
星宿	xīng xiù	the lunar mansions
星星	xīng xing	Star, in the affectionate reduplicated form used in speech and songs. Single 星 does the compounding work: 星空 (starry sky), 星系 (galaxy, "star system"), 星座 (constellation, and the zodiac sign small talk runs on).
星座	xīng zuò	constellation; zodiac sign — 星 + 座 (seat): the seats of the stars. In young urban small talk it means the Western zodiac sign: 你是什么星座的? has largely displaced asking one's Chinese zodiac animal among the under-forty, an ironic import given the antiquity of native Chinese astral lore.
宇宙	yǔ zhòu	The universe — The universe: 宇 all space, 宙 all time, per the Huainanzi's definition. 元宇宙 (yuán yǔzhòu) is the metaverse, the newest tenant in a very old word.
月亮	yu è liàng	the moon (colloquial) — 月 yuè + 亮 liàng (bright; to shine). The bright moon , the standard colloquial word for "the moon." More grounded and everyday than the poetic 明月 míngyuè. 看月亮 "to look at the moon" · 月亮出来了 "the moon has come out."
