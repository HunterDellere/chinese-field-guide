//角落書屋/Slices/Weather
候	hòu	to wait, time, season, climate — A person standing watch beside the road. From the figure of one who waits and watches grows the everyday word for when (时候), the act of awaiting (等候), and the long rhythm of the weather (气候).
雪	xu ě	snow, whiteness, and vengeance — Rain over a sweeping hand: snow is the precipitation you can hold. The same character means dazzling white and, by a buried metaphor, to wipe clean an old grievance.
阴	yīn	the shaded slope, the lunar, the covert — The shadow half of the cosmos and the everyday word for an overcast day. From the dark side of a hill grew the lunar calendar, the hidden plot, and time itself as light and shade.
报仇雪恨	bào chóu xu ě hèn	avenge a wrong and wipe out a grudge — 报仇 (take revenge) paired with 雪恨 (wash away the hatred). The standard four-character phrase for settling a blood debt, frequent in martial-arts fiction and historical drama.
冰天雪地	bīng tiān xu ě dì	ice in the sky, snow on the ground — a frozen, bitterly cold world — 冰 bīng (ice) + 天 tiān (sky) + 雪 xuě (snow) + 地 dì (ground). A vivid set phrase for a landscape locked in deep winter, ice above and snow below, everything frozen. Used to describe the far north, high mountains, or any scene of extreme cold. 探险队在冰天雪地里走了好几天。
光阴似箭	guāng yīn sì jiàn	time flies like an arrow — the days pass with frightening speed — 光阴 guāngyīn (time, light-and-shadow) + 似 sì (resembles) + 箭 jiàn (arrow). Time shoots past like a loosed arrow, gone before you can grasp it. Usually paired with its twin 日月如梭 (the sun and moon shuttle by like a weaver's shuttle). 光阴似箭，日月如梭，一晃十年就过去了。
绿树成阴	lǜ shù chéng yīn	green trees forming shade — lush, leafy, and cool — 绿树 lǜshù (green trees) + 成 chéng (to form) + 阴 yīn (shade). Trees grown so full that their crowns join into continuous shade, a picture of a flourishing, well-grown garden or avenue in high summer. Here 阴 is the welcome, cooling shadow, the same positive sense as 阴凉. 几十年过去，当年的小树苗如今已绿树成阴。
瑞雪兆丰年	ruì xu ě zhào fēng nián	a timely snow foretells a good harvest — 瑞 ruì (auspicious) + 雪 xuě (snow) + 兆 zhào (to portend) + 丰年 fēngnián (a year of abundance). A farmer's proverb with real agronomy behind it: a heavy winter snow insulates the wheat from killing cold, kills overwintering pests, and melts into spring soil moisture.
时候不早	shí hou bù zǎo	the hour is not early: it is getting late — A common set expression rather than a classical chengyu. Used to signal politely that time has slipped on, often as a cue to wrap up or take one's leave: 时候不早了，我该走了 "it is getting late, I should go."
守候多时	shǒu hòu duō shí	to have kept watch for a long while: to wait faithfully and at length — Lit: keep-watch-much-time. Said of someone who has been standing by patiently for a long stretch, often awaiting a person or an awaited moment. 守候 pairs the vigil of 守 with the waiting of 候.
雪上加霜	xu ě shàng jiā shuāng	frost on top of snow — to make a bad situation even worse — 雪 xuě (snow) + 上 shàng (on top of) + 加 jiā (to add) + 霜 shuāng (frost). Frost piled on snow already covering the ground: one hardship stacked on another. The everyday equivalent of "to rub salt in the wound" or "when it rains, it pours." 他刚失业，妻子又生病了，真是雪上加霜。
雪中送炭	xu ě zhōng sòng tàn	to send charcoal in the snow: timely help when it is needed most — The image is bringing fuel to someone freezing in a blizzard. Praises aid that arrives at the moment of real need, the warm counterpart of 锦上添花 (adding flowers to brocade), which only embellishes what is already fine. Still in everyday use.
阳奉阴违	yáng fèng yīn wéi	obey openly, defy in secret — to comply in appearance while resisting in private — 阳 yáng (openly) + 奉 fèng (to obey; to comply) + 阴 yīn (covertly) + 违 wéi (to disobey). To nod and agree to someone's face while quietly doing the opposite behind their back. The phrase turns on the 阴 / 阳 contrast of overt and covert: the visible compliance is 阳, the hidden defiance is 阴.
阴差阳错	yīn chā yáng cuò	yin off, yang astray — a chain of coincidences producing an unexpected result — 阴 yīn + 差 chā (off; awry) + 阳 yáng + 错 cuò (wrong). A tangle of small errors and coincidences, some hidden (阴), some accidental (阳), that combine to produce an outcome nobody intended, often a missed chance or a strange twist of fate. 因为阴差阳错，他们错过了那班火车。
暗	àn	dim; dark; hidden
冰	bīng	ice
春天	chūn tiān	spring; springtime — 春 (spring) + 天 (tiān, day/season). The everyday word for the season, the one you reach for in conversation. 天 here marks a stretch of time rather than a single day, the same suffix in 夏天 (summer) and 冬天 (winter).
地名	dì míng	place names
等	děng	to wait
等候	děng hòu	to wait; to await — 等 děng (to wait) + 候 hòu (to await). A more formal, written-leaning word for waiting than plain 等. Common on signs and announcements: 请耐心等候 "please wait patiently."
冬天	dōng tiān	winter
光阴	guāng yīn	time; the passing days (literally "light and shadow") — 光 guāng (light) + 阴 yīn (shadow). The most poetic word for time in Chinese, naming the days by the very thing that measures them: the alternation of sunlight and shade, day and night, sweeping across the ground like the shadow on a sundial.
候车	hòu chē	to wait for a bus or train — 候 hòu (to wait) + 车 chē (vehicle). To wait for transport. Seen everywhere on station signage: 候车室 hòuchēshì "waiting room," 候车厅 "waiting hall."
候鸟	hòu niǎo	migratory bird — 候 hòu (season) + 鸟 niǎo (bird). The bird that moves with the season, contrasted with 留鸟 liúniǎo, the resident bird that stays year-round.
候诊	hòu zhěn	to wait to see a doctor — 候 hòu (to wait) + 诊 zhěn (to examine medically). To wait for a medical consultation. The room where patients sit is the 候诊室 hòuzhěnshì.
滑雪	huá xu ě	to ski; skiing — 滑 huá (to slide; slippery) + 雪 xuě (snow). To ski, literally "to slide on snow." The word covers downhill skiing and snowboarding alike in casual use.
火候	huǒ hou	the right heat and timing; (by extension) the right moment or level of skill — 火 huǒ (fire) + 候 hou (the timing of). In cooking, the exact control of heat and duration that makes or ruins a dish. Figuratively, the ripeness of a moment or the maturity of a skill: 火候到了 "the moment is ripe."
季节	jì jié	season
冷	lěng	Cold — Cold. 天冷了，多穿点 ("it's gotten cold, wear more") is the all-purpose expression of care from mothers, landladies, and group chats.
凉	liáng	cool
农历	nóng lì	the lunar calendar — 农 (farming) plus 历 (calendar). The traditional lunisolar calendar, named for farmers because its solar terms tell you when to plant and harvest. 春节 (chūnjié) falls on 农历 New Year.
气候	qì hòu	climate; the long-term atmospheric pattern — 气 qì + 候 hòu (season; to wait; signs). 候 originally referred to the five-day intervals used in classical Chinese to track seasonal changes. 气候 is the patterned behavior of atmospheric qi over time: climate, not weather. Also used metaphorically: 政治气候 (political climate).
晴	qíng	clear; sunny (the opposite weather)
霜	shuāng	frost
台风	tái fēng	Typhoon — Typhoon. The Chinese word and the English one echo each other, and scholars still argue over which direction the borrowing ran. Coastal summer news vocabulary.
太极	tài jí	the Supreme Ultimate; Taiji (the source of yin and yang) — 太 tài (supreme) + 极 jí (the pole; the ultimate). In the Yijing tradition, the Supreme Ultimate from which 阴 yīn and 阳 yáng divide. The familiar swirl symbol is the 太极图. The slow martial art is 太极拳 tàijíquán.
天气	tiān qì	weather — 天 tiān (sky) + 气 qì (breath; air; vital force). Literally: the sky's breath. In traditional Chinese cosmology, qì 气 is the invisible force that animates all phenomena; weather is the sky breathing. HSK 1, but it carries this depth beneath the surface.
天文	tiān wén	Astronomy — Astronomy: heaven's patterns. 天文数字 ("an astronomical figure") is the standard idiom for an unthinkably large sum, same metaphor as English.
温度	wēn dù	Temperature, in 度 (degrees Celsius) — Temperature, in 度 (degrees Celsius). 气温 is the air temperature of forecasts; 体温 is body temperature, the one measured at clinic doors.
问候	wèn hòu	to send regards; to greet; to ask after someone — 问 wèn (to ask after) + 候 hòu (to wait on; to attend). To inquire warmly after another person, in speech or in writing: 致以亲切的问候 "to extend warm regards."
雾霾	wù mái	Smog — Smog: fog plus the revived haze character 霾. 今天有雾霾，戴口罩 was the 2010s urban parent's daily instruction; 蓝天 is its cheerful antonym.
下雪	xià xu ě	to snow; snow is falling — 下 xià (to come down; to fall) + 雪 xuě (snow). The standard way to say "it is snowing," built on the same 下 used for 下雨 (to rain). Chinese treats weather that falls from the sky as something the sky "sends down," so snow, rain, and frost all take 下.
下雨	xià yǔ	to rain; it's raining — 下 xià (to descend; to fall from above) + 雨 yǔ (rain). Rain descending from the sky. The 下 construction extends to all precipitation: 下雪 (to snow), 下冰雹 (to hail). Notably, 下 is the standard verb for precipitation in Mandarin , not "fall" or "pour" but "descend." The sky issues weather downward.
小时候	xiǎo shí hou	in childhood; when (one) was young — "When little," a fixed expression for one's childhood. 我小时候 = "when I was a child." Compare 那时候 (back then, at that time) and 到时候 (when the time comes). Note: 小时 (without 候) means "an hour," a different word, so the 候 matters.
雪白	xu ě bái	snow-white; pure, dazzling white — 雪 xuě (snow) + 白 bái (white). The brightest, purest white, with snow as the standard of comparison, exactly parallel to English "snow-white." This is a productive pattern in Chinese: a vivid object plus a color word yields an intensified shade.
雪耻	xu ě chǐ	to wipe out a humiliation

雪人	xu ěr én	snowman
阴历	yīn lì	the lunar calendar; the traditional Chinese calendar — 阴 yīn (moon; lunar) + 历 lì (calendar). The traditional calendar reckoned by the phases of the moon, as opposed to the 阳历 (yánglì, the sun-based Gregorian calendar). Strictly the Chinese traditional calendar is 阴阳合历 (a combined lunisolar calendar), but in everyday speech 阴历 is what people call it.
阴凉	yīn liáng	shady and cool; a shaded cool spot — 阴 yīn (shade) + 凉 liáng (cool). Shaded and pleasantly cool, the welcome shade of a tree on a hot day. Here the shadow is entirely positive, the relief the shaded slope offers in summer.
阴谋	yīn móu	conspiracy; sinister plot; scheme operating in darkness — 阴 yīn (dark; covert; hidden) + 谋 móu (to plot; to scheme). A plan that operates in shadow, unseen , the hidden face that 阴 takes when it manifests as human deception. The contrast with open, legitimate action (which would be 阳) is built into the word.
阴天	yīn tiān	an overcast, cloudy day — 阴 yīn (shaded; clouded) + 天 tiān (sky; day; weather). A day when clouds cover the sun, the most concrete everyday use of 阴. This is the original geographic shade made meteorological: the whole sky becomes the shaded slope.
阴影	yīn yǐng	shadow; a shadow over the heart; a lingering trauma — 阴 yīn (shade) + 影 yǐng (shadow; image). A literal shadow, the dark shape cast where light is blocked, and also a medical shadow on an X-ray (肺部阴影, a shadow on the lung).
影子	yǐng zi	shadow
