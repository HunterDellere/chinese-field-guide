//角落書屋/Slices/Time & Tense
才	cái	talent, ability, only-then, not until — A sprout breaking ground, the first sign of growth. From that image grow two threads: talent as raw promise, and the adverb 才 that marks something later, harder, or smaller than expected.
常	cháng	constant, ordinary, often — A long banner of cloth that came to mean what endures: the constant and the lasting, the ordinary and the common, and the things that happen again and again.
春	chūn	spring, springtime, vitality, the stir of desire — Sun pressing up through young shoots: the season of growth, and the oldest Chinese metaphor for life-force and erotic longing.
当	dāng	to be, just at, ought; (dàng) appropriate, to treat as — A high-frequency polyphone with two readings. As dāng it means to serve as, to be at a given time or place, and ought to; as dàng it means appropriate, to treat something as, and to pawn. The tone tells you which sense is in play.
点	diǎn	dot, point, a little, o'clock — From a single dot of ink the character spreads into points and decimals, into the hours of the clock, into ordering food and lighting a flame, and into the small quantity that softens a sentence.
定	dìng	to fix, settle, decide, surely — A settling element coming to rest under a roof. From that image of a thing made still and fixed in place grows the whole field of 定: to decide, to set, the fixed and stable, the certain and sure.
分	fēn	to divide, minute, point, cent — A knife splitting a thing in two. From the cut grows everything that comes in parts: the minute on the clock, the point on a test, the cent in your pocket, and the act of separating itself.
更	gèng	even more; to change; night watch — A character that split across two readings: the comparative pulse of spoken Mandarin and the sonic clock of premodern China.
后	hòu	back, behind, after; empress — One simplified character collapsed two completely separate traditional forms: 後 (a trailing foot in motion) and 后 (a commanding mouth), binding the spatial sense of behind with the imperial sense of empress in a single glyph.
候	hòu	to wait, time, season, climate — A person standing watch beside the road. From the figure of one who waits and watches grows the everyday word for when (时候), the act of awaiting (等候), and the long rhythm of the weather (气候).
间	jiān	space between, interval, room — Sunlight in the crack between two doors: the character for the space between things. From that gap grow time, the middle ground, the human world, and the room you count one by one.
节	jié	joint, node, festival, to economize — A bamboo joint, the segment between the knots. From that node grows everything that comes in sections: the joint of a limb, the chapter of a book, the festival that knots the calendar, and the restraint that keeps a thing within bounds.
历	lì	to experience, history, calendar — One simplified glyph that absorbed two traditional characters: 歷 (to pass through, history) and 曆 (calendar). The word that holds both lived experience and reckoned time.
明	míng	bright, clear, tomorrow — Sun and moon combined: brightness, clarity, dawn, and understanding.
前	qián	front, before, forward; former — Chinese places the past in front (前) and the future behind: the known faces you while the unknown approaches from behind, the inverse of the English spatial metaphor for time.
时	shí	time, hour, season — The sun moving: time as something visible, measurable, and morally weighted.
现	xiàn	to appear, present, now — The luster of jade coming into view. From that glint of something becoming visible grows the whole vocabulary of appearing, of the present moment, and of what is ready and on hand.
月	yu è	moon, month — A crescent moon in profile, and one of the most poetically charged characters in Chinese literature.
再	zài	again, then, further — The adverb that sequences and repeats: again, one more time, and the subtle art of "and then."
在	zài	to be at, located, in progress — The character that locates: a seedling rooted in earth, expanded into location, preposition, and progressive aspect.
正	zhèng	upright, correct, exactly, in the midst of — A foot halting squarely on the target line. From that image grows everything straight and correct: the upright, the standard, the exact moment, and the ongoing right-now.
功夫	gōng fu	effort, skill over time — Mastery earned through time: martial arts is the paradigm case, but the word covers any deeply cultivated skill.
然后	rán hòu	then; after that — The everyday sequence word for "then, after that," the 先…然后… frame for ordering steps, its overuse as a spoken filler, and how it differs from 接着, 后来, and 于是.
时代	shí dài	era, age — The word for a historical era or age, built from 时 (time) and 代 (generation), and how it differs from 时期 a period, 年代 a decade, 时候 a moment, and 现代 modern times.
时候	shí hou	time, moment — The everyday word for a point in time, and the indispensable 的时候 ("when") pattern that every learner needs.
时间	shí jiān	time — Time: the compound that anchors all temporal expression in Mandarin.
现代	xiàn dài	modern times — The word for the modern era, built from 现 (present, manifest) and 代 (generation, age), how Chinese history slices time into 古代 / 近代 / 现代 / 当代, and how 现代 differs from 现在 now.
现在	xiàn zài	now — The everyday word for now, where it sits in a Chinese sentence, and how it differs from 目前 (at present), 此刻 (this very moment), and 如今 (nowadays).
一会儿	yí huì r	a short while — The everyday word for a little while, how it marks both a short duration and the near future, and how it differs from the verb-softener 一下 and from 等会儿.
正在	zhèng zài	currently doing, progressive aspect — The primary progressive marker in Mandarin -- placed before a verb to signal an action caught in mid-motion, exactly now, still in progress.
终于	zhōng yú	finally, at last — The adverb for "finally, at last," used when something long-awaited or hard-won comes to pass, where it sits in a sentence, and how it differs from 最后, 总算, and 到底.
安步当车	ān bù dàng chē	to walk calmly in place of riding: to be content with a simple, unhurried life — Lit: walk at ease, treat it as a carriage. Here 当 takes the dàng reading, "to treat as." From a Warring-States anecdote: to stroll on foot and reckon it as good as riding, content without luxury.
按部就班	àn bù jiù bān	to follow the proper sequence: to do things step by step in due order — Lit: according-to-section, settle-into-rank. Originally a writer's image of arranging material into its right divisions and lines; now it means to proceed methodically by the established order, neither rushing nor skipping steps. Here 部 keeps its old sense of an ordered division.
不了了之	bù liǎo liǎo zhī	to let a matter fade away unresolved; to end without proper conclusion — Lit: not-conclude-conclude-it. Uses 了 liǎo as a verb twice , "to not conclude [a matter], to conclude [it anyway]." A set phrase for when a problem or investigation is allowed to dissolve without resolution , bureaucratic negligence, diplomatic evasion, quiet abandonment. 这件事就这样不了了之了。
不着边际	bù zháo biān jì	wide of the mark; irrelevant; rambling without getting to the point — 不 + 着 zháo (achieve, land) + 边际 (edge, boundary). Literally "not landing on any boundary" , the talk or argument fails to touch the relevant point. Said of speech, writing, or proposals that circle around without engaging the actual issue.
才华横溢	cái huá héng yì	talent brimming over: overflowing with brilliance — Lit: talent-brilliance-spilling-overflowing. 才华 is creative or literary brilliance, and 横溢 paints it as water flooding past its banks. Said of someone whose gifts are too abundant to contain.
才思敏捷	cái sī mǐn jié	quick-witted; rapid and agile in thought and creative expression — 才思 cáisī = literary talent and flow of ideas (才 here is the noun "talent," not the adverb). 敏捷 = swift and agile. A high compliment for writers, poets, and scholars who produce brilliant work quickly.
尘埃落定	chén āi luò dìng	the dust has settled; a situation has finally reached resolution — Lit: dust-settle-fixed. Evokes the moment after turmoil when everything finally comes to rest and the outcome is clear. Title of Alai's celebrated Tibetan novel.
承前启后	chéng qián qǐ hòu	carry on the past and open the way for the future — To inherit what came before and pass it on, serving as the bridge between earlier and later generations, a 代 to 代 handing-on. Said of a person, work, or era that links what preceded it to what follows, the connective role between ages.
春风得意	chūn fēng dé yì	riding the spring wind, full of pride: flush with success — From Mèng Jiāo's Tang poem on passing the imperial exam. Describes someone on a winning streak, often with a hint that the pride may be premature.
春暖花开	chūn nuǎn huā kāi	spring is warm and flowers bloom: the good season has come — The stock image of spring at its best. Used literally for the season and figuratively for the right moment, a turn for the better after hardship.
大功告成	dà gōng gào chéng	the great work is accomplished — mission complete — Lit: great-achievement-announce-accomplished. A formal declaration that a major undertaking (a construction project, a campaign, a long creative work) has been completed. Often used with a tone of proud announcement: 大功告成！(We did it!).
当机立断	dāng jī lì duàn	to decide decisively at the right moment; not to hesitate when the time comes — Lit: facing-the-opportunity-immediately-decide. The complement of 把握时机 , not just grasping the moment but acting on it decisively. 断 here means to cut through, to decide without wavering. The virtuous counterpart to 犹豫不决 (hesitating and unable to decide).
德才兼备	dé cái jiān bèi	to have both virtue and talent; the ideal of the capable and morally upright person — 德 dé (virtue; moral character) + 才 cái (talent; ability; practical capability) + 兼 jiān (simultaneously; to combine) + 备 bèi (to possess; to be equipped with). The ideal of the complete person , someone who is not only capable and skilled (才) but also genuinely good (德).
的确	dí qu è	indeed; certainly — a frozen adverb containing 的 — 的确 is pronounced dí-què (not de), showing that 的 in fixed compounds retains its old full pronunciation. 这的确是个好主意 "This is indeed a good idea." A reminder that 的 has a fuller historical identity beyond its grammatical particle role.
等着瞧	děng zhe qiáo	wait and see; you'll find out (a mild challenge or prediction) — 等着 (keep waiting, durative 着) + 瞧 (to look, see). The 着 holds the waiting in place: don't stop waiting, the answer is coming.
颠倒是非	diān dào shì fēi	to invert right and wrong; to call black white and white black — Lit: overturn-invert-right-wrong. A severe accusation: to deliberately misrepresent the facts, to claim something wrong is right and vice versa. The logical extreme of a bad-faith 是…的 , not just correcting a focus, but inverting the entire reality. Common in political rhetoric and moral arguments.
点石成金	diǎn shí chéng jīn	to touch stone and turn it to gold: to transform the ordinary into the priceless — Lit: touch-stone-become-gold. From Daoist immortal lore, the touch that turns base rock to gold. Now used of an editor, teacher, or craftsman who makes something dull suddenly shine.
多才多艺	duō cái duō yì	of many talents and many skills: versatile, multitalented — Lit: much-talent-much-skill. High praise for a person gifted in several arts at once, the painter who also writes and plays music. 艺 yì is craft or art, the paired half of 才.
反复无常	fǎn fù wú cháng	to flip back and forth with no constancy: fickle and unpredictable — Lit: repeatedly-without-constancy. A criticism of someone whose moods, promises, or positions keep changing, so that no one can rely on them.
方寸之地	fāng cùn zhī dì	a space of mere inches; a tiny place — also: one's heart and mind — Lit: square-inch-of-ground. 方寸 = one square cun (about an inch). Used literally for extremely small physical spaces, and metaphorically for the heart/mind (one's inner space).
分秒必争	fēn miǎo bì zhēng	to fight for every minute and second: to lose not a moment — Lit: minute-second-must-contend. Said when time is so pressing that each unit counts, in a race, an emergency, a deadline. 争分夺秒 is the common variant.
改弦更张	gǎi xián gēng zhāng	change the string and retune — fundamental reform, not adjustment — 改弦 = to change the string (of a stringed instrument); 更张 = to restring and retune. The 更 is gēng, first tone. A musician who only retunes (改弦) is making incremental adjustments; one who also rerestrings (更张) is starting fresh.
改邪归正	gǎi xié guī zhèng	to abandon the crooked and return to the straight: to mend one's ways — Lit: change-evil-return-upright. Said of someone who gives up wrongdoing and comes back to honest living. 邪 (crooked, evil) is the standing opposite of 正.
高风亮节	gāo fēng liàng jié	lofty bearing and shining integrity: noble character of the highest order — Lit: high-wind-bright-node. Here 节 is integrity, the firm node that does not bend. High praise for a person of unbending principle and selfless conduct.
公正无私	gōng zhèng wú sī	fair and selfless: impartial, with no private interest — Lit: fair-upright-without-self. The ideal of the just official or judge who holds to the line without bending it toward kin, friends, or personal gain.
功夫不负有心人	gōng fu bù fù yǒu xīn rén	effort does not fail the determined person — hard work pays off; persistence wins — One of the most commonly cited Chinese sayings about effort. 有心人 = a person with resolve and intention. The saying frames determination as the prerequisite: effort pays off for those who truly commit to it. 只要坚持，功夫不负有心人。
功亏一篑	gōng kuī yī kuì	to fail for the lack of one final basketful: to fall short at the very end — Lit: achievement-short-one-basket. From the image of building a mound and stopping one last basket of earth before completion. From the Book of Documents (尚书). The lesson: do not abandon a near-finished effort, or all the work before it is wasted.
古往今来	gǔ wǎng jīn lái	from ancient times to the present — Literally "the ancient goes, the present comes," spanning all of history up to now. Used to introduce a truth that has held across the ages: 古往今来，人人都怕死 ("from ancient times to now, everyone fears death"). Here 今 is the classical single character for "the present, now."
古为今用	gǔ wéi jīn yòng	make the past serve the present — To draw on the heritage of antiquity for present-day purposes, using old wisdom, forms, or techniques in modern life. Often paired with 洋为中用 ("make the foreign serve China"), the two together forming a well-known formula for selectively adapting the old and the foreign to modern Chinese needs.
光明正大	guāng míng zhèng dà	open, upright, and honest — nothing to hide — Lit: light-bright-correct-great. Used to describe someone who acts with complete transparency and moral integrity. 他做事光明正大 "He does things openly and uprightly."
后顾之忧	hòu gù zhī yōu	"the worry of looking back" — concern for what you leave behind; anxiety about what is happening at the rear — 后 (behind; the rear) + 顾 (to look back; to attend to) + 之 (classical possessive particle) + 忧 (worry; anxiety).
后生可畏	hòu shēng kě wèi	"the young are to be feared" — the next generation will surpass those before them — From the Analects of Confucius (论语·子罕): 后生可畏，焉知来者之不如今也？(The young are to be feared , how do we know the ones still to come will not surpass those of today?) 后生 = those born later, the younger generation. 可畏 = worthy of awe, of apprehension.
花好月圆	huā hǎo yu è yuán	flowers in full bloom, moon perfectly round — a moment of perfection and happiness — The classic wedding blessing and New Year wish. Flowers (spring beauty) + full moon (reunion, completeness) = the ideal of a happy, complete life. Often inscribed on wedding gifts and red envelopes.
花甲之年	huā jiǎ zhī nián	the year of the flowery cycle — age sixty
画龙点睛	huà lóng diǎn jīng	drawing a dragon and dotting the eye — the small finishing touch that brings the whole alive — From a Tang anecdote: the painter Zhang Sengyou painted dragons but refused to add eyes; when pressed, he dotted the eyes of two , and they immediately flew off the wall. The dot is what brings the form to life. Used today for the small but decisive move that makes a piece of work click.
怀才不遇	huái cái bù yù	to harbor talent yet meet no chance: gifted but unrecognized — Lit: cradle-talent-not-encounter. The lament of the able person who never finds a ruler, patron, or era that will use their gifts. A recurring theme in classical poetry, where the unemployed scholar mourns his wasted ability.
家常便饭	jiā cháng biàn fàn	an everyday home meal: a common, unremarkable occurrence — Lit: home-ordinary-simple-meal. Literally plain home cooking, used figuratively for anything that happens so routinely it is no big deal: 加班对他来说是家常便饭 "working late is routine for him."
间不容发	jiān bù róng fà	the gap admits not a hair: a situation of extreme danger or urgency — Lit: the-gap-does-not-allow-a-hair. The interval is so narrow not even a single hair could fit, so the margin for error is none at all. Here 间 takes the jiān reading for the gap itself.
节外生枝	jié wài shēng zhī	a branch sprouts from the joint: new complications arise unexpectedly — Lit: joint-outside-grow-branch. A side branch springs from the node of a stalk where none was wanted. Said when an already settled matter throws up fresh, unwelcome problems.
节衣缩食	jié yī suō shí	to scrimp on clothes and food: to live frugally and pinch every penny — Lit: economize-clothing-cut-back-food. To deny oneself even basics in order to save, said of those who tighten the belt hard, often to support a family or reach a goal.
今非昔比	jīn fēi xī bǐ	the present cannot compare with the past — Literally "today is not what the past compared to," meaning things have changed greatly, usually for the better, so that now far surpasses before. 今 (now) versus 昔 (former times). A common way to mark dramatic progress or transformation.
惊魂未定	jīng hún wèi dìng	the startled spirit has not yet settled: still shaken after a fright — Lit: startled-soul-not-yet-settled. Describes someone whose nerves are still rattled in the aftermath of a shock or narrow escape, the heart not yet returned to rest.
就地取材	jiù dì qǔ cái	to use local materials; to make do with what is immediately available — Lit: just-ground-take-materials. 就地 = "right here on the spot" (from 就 = to approach what is near). 取材 = to gather materials. The idiom celebrates resourcefulness , using what is immediately at hand rather than waiting for something better. The 就 captures the "readiness without delay" quality.
举棋不定	jǔ qí bù dìng	to hold a chess piece without setting it down: to hesitate and waver — Lit: raise-chesspiece-not-settle. The image of a player holding a piece in the air, unable to commit to a move. Said of anyone caught in indecision who cannot fix on a choice.
开源节流	kāi yuán jié liú	open up the source and restrain the flow: increase income and curb spending — Lit: open-source-restrain-flow. The classic principle of sound finance: widen what comes in while narrowing what goes out. Applied to household budgets, companies, and the state alike.
空前绝后	kōng qián ju é hòu	"empty before, cut off behind" — unprecedented and unrepeated; without equal in all of history — 空 kōng (empty; void) + 前 (before; the past) + 绝 jué (cut off; without) + 后 (after; the future). Nothing like it has existed in the past (the front is empty of precedent) and nothing like it will exist in the future (the back is cut off from repetition).
来历不明	lái lì bù míng	of unknown origin: provenance unclear, background not established — 来历 láilì = origin, background, where something or someone came from (历 here is the path-walked-through that produced the present state); 不明 bù míng = not clear. Said of a person whose background cannot be verified, or an object whose provenance is suspect. It carries a note of suspicion.
老生常谈	lǎo shēng cháng tán	an old scholar's stock talk: a hackneyed, oft-repeated remark — Lit: old-scholar-often-says. A truism so worn from repetition that it carries little weight, the Chinese equivalent of a tired platitude.
理所当然	lǐ suǒ dāng rán	as it should be by reason; a matter of course — Literally "what reason makes ought-to-be-so," something so right and natural that it needs no defense. 当然 ("of course") and 应当 ("ought to") share the 当 of fitting necessity. Sometimes used critically, of someone who takes a kindness as 理所当然 ("for granted").
历久弥新	lì jiǔ mí xīn	grows better with time: the longer it lasts, the fresher and more valued it becomes — 历久 lìjiǔ = to pass through a long time, to endure; 弥 mí = all the more, increasingly; 新 xīn = new, fresh. Lit: "having lasted long, all the more new." Said of friendships, classic works, traditions, and well-made things that do not fade but gain in worth as the years accumulate. 这部经典作品历久弥新。
历历在目	lì lì zài mù	vivid before the eyes: every detail clear in memory as if still happening — 历历 lìlì is a reduplication meaning "one by one, distinctly, item by item," the same clarity-of-sequence sense that runs through 历; 在目 zài mù = "before the eyes." The phrase describes a memory so sharp that each scene appears in clear order in the mind's eye. 童年的往事至今历历在目。
六十而耳顺	liù shí ér ěr shùn	at sixty, the ear follows — one can hear without resistance
妙手回春	miào shǒu huí chūn	miraculous hands bring back the spring; masterly healing — 妙手 (wondrous hands) + 回春 (return spring, restore life's season). The stock phrase of gratitude to a doctor, embroidered on the silk banners (锦旗 jǐnqí) that patients present to hospital departments, a living custom: the walls of a respected clinic hang red with them.
名正言顺	míng zhèng yán shùn	the name is right and the speech follows smoothly — acting with proper authority and justification — Lit: name-correct speech-flows. Directly from the Analects 13.3 argument: when the name is rectified, speech follows naturally, and what follows from speech can be accomplished. Used today when someone has the proper credentials or title to do something , "now that you have the title, you can act."
明察秋毫	míng chá qiū háo	to perceive the finest detail — exceptional discernment — Lit: bright-observe-autumn-hair-tip. 秋毫 = the impossibly fine tip of an animal's autumn coat. Used of leaders and judges who miss nothing. Its opposite: 不见舆薪 bùjiàn yú xīn "unable to see a cartload of firewood", missing what's obvious.
明日黄花	míng rì huáng huā	yesterday's news — past its prime, no longer relevant — Lit: tomorrow's chrysanthemum. Chrysanthemums peak at the Double Ninth Festival (重阳节); by tomorrow they are wilted and past. Used of news, opportunities, or people who are no longer timely.
明知故问	míng zhī gù wèn	to ask knowing the answer — a rhetorical or teasing question — Lit: clearly-know-yet-ask. For when someone already knows the answer but asks anyway, either to tease, to confirm, or to draw out a confession. 你明知故问！"You already know the answer; why are you asking?"
难分难解	nán fēn nán jiě	hard to divide and hard to untangle: locked together, evenly matched — Lit: hard-to-separate-hard-to-loosen. Of a contest too close to call, or of people so entwined, whether fighting or in love, that they cannot be pulled apart.
恰如其分	qià rú qí fèn	exactly to its measure: just right, neither too much nor too little — Lit: precisely-matching-its-portion. Praise for words or actions pitched at exactly the right degree. Here 分 takes the fèn reading, the proper allotted measure.
前车之鉴	qián chē zhī jiàn	"the lesson of the overturned cart ahead" — learn from the mistakes of those who came before — 前 (in front) + 车 (cart) + 之 (possessive) + 鉴 (mirror; lesson; to take as a warning). A cart that has already overturned lies visible on the road ahead of you , it is the past made legible, a warning you can see precisely because the past stands in front.
前仆后继	qián pū hòu jì	"the front one falls, the next one follows" — wave after wave of sacrifice; undeterred succession — 前 (front; those ahead) + 仆 pū (to fall forward, to fall face-down) + 后 (back; those behind) + 继 jì (to continue; to succeed). Those in the front ranks fall; those behind step up to continue.
亲密无间	qīn mì wú jiàn	close with no gap between: utterly intimate, inseparable — Lit: intimate-close-without-gap. Of a friendship or bond so tight that no space, no estrangement, divides the two. Here 间 takes the jiàn reading, the gap as separation.
人在曹营心在汉	rén zài Cáo yíng xīn zài Hàn	body in Cao's camp, heart in Han — physically present but mentally elsewhere; loyalty divided — From Romance of the Three Kingdoms: Guan Yu was held in Cao Cao's camp but his heart remained loyal to Liu Bei's Han cause. The 在…在 parallel structure contrasts physical location (body) with emotional location (heart).
日积月累	rì jī yu è lěi	accumulate day by day, build up month by month — the power of small consistent effort — Lit: day-accumulate-month-build. The Chinese formulation of compounding: small daily efforts accumulate into transformative results over time. Used to encourage consistent study, practice, and work. 日积月累，功到自然成 "accumulate consistently and success will naturally follow."
日新月异	rì xīn yu è yì	changing by the day and the month — rapid, ceaseless progress — Literally "new each day, different each month," for change so fast it is visible from one stretch of time to the next. The standard phrase for the breakneck pace of modern development, especially technology and the transformation of cities, the felt texture of living in the 现代 age.
锐不可当	ruì bù kě dāng	too sharp to be withstood: an irresistible force — Lit: so sharp it cannot be matched. Here 当 means to stand against or block. Said of an advance, an army, or a momentum that nothing can stop.
三更半夜	sāng ēng bàn yè	the dead of night — the third watch, past midnight — 三更 (third night watch, 11 pm to 1 am) + 半夜 (middle of the night). The 更 is gēng, first tone. A fixed phrase for any unreasonably late hour. 你三更半夜打电话干什么? "What are you calling at this hour for?" Still in active everyday use; most speakers do not connect it to the premodern watch system.
善始善终	shàn shǐ shàn zhōng	good beginning, good ending; to see something through properly from start to finish — Lit: good-start-good-end. The virtue of completing what one begins, and completing it well. 终 zhōng (end, conclusion) is the Classical Chinese counterpart to the grammatical 了 , both mark the reaching of an end. This idiom captures the moral weight of completion.
稍等片刻	shāo děng piàn kè	please wait a moment — A polite, slightly formal way to ask someone to wait briefly, 片刻 ("a short moment") being the written cousin of 一会儿. Heard in service settings and on the phone, the courteous register where 等一会儿 would be too casual.
时不我待	shí bù wǒ dài	time will not wait for me — seize the moment; there is no time to lose — Lit: time-not-me-wait. A classical inversion , the subject (时, time) is placed before the negation, with "me" as object, producing the archaic feel of a proverb. The phrase frames time as an agent with no personal loyalty: it moves whether you are ready or not.
时过境迁	shí guò jìng qiān	time passes, circumstances change — things are different now; that was then — 时过 (time has passed) + 境迁 (the situation has shifted , 境 jìng: circumstances, context; 迁 qiān: to move, to change). Used to acknowledge that what was true, appropriate, or possible in the past no longer applies , without judgment, simply as a statement of temporal fact.
时候不早	shí hou bù zǎo	the hour is not early: it is getting late — A common set expression rather than a classical chengyu. Used to signal politely that time has slipped on, often as a cue to wrap up or take one's leave: 时候不早了，我该走了 "it is getting late, I should go."
时势造英雄	shí shì zào yīng xióng	the times produce their heroes — Great figures are made by the conditions of their age, not by innate destiny alone, the circumstances create the opportunity for greatness.
实事求是	shí shì qiú shì	to seek truth from facts — address problems realistically — A classical phrase, later a famous modern slogan, meaning to approach a 问题 on the basis of facts rather than wishful thinking or ideology. 实事 (real matters) + 求是 (seek what is so). The standard expression for a pragmatic, evidence-based way of analysing and solving problems.
事半功倍	shì bàn gōng bèi	half the work, double the result: maximum output from minimum effort — Lit: work-half-achievement-double. The ideal of efficiency: accomplishing more by working smarter. From Mencius (孟子). Its mirror image is 事倍功半 shì bèi gōng bàn (double the work, half the result), wasted, inefficient effort.
事到如今	shì dào rú jīn	now that things have come to this — Literally "matters have arrived at the present," a set opener used when a situation has reached its current, often difficult, point and one must face it: 事到如今，只能这样了 ("now that it's come to this, this is all we can do"). Built on 如今 (nowadays, the present).
事在人为	shì zài rén wéi	things depend on human effort — nothing is impossible with effort — Lit: matters-located-in-human-doing. The outcome of things lies in human action, not fate or circumstance. An optimistic proverb of agency: no matter how difficult, the result is in human hands. 不要灰心，事在人为。
是非对错	shì fēi duì cuò	right and wrong, correct and incorrect — the full moral spectrum of a situation — Lit: right-wrong-correct-incorrect. A compound of two pairs: 是非 (right/wrong as a classical pair) and 对错 (correct/incorrect as a modern pair). Used to describe careful moral judgment of a complex situation. 分清是非对错 = "to clearly distinguish right from wrong in the situation."
是非分明	shì fēi fēn míng	right and wrong clearly divided: drawing a sharp line between them — Lit: right-wrong-divide-clear. Said in praise of someone with firm moral judgment who does not blur what is right with what is wrong.
守候多时	shǒu hòu duō shí	to have kept watch for a long while: to wait faithfully and at length — Lit: keep-watch-much-time. Said of someone who has been standing by patiently for a long stretch, often awaiting a person or an awaited moment. 守候 pairs the vigil of 守 with the waiting of 候.
水到渠成	shuǐ dào qú chéng	when water flows, a channel forms — Literally "when the water arrives, the channel is made", success comes naturally once conditions ripen, with no forcing. It is the gentle counterpart to 终于's hard-won arrival: the long-awaited outcome that, in the end, simply falls into place.
水中捞月	shuǐ zhōng lāo yu è	fishing the moon out of water — chasing an illusion — Lit: water-inside-fish-moon. The moon's reflection in a pond looks real but cannot be grasped. Used for futile efforts chasing something impossible. A Buddhist parable: mistaking the reflection for reality.
死而复生，再世为人	sǐ ér fù shēng , zài shì wéi rén	to die and come back to life; to be reborn into the world — 再世 (again-in-the-world) is the classical expression for reincarnation or miraculous revival. Used both literally (near-death recovery) and figuratively (total transformation of a situation or person). 再世英雄 (a hero reborn in a new era).
似是而非	sì shì ér fēi	seemingly right but actually wrong; plausible but false — Lit: seem-right-but-wrong. Used for arguments, claims, or reasoning that appear correct on the surface but are fundamentally flawed. The danger zone of logical errors , specious reasoning that passes for truth. Appears in classical philosophy and remains common in intellectual criticism.
岁月不饶人	suì yu è bù ráo rén	the years spare no one — time is merciless; age catches up with everyone — 岁月 suìyuè (years and months , a classical compound for the flow of time) + 不饶 bù ráo (shows no mercy, does not spare). A cousin-phrase to 时不我待, but the mood is different: less urgent, more resigned. Where 时不我待 urges action, 岁月不饶人 acknowledges what cannot be undone.
昙花一现	tán huā yí xiàn	to flower briefly and vanish — Literally "the night-blooming cereus shows itself once," for something splendid that lasts only a moment and is gone. The flower opens for a single short while in the night, then withers. A poignant image of fleeting brilliance, the shadow side of "a little while."
堂堂正正	táng táng zhèng zhèng	dignified and upright: open, honorable, with nothing to hide — Reduplicated from 堂正. Said of someone who acts squarely and aboveboard, facing things head-on rather than scheming in the shadows. 堂堂正正做人 "to live as an upright person."
挑拨离间	tiǎo bō lí jiàn	to stir up trouble and drive a wedge: to set people against each other — Lit: stir-up and sow-division. To deliberately create a gap between people who were close, by rumor or provocation. 离间 (jiàn) is to open a rift where there was none.
推陈出新	tuī chén chū xīn	push out the old and bring forth the new — To discard the outdated and create something new, the constant renewal that drives a thing forward into its modern form. Used of art, technology, and institutions that modernize by building fresh work on inherited foundations rather than simply repeating the past.
万象更新	wàn xiàng gēng xīn	all things renewed — the entire world refreshed and reborn — 万 (ten thousand; all) + 象 (phenomena; appearances) + 更新 (to renew). The 更 is gēng, first tone. A classic New Year phrase; appears on Spring Festival banners alongside 春回大地 (spring returns to the earth).
无处不在	wú chù bú zài	everywhere; omnipresent — present in every place — Lit: no-place-not-present. The double negative (无处 "no place" + 不在 "not present") creates a strong universal affirmative: present in every place without exception. Used for things that are all-pervasive , technology, a scent, an influence. 他的影响力无处不在。
习以为常	xí yǐ wéi cháng	to grow so used to a thing that it seems ordinary — Lit: practice-take-to-be-normal. What was once strange or hard becomes routine through repetition, until one no longer notices it. Often said of bad habits or hardships one has stopped questioning.
先来后到	xiān lái hòu dào	first come, first served — Literally "the early-comers and the later-arrivers", the order of precedence set by who came first. It rests on the same 先 ("first") and 后 ("after") that frame 然后, here applied to fairness in turn-taking and priority. The natural order of a queue.
现身说法	xiàn shēn shuō fǎ	to appear in person and teach by example: to instruct from one's own experience — Lit: show-the-body-and-expound-the-teaching. Originally Buddhist, of a deity appearing in bodily form to preach. Now it means to teach others by drawing on what one has personally lived through.
心不在焉	xīn bù zài yān	absent-minded; not mentally present — Lit: heart-not-present-there. 焉 yān is a classical demonstrative "there." The heart/mind (心 xīn , the seat of consciousness in Chinese tradition) is absent from the place where the body sits. Used to describe distraction, daydreaming, or emotional distance during a conversation or task. 他上课时总是心不在焉。
心神不定	xīn shén bù dìng	heart and mind unsettled: distracted and uneasy — Lit: heart-spirit-not-settled. Said of a restless, distracted state in which the mind cannot come to rest, often from worry or anticipation. The opposite of 镇定.
循序渐进	xún xù jiàn jìn	to advance step by step in order — Literally "following the sequence, advancing gradually", to progress in proper order without rushing. It is the principle behind any 先…然后…最后 plan: each step in its turn, the next only after the last. A watchword in learning and training.
一蹴而就	yí cù ér jiù	to succeed in one step — Literally "one stamp of the foot and it is done," to accomplish something in a single easy stroke. Most often used in the negative, to warn that hard things cannot be done all at once: 学语言不能一蹴而就 ("you can't master a language in one go"). The single-action 一 again, the cousin of 一下子.
一点一滴	yì diǎn yì dī	a dot and a drop: bit by bit, little by little — Lit: one-dot-one-drop. The small increments by which patient effort accumulates. 一点一滴地积累 "to build up bit by bit."
一而再，再而三	yī ér zài , zài ér sān	once, then again, then a third time — time after time; repeatedly — The fullest expression of Chinese repetition idiomatics. 一而再 (once to twice), 再而三 (twice to three times): the escalating structure conveys frustration or persistence. Almost always used with a negative coloring: someone has done something they shouldn't have, repeatedly. 他一而再，再而三地失约，让人很失望。
一举两得	yī jǔ liǎng dé	one move, two gains — kill two birds with one stone — Lit: one-raise/move-two-gains. 举 here is a verbal measure word (one lift/movement). A perfect analogue of "kill two birds with one stone." 这样做一举两得 "Doing it this way achieves two goals at once." Among the most common four-character idioms in business and daily speech.
一目了然	yī mù liǎo rán	"one glance makes it clear" — obvious at a single look — 一 (one) + 目 (eye-glance) + 了然 (clearly understood). Describes things immediately comprehensible , a diagram that explains itself, an argument whose logic is visible. The phrase appears in Qing dynasty texts and entered general usage by the early Republic period.
一时半会儿	yì shí bàn huì r	in a short time; for a little while — A colloquial set phrase, "one moment, half a spell," meaning a brief span of time, most often in the negative: 这事一时半会儿说不清 ("this can't be explained in a moment"). It builds directly on the 会儿 of 一会儿, stretching it into a fixed expression for "any time soon."
一言为定	yì yán wéi dìng	one word and it is settled: a deal is a deal — Lit: one-word-makes-settled. Said to seal an agreement on the spot: a single word given, and the matter is fixed, with no going back. The everyday way to say "deal, it's settled."
有始有终	yǒu shǐ yǒu zhōng	to see a thing through to the end — Literally "to have a beginning and have an end", to finish what one starts rather than abandon it midway. The 终 of completion again, valued as a virtue of persistence. The opposite is 有始无终, all start and no finish.
雨后春笋	yǔ hòu chūn sǔn	spring bamboo shoots after rain: springing up everywhere at once — The image of bamboo shoots erupting after spring rain. Modern usage describes new businesses, buildings, or trends multiplying rapidly.
原形毕现	yuán xíng bì xiàn	the original form is fully revealed: a true nature laid completely bare — Lit: original-shape-completely-appears. Said when a disguise drops and someone's real, usually unflattering, character or identity becomes plain for all to see.
再接再厉	zài jiē zài lì	to make further efforts; to keep working hard; to do even better next time — 再接 (take it up again) + 再厉 (sharpen again; work harder again). Used as encouragement after success or after failure: "keep it up" or "do even better." Common in school settings, performance reviews, and sports. 你这次考得很好，希望你再接再厉。
瞻前顾后	zhān qián gù hòu	"look ahead and glance behind" — to be overly cautious; to hesitate by considering every angle — 瞻 zhān (to look ahead; to gaze forward) + 前 qián (front) + 顾 gù (to look back; to attend to) + 后 hòu (back). Looking anxiously in both temporal directions , toward the past and the future , before acting. Used critically of someone who deliberates so carefully they become paralyzed.
着手	zháo shǒu	to set about; to begin tackling (a task) — 着 zháo (second tone, resultative) + 手 hand. Literally "to have the hands land on something" , to get a grip on a task and begin. The image is of hands making contact with work.
着眼	zháo yǎn	to focus on; to have one's eye on; to consider from the angle of — 着 zháo + 眼 eye. "To land the eye on" a target or consideration. Most commonly found as 着眼于 (focused on, with an eye to) or 着眼点 (focal point, angle of concern). Common in political and analytical writing: 着眼长远 "with an eye to the long term." A formal way to name what frame or priority is operative.
指指点点	zhǐ zhǐ diǎn diǎn	to point and gesture: to criticize or gossip about others behind their backs — The reduplicated 指点 zhǐdiǎn (to point out, to instruct) turns sour here: to keep pointing fingers and passing judgment on what is not one's business.
字里行间	zì lǐ háng jiān	within the words and between the lines: the meaning an author implies but does not state — Lit: inside-the-characters, between-the-lines. The unspoken sense that lives in the spaces of a text. 字里行间流露出真情 "true feeling shows between the lines."
过	guò / guo	the experiential aspect marker — The particle that says 'I have ever done this'. Not when, not how often, just that it once happened. The most-confused pair with 了.
还	hái	still, also, even: the continuative adverb — One adverb, four distinct jobs, and a single thread connecting all of them: something continues past the expected boundary.
结果补语	jié guǒ bǔ yǔ	resultative complements — Chinese verbs describe the attempt; a second syllable reports the outcome. Listening is 听, but understanding what you heard is 听懂.
就 vs. 才	jiù / cái	sooner vs. later — The timing and emphasis pair: speaker attitude encoded in one syllable.
了	le	the perfective particle — Post-verb 了 marks a completed action; sentence-final 了 announces a new situation, and the two can stack in one sentence.
没有	méi yǒu	negation of possession and past action — The two most important negation characters, 不 and 没, and why they cannot be swapped.
起来	qǐ lái	directional complement: upward, inward, beginning — The most versatile directional complement in Mandarin: three distinct grammatical lives.
是…的	shì … de	the cleft construction — Focus and past-detail marking: the construction that trips every learner.
一边…一边	yī biān yī biān	while doing; at the same time — Simultaneous actions: the frame for describing two things happening at once.
一下	yí xià	the verb-softener — The verbal-quantity word that makes an action brief and casual, how verb + 一下 softens a request, and how it differs from verb reduplication and from the time-word 一会儿.
又	yòu / zài	再 · again, twice over — Chinese has two agains: 又 looks backward at a repetition that happened, 再 looks forward to one still to come. 再见 is the proof.
在	zài	location, existence, and the progressive — One of the busiest words in Mandarin: the verb "to be at," the location coverb "at/in," and the progressive marker for ongoing action.
着	zhe	the continuous aspect marker — Three distinct jobs: holding a state, linking two actions, and commanding attention. All from a single particle that beginners collapse into one.
艾草	ài cǎo	mugwort; wormwood (Artemisia argyi) — 艾 (ài, mugwort) + 草 (cǎo, herb, grass). Hung over doorways on the Double Fifth to repel evil and insects, owing to its strong fragrance. Also used in traditional Chinese medicine (艾灸 àijiǔ, moxibustion) and in cooking (青团 qīngtuán, green rice cakes flavored with mugwort, eaten at Qīngmíng).
安定	ān dìng	settled; secure; to settle (a society, a life) — 安 ān (peace; safe) + 定 dìng (settled). A secure, untroubled state of life or society, with the calm of 安 added to the fixity of 定: 安定的生活 "a settled life," 安定团结 "stability and unity."
暗	àn	dim; dark; hidden
帮	bāng	to help
帮我一下	bāng wǒ yí xià	give me a hand; help me out — A model of 一下 as a politeness softener. The bare 帮我 ("help me") is blunt; adding 一下 frames it as a small, quick favor and sounds far friendlier. Note that 一下 can follow the object here (帮我一下) because 帮 takes a person directly. The same softening works across requests: 看一下, 等一下, 问一下.
背	bèi	back; to carry on the back
背后	bèi hòu	behind someone's back; in secret — 背 bèi (back of the body; to turn one's back on) + 后 (behind). The space behind a person's back , the zone they cannot see. In figurative use, 背后 means to act covertly, without someone's knowledge or consent. 在背后说人坏话 (to speak ill of someone behind their back) is a strong social criticism.
本领	běn lǐng	skill; capability
表现	biǎo xiàn	to show; to display; (noun) performance, conduct, showing — 表 biǎo (outer; to express) + 现 xiàn (show). To make an inner quality visible on the outside. As a noun it is one's showing or conduct: 他今天表现很好 "he did very well today."
不得不	bù dé bù	cannot but; have no choice but to — 不得不 (not-obtain-not) = double negation creates strong obligation: "cannot not do" = must do, compelled by circumstances. Stronger than 必须 bìxū in the sense of reluctant necessity. The speaker has no choice.
不来了	bù lái le	not coming anymore; decided not to come — 不…了 is the negation of sentence-final 了 , it signals a reversal of an expected or prior state. The 了 here paradoxically signals a new situation: the new situation is "not doing X anymore." Extremely common for announcing changed plans, habits that have been given up, or states that have ended.
不是我说的	bú shì wǒ shuō de	it wasn't me who said it (correcting the agent) — The classic defensive use of 不是…的: denying agency for a known past statement or action. Both parties know something was said , the 不是…的 corrects the attribution. This is one of the most practically useful forms of the construction in daily social life.
不是昨天买的	bú shì zuó tiān mǎi de	it wasn't bought yesterday (correcting the time) — Time correction: both speakers know the item was bought , the correction is about when. 不是昨天 denies the specific time; the speaker may then supply the correct time with 是…的.
部分	bù fen	part; section; portion — 部 bù (section) + 分 fen (a share). A part of something larger, the everyday word for a portion: 大部分 dàbùfen "the greater part, most of," 一部分 yí bùfen "a portion." Note the second syllable goes neutral.
才华	cái huá	brilliance; flair
才两个人	cái liǎng gè rén	only two people (not enough — disappointing) — 才 before a quantity expresses that the amount is smaller than expected or desired. This is the scarcity / insufficiency use of 才: the number is barely enough, disappointing, a minimum.
才能	cái néng	ability; talent; capability — 才 cái (talent) + 能 néng (capacity). The abilities a person actually possesses and can put to use: 发挥才能 "to give full play to one's talents." Note the toned reading cáinéng (the noun); the adverb 才 plus 能 (cái néng, "only then can") is a separate construction.
曾经	céng jīng	once, formerly
插茱萸	chā zhū yú	wearing zhuyu sprigs — apotropaic plant, festival shorthand — 插 chā (to insert; to wear tucked in) + 茱萸 zhūyú (Cornus officinalis; Japanese cornelian cherry). The zhuyu shrub, with its fragrant berries and pungent scent, was believed to repel evil and disease. On Double Ninth, people wore sprigs tucked into their hair or carried them in small pouches.
差不多	chà bu duō	almost; about the same
常常	cháng cháng	often; frequently; time and again — The reduplicated 常. A common, conversational adverb for frequent action. Its negative is usually 不常 bùcháng "not often," since 不常常 is avoided.
常识	cháng shí	common sense; general knowledge — 常 cháng (constant; common) + 识 shí (knowledge). The basic knowledge that holds across a community, what one is expected to know: 生活常识 "everyday know-how," 缺乏常识 "to lack common sense."
嫦娥	Cháng é	Chang'e — the Moon Goddess
朝代	cháo dài	dynasty: a reigning house and its era — 朝 cháo (court, reign) + 代 dài (generation, age). The standard word for a dynasty, the span during which one ruling house held the throne. Chinese history is told as a sequence of 朝代, each with its own name, capital, and character.
趁	chèn	taking advantage of; while
成功	chéng gōng	success: to succeed — 成 chéng (to accomplish; to become) + 功 gōng (achievement). Literally "to complete the achievement." Both verb (to succeed) and noun (success). One of the most common words in modern Chinese, the natural opposite of 失败 shībai (failure). 成功人士 = a successful person. 取得成功 = to attain success.
吃了	chī le	ate; has eaten (the action is complete) — The most basic post-verb 了 construction. The 了 immediately follows the verb and signals that the action was completed. With a bare monosyllabic object, the 了 sits between verb and object. With a quantified object, this placement is even clearer and more natural.
吃完饭就睡	chī wán fàn jiù shuì	sleeps right after eating (immediately, habitual) — 就 in habitual statements describes characteristic immediate sequences , what this person always does right after something else. Often used with mild commentary (it might be a bad habit, or an endearing one). The 就 here encodes: no delay at all between finishing dinner and falling asleep.
充分	chōng fèn	ample; full; sufficient — 充 chōng (to fill) + 分 fèn (measure; share). Filled to the proper measure, fully sufficient: 充分的准备 "ample preparation," 充分利用 "to make full use of." Note the fèn reading.
出来	chū lái	to come out (toward speaker)
出现	chū xiàn	to appear; to emerge; to come into view — 出 chū (emerge) + 现 xiàn (appear; manifest; current). To emerge into visibility , applicable to everything from a rainbow appearing after rain to a new problem appearing in a system. One of the most common verbs in news language and everyday description.
楚辞	Chǔ Cí	Songs of Chu
处于	chǔ yú	to be in (a state or situation)
穿着	chuān zhe	wearing; dressed in (current state) — 穿 (to put on clothing) + 着 = the dressed state is in force. This is among the most natural uses of durative 着, because clothing occupies a position continuously. The same pattern applies to 戴着 (wearing a hat/glasses), 拿着 (holding), 带着 (carrying/bringing).
春风	chūn fēng	spring breeze; warmth, favor — 春 (spring) + 风 (fēng, wind). The mild wind of spring, and by extension any warm, reviving influence, a kind teacher, good news, a stroke of fortune. The phrase 春风得意 (chūnfēng déyì) describes riding high on success.
春季	chūn jì	spring quarter
春联	chūn lián	new-year couplets
春天	chūn tiān	spring; springtime — 春 (spring) + 天 (tiān, day/season). The everyday word for the season, the one you reach for in conversation. 天 here marks a stretch of time rather than a single day, the same suffix in 夏天 (summer) and 冬天 (winter).
春天了	chūn tiān le	it's spring now; spring has arrived (no verb needed) — One of the most elegant features of sentence-final 了: it can attach directly to a noun phrase, without any verb, to announce that the situation has become that thing. This works because 了 marks change of state , and "it has become spring" is a state change even with no overt verb present.
春意	chūn yì	the feel of spring; signs of spring — 春 (spring) + 意 (yì, sense/intent). The atmosphere of spring in the air, the green budding and softening light that signal the season has arrived. 春意盎然 (chūnyì àngrán) describes a scene brimming with spring.
春游	chūn yóu	spring outing
春雨	chūn yǔ	spring rain
此刻	cǐ kè	this very moment
聪明	cōng míng	clever; intelligent; sharp-minded — 聪 (cōng, sharp-eared; quick-witted , 耳 radical) + 明 (bright, clear-sighted , 日 sun + 月 moon). Intelligence as a compound of keen hearing and clear sight. 聪 alone originally meant "sharp of ear" in classical texts; combined with 明 (bright), it became the standard adjective for intelligence.
从来	cóng lái	always; never (with negation); at all times up to now — 从 cóng (from; since) + 来 lái (coming; up to now). "Coming from the past all the way here," from some starting point up to the present moment. Almost always paired with negation: 从来不 (never; has never) / 从来没有 (never; has never happened).
从前	cóng qián	once upon a time; formerly; in the old days — 从 cóng (from; following) + 前 qián (front; before). "From the front / from the time that was in front": an even more remote and literary sense of "formerly" than 以前.
从现在起	cóng xiàn zài qǐ	from now on — A fixed frame meaning "starting from now." 从 (from) plus 现在 plus 起 (to start). It marks a turning point or a resolution: 从现在起，我要早睡 ("from now on, I'll go to bed early"). A close variant is 从现在开始, with 开始 (to begin) in place of 起.
存在	cún zài	to exist; existence
错误	cuò wù	mistake, error; wrong, mistaken — 错 cuò (wrong) + 误 wù (to err, mistake). Both a noun, the mistake itself, and an adjective, mistaken or incorrect: 犯了一个错误 "made a mistake," 错误的决定 "a wrong decision." The standard, slightly formal word for an error of fact or judgment.
担当	dān dāng	to take on; to shoulder (responsibility); accountability — 担 dān (to carry) + 当 dāng (to take up a role). To take responsibility upon oneself; as a noun, the quality of being willing to do so. 有担当 yǒu dāndāng describes a person who steps up rather than dodging.
当兵	dāng bīng	to be a soldier
当成	dàng chéng	to treat as
当代	dāng dài	contemporary era
当地	dāng dì	local; of the place in question — 当 dāng (right at) + 地 dì (place). "Of the very place" you are speaking about: 当地人 dāngdì rén (locals), 当地政府 dāngdì zhèngfǔ (the local government). The spatial twin of 当时.
当面	dāng miàn	face to face; to one's face; in person — 当 dāng (right at) + 面 miàn (face). To do something in someone's presence rather than behind their back: 当面说清楚 (sort it out face to face). Carries a note of directness and accountability.
当铺	dàng pù	pawnshop
当然	dāng rán	of course; naturally; certainly — 当 dāng (ought, fitting) + 然 rán (so). "As it should be," and so "of course." Stands alone as a full answer (当然！"of course!") or leads a clause: 当然可以 "certainly you may."
当时	dāng shí	at that time; then; at the time in question — 当 dāng (right at) + 时 shí (time). Points back to a specific past moment and the state of things in it: "at the time." Often paired with a later contrast, as in "back then I did not understand, but now I do."
当作	dàng zuò	to treat as; to regard as; to take for — 当 dàng (to equate) + 作 zuò (to make, to do). To regard X as Y: 把困难当作机会 (treat difficulty as opportunity). A near-synonym is 当成 dàngchéng. Note the falling tone, which marks it apart from level-tone dāng.
到底	dào dǐ	after all; in the end; (interrogative intensifier) — 到 dào (arrive at) + 底 dǐ (bottom; base). Arriving at the bottom: getting to the root of the matter, reaching the real answer. In interrogative sentences, 到底 intensifies the question and demands a genuine response , it presses the interlocutor to stop evading and give a straight answer.
到现在	dào xiàn zài	up to now; so far — "Up to the present," used to measure a stretch reaching the present moment, often with 还 (still) or 都 to stress that something has or has not happened by now: 到现在他还没来 ("he still hasn't come, even now"). The fuller form 到现在为止 ("up to now") adds 为止 ("as the limit").

地点	dì diǎn	place; location; site; venue — 地 dì (ground) + 点 diǎn (point). A specific point on the ground, the precise spot where something happens: a meeting place, an event venue, the scene of an incident. More exact than 地方 dìfang.

分数	fēn shù	score; mark; (math) fraction — 分 fēn (point) + 数 shù (number). A test score, and in mathematics a fraction. Both senses share the idea of a number standing below or against a whole.
分钟	fēn zhōng	minute (of time) — 分 fēn (a divided unit) + 钟 zhōng (clock). A minute as a span of time: 等五分钟 "wait five minutes." To tell the time on the clock face, 分 alone is enough: 两点十五分 "two fifteen."

夫妻	fū qī	husband and wife — the household foundation — The marital relationship orders the household 家 jiā, which is the structural unit between the individual and the state. The husband is obligated to provide and to lead with moral example; the wife is obligated to manage the inner household and to support the family's moral life.
佛教	fó jiào	Buddhism (the religion) — 佛 (Buddha) + 教 jiào (teaching; religion; to teach). The standard modern term for Buddhism as a religion or institutional tradition. Entered China officially during the Han dynasty (around the 1st century CE), though oral transmission and merchant routes likely brought it earlier.
附近	fù jìn	nearby; in the vicinity; around here — 附 fù (to attach to; to be adjacent to) + 近 (near). The area attached nearby , the general term for the vicinity of a place. 附近 functions as both noun (学校附近, in the vicinity of the school) and adverb-like modifier.
改正	gǎi zhèng	to correct; to put right; to amend — 改 gǎi (to change) + 正 zhèng (straight). To change something crooked back to straight: 改正错误 "to correct a mistake." The act of bringing a thing back onto the line.
感觉动词 + 起来	gǎn ju é dòng cí + qǐ lái	"when it comes to [sense]: [adjective]" — When a perception verb (看 kàn, 听 tīng, 吃 chī, 闻 wén, 用 yòng, 做 zuò) is followed by 起来 and then an adjective, the meaning is: "judging by [sense] / when you [sense] it, [it is adj]." This is one of the most common patterns in spoken Mandarin for expressing impressions and evaluations.
刚才	gāng cái	just now; a moment ago — 刚 gāng (just; barely) + 才 cái. A time word for the very recent past, the moment that has only just passed: 刚才他还在这儿 "he was still here just now." Here 才 keeps its sense of a threshold only just crossed.
更多	gèng duō	even more (quantity) — 更 gèng (even more) + 多 duō (many; much). The most frequent collocation for comparative quantity. Appears in both spoken and written registers without restriction.
更改	gēng gǎi	to alter; to change (formal) — 更 gēng (to change) + 改 gǎi (to alter; to revise). A formal compound for changing plans, decisions, or documents. More official in tone than 改变 gǎibiàn and preferred in administrative contexts.
更好	gèng hǎo	even better — 更 gèng + 好 hǎo (good). The quintessential comparison pair. Appears in everyday speech, advertising, and formal writing with equal frequency.
更何况	gèng hé kuàng	let alone; much less; to say nothing of — 更 gèng + 何况 hékuàng (how much more; not to mention). A rhetorical connective that escalates from a difficult or surprising premise to an even more extreme conclusion. Introduces the harder case: if X is already the case, 更何况 Y is the clincher.
更换	gēng huàn	to replace; to switch out — 更 gēng (to change) + 换 huàn (to swap; to exchange). To replace one thing with another of the same kind: a part, a component, a person in a role. Emphasizes substitution more than 更改, which emphasizes revision.
更加	gèng jiā	even more; still more (emphatic) — 更 gèng + 加 jiā (to add; to increase). A compound adverb that amplifies the degree of 更 further. Preferred in formal writing, speeches, and academic prose. In speech, bare 更 is more natural; 更加 in conversation can sound slightly elevated.
更新	gēng xīn	to update; to renew; to replace with something new — 更 gēng (to change; to replace) + 新 (new). To make new again: to update software (更新系统), to renew a contract (更新合同), to replace aging infrastructure (更新设备). A standard verb in technology, administration, and policy. 持续更新 (continuous updating) is a phrase familiar to every software user.
更衣	gēng yī	to change clothes; dressing room — 更 gēng (to change) + 衣 yī (clothes; garment). To change one's clothing. Formal and classical in register; in modern spoken Chinese 换衣服 huàn yīfu is more natural. Survives today primarily in 更衣室 gēngyīshì (changing room; locker room).
工夫	gōng fu	time and effort; trouble spent on a task — 工 gōng (work, labor) + 夫 fū (effort). The time and care put into doing something: 花工夫 to spend effort, 没工夫 to have no time. It measures the investment a task demands rather than the skill it yields, the cost side of mastery.
公历	gōng lì	the common (Gregorian) calendar
公平	gōng píng	fair; just; impartial — 公 gōng (public; impartial) + 平 píng (level). Even-handed dealing that favors no side: 公平竞争 "fair competition," 这不公平 "that's not fair." The everyday word for fairness.
公正	gōng zhèng	just; impartial; fair-minded — 公 gōng (impartial) + 正 zhèng (upright). The public standard joined to the straight line: justice as judgment held to the same mark for all. Common of judges, verdicts, and arbiters: 公正的判决 "a fair verdict."
功课	gōng kè	homework; schoolwork; preparation; research done before a task — 功 gōng (work; effort) + 课 kè (lesson; course). Originally: the work (功夫) one does for one's lessons (课).
功劳	gōng láo	merit: credit for meritorious service — 功 gōng (merit) + 劳 láo (toil; labor). The credit owed to someone for hard work that produced real results. Used to acknowledge contribution: 这是大家的功劳 = this is everyone's accomplishment. The modest counterpart is 苦劳 kǔláo, credit merely for effort even without success.
功能	gōng néng	function; capability; feature — 功 gōng (work; merit) + 能 néng (ability; can). The work that something is able to do , its function. 古代	gǔ dài	ancient times: antiquity — 古 gǔ (ancient) + 代 dài (era). The distant past as a historical age, the time of the old dynasties, sages, and classics: 古代文学 "classical literature," 古代史 "ancient history." Stands opposite 现代 (modern) and 近代 (the recent, early-modern past).
固定	gù dìng	fixed; to fix in place; regular — 固 gù (solid; firm) + 定 dìng (fixed). Held firmly in one place or pattern, not movable or variable: 固定收入 "a fixed income," 把架子固定在墙上 "to fix the shelf to the wall."
关节	guān jié	joint (of the body); juncture — 关 guān (to join; pass) + 节 jié (joint). An anatomical joint, the hinge where bones meet: 膝关节 "knee joint." By extension, a critical juncture in a matter, the place where things turn.
光明	guāng míng	light; radiance; a bright future — 光 guāng (light; beam) + 明 míng (bright). More elevated and abstract than 明亮. Used in spiritual and political registers: 光明大道 "the road of light" (a righteous path), 走向光明 "moving toward the light." The opposite is 黑暗 hēi'àn darkness.

经过	jīng guò	to pass through; to go by; (after) the course of — 经 jīng (pass through) + 过 guò (cross over). To travel through a place, or to go through a process. As a noun it names the whole course of events: 事情的经过 "how the matter unfolded."
经历	jīng lì	to experience; experience; what one has been through — 经 jīng (to pass through; warp threads; to undergo) + 历 lì (to go through). The most common everyday word for living through something and for the sum of what one has lived through. As a verb it takes events as objects: you 经历 a war, a hardship, a change.
经验	jīng yàn	experience (accumulated know-how) — 经 jīng (pass through) + 验 yàn (to test; verify). The practical know-how gathered by having passed through and tested much: 工作经验 "work experience." Distinct from 经历, which is the events themselves rather than the lessons drawn from them.
精通	jīng tōng	to be proficient in; to master; to have a thorough command of — 精 jīng (refined; essence; finely worked) + 通 (to be all the way through). To have passed thoroughly through a field down to its fine grain: full mastery, not mere acquaintance. 精通 is reserved for genuine expertise: 精通三门外语 (fluent in three foreign languages), 精通法律 (thoroughly versed in law).
九九	jiǔ jiǔ	double nine — colloquial shorthand; homophonic longevity wish — 九 jiǔ (nine) + 九 jiǔ (nine). The colloquial compressed name for the festival, and a sound-based wish: 九九 is homophonous with 久久 (jiǔ jiǔ, for a long time; lasting), turning the calendar date into an expression of the wish for long life.
就	jiù	then; as soon as
就是	jiù shì	precisely; exactly; even if; it's just that — 就 jiù (then; precisely; at once) + 是. Multiple functions make 就是 one of the most versatile particles in spoken Mandarin.
就要	jiù yào	about to; soon (imminent)
就这么简单	jiù zhè me jiǎn dān	it's just that simple; merely this easy — 就 in this usage minimizes or dismisses: it is merely, just, only this , simpler or smaller than one might have expected. 就这样 "just like this, that's all" and 就这么 "it's simply this" are extremely common in casual speech for dismissing complexity or closing a matter.
菊花	jú huā	chrysanthemum — the flower of the ninth month — 菊 jú (chrysanthemum) + 花 huā (flower; blossom). The chrysanthemum blooms in the ninth and tenth lunar months, when most flowering plants have finished. This timing made it a literary symbol of resilience, late-life achievement, and the refusal to conform to expected seasonal rhythms.
决定	ju é dìng	to decide; a decision — 决 jué (to resolve; to break through) + 定 dìng (to settle). To bring a choice to rest, and also the decision itself. 做决定 zuò juédìng "to make a decision." The most common everyday word for deciding.
看了两遍	kàn le liǎng biàn	watched twice; read it through twice — 了 with a frequency complement (遍 biàn = complete passes through, 次 cì = times, 回 huí = times in colloquial speech). Marks the action as having been performed a counted number of complete cycles. The quantification of repetition implies completion of each cycle.
看起来	kàn qǐ lái	looks like; visually appears
科技	kē jì	science and technology
肯定	kěn dìng	certainly; to affirm; positive; sure — 肯 kěn (to be willing; to assent) + 定 dìng (to settle). To settle a thing as yes: as an adverb, "certainly," and as a verb, "to affirm, to confirm." Its opposite is 否定 fǒudìng (to negate; negative).
空间	kōng jiān	space; room (abstract); scope — 空 kōng (empty) + 间 jiān (space). Empty space, both physical and figurative: 没有空间 "no room," 发展空间 "room to grow." Paired with 时间, the two name space and time together.
口才	kǒu cái	eloquence; a gift for speaking — 口 kǒu (mouth) + 才 cái (talent). Literally "mouth-talent," the ability to speak well and persuasively: 他很有口才 "he is very eloquent." One of a family of 才 compounds naming a specific gift, alongside 文才 wéncái (literary talent).
来去	lái qù	directional complements
浪费	làng fèi	to waste
老人节	lǎo rén jié	Elders Day; Senior Citizens Day — 老人 lǎorén (elderly person; old person) + 节 jié (festival; designated day). The official civic designation established in 1989, co-locating a state-recognized day honoring elderly citizens with the existing Double Ninth Festival.
了不起	liǎo bu qǐ	remarkable; extraordinary; amazing — 了 liǎo (to conclude; to surpass) + 不 + 起 (unable to rise above; potential complement marker). Literally "cannot be surpassed or concluded" , what cannot be surpassed is extraordinary. A high-praise adjective in everyday speech.
了结	liǎo jié	to conclude; to settle; to bring to a definitive end — 了 liǎo (to conclude) + 结 jié (knot; tied up; settled). To tie off and close a matter definitively. Used for settling disputes, concluding legal cases, or resolving something that has dragged on without resolution. Has a formal or literary flavor , matters of weight are 了结d.
了解	liǎo jiě	to understand thoroughly; to have rounded knowledge of — 了 liǎo (clear-sighted; complete) + 解 jiě (to unravel; to untie). The full unraveling of something until it is entirely clear , understanding that goes all the way through, not just surface knowledge. More thorough than 知道 zhīdao (to know a fact). Common across casual speech and formal registers.
黎明	lí míng	dawn; daybreak
理想	lǐ xiǎng	ideal; aspiration; ideal (adjective) — 理 lǐ (principle; pattern; the inherent structure of things) + 想 xiǎng (thought; thinking). Principled thinking: the vision of how things ought to be, arrived at by sustained reflection on value and order. Functions as a noun (an ideal, a dream) and as an adjective (ideal conditions, ideal outcome).
历代	lì dài	successive dynasties; through the generations — 历 lì (successive; passing through) + 代 dài (generation; dynasty; era). Refers to the whole sequence of past dynasties or generations taken together: "every dynasty in turn," "successive ages." Common in historical and cultural writing to describe traditions, emperors, scholars, or customs that recur…
历法	lì fǎ	lunisolar calendar
历经	lì jīng	to go through; to undergo (a long or arduous process) — 历 lì (to pass through) + 经 jīng (to undergo). The same two characters as 经历 in reverse order, but the register and feel differ: 历经 is more literary and emphasizes a long, often difficult passage through many stages.
历来	lì lái	always; throughout history; from of old up to now — 历 lì (passing through time) + 来 lái (coming up to the present). An adverb meaning "all along throughout the past until now," used to assert that something has consistently been the case across the whole span of history.
立春	lì chūn	Start of Spring (solar term) — 立 (lì, to stand/establish) + 春 (spring). The first of the twenty-four solar terms (节气 jiéqì), falling around February 4, when the year's farming cycle is reckoned to begin. 立 marks a season being set up, the same verb in 立夏, 立秋, 立冬.
立刻	lì kè	immediately; at once
连词	lián cí	conjunction; linking word
练习	liàn xí	to practice; an exercise — 练 (drill) + 习 (to study, to be habituated). The two near-synonyms stack into the all-purpose word for practicing, and as a noun for a practice exercise or drill set.
亮	liàng	bright; light up
另外	lìng wài	"Besides" or "in addition — "Besides" or "in addition." Marks a related but separate point. Useful when you want to add information that is not strictly part of the same argument: 另外, 我想问你一件事 ("also, I want to ask you something").
龙舟	lóng zhōu	dragon boat — 龙 (dragon) + 舟 (boat, the classical word for it; 船 chuán is more common in modern speech). The ornate long boat with a dragon head at the prow. 赛龙舟 (to race dragon boats) is the verb phrase. 龙舟竞渡 (lóngzhōu jìngdù) is the literary term for the race.
马上	mǎ shàng	immediately; right away; at once — 马 mǎ (horse) + 上 shàng (on top of; mounted on). Literally "on horseback," from the military image of an order being carried out while still mounted, without dismounting. One of the most common adverbs in spoken Mandarin.
买了	mǎi le	bought; purchased (transaction complete) — Transactional and decisional verbs with 了 are extremely common in everyday conversation. The completion is commercially and practically significant , the item has been acquired, the deal is done, the decision has been executed.
没	méi	not; have not
没吃	méi chī	didn't eat; have not eaten — 没(有) is the standard negator for completed actions. It replaces 了 entirely , you never say 没吃了, because 没 already carries the information that no completion occurred. The 了 is superfluous and wrong. This is a near-universal rule: 没 and post-verb 了 are mutually exclusive.
没错	méi cuò	that's right; exactly; no mistake — 没 méi (not have) + 错 cuò (error). "No error here," used to agree firmly: 没错，我就是这个意思 "exactly, that's just what I mean." Often softened to 没错儿 in northern speech.
没关系	méi guān xi	it doesn't matter; never mind; that's okay — One of the most indispensable phrases in Mandarin. The standard response to 对不起 (sorry). Literally "no relationship" , meaning "it has no effect on us; it carries no weight." Learn this before your first week of speaking Chinese.
没什么	méi shén me	nothing / it's nothing / no big deal — 没 + 什么 (what; anything). 没什么 = "nothing; it's nothing." As a response to thanks or apologies: 没什么！= "No worries! / It's nothing!" Also as a statement: 冰箱里没什么吃的 "There's nothing much to eat in the fridge."
秒	miǎo	second (time)
民间信仰	mín jiān xìn yǎng	folk religion
明亮	míng liàng	bright; luminous; well-lit — 明 míng + 亮 liàng (bright; shining). The compound for physical and figurative luminosity. Used for rooms, eyes, futures, and voices (a 明亮的嗓音 is a clear, bright singing voice).
明年	míng nián	next year — 明 míng + 年 nián (year). The same "bright/coming" sense extended to years. Pattern: 明天 tomorrow · 明年 next year · 明月 the bright moon (classical). The character 明 reaches across all timescales.
明确	míng qu è	clear and definite; explicit; to specify — 明 míng + 确 què (definite; certain; firm). Used for rules, policies, goals, and instructions that leave no room for ambiguity. More formal than 明白. 请明确你的要求 = "Please specify your requirements."
明天	míng tiān	tomorrow — 明 míng (bright; dawn; the next) + 天 tiān (day). The "bright day," the day the sun will rise again. 明 also means "next" in 明年 míngnián (next year) and 明月 míngyuè (the bright moon, a poetic image for longing). Note: in classical Chinese, 明日 míngrì is the literary form; 明天 is modern spoken Mandarin.
明天就到了	míng tiān jiù dào le	arrives as soon as tomorrow; it'll be here by tomorrow already — 就 can also point toward an imminent future event, expressing that something will happen sooner than might be expected. The speaker's attitude: "it's coming up fast, it'll be here before you know it." This future-pointing 就 often co-occurs with 了 at the sentence end.
明显	míng xiǎn	obvious; evident; marked — 明 míng + 显 xiǎn (to show; visible; eminent). What is bright is also visible to all. Used when something is undeniably apparent: improvement, change, a lie. 明显进步 "marked improvement."
明智	míng zhì	wise; sensible; showing good judgment — 明 míng (clear, bright) + 智 zhì (wisdom; intelligence). A bright mind, one that sees clearly. The compliment given to good decisions, prudent actions. 这是个明智的选择 "That is a wise choice."
目连	Mù lián	Mulian (Maudgalyāyana), the filial monk
目前	mù qián	at present; currently
那时候	nà shí hou	at that time
内功	nèi gōng	internal martial cultivation
能力	néng lì	ability; capability; capacity — 能 néng (be able) + 力 lì (strength). The general word for ability, what a person is capable of doing: 工作能力 "work ability," 有能力 "to be capable."
你在做什么？	nǐ zài zuò shén me ?	What are you doing? — The standard colloquial question for asking what someone is currently doing. 在 alone (not 正在) is the norm in this question. 你正在做什么？is grammatical but sounds slightly unnatural or formal in casual conversation. The question naturally invites a 在/正在 answer.
年代	nián dài	decade; period
农历	nóng lì	the lunar calendar — 农 (farming) plus 历 (calendar). The traditional lunisolar calendar, named for farmers because its solar terms tell you when to plant and harvest. 春节 (chūnjié) falls on 农历 New Year.
努力	nǔ lì	to make an effort; to strive; hardworking — 努 nǔ (to put forth) + 力 lì (effort). To bend one's strength toward a goal, the everyday word for trying hard: 努力工作 "to work hard," 努力学习 "to study diligently." Works as both verb and noun.
偶尔	ǒu ěr	occasionally
旁边	páng biān	beside; next to
平常	píng cháng	ordinary; common; usually, ordinarily — 平 píng (level; plain) + 常 cháng (ordinary). Both an adjective (unremarkable, common) and a time word (in ordinary times, usually): 平常的一天 "an ordinary day," 我平常六点起床 "I usually get up at six."
普度	pǔ dù	universal deliverance — community feast for the unnamed dead — 普 pǔ (universal; general) + 度 dù (to cross over; to ferry across; to deliver). A feast laid out publicly , on street corners, in courtyards, at temple forecourts , specifically for the hungry ghosts who have no living descendants to offer for them.
七月	qī yu è	July; the seventh month — 七 qī + 月 yuè (month). July in the solar calendar. The seventh lunar month is the Ghost Month (鬼月), when the gates of the underworld are believed to open and spirits roam among the living; its fifteenth day is the Ghost Festival (中元节).
期间	qī jiān	period; during the time of — 期 qī (a fixed period) + 间 jiān (interval). A defined stretch of time, often used after an event to mean "during": 假期期间 "during the holidays," 会议期间 "in the course of the meeting."
气候	qì hòu	climate; the long-term atmospheric pattern — 气 qì + 候 hòu (season; to wait; signs). 候 originally referred to the five-day intervals used in classical Chinese to track seasonal changes. 气候 is the patterned behavior of atmospheric qi over time: climate, not weather. Also used metaphorically: 政治气候 (political climate).
恰当	qià dàng	apt; fitting
前辈	qián bèi	predecessor; senior; elder in one's field — 前 qián (before; preceding) + 辈 bèi (generation; people of the same era). Those of earlier generations who walked the path before you in your field, institution, or family. A term of respect: addressing someone as 前辈 acknowledges their experience and positions you as the newer arrival.
前朝	qián cháo	former dynasty; previous ruling house — 前 qián (former; preceding) + 朝 cháo (dynasty; court; morning). The dynasty that came before the current one , a term of historical reference used constantly in Chinese historical writing, since each dynasty defined itself partly in relation to what it replaced.
前方	qián fāng	ahead; the forward direction; the front (military) — 前 qián (front) + 方 fāng (direction; area; square). A more formal and often grander term than 前面 , used in military contexts, official broadcasts, and literary or elevated speech. 前方记者 (correspondent at the front) is a journalism term.
前面	qián miàn	in front; ahead; the front side — 前 qián (front) + 面 miàn (face; surface; side). The most common everyday expression for spatial "in front." Works for physical space (前面有个红绿灯 , there is a traffic light ahead) and for sequential position (前面的章节 , the preceding chapter).
前排	qián pái	front row — 前 qián (front) + 排 pái (row; line; rank). The front row of a theater, cinema, classroom, or any seated arrangement. Also used figuratively for first-rank prominence: 前排位置 (front-row position) implies visibility and status in any field.
前任	qián rèn	former occupant of a role; the "ex" (ex-partner, ex-boss) — 前 qián (former; preceding) + 任 rèn (appointment; term of office; to hold a post). The person who formerly held a given role or position.
前天	qián tiān	the day before yesterday — 前 qián (front; before) + 天 tiān (day; sky). The day that stands two steps "in front of" today.
前途	qián tú	future prospects; "front road"
强调	qiáng diào	to emphasize
青春	qīng chūn	youth; the prime of life — 青 (qīng, green/blue, the color of new growth) + 春 (spring). "Green spring," the years of youth at their freshest. Used for the whole season of being young, often with a note of how briefly it lasts.
青团	qīng tuán	green glutinous rice cakes — the festival food of the Jiangnan region
清楚	qīng chu	clear, distinct — 清 (clear water) + 楚 (distinct). The clarity of things and statements: 说清楚 (say it clearly), 看清楚 (see it distinctly). Pairs with 明白 across the divide of world and mind: the map is 清楚, the reader is 明白.
清明节	Qīng míng jié	Tomb-Sweeping Festival
请	qǐng	please; to invite
秋天	qiū tiān	autumn
屈原	Qū Yuán	Qū Yuán (c. 340–278 BCE) — The Chǔ minister-poet whose drowning the festival commemorates. Author of 离骚 Lí Sāo (Encountering Sorrow) and associated poems in the 楚辞 Songs of Chu anthology. The first named author in Chinese literary history whose works survive in substantial quantity.
缺点	qu ē diǎn	shortcoming
确定	qu è dìng	to confirm; to be sure; certain — 确 què (sure; firm) + 定 dìng (to settle). To pin a thing down as fixed and beyond doubt, or to be sure of it oneself. 我不确定 "I'm not sure," 确定时间 "to fix the time."
人才	rén cái	a talented person; talent; capable people — 人 rén (person) + 才 cái (talent). A person of real ability, and by extension the pool of skilled people an organization or country can draw on: 培养人才 "to cultivate talent," 人才市场 "the job market for skilled workers."
壬癸	rén guǐ	Stems 9–10 (Water)
仍然	réng rán	still; as before; yet remaining — 仍 réng (still, as before) + 然 rán (so). Marks that a state holds unchanged despite what might have altered it: 仍然 here, the situation is still thus. Close to 还是 háishì but more written.
日常	rì cháng	daily; everyday; routine — 日 rì (day) + 常 cháng (constant; ordinary; everyday). The standard adjective for "everyday, routine, daily life." 日常生活 rìcháng shēnghuó = everyday life. 日常用语 rìcháng yòngyǔ = everyday expressions (a category every Chinese textbook includes). 日常工作 = daily work/routine.
日历	rì lì	calendar; date pad; daily calendar — 日 rì (sun; day) + 历 lì (from 曆, the calendar). The everyday word for a calendar as a physical or digital object: the thing on the wall or in your phone that lists the days. This is the 曆 branch of 历, fully about counting and displaying days. For the tear-off page-a-day kind, the same word applies.
日期	rì qī	date; calendar day
如今	rú jīn	nowadays; these days
扫墓	sǎo mù	tomb sweeping — the annual visit to the graves — 扫墓 (lit. "sweep the grave") is the Qingming practice of clearing the tomb of weeds and debris, adding fresh soil, pouring libations of wine, burning paper offerings, and sharing food at the grave site.
赏菊	shǎng jú	admiring chrysanthemums — the flower of late autumn resilience — 赏 shǎng (to appreciate; to admire) + 菊 jú (chrysanthemum). The chrysanthemum (菊花 júhuā) blooms in autumn, long after spring flowers have finished. This timing made it a symbol of resilience, longevity, and the virtuous person who thrives when others have withered.
赏月	shǎng yu è	to appreciate the moon; moon-gazing
上当	shàng dàng	to be fooled; to be taken in; to fall for a trick — 上 shàng (to go up to) + 当 dàng (the pawnshop). From the image of being shortchanged at the pawnbroker's counter, it now means to be cheated or deceived. 上当受骗 shàngdàng shòupiàn is the common four-character pairing.
上来	shàng lái	come up; manage to (ability)
上去	shàng qù	go up; from outside appearance
烧纸钱	shāo zhǐ qián	burning paper money — offerings for the dead — 烧 shāo (burn) + 纸 zhǐ (paper) + 钱 qián (money). Paper replicas of currency, goods, and , in contemporary practice , smartphones, cars, designer bags, and houses, burned so that the smoke carries them to the deceased in the underworld.
少林功夫	Shào lín gōng fu	Shaolin Kungfu — From the 少林寺 Shàolín Sì (Shaolin Monastery) on Mount Song 嵩山, Henan. The most internationally recognized style, blending Chan Buddhist meditation with rigorous physical training. Historically credited with the development of northern Chinese external styles.
身份	shēn fèn	identity; status — 身 (self) + 份 (share, allotment): one's allotted position. 身份证 (shēnfènzhèng) is the national ID card, the document requested at every train station, hotel, and SIM-card counter, which makes this compound survival vocabulary rather than abstraction.
什么时候	shén me shí hou	when? (question word) — The question word "when," literally "what time/moment." It sits where the time answer would go, not at the front as in English: 你什么时候走？("When are you leaving?"). One of the core question patterns alongside 哪儿 (where), 谁 (who), 怎么 (how).
生机	shēng jī	vitality, life-force
生肖	shēng xiào	zodiac animals; birth sign
十二生肖	shí èr shēng xiào	The twelve zodiac animals
时辰	shí chen	two-hour period (traditional)
时代精神	shí dài jīng shén	the spirit of the age; the zeitgeist — The defining outlook and values of an era, the calque that matches the German "Zeitgeist." 时代 (the age) plus 精神 (spirit). Used in cultural and political commentary to name what an age believes about itself: 把握时代精神 ("grasp the spirit of the times").
时光	shí guāng	time (poetic)
时机	shí jī	the right moment; timing; an opportune occasion — 时 shí (time) + 机 jī (machine; pivot; crucial moment; opportunity). 时机 is the Confucian classical meaning of 时 made into a compound: the moment that is ripe, when conditions align. It is strategic time, the narrowing window before it closes.
时期	shí qī	period; phase
实现	shí xiàn	to realize; to bring about; to achieve — 实 shí (real) + 现 xiàn (to appear; to manifest). To make something real, to turn a plan, goal, or dream into an accomplished fact: 实现目标 "to achieve a goal," 实现梦想 "to make a dream come true."
史	shǐ	history; the scribe; chronicle
是的	shì de	yes; that's right; indeed (affirming something just said) — 是的 in its collapsed, fully reduced form is simply an affirmation: "yes, that is the case." The 是 confirms the truth of what was said; the 的 creates a sense of definitiveness or "that's just how it is." It is warmer and more emphatic than a bare 对, and more measured than an enthusiastic 当然.
是非	shì fēi	right and wrong; the rights and wrongs; trouble; gossip — 是 shì (right; this; is) + 非 fēi (wrong). The pairing of right and wrong as a single notion: 明辨是非 "to clearly distinguish right from wrong." By a common slide it also means the quarrels and gossip that arise from disputes: 惹是非 "to stir up trouble."
是谁告诉你的	shì shéi gào su nǐ de	who told you? (FOCUS: which agent, unknown) — The agent-focus question: 是谁 + Verb + 的. Both speakers know that someone told this person something , the unknown is who. 谁 occupies the focus position between 是 and the verb phrase. This is one of the most natural everyday uses of 是…的: interrogating the agent of a known past action.
适当	shì dàng	appropriate; suitable; proper — 适 shì (to suit) + 当 dàng (to fit). Describes a measure, amount, or action that is right for the situation: 适当的时候 (at an appropriate time), 适当休息 (rest as appropriate).
受不了	shòu bu liǎo	can't stand it; unbearable; past one's limit — 受 shòu (to endure; to receive) + 不了 liǎo (cannot complete the action of enduring). The potential complement with 不了 means the action cannot reach completion , endurance hits its limit. One of the most frequently used expressions in daily speech for frustration, exhaustion, or overwhelm.
书是他借的	shū shì tā jiè de	the book was borrowed by him (FOCUS: by him, not by someone else) — Agent focus: who performed the known action. The agent (他) is placed between 是 and the verb + 的. Both speakers know the book was borrowed , the question or point of emphasis is who borrowed it. This is also how 是…的 can function like a passive: the book (subject) + 是 + agent + verb + 的.
熟能生巧	shú néng shēng qiǎo	practice makes perfect
瞬间	shùn jiān	an instant; the blink of an eye — 瞬 shùn (a blink) + 间 jiān (interval). The smallest interval of time, the moment it takes to blink: 一瞬间 "in a flash." The interval shrunk almost to nothing.
说了半天才明白	shuō le bàn tiān cái míng bai	finally understood after talking for ages — After a long period of effort, explanation, or struggle , 才 finally. The effort is encoded in the first clause (说了半天 = talked for half a day, idiom for "a long time"); the 才 marks that only after all that effort did the result occur. This is 才 at its most expressive: hard-won achievement.
说着说着她哭了	shuō zhe shuō zhe tā kū le	as she kept talking, she started to cry — The model sentence for this pattern. The action (talking) repeated in the V + 着 frame signals continuation; the main clause introduces the unplanned outcome (crying). Used whenever a shift happened gradually, through sustained activity, not at a discrete moment.
算盘	suàn pán	The abacus, literally "calculating tray — The abacus, literally "calculating tray." The standard Chinese bead-frame instrument with 2 upper beads and 5 lower beads per rod, in use in its canonical form since at least the 16th century.
所在	suǒ zài	where something is; the location of; what something lies in — 所 suǒ (that which; nominalizing particle) + 在 (to be located). A formal noun meaning "the place/point where something is located; the essence of what something consists in." Commonly appears in 问题所在 (the crux of the problem; where the problem lies) and 关键所在 (the key point; where the key lies).

This is the "as soon as" function: the first event triggers the second instantly. No 一 is needed here , just the bare sequence: Clause 1 + 就 + Clause 2.
他结婚了	tā jié hūn le	he got married; he is now a married man — Major life transitions , marriage, graduation, birth, death, career changes , are natural territory for sentence-final 了. The 了 frames the event as a change that affects the current situation: the relevant new fact is that he is now married. It answers an implicit question about the present.

The 在上海 sits between 是 and 见面的, specifying the location of the known meeting. Common in stories, biographical accounts, and conversations about how relationships began.
他们正在开会	tā men zhèng zài kāi huì	They are in a meeting right now — One of the most useful office and workplace phrases. 开会 kāihuì = to hold/attend a meeting. The progressive here signals that interrupting is not appropriate. Variant: 他们在开会，请稍后再来 (They are in a meeting , please come back later). 正在 gives it a slight formality appropriate to professional contexts.
他三十岁才结婚	tā sān shí suì cái jié hūn	he didn't marry until thirty (late, in the speaker's view) — From the speaker's perspective, thirty is late to marry , perhaps later than expected or social norms dictate.
他是开车去的	tā shì kāi chē qù de	he went by car (FOCUS: by car, not by train, not walking) — Manner focus: the verb of transportation or method of doing something sits between 是 and 去的 (or the main verb + 的). The presupposition is that he went somewhere , the new information is how. 开车 (drive a car) is the manner. Other manners: 坐飞机 by plane, 骑车 by bike, 走路 on foot, 打电话 by phone.
他是去年来的	tā shì qùn ián lái de	he came last year (FOCUS: last year, not any other year) — Both speakers know he came; the question is when. 是去年 places "last year" in the focus position, bracketed by 是 and …来的. The construction answers a question or corrects a misunderstanding about the timing: "No, it wasn't three years ago, it was last year."
他五岁就会读书了	tā wǔ suì jiù huì dú shū le	he was already reading at age five! (impressively early) — Age five is the timeline; 就 signals it is earlier than the expected baseline for reading ability. The sentence-final 了 reinforces the new-situation quality , at that point, as early as five, he had already achieved this. The combination 就…了 is extremely common for describing precocious achievements.
她来了两个星期了	tā lái le liǎng gè xīng qī le	she has been here for two weeks (and is still here) — The double-了 construction with a duration complement is one of its most important uses. The first 了 marks the initial arrival (completed action of coming); the second 了 marks the current relevance , she is still here now. Without the second 了, the sentence implies the visit is concluded.
她是今天早上走的	tā shì jīn tiān zǎo shang zǒu de	she left this morning (FOCUS: this morning, not yesterday) — More precise time focus , this morning, as opposed to last night or yesterday. The presupposition is clear: she has already left (both speakers know this). The new information is the precise time of departure. Commonly used in conversations about arrivals, departures, and events.

太极拳	tài jí quán	Tai Chi Chuan — the soft internal martial art based on yin-yang principles — 太极 tàijí (the Supreme Ultimate , the cosmological concept) + 拳 quán (fist; boxing style). The soft, circular martial art based on the principles of 阴阳: yielding overcomes force, softness overcomes hardness, stillness overcomes motion. A paradigm of 内功 (internal cultivation).
太阳	tài yáng	the sun — 太 tài (supreme; ultimate; the greatest) + 阳 yáng (yang force; light). The Supreme Yang , the ultimate source of yang energy in the cosmos. In the 五行 framework, the sun is the source of Fire phase energy. 太阳能 (solar energy) uses exactly this logic.
提前	tí qián	to move up in time; to do in advance; ahead of schedule — 提 tí (to lift; to bring forward; to raise up) + 前 qián (front; before). Literally "to lift forward": to move a scheduled event earlier than planned, to do something in advance. The opposite is 推迟 tuīchí (to postpone, to push back).
天才	tiān cái	genius; heaven-bestowed talent — 天 tiān (heaven) + 才 cái (talent; ability). Heaven-bestowed ability , the kind of capacity that seems to have come from beyond ordinary human effort. The etymology encodes a theory of talent: genius is a gift, not a product.
天干	tiāng ān	Heavenly Stems
天气	tiān qì	weather — 天 tiān (sky) + 气 qì (breath; air; vital force). Literally: the sky's breath. In traditional Chinese cosmology, qì 气 is the invisible force that animates all phenomena; weather is the sky breathing. HSK 1, but it carries this depth beneath the surface.
听懂	tīng dǒng	to understand what one hears; to follow spoken language — 听 (to listen) + 懂 dǒng (to understand). The resultative complement 懂 marks successful comprehension , listening that reached understanding. Constant in language learning contexts: 你听懂了吗？ (Did you understand what you heard?) contrasts with 你听到了吗？ (Did you hear it? — did the sound reach you?).
听起来	tīng qǐ lái	sounds like
听着！	tīng zhe !	Listen! / Listen up! (and stay listening) — The sustained-attention imperative with 着. 听 alone is a simple command to listen; 听着 adds the sense of "maintain that listening state." Common before announcements, reprimands, or instructions that require focused, continuous attention.
通常	tōng cháng	usually; ordinarily; generally; as a rule — 通 (general; holding throughout) + 常 cháng (constant; usual; frequent). The case that holds across most instances: usually, as the general rule. 通常 sets up the ordinary expectation against which exceptions are measured: 我通常七点起床 (I usually get up at seven).
同时	tóng shí	at the same time; meanwhile; moreover — 同 tóng (same) + 时 shí (time). The same moment: 同时进行 "to proceed simultaneously." As a connector it also means "at the same time, moreover," adding a further point.
团圆	tuán yuán	reunion; to be reunited (as a family)
歪	wāi	crooked; tilted
王后	wáng hòu	queen; consort of a king — 王 wáng (king; ruler of a principality) + 后 (consort). The consort of a 王 (king or prince). In historical Chinese usage, 王 ranked below 皇帝 (emperor), so 王后 was below 皇后. In modern usage, 王后 translates "queen consort" in Western contexts.
卫生间	wèi shēng jiān	bathroom; toilet; restroom — 卫生 wèishēng (hygiene) + 间 jiān (room). The polite, standard word for a bathroom in homes and public buildings. A common synonym is 洗手间 xǐshǒujiān.

问候	wèn hòu	to send regards; to greet; to ask after someone — 问 wèn (to ask after) + 候 hòu (to wait on; to attend). To inquire warmly after another person, in speech or in writing: 致以亲切的问候 "to extend warm regards."
我明白了	wǒ míng bai le	I understand now; I get it (I have entered a state of understanding) — State-change verbs like 明白 (understand), 知道 (know), 懂 (grasp), 累 (tired), 饿 (hungry), 高兴 (happy) pair powerfully with sentence-final 了. The 了 marks the entry into that state , the moment the change occurs. Before: didn't understand. After: understands.

The presupposition is that the speaker was born , the focus is the location. This is how Chinese speakers typically answer "Where were you born?" — using 是…的 to frame the location as the focused new information.

The word also has a classical sense of military accomplishment, the deeds that win wars.

下功夫	xià gōng fu	to put in the work; to make a serious effort; to buckle down — 下 xià (to place; to put down) + 功夫 (effort/work). The verb phrase for serious, sustained investment of effort. The directional 下 is crucial: you are depositing effort into something, like placing a foundation. Not a momentary burst of energy but a committed, extended investment.

下去	xià qù	continue; go down
下雨了	xià yǔ le	it's raining now; it has started to rain — The canonical example of sentence-final 了 announcing a new situation. 现实	xiàn shí	reality; actual; (of a person) realistic, practical — 现 xiàn (present) + 实 shí (real; solid). What is actually the case here and now, set against 理想 lǐxiǎng (the ideal). As an adjective for a person it can mean clear-eyed, or pointedly materialistic.
乡愁	xiāng chóu	homesickness; nostalgia for one native place — 乡 xiāng (native place; hometown; rural area) + 愁 chóu (sorrow; melancholy; anxiety). The specific emotional register of Double Ninth as a festival: not grief for the dead (清明节), not fear of wandering spirits (中元节), but the longing of those separated from home by distance.
向前	xiàng qián	forward; toward the front; onward — 向 xiàng (toward) + 前 qián (front). Directed at the space ahead: 向前走 "walk forward," 向前看 "look ahead," both literally and in the sense of looking to the future rather than dwelling on the past.
小时	xiǎo shí	hour (unit of time) — 小 xiǎo (small) + 时 shí (time; hour). A small slice of time: one hour. 正常	zhèng cháng	normal; regular — 正 zhèng (proper; upright) + 常 cháng (ordinary). On the standard track, as things should be: 一切正常 "everything is normal." Its opposite is 反常 fǎncháng or 不正常.
正好	zhèng hǎo	just right; exactly; as it happens — 正 zhèng (exactly) + 好 hǎo (good). Landing exactly on the mark of fit or timing: 大小正好 "exactly the right size," 你来得正好 "you've come at just the right moment."
正确	zhèng qu è	correct; right; accurate — 正 zhèng (straight; right) + 确 què (sure; firm). Correct in the sense of matching the truth or the standard: 正确答案 "the correct answer," 正确的方法 "the right method." Its opposite is 错误 cuòwù.
正式	zhèng shì	formal; official
正是	zhèng shì	precisely; exactly (formal confirmation)
正在进行中	zhèng zài jìn xíng zhōng	Currently in progress; underway — A more formal and written version of the progressive, combining 正在 with 进行 jìnxíng (to carry out, to proceed) and 中 zhōng (amid, in the middle of). Common in announcements, project status updates, and written notices. 活动正在进行中 = the event is currently underway.
之后	zhī hòu	after; following
之间	zhī jiān	between; among (more formal) — 之 zhī (classical possessive) + 间 jiān (space between). The bookish, frequent way to say "between": 朋友之间 "between friends," 国与国之间 "between nations." It frames the space within a relationship or a set.
只	zhī	measure word for animals
纸钱	zhǐ qián	spirit money — currency for the afterlife — 纸钱 (also 冥纸 míngzhǐ, 金纸 jīnzhǐ in Taiwan, or 冥币 míngbì) are paper facsimiles of money and goods burnt as offerings; the smoke carries them to the dead.
中间	zhōng jiān	middle; center; in between; among — 中 zhōng (center) + 间 jiān (space). The space in the middle, whether of a row, a room, or a stretch of time: 坐在中间 "to sit in the middle," 在两座山中间 "between two mountains."
中秋节	Zhōng qiū jié	Mid-Autumn Festival
中元节	Zhōng yuán Jié	the Ghost Festival — Daoist name for the 15th of the 7th month — 中 zhōng (middle) + 元 yuán (origin; first; primary) + 节 jié (festival; season). The Daoist name, derived from the Three Officials calendar: 上元 (upper prime, 1st month, 15th), 中元 (middle prime, 7th month, 15th), 下元 (lower prime, 10th month, 15th). Each 元 is the birthday of one official.
钟	zhōng	clock; o'clock
重点	zhòng diǎn	key point; focus; emphasis; priority — 重 zhòng (heavy; important) + 点 diǎn (point). The heavy point, the part that matters most. 重点大学 "key universities," 划重点 "to mark the key points," the things to underline.
重新	chóng xīn	anew; afresh; once more (from the start) — 重 chóng (again) + 新 xīn (new). To do something over from a fresh start: 重新开始 "to start anew," 重新考虑 "to reconsider." Stronger than a plain 又, it implies a clean slate.
重阳	Chóng yáng	Double Ninth; Double Yang — the festival name — 重 chóng (double; repeated; layered) + 阳 yáng (yang; the masculine creative principle; odd numbers; heaven). The name describes the cosmological condition of the date: two nine-yang numbers coinciding.
重阳糕	Chóng yáng gāo	Double Ninth cake — the city dweller substitution — 重阳 Chóngyáng (the festival) + 糕 gāo (cake; steamed or baked confection). The cleverest detail of the festival: 糕 (gāo, cake) is homophonous with 高 (gāo, high).
重阳节	Chóng yáng Jié	Double Ninth Festival (autumn)
茱萸	zhū yú	zhuyu; Japanese cornelian cherry — the apotropaic sprig — 茱萸 is the Chinese name for Cornus officinalis (Japanese cornelian cherry) and related Zanthoxylum species. The plant is pungent and strongly scented, which in Chinese folk medicine and ritual practice made it repellent to malevolent forces and disease.
准备好	zhǔn bèi hǎo	to be ready — Prepare + brought-to-good-state. 准备好了吗? ("ready?") starts every race, road trip, and presentation in the language. The reply 好了 borrows the complement alone, an answer made of pure result.
子午	zǐ wǔ	Midnight and noon branches
总算	zǒng suàn	at last (with relief)
粽子	zòng zi	rice dumpling wrapped in leaves — Sticky rice dumplings wrapped in bamboo or reed leaves, traditionally triangular or cylindrical. Made with various fillings: red bean paste, salted egg yolk, cured pork. The characteristic food of the Dragon Boat Festival, eaten to commemorate Qu Yuan.
最后	zuì hòu	finally; in the end; last — 最 zuì (most; superlative) + 后 (behind; last). The furthest back position in a sequence: the final item, the last moment, the ultimate outcome. Functions as both an adverb before a verb (最后他同意了 , in the end he agreed) and as an adjective modifying a noun (最后一页 , the last page).
最终	zuì zhōng	ultimately; finally
昨天	zuó tiān	yesterday — 昨 zuó (yesterday; past) + 天 tiān (day). The 昨 character is rarely used outside this compound and 昨晚 zuówǎn (last night) in modern Chinese. Note the tonal transition: 昨 zuó is tone 2, 天 tiān is tone 1, two rising pitches in sequence, which requires attention in spoken practice.
