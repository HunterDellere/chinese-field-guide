//角落書屋/Slices/Modal Verbs
必	bì	certainly; must; necessarily — A character rooted in the image of a blade that divides without exception: certainty as the sharpness that leaves no room for maybe.
才	cái	talent, ability, only-then, not until — A sprout breaking ground, the first sign of growth. From that image grow two threads: talent as raw promise, and the adverb 才 that marks something later, harder, or smaller than expected.
功	gōng	merit, achievement, skill, effort — Merit earned through effort: from meritorious service and success to the patient skill the Chinese call gongfu, and even work in physics.
可	kě	can; may; worth; quite — A two-stroke mouth character whose ancient sense of affirming breath became the pivot of permission, capability, and worth in classical and modern Chinese.
力	lì	strength, power, force, effort — A picture of a plough or a flexed sinew, the image of bodily exertion. From that one shape grows everything pushed by force: raw strength, the capacity to do, and the effort poured into a task.
想	xiǎng	to think, to want, to miss — 想 = 相 (mutual, facing) + 心 (heart): the heart turned toward something, carrying the full range from thought to desire to longing in a single gesture.
要	yào	to want, must, essential — Want, need, must, and will: the grammar engine disguised as a single character.
应	yīng	ought, should; (yìng) to respond, to answer, to handle — A high-frequency polyphone with two readings. As yīng it means ought to or should; as yìng it means to respond, to answer, to adapt, and to handle. The tone tells you whether the sense is obligation or response.
必须	bì xū	must, have to (the obligation modal) — 必须 is the strongest of the everyday modal verbs of obligation: a non-negotiable requirement that the speaker presents as not-up-for-discussion.
一定	yí dìng	definitely; certainly — The everyday word for "certainly, definitely," covering both confident prediction and firm resolve, the 一定要 (must) pattern, and how it differs from 肯定, 必须, and 一定会.
必由之路	bì yóu zhī lù	the road one must take; the only viable path forward — 必 (must) + 由 yóu (to pass through; to travel by way of) + 之路 (the road). The path through which one must pass, with no alternative route. Used when describing the single viable way forward in a difficult situation: 改革是必由之路 (Reform is the only path forward).
不可思议	bù kě sī yì	inconceivable; unimaginable — Literally "cannot be thought or discussed," beyond comprehension, originally a Buddhist term for the ineffable. Here 可 ("can / possible"), the first character of 可能, takes its sense of "able to be": what cannot possibly be grasped by thought.
才华横溢	cái huá héng yì	talent brimming over: overflowing with brilliance — Lit: talent-brilliance-spilling-overflowing. 才华 is creative or literary brilliance, and 横溢 paints it as water flooding past its banks. Said of someone whose gifts are too abundant to contain.
才能出众	cái néng chū zhòng	outstandingly talented — ability that stands above the crowd — 才能 cáinéng (talent and ability) + 出众 chūzhòng (to stand out from the crowd). 能 here combines with 才 (talent, capacity) to form the compound 才能. Used in recommendations, eulogies, and formal praise. 她才能出众，前途无量 "Her talents are exceptional; her future is limitless."
得心应手	dé xīn yìng shǒu	handy and skillful: to do something with effortless mastery — Lit: the hand answers the heart. What the mind intends, the hand at once carries out. Praise for a skill so practiced it feels automatic. Here 应 takes the yìng reading, "to respond."
德才兼备	dé cái jiān bèi	to have both virtue and talent; the ideal of the capable and morally upright person — 德 dé (virtue; moral character) + 才 cái (talent; ability; practical capability) + 兼 jiān (simultaneously; to combine) + 备 bèi (to possess; to be equipped with). The ideal of the complete person , someone who is not only capable and skilled (才) but also genuinely good (德).
鼎力相助	dǐng lì xiāng zhù	to render great help: a respectful thanks for another's strong assistance — Lit: with-tripod-strength-to-help. 鼎 is the massive bronze ritual tripod, so 鼎力 is great strength. A polite phrase used to acknowledge or request another person's powerful support.
多才多艺	duō cái duō yì	of many talents and many skills: versatile, multitalented — Lit: much-talent-much-skill. High praise for a person gifted in several arts at once, the painter who also writes and plays music. 艺 yì is craft or art, the paired half of 才.
各有所能	gè yǒu suǒ néng	each has their own abilities — everyone has something they're good at — Lit: each-has-what-[they]-can[do]. A Confucian-flavored observation about the distribution of talent. Used to affirm that different people excel in different domains , a polite and diplomatic formula. 人各有所能，没什么好比较的 "Everyone has their own abilities; there's nothing to compare."
功成名就	gōng chéng míng jiù	achievement complete and fame attained: to have made it — Lit: merit-accomplished-name-attained. The classical picture of a fulfilled career: the work is done and the reputation is secured. Often paired with the idea of retiring gracefully afterward (功成身退, to withdraw once the work is achieved).
功亏一篑	gōng kuī yī kuì	to fail for the lack of one final basketful: to fall short at the very end — Lit: achievement-short-one-basket. From the image of building a mound and stopping one last basket of earth before completion. From the Book of Documents (尚书). The lesson: do not abandon a near-finished effort, or all the work before it is wasted.
怀才不遇	huái cái bù yù	to harbor talent yet meet no chance: gifted but unrecognized — Lit: cradle-talent-not-encounter. The lament of the able person who never finds a ruler, patron, or era that will use their gifts. A recurring theme in classical poetry, where the unemployed scholar mourns his wasted ability.
急功近利	jí gōng jìn lì	eager for quick merit and near-term gain: shortsighted opportunism — Lit: anxious-merit-near-profit. A criticism of those who chase immediate results and quick profit at the expense of long-term, sustainable success. The opposite of the 功夫 ethic of patient, foundational effort.
坚定不移	jiān dìng bù yí	firm and unshakable — Literally "firm and settled, not shifting", steadfast in resolve, never wavering. The 定 ("settled, fixed") at its heart is the same 定 in 一定; here it describes a will that has fixed on its course. The deepest form of the resolve 一定要 voices.
可歌可泣	kě gē kě qì	worthy of song and worthy of tears — so moving as to deserve both celebration and mourning — 可 + 歌 (to sing) + 可 + 泣 (to weep). Used for heroic, sacrificial stories: deeds of such weight they call for both elegy and lament. 革命先烈的事迹可歌可泣。(The deeds of the revolutionary martyrs are worthy of song and tears.) High formal register.
可望不可即	kě wàng bù kě jí	can be seen but not reached — within sight but out of grasp — 可望 (can be hoped for/seen) + 不可即 (cannot be approached). Used for goals, dreams, and people that seem close but remain permanently out of reach. Often carries a wistful rather than bitter tone.
理所当然	lǐ suǒ dāng rán	as it should be by reason; a matter of course — Literally "what reason makes ought-to-be-so," something so right and natural that it needs no defense. 当然 ("of course") and 应当 ("ought to") share the 当 of fitting necessity. Sometimes used critically, of someone who takes a kindness as 理所当然 ("for granted").
力不从心	lì bù cóng xīn	strength falls short of the heart: the will is there but the ability is not — Lit: strength-not-follow-heart. Said when one wants to do something but lacks the strength or means to carry it through, the gap between intention and capacity.
力所能及	lì suǒ néng jí	within one's power; as far as one is able — Literally "what one's strength can reach," the things that lie within one's ability to do. The bright mirror of 无能为力: 我会尽力所能及 ("I'll do all I can"). Again built on 能, the capacity at the root of "possible."
能屈能伸	néng qū néng shēn	able to bend and able to stretch — flexible and adaptable — Lit: can-yield-can-extend. Describes someone who knows when to be humble and when to assert themselves , a virtue prized in Chinese social thought. 大丈夫能屈能伸 "A real man of character can yield and can rise again." 屈 = to yield, bow; 伸 = to stretch out, extend.
全力以赴	quán lì yǐ fù	to go all out: to throw one's full strength at a task — Lit: with-full-strength-to-go-toward. To commit every ounce of effort to something, holding nothing back. The everyday phrase for total commitment, whether to a contest, a project, or a rescue.
身体力行	shēn tǐ lì xíng	to practice with one's own body and strength; to live what one preaches — 身 and 体 here are both verbs in the classical grammar: to embody personally (身) and to enact (体力行, carry out with strength). The Confucian ideal of the teacher whose conduct is the curriculum. Modern usage praises leaders who do rather than direct: 环保不能只喊口号，要身体力行。
事半功倍	shì bàn gōng bèi	half the work, double the result: maximum output from minimum effort — Lit: work-half-achievement-double. The ideal of efficiency: accomplishing more by working smarter. From Mencius (孟子). Its mirror image is 事倍功半 shì bèi gōng bàn (double the work, half the result), wasted, inefficient effort.
势在必行	shì zài bì xíng	bound to happen; imperative — Literally "the momentum lies in must-be-done", a course of action that circumstances make unavoidable. It is certainty of the necessary kind, the 必 ("must") that 一定 and 必须 both touch, applied to events that simply have to proceed.
天经地义	tiān jīng dì yì	self-evident truth; axiomatic — too obvious to argue about — Lit: heaven's warp and earth's weft. 经 jīng = warp threads (the vertical structural threads in a loom); 义 yì = weft and righteous pattern. Together they constitute the fabric of moral reality , what is simply given. "孩子要孝顺父母，这是天经地义的事。
无可奈何	wú kě nài hé	nothing can be done about it — helpless; resigned; having no recourse — 无 (nothing) + 可 (can) + 奈何 (what can be done; how to deal with). The expression of pure helplessness: there is no available action. 无可奈何，只能接受。(There was nothing to be done; one could only accept it.) One of the most-used classical phrases in modern speech.
无能为力	wú néng wéi lì	powerless; unable to do anything about it — Lit: have-no-ability-to-act-with-force. 无能 = lacking ability; 为力 = to exert effort/force. The standard idiom for expressing helplessness in the face of a problem. 对于这件事，我也无能为力 "I am also powerless over this matter." Formal and literary; widely used.
无懈可击	wú xiè kě jī	no gap worth striking — flawless, impeccable, impossible to fault — 无 (no) + 懈 (slack; gap; weak point) + 可 (worth) + 击 (to strike). If there is no gap worth attacking, the argument or plan is airtight. 他的计划无懈可击。(His plan is flawless.) Common in formal debate and written argument.
心想事成	xīn xiǎng shì chéng	may what the heart wishes come true — 心 xīn (heart) + 想 xiǎng (think; wish) + 事 shì (matter; affair) + 成 chéng (to succeed; to be accomplished). A blessing structure: heart-wishes turn into accomplished realities. Among the most common felicitations in Chinese , standard at New Year (新年快乐，心想事成！), birthdays, and before examinations.
言必有中	yán bì yǒu zhòng	words that always hit the mark; speech that is measured and unfailingly accurate — From Analects XI.14: Confucius describes a man who rarely speaks , 不言则已，一言必中: but when he does, always hits the target. 中 zhòng here means "center; bull's-eye," not "middle." The standard for speech that earns its right to be heard: few words, each one aimed and landing.
要风得风，要雨得雨	yào fēng dé fēng , yào yǔ dé yǔ	want wind, get wind; want rain, get rain — getting whatever one wishes — Used to describe someone who gets everything they ask for: a pampered child, a powerful person, someone in a position where nothing is refused. The parallelism of 要…得… (want…obtain…) is the construction backbone. Can be admiring or envious. 他在家里要风得风，要雨得雨，什么都不缺。
应接不暇	yìng jiē bù xiá	too busy to cope: more coming in than one can attend to — Lit: responding and receiving without a spare moment. 应接 is "to attend to"; 不暇 is "no leisure." Said when guests, sights, or tasks arrive faster than one can handle them. The yìng reading, "to meet."
应有尽有	yīng yǒu jìn yǒu	everything one could expect is there — 应有 (what ought to be there) + 尽有 (is exhaustively there). The stock phrase for a well-provisioned shop or feature-complete product: 这家超市商品应有尽有 "This supermarket has everything you could want." The structural mirror of 一无所有: total presence against total absence.
有求必应	yǒu qiú bì yìng	every request met with response — to never refuse a reasonable ask — Lit: have-request-must-respond. Said of a generous, responsive person or deity (Guanyin is described this way). 她对学生有求必应，从不拒绝。"She responds to every student's request and never refuses." High praise for helpfulness and generosity.
鱼和熊掌不可兼得	yú hé xióng zhǎng bù kě jiān dé	you can't have both the fish and the bear's paw — you can't have everything you want — From Mencius: both fish (common) and bear's paw (prized delicacy) are desirable, but you cannot have both. The Chinese equivalent of "you can't have your cake and eat it too." Frequently invoked when someone has to choose between two things they want equally. 鱼和熊掌不可兼得，你只能选一个。"You can't have both.
责无旁贷	zé wú páng dài	a duty one cannot shirk — Literally "the responsibility cannot be passed to another," a duty that falls squarely on you and that you ought to shoulder. Used when one accepts an obligation as rightfully one's own, the firm sense of "should" hardened into "must."
志在必得	zhì zài bì dé	determined to get it; fully committed to winning — 志 zhì (will; ambition) + 在 (located in) + 必 bì (certainly) + 得 dé (to obtain). The ambition is so fixed that obtaining the goal is taken as given, not from arrogance but from total commitment. Used to describe someone who has made a full decision and will not waver. 他对这个项目志在必得，不成功不罢休。
得	de	the degree complement — The bridge between action and result: how 得 links what you did to how well you did it.
会	huì / néng / kě yǐ	能 · 可以 · the three 'can's — Learned ability vs. situational capacity vs. permission: not interchangeable.
可能	kěn éng	maybe, possible — The all-purpose word for possibility, working as adverb, adjective, and noun, and how it differs from 也许, 或许, 大概, and the modal 会.
应该	yīng gāi	the modal "should" — The modal verb for "should, ought to," covering both moral obligation and confident inference, and how it differs from 得, 要, and 必须.
暴力	bào lì	violence; brute force — 暴 bào (violent; cruel) + 力 lì (force). Force used to harm, the dark side of 力: 家庭暴力 "domestic violence," 暴力行为 "violent behavior." Here strength has turned destructive.
本领	běn lǐng	skill; capability
必定	bì dìng	certainly; definitely; without a doubt — 必 bì (certainly) + 定 dìng (settled; fixed; determined). A speaker's confident prediction about the future or about something not yet observed. The certainty is in the speaker's judgment, not a rule or law. Closer to "I am certain that" than to "you are required to."
必然	bì rán	inevitable; necessarily; of necessity — 必 bì (certainly; without exception) + 然 rán (so; thus; naturally so , the word for "naturally" or "as it must be"). Together: that which is naturally and necessarily the case, which could not have been otherwise. The register is philosophical, historical, or scientific.
必胜	bì shèng	certain to win; victory is assured — 必 bì (certainly; without exception) + 胜 shèng (to win; to overcome). A morale-building formula rather than a measured prediction. Used in sports, military contexts, and competitive settings where the declaration of certainty is itself motivational. The certainty is willed, not merely predicted.
必须承认	bì xū chéng rèn	one must admit; it must be acknowledged that — A common rhetorical move in essays and serious conversation, conceding ground before pivoting. The 必须 here is not commanding the listener; it is the speaker acknowledging an inescapable point. English equivalent: "it has to be said that," "one must concede that."
必须遵守	bì xū zūn shǒu	must comply with; must abide by (rules, laws) — The standard pairing in legal, regulatory, and policy language. 必须遵守法律 (must comply with the law). The phrase carries the formal-rules register that 必须 lives in: every Chinese workplace handbook, public notice, and company memo will use this collocation rather than its softer alternatives.
必需	bì xū	essential (thing); necessity
必要	bì yào	necessary; required; essential (for a given purpose) — 必 bì (certainly; without exception) + 要 yào (to want; to need; required). Necessity defined by a purpose or goal: if you want the outcome, this is what the outcome requires. The conditionality is built in.
不必	bù bì	not necessary; need not
不可	bù kě	must not; absolutely cannot; impermissible — The strong negation of 可: not merely "cannot" (unable) but "cannot be permitted to." Formal and classical in flavor. 不可 carries the weight of prohibition rather than incapability.
不可能	bù kěn éng	impossible; cannot be — The flat negation, "impossible, no way." 这不可能 ("this is impossible"), 不可能的事 ("an impossible thing"). It is strong and absolute, unlike the softer 不太可能 ("unlikely") which leaves a sliver of chance. As an exclamation, 不可能！means "no way! / that can't be!"
不要	bú yào	don't (prohibition)
不应该	bù yīng gāi	should not; ought not to — Negates the obligation: putting 不 before 应该 says something ought not to be done: 你不应该对她说谎 ("you shouldn't lie to her"). Often it voices regret about the past, "should not have": 我不应该那样说 ("I shouldn't have said that"). Do not confuse with 应该不, where 不 falls after and negates a guessed event instead.
不用	bù yòng	no need to; don't have to
才华	cái huá	brilliance; flair
才能	cái néng	ability; talent; capability — 才 cái (talent) + 能 néng (capacity). The abilities a person actually possesses and can put to use: 发挥才能 "to give full play to one's talents." Note the toned reading cáinéng (the noun); the adverb 才 plus 能 (cái néng, "only then can") is a separate construction.
成功	chéng gōng	success: to succeed — 成 chéng (to accomplish; to become) + 功 gōng (achievement). Literally "to complete the achievement." Both verb (to succeed) and noun (success). One of the most common words in modern Chinese, the natural opposite of 失败 shībai (failure). 成功人士 = a successful person. 取得成功 = to attain success.
成就	chéng jiù	achievement; accomplishment — 成 chéng (accomplished) + 就 jiù (to attain). The lasting result of effort, the achievement one can point to: 巨大的成就 "a great achievement," 学术成就 "academic accomplishments." Weightier and more public than a single completed task.
聪明	cōng míng	clever; intelligent; sharp-minded — 聪 (cōng, sharp-eared; quick-witted , 耳 radical) + 明 (bright, clear-sighted , 日 sun + 月 moon). Intelligence as a compound of keen hearing and clear sight. 聪 alone originally meant "sharp of ear" in classical texts; combined with 明 (bright), it became the standard adjective for intelligence.
答应	dā ying	to agree; to promise; to answer (a call) — 答 dā (to answer) + 应 yìng (to respond). To answer a request with consent: to agree or promise. Also the literal "to answer" when called: 我叫了半天他都没答应 (I called for ages and he never answered). Note the neutral tone on 应 here.
打算	dǎ suàn	to plan; to intend
大概	dà gài	approximately; probably; roughly — 大 dà (large, rough) + 概 gài (general outline; to level off). A rough outline: approximately, probably. One of the most natural hedges in spoken Mandarin: 大概三点 "around three o'clock."
动力	dòng lì	driving force; motive power; motivation — 动 dòng (to move) + 力 lì (force). The force that drives motion. Literal in machines, where it names motive power, and figurative in people, where it names the motivation that keeps one moving toward a goal.
反应	fǎn yìng	reaction; response; to react — 反 fǎn (back) + 应 yìng (to respond). The response that comes back when something acts on you: a chemical reaction (化学反应), a person's reaction to news, a body's reaction to medicine. Both noun and verb.
该	gāi	should; ought to
敢	gǎn	dare to
刚才	gāng cái	just now; a moment ago — 刚 gāng (just; barely) + 才 cái. A time word for the very recent past, the moment that has only just passed: 刚才他还在这儿 "he was still here just now." Here 才 keeps its sense of a threshold only just crossed.
功夫茶	gōng fu chá	gongfu tea ceremony
功劳	gōng láo	merit: credit for meritorious service — 功 gōng (merit) + 劳 láo (toil; labor). The credit owed to someone for hard work that produced real results. Used to acknowledge contribution: 这是大家的功劳 = this is everyone's accomplishment. The modest counterpart is 苦劳 kǔláo, credit merely for effort even without success.
功率	gōng lǜ	power (physics)
功能	gōng néng	function; capability; feature — 功 gōng (work; merit) + 能 néng (ability; can). The work that something is able to do , its function. Appears everywhere: product features (软件功能 , software features), biology (心脏的功能 , the function of the heart), linguistics (句子的功能 , the function of a sentence).
功效	gōng xiào	efficacy: the effect produced — 功 gōng (effect) + 效 xiào (effect; result). The efficacy of a medicine, treatment, or method, the actual beneficial result it achieves. Especially common in medicine and traditional Chinese health: 这种药的功效很好 = this medicine is very effective.
供应	gōng yìng	to supply; to provide; supply — 供 gōng (to provide) + 应 yìng (to meet a need). To supply goods or services to meet a demand: 供应商 (supplier), 供应链 (supply chain), 供不应求 (supply falls short of demand). The yìng here is the answering of a need with provision.
何必	hé bì	why must; there is no need to; why go so far? — 何 hé (why; what; how , the classical interrogative) + 必 bì (must; certainly). A rhetorical question that questions the necessity of an action, usually suggesting that the action is excessive, unnecessary, or misguided.
回应	huí yìng	to reply; to respond
会说中文	huì shuō Zhōng wén	can speak Chinese — learned through study and practice — 会 indicates that a skill was acquired through learning, training, or practice. The speaker once did not have this ability and now does , through effort. This is the fundamental meaning: know how to.
或许	huò xǔ	perhaps; possibly
基本功	jī běng ōng	basic skills; fundamentals — 基本 jīběn (basic; fundamental) + 功 gōng (skill). The foundational skills of any discipline, the drills a beginner must master before anything else: scales for a musician, stances for a martial artist, strokes for a calligrapher.
尽力	jìn lì	to do one's utmost; to try one's best — 尽 jìn (to exhaust; to the limit) + 力 lì (effort). To use up all the effort one has, to spare nothing: 我会尽力 "I will do my best," 尽力而为 "do all one can."
禁止	jìn zhǐ	forbidden, prohibited
就	jiù	then; as soon as
可爱	kě ài	cute; adorable; lovable — 可 kě (can; worthy of) + 爱 ài (love). Worthy of being loved: cute, adorable. One of the most-used adjectives in everyday Mandarin. Used for children, animals, cartoon characters, and endearing people.
可见	kě jiàn	evidently; it can be seen that; clearly — 可 (can; worth) + 见 (to see; to understand). "Can be seen" , used to introduce a logical inference or conclusion drawn from evidence. A formal connector common in essays and arguments.
可靠	kě kào	reliable; trustworthy; dependable — 可 (worth) + 靠 (to lean on; to rely on). Worth leaning on. Used for people, sources of information, systems, and promises.
可能会	kěn éng huì	might; may well (possibility + likelihood) — A very common stack: 可能 ("possibly") plus the modal 会 ("will / be likely to"), giving "might, may well." 明天可能会变冷 ("it might turn cold tomorrow"). The 会 adds the sense of a future tendency, so 可能会 feels a touch more about what is likely to happen, while bare 可能 just flags that something is possible.
可怕	kě pà	frightening; terrible; dreadful — 可 (worth) + 怕 (to fear). Worth fearing. Used for things that inspire genuine dread , horror films, dangerous situations, extreme weather, powerful people.
可惜	kě xī	unfortunately; what a pity; it is a shame that — 可 (worth) + 惜 (to pity; to regret the loss of something). Worth regretting. Functions as an adjective (这很可惜 , this is a pity) and as a sentential adverb opening a clause (可惜他没来 , unfortunately he did not come).
可以	kě yǐ	can; may; it is permissible to; acceptable; not bad — The most versatile 可-compound. 可 (permitted/possible) + 以 (by means of; using). As a modal verb it covers both capability and permission. As a colloquial adjective it means "acceptable" or "not bad." It is the go-to answer when someone asks if something is okay.
可以进来吗？	kě yǐ jìn lái ma ?	May I come in? — requesting permission — 可以 is the canonical permission modal. It asks or grants social authorization , whether rules, norms, context, or a person in authority allows an action. The asker knows they have the physical ability; the question is whether they have the social right.
肯定	kěn dìng	certainly; to affirm; positive; sure — 肯 kěn (to be willing; to assent) + 定 dìng (to settle). To settle a thing as yes: as an adverb, "certainly," and as a verb, "to affirm, to confirm." Its opposite is 否定 fǒudìng (to negate; negative).
口才	kǒu cái	eloquence; a gift for speaking — 口 kǒu (mouth) + 才 cái (talent). Literally "mouth-talent," the ability to speak well and persuasively: 他很有口才 "he is very eloquent." One of a family of 才 compounds naming a specific gift, alongside 文才 wéncái (literary talent).
苦劳	kǔ láo	credit for hard work
劳动	láo dòng	labor; to work
力量	lì liàng	strength: force, power, capability — 力 lì (force, strength) + 量 liàng (measure, amount). The measurable quantity of force, whether physical, social, or moral: 体力的力量 (bodily strength), 团结的力量 (the power of unity), 知识就是力量 (knowledge is power).
力气	lì qi	physical strength; bodily effort — 力 lì (force) + 气 qì (breath; vital energy). The bodily strength you can feel in your muscles, the kind used to lift, carry, or push: 没力气 "have no strength," 花力气 "to spend effort." Concrete and physical, never abstract.
立功	lì gōng	to render meritorious service: to perform a deed — 立 lì (to establish; to set up) + 功 gōng (merit). To establish merit through a notable deed, originally military, now also civic and professional. A verb-object compound, so it splits: 立了大功 = rendered great service. Often paired with reward: 立功受奖 = to perform a deed and receive a commendation.
明天会下雨	míng tiān huì xià yǔ	it will rain tomorrow — 会 as future/likelihood marker — 会 has a second major function entirely distinct from learned ability: expressing expectation, likelihood, or prediction about a future event. This 会 does not mean "has learned how to" , rain cannot "learn" to rain. Instead, it signals the speaker's confident expectation that something will occur.
脑力	nǎo lì	mental effort
能	néng	can; be able to
能力	néng lì	ability; capability; capacity — 能 néng (be able) + 力 lì (strength). The general word for ability, what a person is capable of doing: 工作能力 "work ability," 有能力 "to be capable."
努力	nǔ lì	to make an effort; to strive; hardworking — 努 nǔ (to put forth) + 力 lì (effort). To bend one's strength toward a goal, the everyday word for trying hard: 努力工作 "to work hard," 努力学习 "to study diligently." Works as both verb and noun.
勤奋	qín fèn	diligent; industrious
权力	quán lì	authority; power
确定	qu è dìng	to confirm; to be sure; certain — 确 què (sure; firm) + 定 dìng (to settle). To pin a thing down as fixed and beyond doubt, or to be sure of it oneself. 我不确定 "I'm not sure," 确定时间 "to fix the time."
人才	rén cái	a talented person; talent; capable people — 人 rén (person) + 才 cái (talent). A person of real ability, and by extension the pool of skilled people an organization or country can draw on: 培养人才 "to cultivate talent," 人才市场 "the job market for skilled workers."
如果	rú guǒ	if; in the event that — 如 rú (suppose) + 果 guǒ (outcome). The default "if," opening the condition clause and usually answered by 就 jiù in the result: 如果你来，我就很高兴 "if you come, I'll be glad."
失败	shī bài	to fail; failure
实力	shí lì	real strength; ability
适应	shì yìng	to adapt; to get used to; to suit — 适 shì (to suit) + 应 yìng (to respond, to meet). To adjust oneself to new circumstances: 适应环境 (adapt to the environment), 适应新工作 (get used to a new job). The response here is the slow one of fitting in over time.
首要	shǒu yào	of prime importance; the foremost — 首 shǒu (first) + 要 yào (important). Marking the single most important matter, ranking above all others. 首要任务 "the primary task," 首要问题 "the chief problem." Stronger and more formal than 重要 zhòngyào (important), since it claims the top spot, not just high rank.
体力	tǐ lì	physical stamina; bodily strength; endurance — 体 tǐ (body) + 力 lì (strength). The body's stamina, the strength that sustains physical effort over time. Often paired against 脑力 nǎolì (mental effort): 体力劳动 "physical labor."
天才	tiān cái	genius; heaven-bestowed talent — 天 tiān (heaven) + 才 cái (talent; ability). Heaven-bestowed ability , the kind of capacity that seems to have come from beyond ordinary human effort. The etymology encodes a theory of talent: genius is a gift, not a product.
头疼，不能去	tóu téng , bù néng qù	headache, can't go — circumstance prevents ability — 能 covers physical capacity (your body can or cannot do something) and circumstantial ability (the situation allows or does not allow).
未必	wèi bì	not necessarily; may not be the case — 未 wèi (not yet; not necessarily , the classical negator of something assumed or expected) + 必 bì (certainly). The combination reverses the certainty: what seemed certain is not settled. A courteous, measured way to challenge an assumption without direct contradiction.
武功	wǔ gōng	martial skill: fighting prowess — 武 wǔ (martial; military) + 功 gōng (skill). Skill in the martial arts, the trained fighting ability central to wuxia fiction and film. 武功高强 = of formidable martial skill. 一身武功 = a whole body of fighting skill. The word also has a classical sense of military accomplishment, the deeds that win wars.
响应	xiǎng yìng	to respond to; to answer (a call); response — 响 xiǎng (to sound, to echo) + 应 yìng (to respond). To answer a call or appeal, often a collective one: 响应号召 (respond to a call), 积极响应 (respond actively). The image is of an echo answering a sound.
需要	xū yào	to need; to require (necessity, not merely desire) — 需 xū (to need; to require, a more formal or objective necessity) + 要. 需要 focuses on objective need rather than personal desire. 我需要帮助 (I need help , genuinely require it) vs. 我要帮助 (I want help , requesting it).
许可	xǔ kě	permission (formal noun)
压力	yā lì	pressure; stress (physical or mental) — 压 yā (to press down) + 力 lì (force). The force pressing from outside. In physics it is pressure on a surface; in daily life it is the weight of stress: 工作压力 "work stress," 压力很大 "under a lot of pressure."
要是	yào shi	if; supposing (colloquial conditional) — 要 (want/will) + 是 shì (be/this). A colloquial conditional meaning "if; supposing that." Slightly warmer and more personally invested than the neutral 如果 rúguǒ. Implies the speaker has some stake in the condition. Typically paired with 就 jiù in the consequence clause.
也许	yě xǔ	maybe; perhaps
一定要	yí dìng yào	must; be sure to; definitely will — The emphatic resolve-and-urging frame. For yourself it is determination: 我一定要赢 ("I'm determined to win"). For others it is earnest pressing: 你一定要照顾好自己 ("you really must take good care of yourself").
应当	yīng dāng	ought to; should; be obliged to — 应 yīng (should) + 当 dāng (be fitting). A modal of obligation, near-identical in meaning to 应该 yīnggāi but slightly more formal, so it shows up often in rules, contracts, and writing.
应付	yìng fu	to cope with; to handle; to deal with — 应 yìng (to meet) + 付 fù (to hand over, to deal out). To handle a task or person, often a difficult one. Can carry a tinge of just getting through it: 应付考试 (manage to get through the exam), 应付了事 (do something perfunctorily just to be done with it).
应聘	yìng pìn	to apply for a job
应用	yìng yòng	to apply; application
用功	yòng ōng	to study hard; to work diligently; to apply oneself — 用 yòng (to use; to deploy) + 功 gōng (effort; merit; achievement). To deploy effort , the literal meaning maps exactly onto the English "to apply oneself." Used particularly of students and academic work, but extends to any disciplined sustained effort.
用力	yòng lì	to exert force; to use one's strength; hard (of physical effort) — 用 yòng (to use) + 力 lì (force). To put physical force into an action: 用力推 "push hard," 用力拉 "pull with all your strength." Describes how an action is done, with muscle behind it.
愿意	yuàn yì	to be willing; to want to — 愿 yuàn (to wish) + 意 yì (intent). The willingness of the heart to do something: 我愿意帮你 "I'm willing to help you." It expresses consent and readiness, not mere ability.
允许	yǔn xǔ	to permit; to allow
值得	zhí de	worth doing; deserving
重要	zhòng yào	important; significant — 重 zhòng (heavy; weighty; important) + 要 (essential; necessary). 重要 means "important" in the sense of having significant weight or consequence. One of the most common adjectives in Chinese. 非常重要 (extremely important) · 最重要的是… (Most importantly…).
主要	zhǔ yào	main; primary; principal — 主 zhǔ (master; host; main; primary) + 要 (essential). "The main/essential one." Used to identify the principal element among several: 主要问题 (the main problem) · 主要原因 (the primary reason) · 主要目标 (the primary goal). Common in analysis, journalism, and academic writing.
