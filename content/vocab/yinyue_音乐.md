faq:
  - q: "Why does 乐 mean both music (yuè) and happiness (lè)?"
    a: "One character, two readings, one ancient intuition. 乐 read yuè is music (音乐); read lè it is joy (快乐). The traditional form 樂 pictures a stringed instrument on a wooden stand, and classical thinkers made the pun into philosophy: the Confucian Record of Music argues that music is joy given proper form."
  - q: "What does 知音 (zhīyīn) mean?"
    a: "Literally 'one who knows the tone': a soulmate or the rare person who truly understands you. It comes from the story of the zither master Boya and the woodcutter Zhong Ziqi, who heard mountains and rivers in his playing; when Ziqi died, Boya smashed his zither and never played again."
  - q: "How do you say concert and instrument in Chinese?"
    a: "音乐会 (yīnyuèhuì, 'music gathering') is a concert, and 乐器 (yuèqì, 'music implement') is an instrument. To play differs by instrument: 弹钢琴 (pluck the piano), 拉小提琴 (draw the violin), 吹笛子 (blow the flute), 打鼓 (strike the drum)."

      <span class="topic-hero-eyebrow">Vocabulary · 词汇 cíhuì</span>
      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">音乐</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">yīnyuè</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">Music and joy share a single character, a soulmate is "the one who knows your tone," and the zither smashed for a dead friend still echoes.</p>
    </header>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="樂">
      <div class="scholar-label">一字两读 yī zì liǎng dú · One Character, Two Readings</div>
      <p><strong>音</strong> (yīn) is the patterned-sound character: the early script differentiated it from 言 (yán, speech) by adding a mark inside the mouth element, sound distinguished from words, and the modern form settles into 立 over 日. <strong>乐</strong> is the interesting one. Its traditional form 樂 is generally read as a stringed instrument, silk strings mounted on wood, and the character carries two readings that split a single idea: <strong>yuè</strong>, music, and <strong>lè</strong>, joy. The same glyph sits inside 音乐 (music) and 快乐 (happy), which means a learner meets the music-joy identity in the first month and spends years discovering how deep it runs.</p>
      <p>Classical writers treated the double reading as a truth rather than an accident. The <em>Record of Music</em> (乐记 Yuèjì), the Han-era treatise embedded in the Book of Rites, states the relation directly: music is what arises when joy takes proper form, and conversely, well-formed music produces harmony in those who hear it. The pun even reaches place names and surnames (the strategist 乐毅 is Yuè Yì, the modern word 可乐 is cola, "permissible joy"), so reading 乐 correctly is a small ongoing test of contextual fluency.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="liyue"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">礼乐</span>
      <span class="sh-py">lǐyuè</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Rites and Music</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="礼">
      <div class="scholar-label">移风易俗 yí fēng yì sú · Music Changes Customs</div>
      <p>Confucian political theory paired 礼 (ritual propriety) with 乐 (music) as the two great instruments of civilization: ritual differentiates people into their proper roles, music harmonizes them back into one body. This is why the <em>Analects</em> shows Confucius so absorbed in music that after hearing the ancient Sháo court music in Qi he did not notice the taste of meat for three months, and why the education of a gentleman included the zither as a matter of course. Bad music was a political symptom: the classics blame the "sounds of Zheng and Wei" for licentiousness the way later moralists blamed jazz or rock, a two-and-a-half-millennium-old genre panic.</p>
      <p>The instrumentarium of that world is still playing. The 古琴 (gǔqín), the seven-string scholar's zither, is the instrument of Boya's story and the UNESCO-listed emblem of literati culture; the 古筝 (gǔzhēng) is its larger, brighter cousin; the 二胡 (èrhú) is the two-string fiddle that carries film scores' instant "China" cue; and the 琵琶 (pípa), the pear-shaped lute, arrived over the Silk Road and gave Tang poetry one of its great scenes, the disgraced official Bai Juyi weeping at a riverside pipa performance in 琵琶行. Modern genres stack the vocabulary transparently: 流行音乐 (pop), 摇滚 (rock, a sound-and-sense loan: "shake and roll"), 民乐 (traditional Chinese orchestral music).</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="zhiyin"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">知音</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zhīyīn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The One Who Knows the Tone</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="琴">
      <div class="scholar-label">高山流水 gāoshān liúshuǐ · High Mountains, Flowing Water</div>
      <p>The zither master Boya (伯牙) played, and the woodcutter Zhong Ziqi (钟子期) listened. When Boya's mind was on high mountains, Ziqi said: majestic, like Mount Tai. When his mind turned to flowing water, Ziqi said: vast, like the rivers. Whatever Boya played, Ziqi heard what was meant, not merely what sounded. When Ziqi died, Boya broke his zither, cut the strings, and never played again, saying the one person worth playing for was gone. The story, told in the <em>Liezi</em> and <em>Lüshi Chunqiu</em>, gave the language <strong>知音</strong> (zhīyīn, "the one who knows the tone"): the friend who truly understands you, a word English needs a phrase like "kindred spirit" to approximate, and Chinese speakers use for everything from soulmates to the rare boss who gets your work.</p>
      <p>The story's title phrase 高山流水 became the name of the most famous guqin pieces and the chengyu for both sublime music and perfect mutual understanding. Its melody, in the 流水 arrangement, was chosen for the Golden Record aboard the Voyager spacecraft in 1977, which means the definitive recording of the friendship-listening story is currently leaving the solar system, awaiting its next 知音.</p>
    </div>

          <span class="card-py">yuèqì</span>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">

    <div class="chengyu-grid">

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">对牛弹琴</span>
        <span class="cy-py">duì niú tán qín</span>
        <span class="cy-en">playing the zither to a cow — wasting refinement on the wrong audience</span>
        <span class="cy-note">From the Han-era Buddhist apologia Mouzi Lihuolun: the musician Gongming Yi played elegant melodies to a grazing cow, which went on grazing; when he imitated flies and a lost calf, the cow flicked its tail and listened. The barb cuts both ways: the audience is dull, but the performer failed to speak the listener's language. Teachers and marketers quote it against themselves as often as against others.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">余音绕梁</span>
        <span class="cy-py">yú yīn rào liáng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">the lingering sound circles the roof-beams — music that haunts after it ends</span>
        <span class="cy-note">From the Liezi: the singer Han E, paying for a meal with song, left a melody that circled the beams of the city gate for three days (余音绕梁，三日不绝). The standard praise for a performance that stays with you, extended freely to speeches, films, and any experience with a long afterglow.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">滥竽充数</span>
        <span class="cy-py">làn yú chōng shù</span>
        <span class="cy-en">faking the yu-pipe to fill out the ensemble — an impostor padding the numbers</span>
        <span class="cy-note">From the Han Feizi: King Xuan of Qi kept a 300-piece yu-pipe ensemble, and Mr. Nanguo, who could not play a note, mimed along on a full salary. The king died; his successor preferred solos; Nanguo fled overnight. The chengyu for coasting inside a big organization, and the classical argument for performance reviews.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">高山流水</span>
        <span class="cy-py">gāo shān liú shuǐ</span>
        <span class="cy-en">high mountains, flowing water — sublime music; perfect mutual understanding</span>
        <span class="cy-note">The Boya and Ziqi story compressed to four characters, doing double duty for masterful art and for the rare listener who deserves it. As a guqin piece, 流水 was included on the Voyager Golden Record, carrying the 知音 ideal out of the solar system.</span>
      </div>
