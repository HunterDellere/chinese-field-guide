faq:
  - q: "What does 医院 (yīyuàn) literally mean?"
    a: "Medicine courtyard. 医 is the healing morpheme (its traditional form 醫 pictures an arrow being extracted and a jar of medicinal wine) and 院 is the walled courtyard that became the suffix for institutions, the same 院 as in 学院 (college) and 法院 (court)."
  - q: "What is the difference between 医院 and 诊所 (zhěnsuǒ)?"
    a: "医院 is a full hospital with departments and inpatient beds; 诊所 is a small clinic. Chinese healthcare routes most complaints straight to the hospital's outpatient department (门诊), so 医院 does much of the everyday work a Western reader would associate with a GP's office."
  - q: "How do you say doctor and nurse in Chinese?"
    a: "医生 (yīshēng, 'healing person') is the doctor, addressed directly as 医生 or 大夫 (dàifu, an old official title that survived as northern spoken usage). The nurse is 护士 (hùshi, 'protecting officer')."

      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">医院</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">yīyuàn</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">The healing courtyard: how an arrow wound, a wine jar, and the institutional suffix 院 assembled the word for hospital.</p>
    </header>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="醫">
      <div class="scholar-label">箭与酒 jiàn yǔ jiǔ · The Arrow and the Wine</div>
      <p>The traditional form of the healing character, <strong>醫</strong>, is a compressed scene of early medicine. The upper left 医 is an arrow (矢) in a quiver or wound-box; the upper right 殳 is a hand wielding an implement; and the bottom 酉 is a wine jar. Read together: extracting an arrow, working with instruments, and administering alcohol, which was antiquity's antiseptic, anesthetic, and solvent for herbal preparations at once. The <em>Shuowen</em> glosses the wine component directly: wine is what treats illness. Simplification kept only the arrow-box 医, which is why the modern character looks like a box with an arrow in it, a battlefield first-aid kit reduced to its emblem.</p>
      <p>医 does the healing work across the lexicon: 医生 (yīshēng, doctor), 医学 (yīxué, the study of medicine), 中医 and 西医 (the traditional and biomedical systems), 医疗 (yīliáo, medical care), and 医保 (yībǎo, medical insurance). Paired with 院 (yuàn), the walled courtyard, it names the institution. Courtyards were how China built: houses, temples, government offices, and academies were all walled compounds, so 院 drifted from architecture into institution, and a hospital is literally the courtyard where medicine happens.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">X + 院 · The Institution Suffix</span>
      <strong>医院 yīyuàn</strong> → hospital : the medicine courtyard<br>
      <strong>学院 xuéyuàn</strong> → college, institute : the study courtyard<br>
      <strong>法院 fǎyuàn</strong> → court of law : the law courtyard<br>
      <strong>电影院 diànyǐngyuàn</strong> → cinema : the electric-shadow courtyard<br>
      <strong>科学院 kēxuéyuàn</strong> → academy of sciences ; 国务院 → the State Council<br>
      <strong>四合院 sìhéyuàn</strong> → the Beijing courtyard house : the architectural original, still just a courtyard
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="院">
      <div class="scholar-label">从建筑到机构 · From Architecture to Institution</div>
      <p>The 院 family is a map of Chinese institutional life, and its members share the flavor of the walled compound: a place you enter through a gate, with its own internal order. The hospital keeps the architecture in its verbs. To be hospitalized is 住院 (zhùyuàn, "live in the courtyard"), to be discharged is 出院 (chūyuàn, "leave the courtyard"), and the head of the institution is the 院长 (yuànzhǎng, "courtyard elder"), a title shared by hospital directors, college deans, and supreme court presidents, which occasionally requires clarification at dinner parties.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">院内</span>

      <span class="sh-en">Inside the Hospital</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="医">
      <div class="scholar-label">白大褂 bái dàguà · The White Coats</div>
      <p>The cast: 医生 (yīshēng, doctor; in the north also 大夫 dàifu, a Song-era official rank that settled onto physicians and stayed), 护士 (hùshi, nurse, "protecting officer"), and 病人 (bìngrén, patient, "ill person") or the more clinical 患者 (huànzhě). The geography: 门诊 (ménzhěn, outpatient clinic, "door consultations"), 急诊 (jízhěn, the ER, "urgent consultations"), 病房 (bìngfáng, the ward), 手术室 (shǒushùshì, the operating room, where 手术 "hand-technique" is surgery), and 药房 (yàofáng, the dispensary window where the visit ends). The whole route, from taking a queue number onward, is walked step by step in this guide's 看病 entry.</p>
      <p>Practical modifiers matter when choosing where to go: hospitals are ranked 三甲 (sānjiǎ, "third-tier grade A") at the top, and 人民医院 (People's Hospital) plus a number is the standard municipal naming scheme, so 市一医院 ("Municipal Hospital No. 1") reads like an address rather than a brand. A 中医院 is a hospital of traditional medicine, and the 妇幼保健院 (women's and children's health center) rounds out the everyday institutional set.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">对症下药</span>
        <span class="cy-py">duì zhèng xià yào</span>
        <span class="cy-en">prescribe according to the symptoms; tailor the remedy to the problem</span>
        <span class="cy-note">对 (match) + 症 (symptoms) + 下药 (administer medicine). From the case lore of the Han physician Hua Tuo, who gave two patients with identical headaches different prescriptions because the causes differed. Now the standard metaphor for any correctly targeted solution: a policy, a lesson plan, a bug fix. 解决问题要对症下药。You must fit the remedy to the disease when solving a problem.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">妙手回春</span>
        <span class="cy-py">miào shǒu huí chūn</span>
        <span class="cy-en">miraculous hands bring back the spring; masterly healing</span>
        <span class="cy-note">妙手 (wondrous hands) + 回春 (return spring, restore life's season). The stock phrase of gratitude to a doctor, embroidered on the silk banners (锦旗 jǐnqí) that patients present to hospital departments, a living custom: the walls of a respected clinic hang red with them.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">病急乱投医</span>
        <span class="cy-py">bìng jí luàn tóu yī</span>
        <span class="cy-en">when the illness is desperate, one runs to any doctor</span>
        <span class="cy-note">A proverb rather than a classical chengyu: desperation abandons judgment. Used far beyond medicine, for panicked job hunts, failing companies grasping at consultants, or students trying every cram school at once. The tone is sympathetic but critical; the flailing rarely works.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">久病成医</span>
        <span class="cy-py">jiǔ bìng chéng yī</span>
        <span class="cy-en">long illness makes the patient a doctor</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Chronic experience becomes expertise. Said of patients who know their condition better than junior doctors, and by extension of anyone whose long exposure to a problem has made them its reluctant expert: the veteran user who knows the software's bugs better than the developers.</span>
