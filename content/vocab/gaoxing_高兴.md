faq:
  - q: "What is the difference between 高兴, 开心, 快乐, and 幸福?"
    a: "All translate as happy, at different depths. 高兴 and 开心 are the everyday glad-right-now words, nearly interchangeable. 快乐 is the wishing word (生日快乐, happy birthday) and a more sustained cheerfulness. 幸福 is the deep one: life-level wellbeing, the happiness of a good marriage or a warm family, never a passing mood."
  - q: "How do you say nice to meet you in Chinese?"
    a: "很高兴认识你 (hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ), literally 'very glad to know you.' It is the standard polite formula at introductions, and the reply can simply echo it: 我也很高兴认识你."
  - q: "Why does 兴 appear in both 高兴 (xìng) and 兴趣 (xìngqù)?"
    a: "兴 read xìng is aroused interest or excitement, the sense in 高兴 (spirits raised high) and 兴趣 (interest). Read xīng, first tone, it means to rise or flourish, as in 兴起 (rise up) and 兴旺 (thriving). One character, the same idea of rising, split between mood and motion."

      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">高兴</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">gāoxìng</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">Happiness as elevation: spirits that rise. The everyday glad word, and how it divides the territory with 开心, 快乐, and 幸福.</p>
    </header>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="兴">
      <div class="scholar-label">兴致高涨 xìngzhì gāozhǎng · Spirits Running High</div>
      <p>高兴 (gāoxìng) is 高 (high) plus 兴 (xìng, aroused interest, animation): spirits raised high, the same vertical metaphor English uses in "high spirits" and "feeling down." 兴 is a polyphone whose two readings split one idea of rising. Read <strong>xīng</strong>, first tone, it is the verb to rise or flourish: 兴起 (rise up), 兴旺 (prosperous), 复兴 (revival, as in the official phrase about national rejuvenation). Read <strong>xìng</strong>, fourth tone, the rising moves indoors and becomes mood: 高兴 (glad), 兴趣 (xìngqù, interest), 兴奋 (xīngfèn is the exception that keeps first tone, excitement), and 尽兴 (jìnxìng, to enjoy oneself to the full, the host's parting wish at a banquet).</p>
      <p>Grammatically 高兴 earns its keep. It takes degree adverbs like any adjective (很高兴, 特别高兴), and it takes a following verb phrase: 很高兴认识你 ("very glad to know you") is the fixed formula of introductions, and 我很高兴你来了 ("I'm glad you came") shows the pattern with a full clause. The negative 不高兴 understates displeasure the way English "not happy" does: 老板不高兴了 means storm clouds, not a neutral absence of joy.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">四种快乐 sì zhǒng kuàilè · Four Kinds of Happy</span>
      <strong>高兴 gāoxìng</strong> → glad, right now : reactive and momentary ; 见到你很高兴<br>
      <strong>开心 kāixīn</strong> → having a good time : "open-hearted" ; 玩得很开心<br>
      <strong>快乐 kuàilè</strong> → the wishing word : 生日快乐! 新年快乐! ; also sustained cheer<br>
      <strong>幸福 xìngfú</strong> → deep wellbeing : a good life, not a good mood ; 幸福的家庭<br>
      <em>Test: you can 祝 (wish) someone 快乐 or 幸福, but not 高兴; gladness can't be scheduled</em>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="福">
      <div class="scholar-label">从心情到人生 · From Mood to Life</div>
      <p>The four words ascend in depth. 高兴 and 开心 are the daily pair, close enough to swap in most sentences, though 开心 ("heart opened") leans toward enjoyment in progress (孩子们玩得很开心, the kids are having a great time) while 高兴 leans toward gladness at something (听到这个消息我很高兴, glad to hear the news). 快乐 (kuàilè, "swift joy," sharing the joy-reading of the music character 乐) owns the festival calendar: every holiday greeting ends in it. 幸福 (xìngfú) is different in kind: built from 幸 (good fortune) and 福 (the blessing character pasted on every door at New Year), it names the settled happiness of a life going well. Advertising sells 幸福; weddings promise it; surveys measure it. Announcing 我很幸福 is a life report, not a mood report.</p>
      <p>The unhappy side of the ledger, for completeness: 难过 (nánguò, sad, "hard to get through"), 伤心 (shāngxīn, grieved, "wounded heart"), and 生气 (shēngqì, angry, "generating qi," the TCM physiology of rage preserved in the everyday verb). The heart radical 忄 or 心 anchors most of the emotion lexicon, filing feelings where classical China located them, in the heart that thinks and feels at once.</p>
    </div>

        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">开心</span>
          <span class="card-py">kāixīn</span>
          <span class="card-en">happy, having fun</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          开 (open) + 心 (heart): the heart opened up. The go-to word for enjoyment in progress and the standard well-wish for outings: 玩得开心! (have fun!). 开心果 ("open-heart fruit") is the pistachio, and by extension the friend who makes everyone laugh.

            <div class="ex-en">I got together with old friends today and had a wonderful time.</div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">快乐</span>
          <span class="card-py">kuàilè</span>
          <span class="card-en">happy (greetings and sustained cheer)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          快 (swift) + 乐 (joy, the lè reading of the music character). The greeting suffix of the entire festival calendar: 生日快乐, 新年快乐, 中秋快乐. The philosophical register belongs to it too; Zhuangzi's famous debate about whether the fish are happy uses 乐.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">祝你生日快乐，万事如意！</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhù nǐ shēngrì kuàilè, wànshì rúyì!</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Happy birthday, and may everything go as you wish!</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">幸福</span>
          <span class="card-py">xìngfú</span>
          <span class="card-en">deep happiness; wellbeing</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj/N 形容词/名词</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          幸 (good fortune) + 福 (blessing). Life-level happiness: 幸福的婚姻 (a happy marriage), 幸福感 (a sense of wellbeing, the survey researchers' term). A warm meal can make you 开心; only a good life makes you 幸福.
        </div>

          <div class="example">

            <div class="ex-en">The whole family together and healthy: that is happiness.</div>
          </div>
        </div>

      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">兴趣</span>
          <span class="card-py">xìngqù</span>
          <span class="card-en">interest, hobby</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          兴 (aroused spirit, the xìng reading) + 趣 (inclination, delight). 对…感兴趣 ("to feel interest toward") is the essential pattern: 我对中国历史很感兴趣. 兴趣班 are the after-school enrichment classes filling Chinese childhood weekends.

            <div class="ex-en">He is very interested in calligraphy and practices every day.</div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">兴高采烈</span>
        <span class="cy-py">xìng gāo cǎi liè</span>
        <span class="cy-en">spirits high, expression ablaze — in jubilant high spirits</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Originally literary criticism: Liu Xie's Wenxin Diaolong used 兴高而采烈 to praise the poet Ji Kang's lofty inspiration and brilliant style. The phrase migrated from describing writing to describing faces, and now marks any scene of visible collective joy: children on the last day of school arrive 兴高采烈.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">乐极生悲</span>
        <span class="cy-py">lè jí shēng bēi</span>
        <span class="cy-en">joy at its extreme gives birth to sorrow</span>
        <span class="cy-note">From the Records of the Grand Historian: the wit Chunyu Kun warned King Wei of Qi, over wine, that drinking to the limit ends in disorder and joy at its limit turns to grief (酒极则乱，乐极则悲). The proverbial ceiling on celebration, cited whenever triumph gets careless: the champagne-soaked team that crashes the trophy.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">喜出望外</span>
        <span class="cy-py">xǐ chū wàng wài</span>
        <span class="cy-en">joy beyond what one hoped — overjoyed by surprise</span>
        <span class="cy-note">喜 (delight) arriving from outside (外) the range of expectation (望). The precise word for good news you did not dare hope for: the scholarship, the recovered wallet, the friend at the airport gate. Stronger and more specific than 很高兴.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">皆大欢喜</span>
        <span class="cy-py">jiē dà huān xǐ</span>
        <span class="cy-en">all are greatly delighted — everyone ends up happy</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Originally the closing formula of Buddhist sutras, where the assembly rejoices at the teaching and departs; now the phrase for win-win endings, amicable settlements, and comedies. It is also the standard Chinese title of Shakespeare's As You Like It, a translation with better provenance than most.</span>
