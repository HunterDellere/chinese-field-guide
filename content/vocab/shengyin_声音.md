faq:
  - q: "What is the difference between 声 (shēng) and 音 (yīn)?"
    a: "In modern compounds they divide labor: 声 leans toward raw sound and the voice (大声 loud, 声音 sound), 音 toward patterned or meaningful sound (音乐 music, 发音 pronunciation, 声调 tone). The classical Record of Music made it a theory: sounds (声) that take on pattern become tones (音)."
  - q: "How do you say loud and quiet in Chinese?"
    a: "大声 (dàshēng, 'big sound') and 小声 (xiǎoshēng, 'small sound'), used adverbially: 请小声一点 ('please keep it down'). Total silence is 安静 (ānjìng), and the command 安静! does what 'quiet!' does in English."
  - q: "What does 声调 (shēngdiào) mean?"
    a: "The lexical tones of Chinese: the pitch contour that distinguishes mā, má, mǎ, and mà. Mandarin has four tones plus a neutral tone, and 声调 is the classroom word for them; getting them wrong changes the word, not just the accent."

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="聲">
      <div class="scholar-label">磬与耳 qìng yǔ ěr · The Stone and the Ear</div>
      <p>The traditional form of the sound character, <strong>聲</strong>, is a percussion scene: its upper part depicts the 磬 (qìng), the L-shaped stone chime that hung in racks in ritual orchestras and rang when struck, and beneath it sits 耳, the ear that receives the ringing. Sound, in the character's anatomy, is what travels from a struck stone to a listening ear, which is as good a definition as physics offers. Simplification kept only a fragment of the chime (声) and discarded the ear, so the modern character rings without anyone listening. 音, its partner, was differentiated in the early script from 言 (speech) by a mark added to the mouth element: sound as distinct from words.</p>
      <p>The compound 声音 (shēngyīn) is the everyday word for both sound and the human voice: 他的声音很好听 means he has a pleasant voice. 声 alone supplies the volume scale, 大声 and 小声 (big and small sound), and the counting word for utterances: 叫了一声 is to give a single cry, and the polite summons 喊我一声 means "give me a shout."</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="theory"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">声与音</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shēng yǔ yīn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Raw Sound, Patterned Tone</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="音">
      <div class="scholar-label">声成文，谓之音 · Sound Patterned Becomes Tone</div>
      <p>The <em>Record of Music</em> (乐记) drew the line that the modern compounds still walk: raw sounds are 声, and when sounds take on pattern and mutual response they become 音; music (乐) is 音 organized further still. The modern lexicon distributes accordingly. Raw and bodily: 声 in 哭声 (the sound of crying), 笑声 (laughter), 雷声 (thunder). Patterned and meaningful: 音 in 发音 (fāyīn, pronunciation), 拼音 (pīnyīn, the romanization system, "spelled sounds"), 口音 (kǒuyīn, accent), 录音 (lùyīn, a recording), and 音乐 itself. Unwanted sound gets its own compound, 噪音 (zàoyīn, noise), the enemy of apartment dwellers everywhere.</p>
      <p>For the learner, the highest-stakes member of the family is <strong>声调</strong> (shēngdiào), the lexical tones. Mandarin's four tones plus the neutral tone are not decoration; they are as phonemic as vowels, the difference between 买 (mǎi, buy) and 卖 (mài, sell) being a tone and a financial catastrophe. The guide's phonology pages carry the details; this word is the label on the whole enterprise. And one register note: 声 reaches into reputation, as in 名声 (míngshēng, one's public name) and 声誉 (shēngyù, prestige), sound as the social echo a person makes.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">声调</span>
          <span class="card-py">shēngdiào</span>
          <span class="card-en">lexical tone</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          声 (sound) + 调 (tune, contour): the pitch shape that distinguishes words. 第一声 through 第四声 name the four tones in class; 轻声 (qīngshēng, "light sound") is the neutral tone that unstressed syllables carry.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">中文的声调很重要，说错了意思就变了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhōngwén de shēngdiào hěn zhòngyào, shuō cuò le yìsi jiù biàn le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Tones in Chinese really matter; say one wrong and the meaning changes.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">发音</span>
          <span class="card-py">fāyīn</span>
          <span class="card-en">pronunciation; to pronounce</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">V/N 动词/名词</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          发 (emit) + 音 (sound). The compliment every learner works for is 你的发音很标准 ("your pronunciation is very standard"), 标准 being the operative cultural value: the broadcast-Mandarin ideal against which accents are measured.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他学了两年中文，发音相当标准。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā xué le liǎng nián Zhōngwén, fāyīn xiāngdāng biāozhǔn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He has studied Chinese for two years, and his pronunciation is quite standard.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">大声</span>
          <span class="card-py">dàshēng</span>
          <span class="card-en">loud; loudly</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adv 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          Big sound. Used adverbially before the verb: 大声说 (speak up), 大声朗读 (read aloud). Its opposite 小声 keeps the peace: 请小声一点，孩子睡了。Quiet please, the child is asleep.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">老师请他大声一点再说一遍。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Lǎoshī qǐng tā dàshēng yìdiǎn zài shuō yí biàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The teacher asked him to say it again a bit louder.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">噪音</span>
          <span class="card-py">zàoyīn</span>
          <span class="card-en">noise</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          噪 (clamor, wearing three mouths) + 音. The urban complaint word: construction 噪音, traffic 噪音, and the neighbor's renovation drill. 噪音污染 (noise pollution) is a recognized legal complaint category in Chinese cities.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">楼上装修的噪音吵得我没法工作。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Lóushàng zhuāngxiū de zàoyīn chǎo de wǒ méi fǎ gōngzuò.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The renovation noise upstairs is so loud I can't work.</div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">掩耳盗铃</span>
        <span class="cy-py">yǎn ěr dào líng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">covering one's ears to steal the bell — self-deception</span>
        <span class="cy-note">From the Lüshi Chunqiu: a thief found the great bell of the fallen Fan clan too heavy to carry, decided to break it up, and covered his own ears against the clang so no one would hear. The definitive image of believing a problem disappears when you stop perceiving it, applied to everything from ignored medical symptoms to suppressed bad news.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">鸦雀无声</span>
        <span class="cy-py">yā què wú shēng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">not a crow or sparrow to be heard — dead silence</span>
        <span class="cy-note">The silence so complete even the loudest birds have stopped. The stock description of a classroom after a hard question, a courtroom before a verdict, a banquet after an unwise toast. Its cousin 悄无声息 describes stealth rather than hush.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">异口同声</span>
        <span class="cy-py">yì kǒu tóng shēng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">different mouths, the same sound — unanimous</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Everyone says the same thing at once: the committee that answers in chorus, the witnesses whose accounts match. Neutral to positive in tone; suspicious unanimity would be phrased differently. The mirror image of 众说纷纭, where every mouth says something else.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">声东击西</span>
        <span class="cy-py">shēng dōng jī xī</span>
        <span class="cy-en">make noise in the east, strike in the west — the feint</span>
        <span class="cy-note">The strategists' classic: sound as decoy. 声 here is a verb, to make noise toward. Catalogued among the Thirty-Six Stratagems and applied ever since to warfare, chess, business competition, and toddlers distracting parents from the cookie jar.</span>
