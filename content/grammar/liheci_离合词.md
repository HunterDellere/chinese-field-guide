faq:
  - q: "What is a separable verb (离合词) in Chinese?"
    a: "A two-syllable verb that is internally verb + object and splits to admit grammar in the middle: 见面 (meet) becomes 见了面 (met), 见个面 (meet up), 见过一次面 (met once). Common members include 睡觉, 吃饭, 唱歌, 帮忙, 结婚, 生气, and 洗澡."
  - q: "Why is 我见面他 wrong?"
    a: "Because 见面 already contains its object: 面 (face). It cannot take another one. The person goes in front with a preposition: 跟他见面 ('meet with him'). The same fix serves 结婚 (跟她结婚) and 聊天 (跟朋友聊天)."
  - q: "How do you say 'help me' if 帮忙 is separable?"
    a: "Either split it around the beneficiary, 帮我一个忙 ('do me a favor'), or drop the 忙 and use 帮 directly: 帮我 ('help me'). What you cannot say is 帮忙我, the classic separable-verb error."

      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">离合词</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">líhécí</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">Words that unzip: 见面 is to meet, but meeting him is 跟他见面 and meeting once is 见一次面, because the "word" is secretly verb plus object.</p>
    </header>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="concept"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">词还是短语</span>
      <span class="sh-py">cí háishi duǎnyǔ</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Word or Phrase?</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="离">
      <div class="scholar-label">离则合，合则离 · Apart When Together, Together When Apart</div>
      <p>The name says it: <strong>离合词</strong>, "separate-combine words." A large class of everyday two-syllable verbs look like single words in the dictionary but are internally verb + object: 睡觉 is sleep + a sleep, 吃饭 is eat + rice/meal, 唱歌 is sing + song, 见面 is see + face, 洗澡 is wash + bath, 结婚 is tie + marriage. Used whole, they behave like intransitive verbs: 我们明天见面 (we meet tomorrow). But the moment grammar needs a place to sit, the object half politely steps back and the machinery moves in between: 见了面 (met), 见过面 (have met before), 见个面 (meet up, casual), 睡了一觉 (had a sleep), 帮了我一个大忙 (did me a big favor).</p>
      <p>Whether these are words or phrases is a genuine dispute in Chinese linguistics, and the honest answer is: both, which is what makes them a category. For the learner the practical rule is simpler. The dictionary lemma is a convenience; the grammar treats the two halves as verb and object, and everything else follows from that single fact.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="splitting"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">怎么拆</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zěnme chāi</span>
      <span class="sh-en">What Goes in the Gap</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">插入什么 chārù shénme · The Gap's Tenants</span>
      <strong>了 / 过</strong> → 睡了觉 ; 见过面 (aspect attaches to the verb half)<br>
      <strong>个 (casualizer)</strong> → 见个面, 洗个澡, 帮个忙 : "grab a quick one"<br>
      <strong>duration / times</strong> → 睡了八个小时的觉 ; 见了三次面<br>
      <strong>possessive target</strong> → 生他的气 (angry at him) ; 帮我的忙 (help me)<br>
      <strong>reduplication</strong> → verb half only : 唱唱歌, 散散步 (never 唱歌唱歌)<br>
      <em>Everything that would follow an ordinary verb slips inside the separable one</em>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="觉">
      <div class="scholar-label">睡了一个好觉 · A Good Sleep, Slept</div>
      <p>The gap's tenants are exactly the things that normally follow a verb: aspect particles, measure phrases, duration, and the beneficiary or target dressed as a possessor. That last one produces the constructions learners find most alien and natives find most natural: <strong>生他的气</strong>, literally "generate his anger," means to be angry at him; 帮我的忙 is to help me; revenge dramas run on 报仇 splitting into 报了大仇. Duration goes inside because Chinese counts time on the object: eight hours of sleep is 八个小时的觉, a possessed sleep.</p>
      <p>Reduplication, the softening doubling covered across this guide's grammar pages, copies only the verb half: 唱唱歌 (sing a little), 见见面 (meet briefly), 散散步 (take a stroll). And the casual 个, semantically almost empty, marks the activity as light and social: 改天见个面 ("let's grab a meet-up sometime") carries exactly the weight of its English translation, none.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="errors"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">宾语问题</span>
      <span class="sh-py">bīnyǔ wèntí</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Object Problem</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="跟">
      <div class="scholar-label">跟他见面 · The 跟 Workaround</div>
      <p>The signature error is bolting a second object onto a verb that already contains one: <em>见面他</em>, <em>结婚她</em>, <em>帮忙我</em> are all impossible, because 面, 婚, and 忙 occupy the object seat. The repair is a preposition fronting the person: <strong>跟他见面</strong> (meet with him), <strong>跟她结婚</strong> (marry her), <strong>跟朋友聊天</strong> (chat with friends), with 和 or 与 as register variants of 跟. For 帮忙 the alternative repair is to abandon the 忙 and use bare 帮, which takes objects freely: 帮我 (help me), 帮我买杯咖啡 (get me a coffee).</p>
      <p>How do you know a verb is separable before it bites you? Heuristics: if the second syllable is a noun a dictionary lists independently (觉, 饭, 歌, 澡, 婚, 气), suspicion is warranted; if you have ever heard it split (见个面), the case is closed. Textbooks mark the class with a // in pinyin (jiàn//miàn), a notation worth learning to read as a warning label. When in doubt, front the person with 跟 and no harm can follow: the workaround is always grammatical, while the bolted-on object never is.</p>
    </div>
