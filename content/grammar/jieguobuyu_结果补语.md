faq:
  - q: "What is a resultative complement in Chinese?"
    a: "A second element glued onto a verb to state the action's outcome: 听 is to listen, 听懂 is to listen and understand; 找 is to look for, 找到 is to find. The verb names the attempt, the complement names the result, and many English verbs (find, see, finish) translate as verb-plus-complement pairs."
  - q: "How do you negate a resultative complement?"
    a: "With 没 before the whole unit: 我没听懂 (I didn't understand). 不 appears only in hypotheticals and the potential form (听不懂). Since the result either happened or it didn't, the completed-action negator 没 does the work."
  - q: "What are the most common resultative complements?"
    a: "完 (finished: 吃完 eat up), 到 (attained: 找到 find, 买到 manage to buy), 见 (perceived: 看见 see, 听见 hear), 懂 (understood: 看懂), 好 (done properly: 准备好 ready), and 错 (wrongly: 说错 misspeak). A dozen complements cover most daily speech."

    <span class="section-anchor" id="concept"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">尝试与结果</span>
      <span class="sh-py">chángshì yǔ jiéguǒ</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Attempt versus Outcome</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="找">
      <div class="scholar-label">找了半天，没找到 · Looked All Day, Didn't Find It</div>
      <p>English verbs quietly bundle outcomes: "find" means your search succeeded, "see" means light reached your understanding. Chinese unbundles them. The bare verb names the activity, and a <strong>结果补语</strong> (resultative complement), welded on directly after, names what came of it. 找 (zhǎo) is to search; 找到 (zhǎodào) is to search-and-attain: to find. 听 is to listen; 听见 is to hear; 听懂 is to understand what you heard. The sentence 我找了半天，可是没找到 ("I looked for ages but didn't find it") is impossible to translate word-for-word into English precisely because English cannot say "find" without the success built in.</p>
      <p>This is the grammatical machine behind pairs a learner otherwise memorizes blindly: 看 (look) versus 看见 (see), 学 (study) versus 学会 (master), 买 (shop for) versus 买到 (get one's hands on). The complement slot takes verbs (见, 懂, 会) and adjectives (好, 错, 干净) alike, and the two syllables behave as one verb: aspect 了 follows the whole unit (吃完了饭), never splits it.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">六个高频补语 liù gè gāopín bǔyǔ · Six High-Frequency Results</span>
      <strong>完 wán (finished)</strong> → 吃完 eat up ; 做完 finish doing ; 说完 finish speaking<br>
      <strong>到 dào (attained)</strong> → 找到 find ; 买到 manage to buy ; 收到 receive ; 想到 think of<br>
      <strong>见 jiàn (perceived)</strong> → 看见 see ; 听见 hear ; 闻见 smell<br>
      <strong>懂 dǒng (understood)</strong> → 听懂 follow (speech) ; 看懂 follow (text)<br>
      <strong>好 hǎo (done properly)</strong> → 准备好 be ready ; 修好 fix ; 坐好 sit properly<br>
      <strong>错 cuò (wrongly)</strong> → 说错 misspeak ; 写错 miswrite ; 认错人 mistake someone's identity
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="完">
      <div class="scholar-label">补语的性格 · Each Complement's Personality</div>
      <p>A dozen complements do most of the work, and each has a personality worth knowing. 完 is pure completion and combines with almost anything consumable or performable. 到 marks attainment of a target and also extends to reaching places and times (走到学校, walk as far as school; 等到十点, wait until ten). 见 is perception registering, which is why 看见 and 听见 pair with the senses. 好 means the action reached a satisfactory, usable state: 饭做好了 announces dinner is ready, and a parent's 坐好! orders the wriggling child into proper position. 错 and its opposite 对 grade the outcome: 猜对了 (guessed right). Two more earn early adoption: 会 (mastery: 学会游泳, learn to swim, as opposed to merely studying it) and 开 (apart/open: 打开 open up, 分开 separate).</p>
    </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">否定与把字句</span>
      <span class="sh-py">fǒudìng yǔ bǎzìjù</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Negation, and Where 把 Fits</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="没">
      <div class="scholar-label">没 + 动结 · The Result That Didn't Happen</div>
      <p>Because a result either obtained or it did not, negation uses 没 (the realized-action negator) before the whole verb-result unit: 我没听懂 (I didn't understand), 作业还没做完 (the homework isn't finished yet). Putting 不 there is the classic learner error; 不 belongs to habitual or hypothetical statements and, crucially, to the potential form (听不懂, "unable to understand"), which this guide treats on its own page. The near-minimal pair is worth staring at once: 没听懂 reports a failure that happened; 听不懂 reports an inability.</p>
      <p>Resultatives also explain why the 把 construction exists. 把 fronts the object so the verb-plus-result can land on it as a verdict: 把作业做完 (get the homework done), 把门关好 (shut the door properly), 把字写错了 (wrote the character wrong). A 把 sentence without some complement or other elaboration after the verb feels amputated to native ears, and the resultative is the most common thing that fills the slot; the two patterns are roommates, and this guide's 把 page holds the lease.</p>
    </div>
