faq:
  - q: "Does Chinese have tenses?"
    a: "No. Mandarin verbs have exactly one form: 吃 chī is 'eat', 'ate', 'eats', and 'will eat'. Time is expressed by time words (昨天 yesterday, 明天 tomorrow), aspect particles (了 completion, 过 experience, 着 ongoing state), the progressive marker 在, and the modals 会 and 要 for the future. The verb itself never changes."
  - q: "How do you express past tense in Chinese?"
    a: "Combine a past time word with the verb, and add 了 le if the action was completed: 我昨天买了一本书 ('I bought a book yesterday'). Use 过 guo for past experience (我去过中国, 'I have been to China'), and 是…的 to focus on the details of a known past event (我是坐飞机来的, 'I came by plane'). To say something did not happen, use 没 méi and drop 了: 我没买."
  - q: "What is the difference between 了 (le) and 过 (guo)?"
    a: "了 marks completion of a specific action: 我去了北京 means 'I went to Beijing' (a particular trip, likely recent or still relevant). 过 marks experience at some indefinite point: 我去过北京 means 'I have been to Beijing at some time in my life'. 了 answers 'what happened?'; 过 answers 'has this ever happened?'."
  - q: "How do you express the future in Chinese?"
    a: "Four main devices: 会 huì for predictions (明天会下雨, 'it will rain tomorrow'), 要 yào for intention or imminence (我要回家, 'I'm going home'), 快要…了 / 就要…了 for 'about to' (火车快要开了, 'the train is about to leave'), and plain time words with a bare verb (我明天去上海, 'I go to Shanghai tomorrow'), which is fully grammatical and very common."

      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">时态</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">shítài</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">Mandarin verbs never change form: time words, 了, 过, 着, 在, and 会 do the work English does with conjugation.</p>

    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源与逻辑</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán yǔ luójí</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; The Logic of a Tenseless Language</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="时">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>时态 shítài</strong> = 时 shí (time, season) + 态 tài (state, form). The word is a modern coinage, built to translate the European grammatical concept of tense. Chinese grammarians never needed it before contact with Latin-modeled grammar: the first systematic Chinese grammar, Mǎ Jiànzhōng's 马氏文通 (Mǎshì Wéntōng, 1898), had to invent vocabulary for categories like tense precisely because the language itself does not mark them.</p>
      <p>The distinction that unlocks the whole system: <strong>tense</strong> locates an event relative to the moment of speaking (before, during, after now); <strong>aspect</strong> describes the internal shape of the event (completed, ongoing, experienced). English fuses both into verb endings. Mandarin drops tense entirely and keeps a precise aspect system: 了 le for completion, 过 guo for experience, 着 zhe for sustained states, 在 zài for actions in progress. Whether the event sits in the past, present, or future is left to time words and context.</p>
      <p>This is why 吃 chī covers "eat", "ate", "eats", and "will eat". 我昨天吃饺子 and 我明天吃饺子 use the identical verb; 昨天 (yesterday) and 明天 (tomorrow) carry all the temporal information. For an English speaker the reflex to "conjugate" 吃 has to be unlearned. Nothing is missing from the Chinese sentence, the time-marking labor has simply moved out of the verb and into the adverbs.</p>
      <p>The payoff for learners is real: there are no irregular verbs, no conjugation tables, no agreement. The cost is a different discipline: choosing the right aspect particle, and placing time words where the grammar demands. Both are learnable systems, and both are laid out below.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">时间词位置 shíjiāncí wèizhì · Where time words go</span>
      <strong>Subject + Time + Verb</strong> → 我<strong>明天</strong>去上海 (I'm going to Shanghai tomorrow)<br>
      <strong>Time + Subject + Verb</strong> → <strong>明天</strong>我去上海 (same meaning, time emphasized)<br>
      ✗ <strong>Never sentence-final:</strong> ✗ 我去上海明天 (English word order does not transfer)
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="先">
      <div class="scholar-label">学者洞见 xuézhě dòngjiàn · Time before the verb, always</div>
      <p>The one hard rule: temporal information precedes the verb. Time words sit either before or after the subject, and both orders are correct; the fronted version (明天我去) foregrounds the time frame, the default version (我明天去) states it neutrally. English speakers produce the ungrammatical ✗我去上海明天 for months because English happily ends sentences with "tomorrow". Mandarin never does.</p>
      <p>Larger units come before smaller ones, the same big-to-small logic as Chinese addresses and dates: 去年三月五号早上八点 (last year, March 5th, 8 a.m.). Once a time frame is established, it persists until changed, so a narrative can set 昨天 once and run a dozen tenseless verbs inside it without repeating the time word.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="past"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">过去</span>
      <span class="sh-py">guòqù</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Expressing the Past</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">动词 + 了</span>
          <span class="card-py">le</span>
          <span class="card-en">completed action, the closest thing to a past tense</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">完成 completion</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          了 after the verb marks the action as completed. With a past time word it produces what English renders as simple past: 我昨天买了一本书 (I bought a book yesterday). But 了 marks completion, not pastness: 明天吃了饭再走 (leave tomorrow after finishing dinner) puts 了 in a future clause. The full anatomy of this particle, including sentence-final 了 and the double-了 construction, has its own entry: 了.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我昨天看了一部电影。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ zuótiān kàn le yī bù diànyǐng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I watched a movie yesterday.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他吃了早饭就去上班了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā chī le zǎofàn jiù qù shàngbān le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He went to work right after eating breakfast.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">动词 + 过</span>
          <span class="card-py">guo</span>
          <span class="card-en">past experience, "have ever done"</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">经历 experience</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          过 marks that the subject has undergone the experience at least once, at some indefinite point. 我去过中国 (I have been to China) says nothing about when; it registers the experience itself. Contrast 我去了中国, a specific completed trip. Negation keeps 过 but uses 没: 我没去过中国 (I have never been to China). Full treatment in the 过 entry.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你吃过北京烤鸭吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ chī guo Běijīng kǎoyā ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Have you ever had Peking duck?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们以前见过面。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen yǐqián jiàn guo miàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We've met before.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 了 vs. 过</span>
          去了北京 answers "what did you do?" (a specific trip). 去过北京 answers "have you ever?" (a life experience). English "went" vs. "have been" catches most of the difference, and the mapping is reliable enough to lean on.
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">是……的</span>
          <span class="card-py">shì … de</span>
          <span class="card-en">spotlighting the details of a known past event</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">焦点 focus</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          When the event itself is already established and the question is when, where, how, or with whom it happened, Mandarin uses 是……的 instead of 了: 我是坐飞机来的 (I came by plane; that I came is old news, the plane is the point). Questions about past details take the same frame: 你是什么时候到的？(When did you arrive?). The construction has its own entry: 是……的.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他是去年来北京的。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā shì qùnián lái Běijīng de.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">It was last year that he came to Beijing.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是在哪儿买的？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì zài nǎr mǎi de?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Where was this bought?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">刚 · 已经 · 以前</span>
          <span class="card-py">gāng · yǐjīng · yǐqián</span>
          <span class="card-en">just now · already · formerly, the past-pointing adverbs</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adv">副词 adverbs</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          刚 gāng (just now) places an action in the immediate past: 他刚走 (he just left). 已经 yǐjīng (already) pairs with 了 to mark a completed state: 我已经吃了 (I've already eaten). 以前 yǐqián (before, formerly) frames a whole clause in the past: 我以前住在上海 (I used to live in Shanghai). Each of the first two has its own entry: 刚 and 已经.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">电影已经开始了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Diànyǐng yǐjīng kāishǐ le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The movie has already started.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我以前不喜欢喝茶，现在喜欢了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ yǐqián bù xǐhuan hē chá, xiànzài xǐhuan le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I didn't use to like tea; now I do.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">现在</span>
      <span class="sh-py">xiànzài</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Expressing the Present</span>

    <div class="cards">

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">光杆动词</span>
          <span class="card-py">guānggǎn dòngcí</span>
          <span class="card-en">the bare verb: habits, facts, general truths</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">习惯 habitual</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          The unmarked verb is the default for habitual actions and standing facts, usually anchored by a frequency word: 我每天喝咖啡 (I drink coffee every day), 他住在北京 (he lives in Beijing), 地球绕太阳转 (the earth orbits the sun). No particle needed; adding 了 here would wrongly signal a completed event.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我每天早上六点起床。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ měitiān zǎoshang liù diǎn qǐchuáng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I get up at six every morning.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">她在一家医院工作。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā zài yī jiā yīyuàn gōngzuò.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">She works at a hospital.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">在 / 正在 + 动词 (……呢)</span>
          <span class="card-py">zài / zhèngzài … (ne)</span>
          <span class="card-en">action in progress right now</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">进行 progressive</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          在 before the verb marks an action in progress: 我在吃饭 (I'm eating). 正在 sharpens the "right at this moment" reading: 他正在开会 (he's in a meeting right now). Sentence-final 呢 can soften or replace either: 我吃饭呢. The progressive combines freely with past time words, 昨天晚上八点我在看书 (at 8 last night I was reading), which is how Mandarin does "was doing". See the 在 entry.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你在做什么？我在写报告。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ zài zuò shénme? Wǒ zài xiě bàogào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">What are you doing? I'm writing a report.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">别打电话，他正在睡觉呢。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Bié dǎ diànhuà, tā zhèngzài shuìjiào ne.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Don't call, he's sleeping right now.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">动词 + 着</span>
          <span class="card-py">zhe</span>
          <span class="card-en">sustained state: the door stands open</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">持续 continuous state</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          着 marks a state that holds: 门开着 (the door is open), 墙上挂着一幅画 (a painting hangs on the wall), 他穿着一件红衣服 (he's wearing a red shirt). Where 在 tracks an activity unfolding, 着 freezes the result and holds it. The contrast is sharpest with verbs like 穿: 他在穿衣服 (he is putting clothes on) vs. 他穿着衣服 (he has clothes on). Full entry: 着.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">桌子上放着一本书。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhuōzi shàng fàng zhe yī běn shū.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">A book is lying on the table.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他笑着说："没关系。"</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā xiào zhe shuō: "Méi guānxi."</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He said with a smile, "It's fine."</div>
          </div>
        </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">会</span>
          <span class="card-py">huì</span>
          <span class="card-en">prediction: what will happen</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">预测 prediction</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          会 asserts that something will happen: 明天会下雨 (it will rain tomorrow), 他会来的 (he'll come, don't worry). The sentence-final 的 adds reassurance. This future sense lives alongside 会's "learned ability" sense (我会游泳, I can swim); the three-way contrast with 能 and 可以 has its own entry: 会 · 能 · 可以.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">别担心，一切都会好的。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Bié dānxīn, yīqiè dōu huì hǎo de.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Don't worry, everything will be fine.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你觉得他会同意吗？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ juéde tā huì tóngyì ma?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Do you think he'll agree?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">要 · 打算 · 准备</span>
          <span class="card-py">yào · dǎsuàn · zhǔnbèi</span>
          <span class="card-en">intention and plan: what someone is going to do</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">打算 intention</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          要 before a verb marks intention or imminence: 我要回家 (I'm going home / I want to go home; context decides). 打算 dǎsuàn (to plan) and 准备 zhǔnbèi (to prepare, to be about to) make the plan explicit: 我打算明年去中国 (I plan to go to China next year). Where 会 predicts the world, 要 and 打算 announce the subject's own agenda.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们要在这儿住三天。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen yào zài zhèr zhù sān tiān.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We're going to stay here for three days.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你打算什么时候结婚？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ dǎsuàn shénme shíhou jiéhūn?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">When do you plan to get married?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">快要……了 · 就要……了</span>
          <span class="card-py">kuàiyào … le · jiùyào … le</span>
          <span class="card-en">imminence: about to happen</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">即将 imminent</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          快要……了 (or 快……了, 就要……了) marks an event on the verge of happening: 火车快要开了 (the train is about to leave), 天快黑了 (it's getting dark). This is the change-of-state 了, announcing that the new situation is imminent. 就要……了 accepts a specific time word in front (八点就要上课了); 快要……了 does not.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">春节快要到了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Chūnjié kuàiyào dào le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Chinese New Year is almost here.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">飞机八点就要起飞了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Fēijī bā diǎn jiùyào qǐfēi le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The plane takes off at eight.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">时间词 + 光杆动词</span>
          <span class="card-py">shíjiāncí + verb</span>
          <span class="card-en">the plain future: tomorrow I go</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">默认 default</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          A future time word with a bare verb is complete and idiomatic: 我明天去上海 (I'm going to Shanghai tomorrow), 下个月放假 (vacation starts next month). No marker is required at all. Learners overuse 会 and 要 here; native speech leaves scheduled, settled future events unmarked, exactly like English "the flight leaves at nine".
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们下星期考试。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen xià xīngqī kǎoshì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We have an exam next week.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他后天回国。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā hòutiān huíguó.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He returns to his country the day after tomorrow.</div>
          </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="negation"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">否定与时间</span>
      <span class="sh-py">fǒudìng yǔ shíjiān</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Negating in Time: 不 vs. 没</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">两个否定词 liǎng gè fǒudìngcí · The two negators split by time</span>
      <strong>没 (有)</strong> → negates completed/experienced events → 我<strong>没</strong>去 (I didn't go) · 我<strong>没</strong>去过 (I've never been)<br>
      <strong>不</strong> → negates habits, states, volition, the future → 我<strong>不</strong>喝咖啡 (I don't drink coffee) · 我明天<strong>不</strong>去 (I'm not going tomorrow)<br>
      ✗ <strong>没 + 了 never combine:</strong> ✗ 我没吃了 → 我没吃
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="没">
      <div class="scholar-label">学者洞见 xuézhě dòngjiàn · Negation is where tense hides</div>
      <p>Mandarin's clearest time distinction lives in its negators. 没 méi denies that an event happened or was completed, so it handles everything English negates with "didn't" or "haven't": 我没看 (I didn't watch it), 他还没来 (he hasn't come yet). 不 bù denies everything else: habits (我不抽烟, I don't smoke), states (我不累, I'm not tired), willingness (他不去, he won't go), and the future (明天不上班, no work tomorrow).</p>
      <p>The trap: 没 replaces 了 rather than combining with it. 我吃了 flips to 我没吃, with 了 gone. Keeping 了 after 没 is among the most persistent beginner errors, and dropping it instantly makes the sentence sound right. The full contrast, including 没有 as a verb of non-possession, is in the 没有 entry.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="timewords"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">时间词</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shíjiāncí</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Time Words: the Real Tense System</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">昨天 · 今天 · 明天</span>
          <span class="card-py">zuótiān · jīntiān · míngtiān</span>
          <span class="card-en">the day series, and its symmetric siblings</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">日 days</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          The day series runs 前天 (day before yesterday) · 昨天 · 今天 · 明天 · 后天 (day after tomorrow). Years mirror it exactly: 前年 · 去年 · 今年 · 明年 · 后年. Weeks and months use 上/下 (above/below) instead: 上个星期 (last week), 下个月 (next month). In the Chinese spatial metaphor, the past sits above and in front (前), the future below and behind (后), the reverse of the English "looking forward to the future".
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">前天下雨，昨天晴天，今天又下雨了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qiántiān xià yǔ, zuótiān qíngtiān, jīntiān yòu xià yǔ le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">It rained the day before yesterday, was clear yesterday, and is raining again today.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">以前 · 现在 · 以后</span>
          <span class="card-py">yǐqián · xiànzài · yǐhòu</span>
          <span class="card-en">before · now · after: the relative frame</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">相对 relative</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          以前 and 以后 attach to any reference point: 三年以前 (three years ago), 吃饭以后 (after eating), or stand alone to mean "formerly" and "from now on". 的时候 de shíhou (when …) frames simultaneity: 我小的时候 (when I was little). These clause-level frames, placed before the verb like all time expressions, let Mandarin build every relative-time meaning English handles with "before/after/when" clauses.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">毕业以后，我想去国外工作。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Bìyè yǐhòu, wǒ xiǎng qù guówài gōngzuò.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">After graduating, I want to work abroad.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">来中国以前，他没用过筷子。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Lái Zhōngguó yǐqián, tā méi yòng guo kuàizi.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Before coming to China, he had never used chopsticks.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <span class="cy-cn">光阴似箭</span>
        <span class="cy-py">guāng yīn sì jiàn</span>
        <span class="cy-en">time flies like an arrow</span>
        <span class="cy-note">光阴 (light and shade, i.e. the passage of days) + 似箭 (like an arrow). Often paired with 日月如梭 "sun and moon like a weaver's shuttle." The standard sigh at reunions and graduations: 光阴似箭，一转眼十年过去了 "Time flew; ten years passed in the blink of an eye."</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">未雨绸缪</span>
        <span class="cy-py">wèi yǔ chóu móu</span>
        <span class="cy-en">bind the window before it rains, prepare in advance</span>
        <span class="cy-note">From the Book of Odes (诗经·豳风·鸱鸮): a bird repairs its nest before the storm. 未 (not yet) + 雨 (rain) + 绸缪 (to bind tightly). The idiom is the future tense as virtue: act now for what has not yet happened. Common in business and policy speech.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">事过境迁</span>
        <span class="cy-py">shì guò jìng qiān</span>
        <span class="cy-en">the affair has passed and the circumstances have changed</span>
        <span class="cy-note">事 (the matter) 过 (has passed) 境 (circumstances) 迁 (have shifted). Note 过 in its full verbal sense, the same morpheme that grammaticalized into the experiential particle. Used to wave off old grievances: 事过境迁，别再提了 "It's all in the past, don't bring it up again."</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">来日方长</span>
        <span class="cy-py">lái rì fāng cháng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">the days to come are long, there will be time</span>
        <span class="cy-note">来日 (days that are coming) + 方长 (are just now long). A consolation and a promise: no need to rush or grieve a parting, the future holds more chances. 来 "coming" for the future mirrors 去 "gone" for the past, the same motion metaphor as 去年 (last year).</span>
