faq:
  - q: "What is an A-not-A question in Chinese?"
    a: "A yes-no question formed by stating the verb or adjective in positive and negative back to back: 你去不去? ('are you going or not going?'), 好不好? ('good or not?'). It replaces 吗 and cannot combine with it: 你去不去吗 is ungrammatical."
  - q: "How do you abbreviate A-not-A with two-syllable verbs?"
    a: "Repeat only the first syllable before 不: 喜欢 becomes 喜不喜欢, 知道 becomes 知不知道, 可以 becomes 可不可以. The full form 喜欢不喜欢 is also correct but heavier."
  - q: "When should I use A-not-A instead of 吗?"
    a: "Both are neutral yes-no questions, and in most contexts they are interchangeable. A-not-A can sound slightly more direct or insistent, and it is the required form after 你说 ('tell me: …'). 吗 is the only choice when the question carries an assumption, as in 你不去吗? ('you're not going?')."

      <h1 class="topic-hero-title">正反问</h1>
      <span class="topic-hero-title-py">zhèngfǎn wèn</span>
      <p class="topic-hero-desc">Chinese asks yes-no questions by offering both answers: 去不去 is "going, not going?", and the pattern replaces 吗 with built-in neutrality.</p>
    </header>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="pattern"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">去不去</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qù bu qù</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Pattern</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">正 + 不 + 反 · Positive, Negative, Question</span>
      <strong>verbs</strong> → 你去不去? (going or not?) ; 你吃不吃? (eating or not?)<br>
      <strong>adjectives</strong> → 累不累? (tired?) ; 好不好吃? (tasty?)<br>
      <strong>是</strong> → 是不是? : the universal confirmation-seeker<br>
      <strong>有</strong> → 有没有? : with 没, not 不, since 有 negates with 没<br>
      <strong>modals</strong> → 会不会? 能不能? 可不可以?<br>
      <em>The middle 不 goes neutral-tone and quick: qù-bu-qù, three beats</em>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="问">
      <div class="scholar-label">把两个答案都摆上桌 · Both Answers on the Table</div>
      <p>The <strong>正反问</strong> ("positive-negative question") forms a yes-no question by presenting the two possible answers and letting the listener pick: 你去不去? is literally "you go not go?" No question particle is needed or allowed; 去不去吗 is a beginner's double-marking error, since the pattern is already a question. Answers echo one of the offered poles rather than saying yes or no: 去 ("going") or 不去 ("not going"), which is why Chinese famously has no single word for yes; the verb itself does the confirming.</p>
      <p>The object can sit inside or after the frame: 你吃不吃苹果? and 你吃苹果不吃? both work, with the first now dominant in the standard language. For completed actions the negative pole switches to 没: 你吃了没有? ("have you eaten?", the greeting), often clipped in speech to 你吃了没?, which is the A-not-A pattern wearing streetwear.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="workhorses"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">是不是、有没有</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shìbushì yǒuméiyǒu</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Workhorses</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="是">
      <div class="scholar-label">缩短的艺术 · The Art of the Abbreviation</div>
      <p>Two members of the family do outsized duty. <strong>是不是</strong> seeks confirmation of a whole proposition and can slide around the sentence: 你是不是明天走? / 你明天是不是走? / 你明天走，是不是? all ask "you're leaving tomorrow, right?", with the final position shading toward a tag question. <strong>有没有</strong> owns possession and existence (有没有问题? any questions?) and, before a verb, past experience: 你有没有去过北京? ("have you been to Beijing?"), a construction southern Mandarin favors and the standard language has fully absorbed.</p>
      <p>Two-syllable verbs and adjectives abbreviate by repeating only their first syllable: 喜不喜欢 (like it or not?), 知不知道 (do you know?), 可不可以 (may I?), 高不高兴 (happy or not?). The full forms (喜欢不喜欢) remain correct but feel heavier, like unabbreviated legalese. The abbreviation is obligatory listening vocabulary: natives produce 喜不喜欢 at speed, and a learner parsing syllable-by-syllable hears "like-not-like" and must not go looking for a verb 喜.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="vs-ma"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">与"吗"之别</span>
      <span class="sh-py">yǔ ma zhī bié</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Versus 吗</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="吗">
      <div class="scholar-label">中立、追问、预设 · Neutral, Pressing, Presuming</div>
      <p>In most daily exchanges 你去吗? and 你去不去? are interchangeable, and learners can treat them so. The differences live at the edges. A-not-A is structurally neutral, both answers explicitly offered, which makes it the form of surveys, forms, and genuine open inquiry; repeated, it turns insistent, the parent's 你到底去不去? ("are you going or NOT?") where 到底 tightens the screws. 吗, by contrast, can carry loaded propositions: 你不去吗? ("you're not going?") questions a negative assumption, something A-not-A cannot do, since its poles are fixed. And after verbs of asking, the embedded question must be A-not-A: 他问我去不去 ("he asked whether I was going"), never 他问我去吗.</p>
      <p>Regional texture, for the curious ear: Taiwan Mandarin and southern speech reach for 有没有 and V-了-没 more freely, while 吗 leans slightly more northern and written. None of it will get a learner misunderstood; all of it is audible within one episode of any Chinese drama.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">好不好</span>
          <span class="card-py">hǎo bu hǎo</span>
          <span class="card-en">okay? — the agreement-seeking tag</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">Tag 附加问 fùjiāwèn</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          Appended to a proposal, it softens a request into a negotiation: 我们明天再说，好不好? Its siblings 行不行 and 可不可以 climb the same ladder of politeness; children deploy 好不好 serially until granted.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">周末我们去爬山，好不好？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhōumò wǒmen qù páshān, hǎo bu hǎo?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Let's go hiking this weekend, okay?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">是不是</span>
          <span class="card-py">shìbushì</span>
          <span class="card-en">is it that…? — the confirmation frame</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">Frame 句式 jùshì</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          Wraps any proposition for confirmation, and placed sentence-finally becomes "right?". Also the gentle accusation opener: 你是不是忘了? ("you forgot, didn't you?"), where the both-answers structure politely pretends the case is open.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你是不是把钥匙忘在家里了？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ shìbushì bǎ yàoshi wàng zài jiā lǐ le?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">You left the keys at home, didn't you?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">有没有</span>
          <span class="card-py">yǒuméiyǒu</span>
          <span class="card-en">is there…? have you…?</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">Frame 句式 jùshì</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          The existence-and-experience question. Before nouns: 有没有时间? (got time?). Before verbs: 有没有看过? (ever seen it?). The negative pole is 没 because 有 is the one verb 不 refuses to negate.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你有没有吃过北京烤鸭？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ yǒuméiyǒu chī guo Běijīng kǎoyā?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Have you ever had Peking duck?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">对不对</span>
          <span class="card-py">duì bu duì</span>
          <span class="card-en">right? correct?</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">Tag 附加问 fùjiāwèn</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          The teacher's tag and the explainer's checkpoint, punctuating reasoning the way English "right?" does. The listener's murmured 对对对 in reply is the sound of a Chinese conversation agreeing with itself.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">先坐地铁再换公交，对不对？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Xiān zuò dìtiě zài huàn gōngjiāo, duì bu duì?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Take the metro first, then change to the bus, right?</div>
