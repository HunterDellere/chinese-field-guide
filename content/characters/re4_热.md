    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="热">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>The structure stacks two layers: 热 = 执 + 灬. The four dots along the bottom, 灬, are the squashed-flat form of 火 (huǒ, fire), the semantic radical that places this character squarely in the domain of burning and warmth. Above it sits 执 (zhí, to grasp), which carries the sound rather than the meaning, marking 热 as a classic pictophonetic compound.</p>
      <p>The seal-script ancestor was busier than the modern glyph. It paired the fire element with a figure holding something over the flames, the older graph 埶 that fed both 执 and 势. Read the picture literally: a hand tending a fire, and the warmth that rises off it. As the script regularized through the clerical reform, the upper element simplified to today's 执 and the fire collapsed into its four-dot variant.</p>
      <p>From the fire pit the meaning fanned outward. First the plain physical sense, hot to the touch, the heat of summer, water near boiling. Then the figurative reach: a fever burning in the body (发热 fārè), a crowd's energy boiling over (热闹 rènao), a person's ardor running high (热情 rèqíng). Modern Mandarin pushes it one step further to mean popular or trending, the way English calls a song or a stock hot. The same fire heats a kettle, a fever, and a fashion.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps the character as 熱 (netsu, atsui), preserving the fuller fire base in its standard form, and Korean reads it 열 (yeol) in compounds like 열정 (yeoljeong), the direct cognate of 热情.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">温度</span><span class="sh-py">wēndù</span><span class="sh-en">Heat &amp; Water</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>

        <div class="card-def">热 (hot) plus 水 (shuǐ, water). The everyday word for hot water, the staple drink offered to a guest and the cure a Chinese parent prescribes for almost anything.</div>
        <div class="examples">

        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">热</span><span class="card-py">rè</span><span class="card-en">to be hot</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">On its own 热 is the bare adjective for hot weather or a hot object. It needs no 是; the character itself is the predicate, often softened or intensified by 很 (hěn), 太 (tài), or 真 (zhēn).</div>

        </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="warmth"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">人情</span><span class="sh-py">rénqíng</span><span class="sh-en">Warmth &amp; Bustle</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="热">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义延伸 yǔyì yánshēn · Sense Extension</div>
      <p>Carry the fire into human company and it becomes warmth of feeling and warmth of a crowd. 热情 is the heat of personal ardor and hospitality; 热闹 is the heat of a packed, lively, noisy scene. Both turn raw temperature into social energy, the difference between a cold formal room and a wedding banquet roaring with toasts.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-violet">

        <div class="card-def">热 (hot) plus 情 (qíng, feeling): hot feeling. As an adjective it praises someone warm and welcoming; as a noun it names the enthusiasm itself.</div>
        <div class="examples">

          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他对工作充满热情。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā duì gōngzuò chōngmǎn rèqíng.</div><div class="ex-en">He is full of enthusiasm for his work.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 热情 vs. 热心</span> 热情 is warmth of manner and feeling; 热心 (rèxīn) is warmth shown through action, a willingness to help. A 热情 host greets you warmly; a 热心 neighbor carries your bags upstairs.</div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="热" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a blacksmith's hand, 执, gripping tongs over a forge, and under it four glowing embers, 灬, the four dots of flame. Heat shimmers off the iron. That single image holds the whole character: hand-on-fire, the upper 执 doing the gripping and lending the sound, the lower four dots burning.</p>
      <p>Now let the forge throw its heat in three directions. Toward objects it is 热水 and 加热, water and food brought up to temperature. Toward people it is 热情 and 热闹, the warmth of a welcome and the buzz of a crowd. Toward the body it is 发热, the fever that makes a forehead burn. One fire, three kinds of heat.</p>
      <p>When you reach for the word, hear the falling fourth tone drop like a hammer onto hot iron: rè, the clang of 趁热打铁, striking while it's hot.</p>
