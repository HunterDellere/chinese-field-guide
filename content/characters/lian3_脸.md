    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="脸">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>脸 splits cleanly down the middle: 脸 = ⺼ + 佥. The left is ⺼, the flesh-moon radical (肉 in its squeezed-in writing form), which marks body parts across the whole lexicon, from 肝 (gān, liver) to 腿 (tuǐ, leg). The right is 佥 (qiān), here a phonetic component carrying the sound. This is a textbook pictophonetic build: meat on the left tells you the domain, 佥 on the right tells you how to say it.</p>
      <p>The phonetic 佥 belongs to a family of characters that once shared an initial cluster, which is why its modern descendants scatter across readings like jiǎn (检, 捡), liǎn (脸), and qiān (签). The lips and teeth that produced those sounds drifted apart over the centuries, but the written marriage of flesh plus 佥 froze 脸 at liǎn.</p>
      <p>The earliest sense was narrow: 脸 named the part of the face below the eyes, the cheeks where rouge sat and blushes rose. Classical texts more often used 面 (miàn) for the face as a flat surface. 脸 was the warmer, fleshier word, and over time it swallowed the whole front of the head. From there it made the leap every body word in Chinese seems to make, from skin to standing: the 脸 you wash in the morning is the same 脸 you can lose in public, the visible flesh that society reads as a measure of your worth.</p>
      <p>Japanese kept 顔 and 面 for the face and rarely borrowed 脸, which is one reason 脸 feels distinctly modern-Chinese in flavor, a colloquial word that climbed from cheek to character to reputation entirely on home ground.</p>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="expression"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">神色</span><span class="sh-py">shénsè</span><span class="sh-en">The Face That Shows</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">脸色</span><span class="card-py">liǎnsè</span><span class="card-en">complexion; facial expression; mood read off the face</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">脸 (face) + 色 (color, look). Literally the color of the face, used both for physical complexion and for the mood it betrays. To 看脸色 (kàn liǎnsè) is to read someone's expression and act accordingly, a core social skill.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他今天脸色不太好，是不是病了？</div><div class="ex-py">Tā jīntiān liǎnsè bú tài hǎo, shì bú shì bìng le?</div><div class="ex-en">He doesn't look well today, is he sick?</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">在公司里，谁都得看老板的脸色。</div><div class="ex-py">Zài gōngsī lǐ, shéi dōu děi kàn lǎobǎn de liǎnsè.</div><div class="ex-en">At the company, everyone has to read the boss's mood.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">笑脸</span><span class="card-py">xiàoliǎn</span><span class="card-en">smiling face; a smile worn for others</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">笑 (laugh, smile) + 脸 (face). A face shaped into a smile, especially the welcoming one you put on for guests or customers. The opposite expression is 板着脸 (bǎnzhe liǎn), to hold the face stiff and stern.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">她总是带着笑脸迎接客人。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā zǒngshì dàizhe xiàoliǎn yíngjiē kèrén.</div><div class="ex-en">She always greets guests with a smile.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 笑脸 vs. 微笑</span> 笑脸 is the face as a whole worn smiling; 微笑 (wēixiào) is the act of smiling itself, the gentle curve of the mouth.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">红脸</span><span class="card-py">hóngliǎn</span><span class="card-en">to blush; to flush red (with anger or embarrassment)</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">红 (red) + 脸 (face). The cheeks going red, whether from shyness or from a flare of temper. 脸红 (liǎnhóng) reverses the order for the same blush; 红脸 also names the stern, blunt role someone plays in a good-cop bad-cop pairing.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">被这么一夸，她马上就脸红了。</div><div class="ex-py">Bèi zhème yì kuā, tā mǎshàng jiù liǎnhóng le.</div><div class="ex-en">Praised like that, she blushed at once.</div></div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="dignity"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">面子</span><span class="sh-py">miànzi</span><span class="sh-en">The Face You Keep</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="脸">
      <div class="scholar-label">文化洞见 wénhuà dòngjiàn · Cultural Insight</div>
      <p>In Chinese the face is also a social account. 脸 here means dignity, the standing you hold in the eyes of others. You can 丢脸 (lose face) through a public misstep, 给面子 (give face) by honoring someone in front of a crowd, or 不要脸 (have no shame) by acting past all decency. 脸 and its near-twin 面子 (miànzi) carry this weight: a single embarrassment in front of the wrong people costs more than the act itself, because the audience is the currency.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">丢脸</span><span class="card-py">diūliǎn</span><span class="card-en">to lose face; to be disgraced</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">丢 (to lose, drop) + 脸 (face). To suffer public embarrassment, to have your standing slip in front of others. The image is literal: dignity dropped like a coin in the street.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">考试不及格让他觉得很丢脸。</div><div class="ex-py">Kǎoshì bù jígé ràng tā juéde hěn diūliǎn.</div><div class="ex-en">Failing the exam made him feel humiliated.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">别在客人面前吵架，太丢脸了。</div><div class="ex-py">Bié zài kèrén miànqián chǎojià, tài diūliǎn le.</div><div class="ex-en">Don't argue in front of the guests, it's too embarrassing.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 丢脸 vs. 丢人</span> 丢脸 and 丢人 (diūrén) are near-synonyms for losing face; 丢人 leans slightly more colloquial and personal, 丢脸 a touch more about social standing.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">翻脸</span><span class="card-py">fānliǎn</span><span class="card-en">to turn hostile; to fall out with someone</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">翻 (to flip, turn over) + 脸 (face). To flip the face from friendly to cold in an instant, breaking off goodwill. The vivid version is 翻脸不认人 (fānliǎn bú rèn rén), to turn cold and pretend you never knew the person.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">为了一点小事，他们俩就翻脸了。</div><div class="ex-py">Wèile yìdiǎn xiǎoshì, tāmen liǎ jiù fānliǎn le.</div><div class="ex-en">Over one small thing, the two of them fell out.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">脸皮</span><span class="card-py">liǎnpí</span><span class="card-en">facial skin; (figurative) sense of shame; nerve</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">脸 (face) + 皮 (skin). The skin of the face, and by extension one's capacity for shame. 脸皮厚 (liǎnpí hòu), thick-skinned, means shameless or unembarrassable; 脸皮薄 (liǎnpí báo), thin-skinned, means easily mortified.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他脸皮太厚了，被拒绝了还接着问。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā liǎnpí tài hòu le, bèi jùjué le hái jiēzhe wèn.</div><div class="ex-en">He's so thick-skinned, he keeps asking even after being turned down.</div></div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="脸" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a butcher's slab at dawn, a slice of pale flesh (⺼) laid out under the light. Now stamp it with a maker's seal, the character 佥, pressed into the meat to certify whose it is. That stamped cut of flesh is your 脸: a piece of the body that the world reads like a label.</p>
      <p>Hold that double image and every compound falls into place. The flesh side gives you the morning rituals, 洗脸 at the basin, the rosy 脸蛋 of a child, the 脸色 a doctor checks. The stamped-seal side gives you the social ledger, where you can 丢脸 (drop the seal in the street), 翻脸 (flip it over and show a cold blank back), or wear 脸皮 thick enough that no stamp embarrasses you.</p>
      <p>The cheeks redden, the seal smudges, the standing slips: in Chinese the meat and the mark are one word, which is why a blush and a disgrace both live in 脸.</p>
