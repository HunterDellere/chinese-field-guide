    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="化">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>化 is one of the oldest and most economical pictures of change in the script. The left side is 亻 (the standing-person form of 人 rén), a figure upright on its feet. The right side is 匕, which in this character is not the later "spoon" or "dagger" but a person drawn upside down, head where the feet should be. Set the two together and you have a single image: one person right-side up beside the same person inverted. The whole glyph reads as a body in the act of turning over, of becoming its own opposite. Change is not abstract here; it is one figure flipping into another.</p>
      <p>Because of this, 化 carried from the start the sense of a complete transformation of one state into another: the living into the dead (化 was an early euphemism for dying, 物化 wùhuà = to pass away), the raw into the cooked, the solid into the liquid, the foreign into the assimilated. The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses the character around this idea of turning and transforming. What unites every later sense is the picture in the bones: not surface variation but a turning-over, X genuinely becoming Y.</p>
      <p>The radical of record is 匕, the inverted figure that supplies the meaning; the standing 亻 on the left completes the pair. 化 is best read not as one part carrying sound and another carrying sense, but as a compound of two human figures whose relationship, upright against inverted, is itself the meaning. This is why dictionaries file 化 under 匕 rather than under 人.</p>
      <p>From this single glyph the language built an entire machinery of transformation. 化 became the standard Chinese rendering of the European suffix -ize and -ification (现代化 modernization, 工业化 industrialization), the name for the science of how substances turn into one another (化学 chemistry), the verb for melting and digesting, and the deep word for the slow turning of a people toward refinement (文化 culture, 教化 to civilize). Few characters do so much work.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── THE -IZE SUFFIX PATTERN ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="patterns"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">构词</span>
      <span class="sh-py">gòucí</span>
      <span class="sh-en">化 as the -ize Suffix: The Most Productive Pattern</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">构词规律 gòucí guīlǜ · Pattern: [noun / adjective] + 化 → "to make into / cause to become" (verb or abstract noun)</span>
      <strong>现代化</strong> xiàndàihuà : modernization; to modernize (现代 modern + 化)<br>
      <strong>工业化</strong> gōngyèhuà : industrialization (工业 industry + 化)<br>
      <strong>国际化</strong> guójìhuà : internationalization (国际 international + 化)<br>
      <strong>美化</strong> měihuà : to beautify (美 beautiful + 化)<br>
      <strong>简化</strong> jiǎnhuà : to simplify (简 simple + 化)<br>
      <strong>绿化</strong> lǜhuà : to make green; afforestation, planting trees (绿 green + 化)<br>
      <strong>自动化</strong> zìdònghuà : automation (自动 automatic + 化)<br>
      <strong>恶化</strong> èhuà : to worsen, to deteriorate (恶 bad + 化)<br>
      Appended to a noun or adjective, 化 turns it into a process verb meaning "to cause to become that thing" or the abstract noun naming that process. The pattern is fully productive and is the workhorse of modern technical, political, and academic Chinese: nearly any concept can be made into a verb of transformation by suffixing 化. This is the direct counterpart of English -ize and -ification, and most twentieth-century coinages took this shape.
    </div>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">变化</span>
          <span class="card-py">biànhuà</span>
          <span class="card-en">change; transformation; variation</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          变 biàn (to change) + 化 huà (to transform; to dissolve into something new). The most general and most frequent word for change in modern Mandarin. 变 supplies the sense of becoming different; 化 adds the sense of a gradual dissolving and reconstituting. Together they name change as an observable phenomenon: something that can be measured, tracked, and discussed.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这几年城市变化很大。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jǐ nián chéngshì biànhuà hěn dà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The city has changed a great deal over the past few years.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">天气变化无常。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tiānqì biànhuà wúcháng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The weather changes unpredictably.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他的态度发生了变化。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā de tàidù fāshēng le biànhuà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">A change came over his attitude.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">语法 yǔfǎ · Grammar</span>
          发生变化 (fāshēng biànhuà), "undergo a change," is the standard nominal frame; you do not say 有变化 for an ongoing process. 千变万化 qiānbiàn wànhuà describes something endlessly changeable.

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">转化</span>
          <span class="card-py">zhuǎnhuà</span>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          转 zhuǎn (to turn; to shift) + 化 huà (to transform). 转化 names a conversion across a boundary: one form, type, or state turning into another. It is the standard word in science, philosophy, and economics for systematic conversion, the conditions under which A becomes B. Where 变化 is change in general, 转化 is directed conversion with a starting form and a destination form.

        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">

            <div class="ex-en">Energy can be converted from one form into another.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们要把压力转化为动力。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen yào bǎ yālì zhuǎnhuà wéi dònglì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We should turn pressure into motivation.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">矛盾在一定条件下会相互转化。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Máodùn zài yīdìng tiáojiàn xià huì xiānghù zhuǎnhuà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Under certain conditions, opposites convert into each other.</div>
          </div>
        </div>

      <!-- 化为 -->
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">化为</span>
          <span class="card-py">huàwéi</span>
          <span class="card-en">to turn into; to become; to be reduced to</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          化 huà (to transform) + 为 wéi (to be; to become), a literary verb of becoming. 化为 marks a complete turning of one thing into another, often dramatic and frequently with a sense of dissolution or loss. It is more formal and more vivid than 变成, and is the natural choice for figurative ruin (化为灰烬, reduced to ashes) or for transmutation in poetry and myth.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">大火过后，房子化为灰烬。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Dàhuǒ guòhòu, fángzi huàwéi huījìn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">After the fire, the house was reduced to ashes.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他的愤怒最终化为沉默。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā de fènnù zuìzhōng huàwéi chénmò.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">His anger eventually turned into silence.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">所有的努力都化为泡影。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Suǒyǒu de nǔlì dōu huàwéi pàoyǐng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">All the effort came to nothing.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 化为 vs 变成</span>
          变成 biànchéng is the neutral, everyday "turn into" and takes a plain noun destination. 化为 is literary and weighted, favoring images of dissolution, ruin, or transmutation; it would feel overwrought for ordinary changes that 变成 handles plainly.
        </div>

          <span class="card-py">wénhuà</span>
          <span class="card-en">culture; civilization; literacy</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          文 wén (writing; pattern; refinement; the civil) + 化 huà (to transform). The literal sense is "transformation through refinement," the slow shaping of a people by writing, ritual, and learning. 文化 is the modern word for culture in every sense (Chinese culture, pop culture, corporate culture), and in another register it means basic literacy: 有文化 means educated, 没文化 means unschooled.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">中国文化历史悠久。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhōngguó wénhuà lìshǐ yōujiǔ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Chinese culture has a long history.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他虽然没读过书，但很有文化。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā suīrán méi dú guo shū, dàn hěn yǒu wénhuà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Although he never went to school, he is quite cultured.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">不同的文化有不同的习俗。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Bùtóng de wénhuà yǒu bùtóng de xísú.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Different cultures have different customs.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 文化 vs 教化</span>
          文化 wénhuà is culture as a noun, the whole web of refinement a people carries. 教化 jiàohuà is the active verb of civilizing: a ruler or teacher transforming subjects through moral instruction. 教化 is older and carries a top-down, Confucian flavor of moral cultivation imposed from above.

        <span class="cy-cn">千变万化</span>
        <span class="cy-py">qiān biàn wàn huà</span>
        <span class="cy-en">a thousand changes, ten thousand transformations: endlessly changing; protean</span>
        <span class="cy-note">千 qiān = thousand, 万 wàn = ten thousand, the two largest round numbers paired for the sense of "countless." Lit: "a thousand changes and ten thousand transformations." Describes anything in ceaseless, dazzling flux: clouds, markets, a magician's tricks, the moves of a skilled fighter. 舞台上的灯光千变万化。(The stage lighting changes in a thousand ways.) The phrase pairs 变 and 化, the two great verbs of change, to push the idea of variation to its limit.</span>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">出神入化</span>
        <span class="cy-py">chū shén rù huà</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to leave the mundane and enter the magical: superb; consummate; masterly</span>
        <span class="cy-note">出 chū = to leave, 神 shén = the divine, 入 rù = to enter, 化 huà = the transformed, transcendent state. Lit: "emerging into the spiritual, entering the realm of transformation." The highest praise for skill in an art: calligraphy, painting, martial arts, performance, so accomplished it seems to pass beyond ordinary technique into something magical. 他的书法已经到了出神入化的地步。(His calligraphy has reached a level of consummate mastery.) Here 化 names the transcendent state the master enters.</span>
      </div>

        <span class="cy-cn">化险为夷</span>
        <span class="cy-py">huà xiǎn wéi yí</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to turn danger into safety: to avert a crisis; to come through unscathed</span>
        <span class="cy-note">化 huà = to transform, 险 xiǎn = danger, peril, 为 wéi = into, 夷 yí = level ground, flatness, safety. Lit: "transform the perilous into the level." Describes turning a dangerous, threatening situation into a safe one, often through skill, calm, or good fortune. 在关键时刻，他冷静地化险为夷。(At the critical moment, he calmly turned danger into safety.) The construction X为Y (turn X into Y) is the same productive frame seen in 转危为安 and 反败为胜.</span>
      </div>

        <span class="cy-cn">潜移默化</span>
        <span class="cy-py">qián yí mò huà</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to shift unseen and transform in silence: to influence imperceptibly over time</span>
        <span class="cy-note">潜 qián = hidden, submerged, 移 yí = to shift, 默 mò = silent, 化 huà = to transform. Lit: "shifting while submerged, transforming in silence." Describes the slow, unnoticed way an environment, a teacher, or a habit gradually changes a person's character or thinking without any single dramatic moment. 父母的言行对孩子有潜移默化的影响。(A parent's words and conduct shape a child imperceptibly over time.) This is 化 in its deepest cultural sense: transformation so gradual it cannot be seen happening, only recognized afterward.</span>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture the four strokes of 化 as two people standing side by side. The left figure, 亻, is upright on its feet. The right figure, 匕, is the same person turned head over heels, upside down. The whole character is one body caught mid-flip, in the act of becoming its own inversion. Hold that image and every meaning of 化 follows from it: change is not a tweak at the surface but a turning-over, a genuine becoming-other.</p>
      <p>From that single picture, fan out the four great uses. Physical: ice turns over into water, 融化, snow into melt; food turns over into something the body can take in, 消化. Abstract conversion: pressure turns into drive, 转化; one form of energy turns into another. The civilizing turn: a people slowly turned toward refinement by writing and learning, 文化, the transformation so quiet you only notice it afterward, 潜移默化. And the modern engine: stick 化 onto any noun and you have made a verb of transformation, 现代化, 简化, 美化, the suffix that built half of technical Chinese.</p>
      <p>The companion character to keep beside it is 变. 变 and 化 are the two halves of the change-pair, joined in the everyday word 变化. The rough division: 变 is the becoming-different, the fact that a thing is no longer what it was; 化 is the turning-into, the dissolving of the old form and its reconstitution as something new. Learn the upside-down figure in 化 and you hold the second of the two oldest verbs of transformation in the language.</p>
