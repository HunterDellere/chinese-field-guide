    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="饱">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>饱 splits cleanly down the middle: 饱 = 饣 + 包. The left element 饣 is the compressed running-script form of 食 (shí, food), the radical that marks dozens of eating and cooking words. The right element 包 (bāo, to wrap, to bundle) carries the sound. This is a textbook pictophonetic build: 饣 tells you the word lives in the world of the table, and 包 tells you it rhymes with bāo, dipped down to bǎo.</p>
      <p>The phonetic 包 also pulls its weight semantically here. 包 is a wrapper that bulges around its contents, the same shape as a steamed bun (包子, bāozi) swollen with filling. Set that bulge beside the food radical and the picture writes itself: a belly packed tight, wrapped around a finished meal. The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses the older form 飽 as 厭也, sated to the point of having had enough.</p>
      <p>The traditional character is 飽, with the full 食 on the left. The simplified 饱 trims that radical to the three-stroke 饣 but keeps the 包 intact, so the sound and the logic survive untouched. The meaning travels in one straight line: a stomach filled with food becomes any vessel filled to capacity, and from there fullness of feeling, of color, of experience.</p>
      <p>Japanese borrowed the character as 飽 (hō / aku), preserved in 飽きる (akiru, to grow tired of), where being too full of something curdles into weariness. The Mandarin 饱 keeps the warmer end of that range: satisfaction, not surfeit.</p>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="eating"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">吃饱</span><span class="sh-py">chī bǎo</span><span class="sh-en">Eating Your Fill</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      饱 most often lands as a resultative complement after a verb of eating: 吃饱 (chī bǎo) is the verb 吃 plus the result 饱, meaning eat-until-full. The same slot accepts 喝饱 (hē bǎo, drink one's fill) and the colloquial 饿 (è, hungry) as its opposite pole.
    </div>

        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 温饱 vs. 小康</span>温饱 is mere subsistence, having enough to eat and stay warm; 小康 (xiǎokāng) is the next rung up, a moderately comfortable life.</div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="fullness"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">饱满</span><span class="sh-py">bǎomǎn</span><span class="sh-en">Plenitude Beyond the Plate</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="满">
      <div class="scholar-label">引申 yǐnshēn · Extended Sense</div>
      <p>Once the stomach is full, the character lends its fullness to anything that swells to capacity: a plump grain of rice, a person brimming with energy, air saturated with moisture. 饱 stops being only about food and becomes the word for a vessel filled to its limit, with no slack left.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="饱" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a steamed bun (包子, bāozi) pulled from the bamboo steamer, its skin stretched taut and glossy over a filling that bulges against the seams. That bulge is 包, the wrapper swollen with content. Now set it beside the food radical 饣 on the left, the little ladle-and-rice mark that means the table. Food sign plus a bursting wrapper: a belly packed so full it shines like a dumpling skin.</p>
      <p>Hold that swollen-bun shape and the whole word family unfolds from it. 吃饱 is your own stomach stretched to that taut roundness. 饱满 (bǎomǎn) is a grain of wheat plumped to the same fullness. 饱和 (bǎohé) is a glass of water that can dissolve not one more crystal of salt, saturated to the seam. Every sense of 饱 is that same image: a wrapper filled to its limit, with no room left inside.</p>
