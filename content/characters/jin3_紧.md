    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="紧">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>紧 sits on the silk-thread radical 糸 (mì), the bundled skein that anchors the whole 糸-family of words about cord, weave, and binding. Above it rides a hand-pulling element that drags the thread taut. The image is physical and exact: a weaver's fingers haul the strand until there is no slack left to give. That drawn-tight thread is the literal core of the character, and every later sense grows out of it.</p>
      <p>紧 is the simplified form of the traditional 緊, which kept the full 糸 silk component written out below. Both versions carry the same logic of a cord stretched to its limit. The Shuōwén Jiězì reads the word through the silk radical, placing it squarely among the fabric-and-fiber graphs where tension is something you can feel in your hands.</p>
      <p>From a taut thread the meaning fans outward by a single clean metaphor. A rope with no slack is 紧, so a packed schedule with no slack is also 紧; nerves stretched thin are 紧张; a wallet pulled tight is 手头紧 (shǒutóu jǐn). The body lives inside the same picture: jaw clenched, shoulders drawn up, breath held. Whenever something is stretched until it has no give, Chinese reaches for 紧.</p>
      <p>Japanese borrows the character as 緊 (kin), surviving in 緊張 (kinchō, tension) and 緊急 (kinkyū, emergency), the very same compounds Mandarin builds. Korean reads it 긴 (gin) in 긴급 (gingeup, urgent). Across the region the thread stays pulled tight.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">紧张</span><span class="sh-py">jǐnzhāng</span><span class="sh-en">Tension &amp; Strain</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">紧张</span><span class="card-py">jǐnzhāng</span><span class="card-en">nervous; tense; in short supply</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">紧 (taut) + 张 (zhāng, to stretch open). A surface pulled tight from both ends. Used for nerves before an exam, the tension in a thriller, and resources stretched thin (时间紧张, time is short).</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">第一次上台，我特别紧张。</div><div class="ex-py">Dì-yī cì shàngtái, wǒ tèbié jǐnzhāng.</div><div class="ex-en">My first time on stage, I was incredibly nervous.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">最近车票很紧张。</div><div class="ex-py">Zuìjìn chēpiào hěn jǐnzhāng.</div><div class="ex-en">Train tickets have been hard to get lately.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 紧张 vs. 着急</span> 紧张 is the body's tension (held breath, racing pulse); 着急 (zháojí) is the impatience of waiting. You can be 紧张 calmly; you cannot be 着急 calmly.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">要紧</span><span class="card-py">yàojǐn</span><span class="card-en">important; serious; mattering</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">要 (yào, to want, to need) + 紧 (tight). What is held tight is what matters. Most common in the negative 不要紧 (bùyàojǐn), meaning never mind, it doesn't matter, often said to reassure.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">没事，不要紧。</div><div class="ex-py">Méishì, bùyàojǐn.</div><div class="ex-en">It's fine, never mind.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">这件事很要紧，别忘了。</div><div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn shì hěn yàojǐn, bié wàng le.</div><div class="ex-en">This matter is important, don't forget it.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">紧</span><span class="card-py">jǐn</span><span class="card-en">tight (of fit, time, money)</span></div>

        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">这条裤子太紧了。</div><div class="ex-py">Zhè tiáo kùzi tài jǐn le.</div><div class="ex-en">These pants are too tight.</div></div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="紧" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a single silk thread stretched between two hands. The 糸 radical at the base is the spool, the bundled fiber. The hand above grips and hauls until the thread sings with tension, one more pull and it snaps. That trembling, no-slack line is the whole character. Hold the image and you hold 紧.</p>
      <p>Now let the thread stand in for whatever is being stretched. A clenched jaw before you walk on stage is the thread pulled to 紧张. A red emergency notice is the thread yanked to 紧急. A wallet with nothing left to give is 手头紧. A hand that grabs the rail and will not let go is 抓紧. Every compound is the same taut line, applied to nerves, to time, to money, to grip.</p>
      <p>The release is its own word: 松 (sōng), slack. When the hand lets go and the thread falls loose, that is 放松 (fàngsōng), to relax. The pair lives in your shoulders. Pull them up tight and you feel 紧; drop them and you feel 松.</p>
