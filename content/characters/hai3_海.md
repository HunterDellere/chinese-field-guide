    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="海">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>海 hǎi is a pictophonetic compound: the water radical 氵 carries the meaning, and 每 (měi) supplies the sound.</strong> On the left stands 氵, the three-dot form of 水 shuǐ (water), which marks the great family of liquid and waterway characters: 河 hé (river), 湖 hú (lake), 洋 yáng (ocean). On the right is 每 měi, here doing phonetic duty rather than contributing its everyday sense of "each." The Shuōwén glosses 海 as the great body of water into which the hundred rivers pour: water, plus a phonetic, naming the place where all water gathers.</p>
      <p>From the literal sea, three senses fan out:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Sea, ocean.</strong> The body of salt water itself: 大海 dàhǎi (the sea), 海洋 hǎiyáng (the ocean), 海边 hǎibiān (the seaside), 海岛 hǎidǎo (an island).<br>
      <strong>2. By the sea, in place and institution names.</strong> A city or thing tied to the coast: 上海 Shànghǎi (the city "upon the sea"), 海关 hǎiguān (customs), 海军 hǎijūn (the navy).<br>
      <strong>3. A vast quantity.</strong> Anything as boundless as the ocean: 人海 rénhǎi (a sea of people), 火海 huǒhǎi (a sea of fire), 海量 hǎiliàng (a great capacity, especially for drink).</p>
      <p>The thread is vastness that gathers. The sea is where every river ends, the widest thing the early Chinese world knew, so 海 came to stamp the idea of the immense and the all-collecting onto whatever it touched, whether a crowd, a blaze, or a person's tolerance for wine.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── COAST ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="coast"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">上海</span>
      <span class="sh-py">Shànghǎi</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Coastal Places &amp; Institutions</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="海">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Because the sea was the edge of the known world and the door to trade, 海 became the natural marker for places and institutions that face the water. <strong>上海</strong> Shànghǎi, China's largest city, is literally "upon the sea," a name it earned as a fishing and shipping town at the mouth of the Yangtze. The same logic builds the apparatus of a maritime state: 海关 hǎiguān (the customs office, the "sea barrier" through which goods pass), 海军 hǎijūn (the navy, the "sea army"), 海港 hǎigǎng (a seaport), 海运 hǎiyùn (sea freight).</p>
      <p>The sea also reaches inland through metaphor. 海外 hǎiwài, "beyond the seas," is the standard word for abroad or overseas, a reminder that for most of Chinese history the wider world was reached by water. To this day 海外华人 means the overseas Chinese, the communities scattered past the coast.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── VAST ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="vast"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">人海</span>
      <span class="sh-py">rénhǎi</span>
      <span class="sh-en">A Vast Quantity: the Sea as Multitude</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="海">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The sea is the largest thing the eye can take in, so 海 became a unit of the immeasurable. Append it to a noun and that noun turns boundless. <strong>人海</strong> rénhǎi is a "sea of people," a crowd so dense it has no edge; <strong>火海</strong> huǒhǎi is a "sea of fire," a blaze that has swallowed everything. The pair 火海 and 刀山 (a "mountain of knives") combine in the vow 上刀山下火海, to climb the knife-mountain and plunge into the fire-sea for someone, meaning to face any danger for them.</p>
      <p>The same vastness can measure an inner capacity. <strong>海量</strong> hǎiliàng is "an ocean's measure," a tremendous capacity, used both for a person who can drink an enormous amount without falling and, in the modern world, for huge volumes of data (海量信息, "a flood of information"). To call someone 海量 at a banquet is half compliment, half dare.</p>
    </div>
