    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="古">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>古 gǔ rests on the mouth radical 口.</strong> The traditional reading of the graph, repeated in the dictionaries, is irresistible for a learner: a 十 (ten) above a 口 (mouth), the old as what ten mouths, ten generations, have passed down by word of mouth. Whatever the strict paleography, this folk-etymology captures the heart of the word exactly: 古 is not merely the distant in time but the inherited, the handed-on, what reaches us through many speakers across many years. The simplified and traditional forms are identical, five strokes unchanged.</p>
      <p>From that core of <em>what the generations have transmitted</em>, three senses unfold:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Ancient, of old times.</strong> The far past as a stretch of time: 古代 gǔdài (ancient times), 古老 gǔlǎo (ancient, age-old), 远古 yuǎngǔ (remote antiquity), 自古 zìgǔ (since ancient times).<br>
      <strong>2. Classical, antique.</strong> The objects, texts, and traces the past left behind: 古典 gǔdiǎn (classical), 古文 gǔwén (classical prose), 古董 gǔdǒng (an antique), 古迹 gǔjì (a historic site), 考古 kǎogǔ (archaeology).<br>
      <strong>3. Quaint, unconventional.</strong> A person or manner that feels out of step with the present, old-fashioned in a slightly odd way: 古怪 gǔguài (eccentric), 古板 gǔbǎn (rigid and old-fashioned).</p>
      <p>The thread is age that carries weight. To be 古 is to come down to us from before, whether as an era, an inherited treasure, or a temperament that belongs to an older world.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── ANCIENT TIMES ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="antiquity"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">古代</span>
      <span class="sh-py">gǔdài</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Ancient Times</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="古">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The first and broadest face of 古 is plain antiquity, the distant past as a span of time. 古代 gǔdài names that past as a historical era, the age of dynasties and classics, the default opposite of 现代 the modern. 古老 gǔlǎo is an adjective: not a date but a quality of great age, applied to a city, a craft, or a civilisation that has lasted. 远古 yuǎngǔ pushes further still, to the remote and almost legendary beginnings before recorded history. Together they sweep from the merely old to the unimaginably ancient.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 古代 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">古代</span>
          <span class="card-py">gǔdài</span>
          <span class="card-en">ancient times: antiquity</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          古 gǔ (ancient) + 代 dài (era). The distant past as a historical age, the time of the old dynasties, sages, and classics: 古代文学 "classical literature," 古代史 "ancient history." Stands opposite 现代 (modern) and 近代 (the recent, early-modern past).
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">古代的人用毛笔写字。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Gǔdài de rén yòng máobǐ xiězì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">People in ancient times wrote with brushes.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这本书介绍了古代的科学技术。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè běn shū jièshào le gǔdài de kēxué jìshù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This book introduces the science and technology of ancient times.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 古代 vs. 古老</span>
          <strong>古代</strong> gǔdài points to a stretch of time, the ancient era as opposed to the modern: it is a noun naming a period. <strong>古老</strong> gǔlǎo is an adjective describing the quality of being very old and long-lasting: an 古老的城市 is an age-old city, but you would not say a city <em>is</em> 古代. One names <em>when</em>, the other describes <em>how old</em>.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 古老 -->
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">

          <span class="card-en">ancient; age-old; long-established</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">

        </div>

          <div class="example">

            <div class="ex-py">Zhōngguó shì yí ge gǔlǎo de guójiā.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">China is an ancient country.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这座桥有着古老的历史。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè zuò qiáo yǒuzhe gǔlǎo de lìshǐ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This bridge has a long, ancient history.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 远古 -->
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">远古</span>
          <span class="card-py">yuǎngǔ</span>
          <span class="card-en">remote antiquity; the distant ancient past</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          远 yuǎn (far, remote) + 古 gǔ (ancient). The very distant past, the dim and often legendary beginnings before clear historical record: 远古时代 "remote antiquity," 远古传说 "ancient myths and legends." Reaches further back than 古代, toward the edge of memory.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">远古的人类住在山洞里。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Yuǎngǔ de rénlèi zhù zài shāndòng lǐ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">In remote antiquity, humans lived in caves.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">构词 gòucí · The depth-of-time scale</span>
          A modifier before 古 measures how far back you reach: 远古 yuǎngǔ remote antiquity, 上古 shànggǔ high antiquity (the earliest recorded ages), 中古 zhōnggǔ the medieval middle ages, 近古 jìngǔ the late pre-modern past. The further the prefix, the deeper into time the word looks.
        </div>
      </div>

    <!-- ── CLASSICAL & ANTIQUE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="classical"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">古典</span>
      <span class="sh-py">gǔdiǎn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Classical &amp; Antique</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="古">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>From the past as time, 古 turns to the past as a body of things: the texts, objects, and traces antiquity has left in our hands. 古典 gǔdiǎn is the adjective for the classical, the canonical works and refined style of the old tradition. 古文 gǔwén is classical prose, the literary language of those works. 古董 gǔdǒng is the antique, the prized old object collected for its age. 古迹 gǔjì is the historic site, a ruin or monument that survives on the ground, and 考古 kǎogǔ is the discipline that digs such traces up and reads them. Here 古 is not abstract age but the concrete inheritance the past has handed down.</p>
    </div>

          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 古迹 vs. 古董</span>

One stays on the land; the other passes from hand to hand.
        </div>

    <!-- ── QUAINT & ODD ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="quaint"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">古怪</span>
      <span class="sh-py">gǔguài</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Quaint &amp; Odd</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="古">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The third face of 古 turns inward, to character. When something belongs too much to an older world, it can seem out of step with the present, strange or stubbornly old-fashioned. 古怪 gǔguài pairs 古 with 怪 (strange) to mean eccentric or odd, a person or temper that does not fit ordinary expectation. 古板 gǔbǎn pairs 古 with 板 (stiff, a board) to mean rigid and inflexible, old-fashioned in a humourless way. In both, the antiquity that elsewhere lends dignity instead lends a faint sense of being left behind.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="chengyu"></span>

    <div class="chengyu-grid">

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">古往今来</span>
        <span class="cy-py">gǔ wǎng jīn lái</span>
        <span class="cy-en">throughout the ages: from ancient times down to the present</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "the ancient passes and the present comes." Spans the whole sweep of time from antiquity to now. Used to introduce a truth or pattern that has held across all of history, as in "throughout the ages, people have longed for peace."</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">古色古香</span>
        <span class="cy-py">gǔ sè gǔ xiāng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">of antique flavor: redolent of the past in look and feel</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "ancient color, ancient scent." Praises a building, room, or object that has a tasteful, antique character, steeped in the atmosphere of an older age. A favorite for describing a classical garden, a tea house, or an old street that has kept its period charm.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">千古绝唱</span>
        <span class="cy-py">qiān gǔ jué chàng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">a peerless masterpiece: a work unmatched through the ages</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "a song unmatched in a thousand ages." Here 千古 means all the ages, and 绝唱 the supreme, unrepeatable performance. Reserved for a poem, song, or work of art so fine that nothing across the centuries can equal it.</span>

        <span class="cy-cn">厚古薄今</span>
        <span class="cy-py">hòu gǔ bó jīn</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to favor the past over the present: to esteem antiquity and slight the modern</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "thick toward the ancient, thin toward the present." Criticizes the habit of prizing everything old while dismissing the contemporary. Its mirror is 厚今薄古, valuing the present over the past. A pointed term in debates over tradition and modernity.</span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="古" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Hold the dictionary picture at the center of 古: a 十 (ten) standing over a 口 (mouth), the old as what ten mouths, ten generations, have handed down by speech. To be 古 is to reach us from before, carried across many speakers and many years.</p>
      <p>From that one image the senses fan out. Let it be a span of time and you get antiquity: 古代 ancient times, 古老 the age-old, 远古 the remote past, 自古 since the beginning. Let it be the things the past handed down and you get the classical and the antique: 古典 the canonical, 古文 the old prose, 古董 the prized object, 古迹 the surviving site, 考古 the digging that reads them. And let it cling too tightly to a person and it turns to the quaint and the odd: 古怪 the eccentric, 古板 the inflexible. Whenever you meet 古, ask what age it carries, and whether that age lends dignity or simply leaves its bearer behind: 古往今来, throughout all the ages.</p>
