    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="蛋">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>蛋 stacks two parts top to bottom: 蛋 = 疋 + 虫. The 虫 underneath is the radical, the ancient pictograph of a coiled snake or worm that came to label every small egg-laying creature, from insects to reptiles to birds. The 疋 riding on top is the phonetic, carrying the <em>dàn</em> sound rather than any meaning of its own. So the glyph is a pictophonetic: a creature-sign on the bottom telling you the category, a sound-sign on top telling you how to say it.</p>
      <p>The semantic logic is direct. Eggs are what the small crawling and slithering things of the world produce. By writing the egg-word under 虫, the script files it with snakes, fish roe, and insect clutches before it ever settled onto the hen's egg you crack at breakfast. That breadth survives: 鱼蛋 (yú dàn) are fish balls shaped like roe, 龙蛋 (lóng dàn) is a dragon's egg in legend, and the radical never lets you forget that an egg begins inside a living animal.</p>
      <p>From the round, fragile object the meaning spread to anything egg-shaped: a ball of dough, a lump, a head. Then it took a sharp turn into insult. Calling someone a 蛋 reduces them to a mere object, a thing hatched rather than raised, and the slang 坏蛋 (huài dàn, bad egg) and 笨蛋 (bèn dàn, dumb egg) hit a Mandarin ear roughly the way English "rotten egg" does, with extra bite.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">厨房</span><span class="sh-py">chúfáng</span><span class="sh-en">Kitchen Eggs</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>

        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">鸡蛋</span><span class="card-py">jīdàn</span><span class="card-en">chicken egg</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">鸡 (jī, chicken) + 蛋 (egg): the default egg in Chinese kitchens. When a recipe or menu just says egg, it means a hen's egg, so 鸡蛋 is one of the first nouns most learners cook with.</div>

          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">西红柿炒鸡蛋是最家常的菜。</div><div class="ex-py">Xīhóngshì chǎo jīdàn shì zuì jiācháng de cài.</div><div class="ex-en">Tomato and egg stir-fry is the most everyday home dish.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">蛋糕</span><span class="card-py">dàngāo</span><span class="card-en">cake</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">蛋 (egg) + 糕 (gāo, a steamed or baked flour cake): a cake gets its name from the eggs whipped into the batter. The word covers birthday cakes, sponge cakes, and the slice you buy at a café.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">生日那天我们买了一个大蛋糕。</div><div class="ex-py">Shēngrì nèitiān wǒmen mǎile yí gè dà dàngāo.</div><div class="ex-en">On the birthday we bought a big cake.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 蛋糕 vs. 饼干</span> 蛋糕 is soft and egg-rich; 饼干 (bǐnggān) is a dry biscuit or cracker. Both are 糕点 (gāodiǎn, pastries), but only 蛋糕 carries the egg in its name.</div>
      </div>

        <div class="card-def">炒 (chǎo, to stir-fry) + 蛋: eggs broken into a hot wok and pushed around until they set in soft curds. The same 炒 powers most Chinese cooking, so 炒蛋 reads as plainly as English "scrambled eggs."</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他不会做菜，只会做炒蛋。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā bú huì zuò cài, zhǐ huì zuò chǎodàn.</div><div class="ex-en">He can't really cook; all he can make is scrambled eggs.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">皮 (pí, skin) + 蛋: a duck egg cured in lime, ash, and salt until the white turns to dark amber jelly and the yolk to creamy green-grey. The leathery surface gives it the "skin" name.</div>
        <div class="examples">

    <span class="section-anchor" id="parts"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">部位</span><span class="sh-py">bùwèi</span><span class="sh-en">Egg Parts &amp; Forms</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="蛋">
      <div class="scholar-label">观察 guānchá · Note</div>
      <p>蛋 also names the parts inside the shell and any object that borrows the egg's shape. 蛋白 (egg white) and 蛋黄 (yolk) split the egg by color, white and yellow, while 鸭蛋 (duck egg) doubles as student slang for a zero on a test, because a fat round 0 looks exactly like an egg.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-sienna">

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">蛋 (egg) + 白 (bái, white): literally the white of the egg. In science it extends to protein in general, and 蛋白质 (dànbáizhì) is the standard word for dietary protein.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">健身的人需要多吃蛋白质。</div><div class="ex-py">Jiànshēn de rén xūyào duō chī dànbáizhì.</div><div class="ex-en">People who work out need to eat more protein.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">蛋 (egg) + 黄 (huáng, yellow): the yolk. It pairs neatly with 蛋白, the two halves of every egg named by their color. Salted yolk, 咸蛋黄 (xián dànhuáng), is a prized flavor in mooncakes and pastries.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">这个月饼里有一个完整的咸蛋黄。</div><div class="ex-py">Zhège yuèbǐng lǐ yǒu yí gè wánzhěng de xián dànhuáng.</div><div class="ex-en">This mooncake has a whole salted egg yolk inside.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">鸭 (yā, duck) + 蛋: a duck egg, larger and richer than a hen's. Among students it means a score of zero, since the round 0 on a test paper looks like an egg sitting there.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他这次考试又吃了个鸭蛋。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā zhè cì kǎoshì yòu chīle gè yādàn.</div><div class="ex-en">He scored another zero on this exam.</div></div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="slang"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">骂人</span><span class="sh-py">màrén</span><span class="sh-en">Scoundrel Slang</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      <p>蛋 turns a quality into a person: take an adjective, attach 蛋, and you label someone as that quality made flesh. 坏 + 蛋 yields a bad egg, 笨 + 蛋 a dumb egg, 滚 + 蛋 a "roll away, egg." The 蛋 reduces the target to an object, which is exactly what makes it sting.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">坏蛋</span><span class="card-py">huàidàn</span><span class="card-en">bad egg, villain</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">坏 (huài, bad, rotten) + 蛋: a scoundrel or villain. It ranges from a child's playground taunt to a story's outright bad guy, lighter than a true profanity but clearly an insult.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">电影里的坏蛋最后被抓住了。</div><div class="ex-py">Diànyǐng lǐ de huàidàn zuìhòu bèi zhuāzhù le.</div><div class="ex-en">The villain in the movie was finally caught.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 坏蛋 vs. 笨蛋</span> 坏蛋 calls someone morally bad; 笨蛋 (bèndàn) calls someone stupid. A 坏蛋 means harm, a 笨蛋 just can't think straight.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">笨蛋</span><span class="card-py">bèndàn</span><span class="card-en">idiot, fool</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">笨 (bèn, stupid, clumsy) + 蛋: a fool or dimwit. Among close friends it softens into teasing, even affection, but aimed at a stranger it lands as a real insult.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">你这个笨蛋，连这个都不会。</div><div class="ex-py">Nǐ zhège bèndàn, lián zhège dōu bú huì.</div><div class="ex-en">You idiot, you can't even do this.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">滚蛋</span><span class="card-py">gǔndàn</span><span class="card-en">get lost, scram</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">滚 (gǔn, to roll) + 蛋: a blunt order to get out. The image is of telling someone to roll away like a dropped egg. It is rude and forceful, used only in anger.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他生气地喊：你给我滚蛋！</div><div class="ex-py">Tā shēngqì de hǎn: nǐ gěi wǒ gǔndàn!</div><div class="ex-en">He shouted angrily: Get the hell out!</div></div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="蛋" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a smooth white egg balanced on the back of a coiled worm. The worm is 虫 at the bottom of the glyph, the radical that files this egg with every small creature that lays one. The shape riding on top, 疋, is mute: it only whispers the sound <em>dàn</em>, like a label stuck on the shell with no recipe inside.</p>
      <p>Now crack that egg open and watch the meanings spill out. The runny 蛋白 (white) and golden 蛋黄 (yolk) name the inside by color. Whip them into batter and you get 蛋糕, cake. Drop the whole egg on a stone floor and it splatters: that splatter is 完蛋, finished, done for. And when you sneer at the broken mess on the tiles and call it a 坏蛋, a bad egg, you have walked the character from the henhouse straight into the insult, with the worm at the bottom holding the whole story together.</p>
