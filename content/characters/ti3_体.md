    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="体">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>体 tǐ = 亻 (rén, person) + 本 (běn, root, base).</strong> The simplified character reads almost as a definition: the body is a person's root, the physical base on which a self stands. The traditional form 體 tells a different story, built from 骨 (gǔ, bone) and 豊 (lǐ): the body seen as the framework of bones. When the script was simplified, the spare 亻 + 本 form was adopted in place of the heavy 體, and it happens to carry its own clear logic.</p>
      <p>From the base meaning of body, three families of sense branch out:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Body, physical form.</strong> The literal flesh: 身体 shēntǐ (body, health), 体育 tǐyù (physical education), 体重 tǐzhòng (body weight).<br>
      <strong>2. Substance, system, form.</strong> A body in the abstract, a thing with its own integrated shape: 具体 jùtǐ (concrete), 整体 zhěngtǐ (the whole), 体制 tǐzhì (a system).<br>
      <strong>3. To experience, to embody.</strong> To take a thing into the body and know it from within: 体验 tǐyàn (to experience), 体会 tǐhuì (to grasp by living through), 体贴 tǐtiē (considerate, feeling for another).</p>
      <p>The thread is the body as the ground of knowing. Whether the body is one's own flesh, the structured whole of a system, or the felt experience of a thing, 体 names what is grasped not in the abstract but in the substance, lived rather than merely thought.</p>

    <!-- ── EXPERIENCE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="experience"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">体会</span>
      <span class="sh-py">tǐhuì</span>
      <span class="sh-en">To Experience &amp; Embody</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="体">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The most distinctive thing 体 does is turn the body into a verb of knowing. To 体会 or 体验 something is to understand it not by being told but by living through it, taking it into the body. This is why a teacher will say 你要自己去体会, "you have to grasp it for yourself," when a thing cannot be handed over in words. The same root gives 体贴 tǐtiē and 体谅 tǐliàng: to feel another person's situation in your own body, and so to be considerate and understanding.</p>
      <p>The contrast worth holding is 体会 against 体验. 体验 tǐyàn is to undergo an experience, to pass through it: 体验生活 "to experience life," 体验一下 "give it a try." 体会 tǐhuì is what you take away from the undergoing, the insight that settles afterward: 我体会到了他的难处 "I came to understand his difficulty." One is the living; the other is the understanding it leaves behind.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="体" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Read the simplified glyph as its own little sentence: a person, 亻, standing beside a root, 本. The body is the root of the person, the physical base everything else rests on. Hold that and the first family of words is obvious: the flesh and its care (身体, 体育, 体重).</p>
      <p>Now let the body turn abstract. A system, a collective, a concrete example all have their own "body," an integrated form you can grasp as a whole (整体, 具体, 集体). And when you take a thing right into your own body and know it from the inside, 体 becomes a verb: to live through (体验), to understand by living (体会), to feel for another (体贴). In every case 体 is knowing held in the substance, not floating free of it.</p>
