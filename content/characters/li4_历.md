    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; the Merge</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="历">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>To understand 历 you have to read the traditional form 歷 first, because the simplified glyph has folded a long history into four strokes. 歷 is built under 厂 (hǎn, a cliff or open shelter) with a doubled 禾 (hé, standing grain) above 止 (zhǐ, a foot). The picture is a foot walking past row after row of grain in the field: to step through, to pass by in sequence, to traverse one thing after another. From that literal walking-through came the core abstract sense: to go through an experience, to pass through time, and then the accumulated record of what has been passed through, which is history.</p>
      <p>The simplified 历 keeps the 厂 shelter and replaces the dense interior with 力 (lì, strength; a plow), which also supplies the modern reading. So the simplified glyph reads naturally as a pictophonetic-style compound: 厂 frames the shape while the inner element carries the lì sound. This is a clean example of how simplification often preserved one sound-bearing piece and dropped the rest of a crowded traditional form.</p>
      <p>Here is the consequential part. Simplified 历 does not stand in for one traditional character but for two. 歷 (to pass through, to experience, successive, history) is one; 曆 (the calendar, the reckoning of days and seasons) is the other. The two were always close cousins, both pronounced lì and both about the ordered passage of time, and 曆 was essentially 歷 with 日 (rì, sun; day) underneath to mark its specialization in days and dates. When the script was simplified, both collapsed into 历. In traditional writing you still must choose: 歷史 for history, 曆法 for the calendar system.</p>
      <p>The character travels across the Sinosphere. In Japanese, 歴 reki gives 歴史 rekishi (history) and 経歴 keireki (career, track record), while the calendar branch survives in 暦 koyomi / reki, as in 西暦 seireki (the Western, that is Gregorian, calendar). Reading 历 well means hearing both branches at once: the lived path walked through, and the formal grid of time laid over it.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">合并规律 hébìng guīlǜ · One simplified 历 covers two traditional characters</span>
      <strong>歷 → 历</strong> lì , to pass through; to experience; successive; history (the walking-through branch)<br>
      <strong>曆 → 历</strong> lì , the calendar; the system of reckoning days and seasons (the day-counting branch)<br>
      In simplified script both are written 历, so context decides the sense.<br>
      <strong>历史</strong> lìshǐ , history (from 歷)<br>
      <strong>经历</strong> jīnglì , to experience; experience (from 歷)<br>
      <strong>日历</strong> rìlì , calendar; date pad (from 曆)<br>
      <strong>农历</strong> nónglì , the lunar (agricultural) calendar (from 曆)<br>
      <strong>历法</strong> lìfǎ , calendar system; the science of the calendar (from 曆)<br>
      The shared thread across both branches is ordered, sequential time: a path walked step by step, or a year laid out day by day. One glyph now carries both readings of time, the lived and the counted.
    </div>

          <span class="card-py">jīnglì</span>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          经 jīng (to pass through; warp threads; to undergo) + 历 lì (to go through). The most common everyday word for living through something and for the sum of what one has lived through. As a verb it takes events as objects: you 经历 a war, a hardship, a change. As a noun it names personal experience as accumulated record, close to "the things that have happened to a person."
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他经历过很多困难。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā jīnglì guò hěn duō kùnnán.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He has been through many hardships.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是一次难忘的经历。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì yī cì nánwàng de jīnglì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This was an unforgettable experience.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">每个人都要经历成长的痛苦。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Měi gè rén dōu yào jīnglì chéngzhǎng de tòngkǔ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Everyone has to go through the pains of growing up.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 经历 vs 经验</span>
          经历 jīnglì is the event itself, the going-through; it can be good, bad, or neutral, and is counted in episodes. 经验 jīngyàn is the know-how distilled from experience, the lesson you keep. You 经历 a failure (the episode) and from it gain 经验 (the wisdom). 工作经历 is your work history (the jobs you held); 工作经验 is your competence on the job (what you learned to do).
        </div>
      </div>

The accumulated worldly experience a person builds up over the years: the breadth of life seen and understood, not just events lived but their lessons absorbed. 阅历 implies maturity and judgment that come only with time; it is the kind of experience that makes someone hard to fool.

      <!-- 历史 -->
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">历史</span>
          <span class="card-py">lìshǐ</span>
          <span class="card-en">history; the past as recorded and studied</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          历 lì (the passed-through past) + 史 shǐ (the scribe; the written record; chronicle). Together: the accumulated record of what has happened. 历史 is both the events themselves and the discipline that studies them, the school subject, the books, and the long arc of the past all at once. It is one of the highest-frequency abstract nouns in the language and carries enormous cultural weight in a civilization that has kept continuous records for millennia.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">中国有悠久的历史。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhōngguó yǒu yōujiǔ de lìshǐ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">China has a long history.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们应该从历史中学习。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen yīnggāi cóng lìshǐ zhōng xuéxí.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We should learn from history.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是历史上的重要一刻。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì lìshǐ shàng de zhòngyào yī kè.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This is an important moment in history.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">文化 wénhuà · Cultural Note</span>
          历史 is inseparable from the Chinese self-understanding of being a 文明古国 (wénmíng gǔguó, an ancient civilization). The phrase 五千年历史 (wǔ qiān nián lìshǐ, "five thousand years of history") is a fixed cultural touchstone. The 二十四史 (Èrshísì Shǐ, the Twenty-Four Histories) form the official dynastic record, with 史记 (Shǐjì) by Sima Qian as the founding model.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 历来 -->
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">历来</span>
          <span class="card-py">lìlái</span>
          <span class="card-en">always; throughout history; from of old up to now</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          历 lì (passing through time) + 来 lái (coming up to the present). An adverb meaning "all along throughout the past until now," used to assert that something has consistently been the case across the whole span of history. It lends weight and a sense of long-standing tradition to the statement it modifies, and sits before the verb.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">中国历来重视教育。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhōngguó lìlái zhòngshì jiàoyù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">China has always placed great value on education.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这条规定历来如此。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè tiáo guīdìng lìlái rúcǐ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This rule has always been so.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 历来 vs 从来</span>
          历来 lìlái stresses the long historical span and is often positive or neutral, asserting a consistent tradition. 从来 cónglái more often appears in negative sentences (从来不, "never") and emphasizes "at no point ever." 历来重视 (has always valued) feels institutional; 从来不喝酒 (never drinks) feels personal and absolute.
        </div>
      </div>

Still in daily use alongside the solar calendar for festivals and birthdays.

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">阳历</span>
          <span class="card-py">yánglì</span>
          <span class="card-en">the solar (Gregorian) calendar</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          阳 yáng (the sun; the yang principle) + 历 lì (calendar). The sun-based calendar, in modern usage the Gregorian calendar used for official and international dates. Also called 公历 gōnglì (the "common," that is internationally standard, calendar). It stands opposite 农历 / 阴历 in the everyday pairing of solar versus lunar reckoning.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">今天阳历几号？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Jīntiān yánglì jǐ hào?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">What is today's date on the solar calendar?</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他的生日阳历和农历不在同一天。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā de shēngrì yánglì hé nónglì bù zài tóng yī tiān.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">His birthday falls on different days in the solar and lunar calendars.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 历法 the system behind the date</span>
          历法 lìfǎ is the calendar as a system or science (the rules for intercalating months, fixing solar terms, and aligning sun and moon), whereas 日历 / 农历 / 阳历 name particular calendars in use. Historically, fixing the 历法 was an imperial prerogative: a new dynasty would proclaim its own calendar as a sign of holding the Mandate of Heaven.
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture the traditional 歷: under an open cliff-shelter (厂) stand rows of grain, and a foot (止) walks through them, past one row, then the next, then the next. That single image holds the whole character. To walk through the rows in order is 经历, to live through events. The trail of rows already passed is 历史, the record of what came before. And laying those same rows out as a counted grid of days gives the calendar branch, 曆, day after sun-marked day.</p>
      <p>The simplified 历 trades the grain and the foot for 力 (strength, a plow) under the same shelter, but the logic survives: it still takes effort to pass through, season by season, year by year. Keep the two readings linked in your mind. When you see 历史, 经历, 历来, hear the foot walking the path; when you see 日历, 农历, 阳历, hear the days being counted off. One glyph, two ways of measuring the passage of time.</p>
      <p>That doubling is itself a lesson about Chinese script. Simplification did not only shrink strokes; in cases like this it merged whole characters, so that 历 now answers to both 歷 and 曆. The walked path and the reckoned year were always neighbors in meaning and identical in sound, and the modern glyph finally houses them together. To know 历 is to hold lived time and counted time in one hand.</p>
