    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="分">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>分 fēn = 八 (bā, to split apart) + 刀 (dāo, knife).</strong> It is an associative compound (会意字): 八 in its original sense is not the number eight but a picture of two strokes turning away from each other, the image of <em>separation</em>; 刀 is a knife. Put a dividing image over a blade and you have the act of cutting one thing into parts. The core meaning is to divide.</p>
      <p>From the cut, everything that comes in parts follows:</p>
      <p><strong>1. To divide, to separate.</strong> The literal act: 分开 fēnkāi (to separate), 分手 fēnshǒu (to part, to break up), 分配 fēnpèi (to allot).<br>
      <strong>2. A small divided unit.</strong> The fraction left after dividing: 分钟 fēnzhōng (a minute, the hour divided), 分 (a point on a test), 分 (a cent, the yuan divided).<br>
      <strong>3. A part, a share.</strong> What a division yields: 部分 bùfen (a part), 成分 chéngfèn (a component), 充分 chōngfèn (ample, fully sufficient).<br>
      <strong>4. The second reading fèn.</strong> When the dividing is settled into a fixed lot, 分 is read fèn and written 份: 身份 shēnfèn (status), 缘分 yuánfèn (the lot that brings people together), 本分 běnfèn (one's proper duty).</p>
      <p>The thread is the cut. Whatever has been split, measured out, or apportioned, a minute, a point, a cent, a share, a duty, traces back to the knife dividing a whole into parts.</p>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">分钟</span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="分">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Because 分 is the small unit left after dividing, the same character measures three different wholes. Divide the hour and 分 is the <strong>minute</strong>: 五分钟 wǔ fēnzhōng (five minutes), 现在三点十分 "it's ten past three." Divide a perfect score and 分 is the <strong>point</strong>: 考了九十分 "scored ninety," 满分 mǎnfēn (full marks). Divide the yuan and 分 is the <strong>cent</strong>, a hundredth of the currency.</p>
      <p>The logic is one and the same in every case: 分 is what you get when a whole is cut into equal small pieces. This is also why 分数 fēnshù means both a test "score" and a mathematical "fraction," the two senses of a number that lives below the whole. When you hear 分, ask what whole is being divided, and the unit names itself.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="分" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>See the knife 刀 at the bottom and the splitting strokes 八 above it: a blade coming down to cut a whole in two. That single image is the whole character. 分 is the cut, and everything it means is a piece left by the cut.</p>
      <p>Divide the hour and the piece is a minute. Divide a perfect score and the piece is a point. Divide the yuan and the piece is a cent. Divide a whole into shares and the piece is a part (部分), a duty, a station in life (身份). When the cut is no longer an action but a settled lot, the sound shifts to fèn. Either way, hold the knife in mind and every sense of 分 falls into place.</p>
