    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="接">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>接 = 扌 + 妾. The left side is 扌, the standing-hand form of 手 (shǒu, hand), and it carries the meaning: this is something a hand does. The right side is 妾 (qiè), and it supplies the sound, giving the old reading that wore down to modern <em>jiē</em>. A pictophonetic character, plain in its parts: a hand on the left, a phonetic marker on the right.</p>
      <p>The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 接 as 交也 (jiāo yě), to bring together, to make two things touch. Read the radical literally and the whole field of meaning unfolds from one gesture. Two hands reaching until fingers close over fingers; one hand stretched out as a thrown ball drops into the palm; an open hand turned upward to take what someone hands across. Every sense of 接 is the moment a gap closes and contact is made.</p>
      <p>From that physical join the meaning spread outward. A hand that closes a gap can splice two ropes (连接 liánjiē, to link), can take a phone to the ear (接电话 jiē diànhuà, to answer a call), can wait at the platform to gather an arriving guest (接人 jiē rén, to meet someone). The wrist never leaves the word: whether the thing joined is a cable, a call, or a person, 接 keeps the image of a hand bridging the last small distance.</p>
      <p>Japanese borrows the same character in 接する (sessuru, to come into contact) and 接続 (setsuzoku, connection), where it does identical duty for joining circuits, lines, and people. Korean reads it 접 (jeop), as in 접촉 (jeopchok, contact). Across all three the hand-that-joins survives intact.</p>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="receive"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">接受</span><span class="sh-py">jiēshòu</span><span class="sh-en">Receive &amp; Take</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">接受</span><span class="card-py">jiēshòu</span><span class="card-en">to accept, to receive</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">接 (receive) + 受 (shòu, to be given). The hand takes what is offered and keeps it. Used for offers, opinions, criticism, conditions, anything you take in and agree to.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">我接受你的道歉。</div><div class="ex-py">Wǒ jiēshòu nǐ de dàoqiàn.</div><div class="ex-en">I accept your apology.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他不愿意接受我们的建议。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā bù yuànyì jiēshòu wǒmen de jiànyì.</div><div class="ex-en">He is unwilling to accept our suggestion.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 接受 vs. 收到</span>收到 (shōudào) is the neutral fact of a thing arriving in your hands; 接受 adds your consent, you take it in and say yes to it.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">直接</span><span class="card-py">zhíjiē</span><span class="card-en">direct, directly</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">ADJ 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">直 (zhí, straight) + 接 (join). The two ends meet with nothing in between, a straight join. As an adverb it means doing a thing with no go-between, no detour.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">你可以直接跟经理说。</div><div class="ex-py">Nǐ kěyǐ zhíjiē gēn jīnglǐ shuō.</div><div class="ex-en">You can speak to the manager directly.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">这是导致失败的直接原因。</div><div class="ex-py">Zhè shì dǎozhì shībài de zhíjiē yuányīn.</div><div class="ex-en">This is the direct cause of the failure.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 直接 vs. 间接</span>间接 (jiànjiē) is the opposite: the join passes through a middle link, an indirect route.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">接电话</span><span class="card-py">jiē diànhuà</span><span class="card-en">to answer the phone</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">VO 动宾 dòngbīn</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">接 (receive) + 电话 (diànhuà, phone call). To take up a ringing call and connect the line. The hand that closes the gap now closes a circuit.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他在开会，没办法接电话。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā zài kāihuì, méi bànfǎ jiē diànhuà.</div><div class="ex-en">He is in a meeting and cannot answer the phone.</div></div>
        </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="continue"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">接着</span><span class="sh-py">jiēzhe</span><span class="sh-en">Connect &amp; Continue</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      <p>Once 接 joins two things end to end, it picks up a sense of <em>sequence</em>: one event taking up where the last left off. 接着 (jiēzhe) chains clauses the way fingers link in a relay, and 连接 (liánjiē) splices two parts into a continuous whole.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">接着</span><span class="card-py">jiēzhe</span><span class="card-en">next, then, to follow on</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">接 (join) + 着 (zhe, continuing aspect). One action takes the baton from the one before it. Use it to mean "and then, right after that," or "go on (doing something)."</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他先洗了脸，接着就出门了。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā xiān xǐ le liǎn, jiēzhe jiù chūmén le.</div><div class="ex-en">He washed his face first, then headed straight out the door.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">请接着说，我在听。</div><div class="ex-py">Qǐng jiēzhe shuō, wǒ zài tīng.</div><div class="ex-en">Please go on, I am listening.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 接着 vs. 然后</span>然后 (ránhòu) just marks the next step; 接着 stresses that the next step follows on directly, with no gap.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">

        <div class="card-def">连 (lián, to be joined in a chain) + 接 (to splice). To make two separate things into one continuous thing. Used for bridges, cables, networks, anything that bridges a gap.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">这座桥连接了两个城市。</div><div class="ex-py">Zhè zuò qiáo liánjiē le liǎng gè chéngshì.</div><div class="ex-en">This bridge connects the two cities.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">请检查网络连接。</div><div class="ex-py">Qǐng jiǎnchá wǎngluò liánjiē.</div><div class="ex-en">Please check the network connection.</div></div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="接" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a relay race at the last exchange. One runner sprints in with the baton; the next stands with an open hand thrown back over the shoulder, palm up, fingers spread. For one stride the baton hangs in the air between them, then a hand closes over it. That closing hand is 接.</p>
      <p>The left side of the glyph, 扌, is literally that reaching hand. The right side, 妾, is only the sound, the announcer's call of the name. Everything that matters is in the wrist on the left: the gap, the reach, the grip.</p>
      <p>Hold the relay image and the whole word family lines up. 接电话 is catching a ringing baton called a phone. 迎接 is running out to take the baton from an arriving guest. 接着 is the next runner taking up the race the instant the last one finishes. Wherever 接 appears, look for the open hand closing the last small distance.</p>
