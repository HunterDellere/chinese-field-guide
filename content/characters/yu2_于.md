    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="于">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>于 yú is a function word, a grammatical preposition rather than a picture of a thing.</strong> Its oracle-bone and bronze forms are usually read as a drawing of breath or sound carrying outward, and from very early on the character was borrowed to do purely grammatical work. Whatever the original image, 于 in classical and modern Chinese is essentially a relation-marker: it sits before a noun and says how the verb connects to it, in space, in time, in direction, or in degree. It is filed under the radical 二 èr (two) by stroke convention, not by meaning.</p>
      <p>There is a second glyph to know. The traditional and classical texts often write this preposition as <strong>於</strong> yú, a separate character that overlapped 于 in many of its uses. In the simplified script the two were largely merged, so mainland writing uses 于 across the board, while 於 survives in traditional typesetting and in a handful of fixed expressions. Treat them as one word with two written shapes.</p>
      <p>The senses that matter to a learner all flow from one idea, "the point a thing is located or measured against":</p>
      <p><strong>1. At, in, on (place and time).</strong> 于 + a location or a date: 生于北京 shēng yú Běijīng (born in Beijing), 始于 shǐ yú (to begin at), 位于 wèiyú (to be located at).<br>
      <strong>2. To, toward, for, about.</strong> The thing the action is directed at or concerned with: 对于 duìyú (regarding), 关于 guānyú (about), 属于 shǔyú (to belong to).<br>
      <strong>3. Than (in comparison).</strong> Placed after an adjective to mark the standard: 大于 dàyú (greater than), 高于 gāoyú (higher than), 优于 yōuyú (superior to).<br>
      <strong>4. Bound into disyllables.</strong> Many common two-syllable words end in 于 as a fossilized tail: 由于 yóuyú (because of), 终于 zhōngyú (finally), 等于 děngyú (equal to), 至于 zhìyú (as for).</p>
      <p>The single thread is anchoring. 于 fixes a verb or a quality to a reference point: a place, a moment, a target, or a measuring line. Once you hear it as "with respect to," every use falls into place.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── PLACE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="place"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">生于</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shēng yú</span>
      <span class="sh-en">At, In, On: Place and Time</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="于">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The most classical job of 于 is to plant a verb at a place or a moment. After the verb, 于 introduces the location or the time: 出生于上海 chūshēng yú Shànghǎi (born in Shanghai), 成立于去年 chénglì yú qùnián (founded last year). This is the formal, written counterpart of the spoken locative 在 zài. In speech you would say 他住在北京 (he lives in Beijing); in a biography or a formal notice you meet the tighter 生于北京, 卒于南京 (born in Beijing, died in Nanjing). The 于 frame is compact and bookish, which is exactly why it dominates formal registers.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── THAN ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="than"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">大于</span>
      <span class="sh-py">dàyú</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Than &amp; the Bound Frames</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="于">
      <div class="scholar-label">语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight</div>
      <p>Placed after an adjective, 于 marks the standard a thing is measured against, giving a tidy "greater than" frame: 大于 dàyú (greater than), 高于 gāoyú (higher than), 多于 duōyú (more than), 优于 yōuyú (better than). 三大于二 "three is greater than two" is how a Chinese maths text reads the symbol &gt;. This is the formal cousin of the spoken 比 bǐ comparison (三比二大); 于 packs the same relation into a single bound word.</p>
      <p>The other place a beginner constantly meets 于 is as the frozen tail of a common disyllable, where it no longer carries a separable meaning of its own. 由于 yóuyú (because of) opens a reason; 终于 zhōngyú (finally, at last) caps a long wait; 等于 děngyú (to equal) states an equation; 至于 zhìyú (as for) shifts to a new topic. You do not parse the 于 in these, you learn each as a unit. Recognizing that the same little particle is hiding inside all of them is what makes the list feel like one family rather than four unrelated words.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Hear 于 as a single English phrase: "with respect to." It never travels alone; it always points at a reference point sitting just after it. Born with respect to a place is 生于; concerned with respect to a topic is 关于; greater with respect to a number is 大于; arriving with respect to the end is 终于.</p>
      <p>So whenever 于 appears, look immediately to its right and ask what reference point it is anchoring the verb to: a location, a moment, a target, a measuring line, or the end of a long wait. Name that anchor and the meaning of 于 is already in hand. And remember its written twin 於, the same word wearing a fuller coat in traditional and classical texts.</p>
