    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="情">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>情 qíng is a pictophonetic compound: 忄 (the heart radical) carries the meaning, and 青 qīng supplies the sound.</strong> The left element 忄 is the squeezed, side-standing form of 心 xīn (heart), the same radical that marks 怕 pà (fear), 忙 máng (busy), and a whole field of inner states. On the right, 青 qīng (the blue-green of growing things) lends its pronunciation and nudges qīng toward qíng. Together they name what stirs inside: the movements of the heart.</p>
      <p>From that core image the character spreads into three senses that anchor a great many words:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Feeling, emotion, mood.</strong> The general inner state: 感情 gǎnqíng (feelings; affection), 心情 xīnqíng (mood), 情绪 qíngxù (emotions; emotional state), 热情 rèqíng (warmth; enthusiasm).<br>
      <strong>2. Affection, love.</strong> Feeling directed at another person: 爱情 àiqíng (romantic love), 友情 yǒuqíng (friendship), 亲情 qīnqíng (family affection), 情人 qíngrén (lover; sweetheart).<br>
      <strong>3. Situation, circumstance.</strong> The shape of how things stand: 情况 qíngkuàng (situation), 情形 qíngxíng (state of affairs), 事情 shìqíng (matter; affair), 情节 qíngjié (plot; circumstances of a case).</p>
      <p>The thread running through all three is the heart facing the world. Turned inward it is mood and feeling; turned toward a person it is affection; turned toward events it is the felt shape of a situation. Chinese keeps these close together on purpose: a 情况 is not a cold set of facts but the way matters actually stand, the circumstances as the heart reads them.</p>
    </div>

      <!-- 心情 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">心情</span>
          <span class="card-py">xīnqíng</span>
          <span class="card-en">mood; state of mind; frame of mind</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          心 xīn (heart) + 情 qíng (feeling). The mood you are in at a given time: 心情好 "in a good mood," 心情不好 "in a bad mood." A passing state of the heart rather than a settled trait.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">今天天气好，我的心情也很好。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Jīntiān tiānqì hǎo, wǒ de xīnqíng yě hěn hǎo.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The weather is nice today, so my mood is good too.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 心情 vs. 情绪</span>
          <strong>心情</strong> is your overall mood, the general weather of how you feel: good, bad, relaxed, heavy. <strong>情绪</strong> qíngxù points more at the raw emotional state and its swings, often the agitated or negative side: 闹情绪 "to sulk; to throw a fit," 情绪不稳定 "emotionally unstable." You can be in a fine 心情 while 情绪 names the specific feeling moving through you.
        </div>
      </div>

    <!-- ── LOVE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="love"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">爱情</span>
      <span class="sh-py">àiqíng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Affection &amp; Love</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="情">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>When 情 turns toward another person it becomes the language of bonds. Chinese sorts these bonds by who they are with. <strong>爱情</strong> àiqíng is romantic love, the love between partners. <strong>友情</strong> yǒuqíng is the feeling between friends, friendship. <strong>亲情</strong> qīnqíng is the tie of blood, the affection within a family. Each pairs 情 with a different first character, and together they map the heart's attachments.</p>
      <p>This is why 情 alone can stand for romance: 情人 qíngrén is a lover or sweetheart, 情书 qíngshū is a love letter, and 情人节 Qíngrénjié is the name for Valentine's Day. The single glyph carries enough of the heart that, in the right company, it reads as love itself.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── SITUATION ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="situation"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">情况</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qíngkuàng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Situation &amp; Circumstance</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="情">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The third great sense of 情 steps away from private feeling toward the wider shape of events. Here 情 means the situation, the circumstances, the way things stand. <strong>情况</strong> qíngkuàng is the everyday word for "situation" or "circumstances": 什么情况 "what is going on," 情况紧急 "the situation is urgent." Its close neighbor <strong>情形</strong> qíngxíng describes the state of affairs as a scene one observes.</p>
      <p>This is also where 情 meets the very common word <strong>事情</strong> shìqíng, a matter or affair, where 事 shì (matter) and 情 sit side by side: 有件事情 "there is a matter (to discuss)." The link is worth holding onto: in Chinese a "matter" is not a bare event but a thing with a felt shape, which is exactly what 情 contributes.</p>
    </div>

      <p>Put your finger on the heart radical 忄 at the left of 情 and feel it as a heart standing on its side, leaning toward the world. The phonetic 青 qīng, the fresh blue-green of growing things, gives the sound and a hint of something alive and stirring. 情 is the heart in motion.</p>
      <p>Then watch which way the heart turns. Turned inward, it is mood and feeling (心情, 情绪, 感情). Turned toward a person, it is affection and love (爱情, 友情, 亲情). Turned toward events, it is the felt shape of a situation (情况, 事情). Whenever you meet 情, ask where the heart is pointing, and the sense will follow.</p>
