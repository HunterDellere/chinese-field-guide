    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="飞">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>飞 is a pictograph of a bird in the act of flapping its wings. There is no semantic-plus-phonetic split here: the whole glyph is one image, a single bird drawn at the moment its wings beat down and its body lifts. The first stroke, a rising sweep, is the line of the bird's neck and back tilting upward; the curved hooks below are the wings and the trailing tail feathers thrown out behind. The verified decomposition treats the form as a single intersecting unit, not as A plus B, because nothing in it is borrowed for sound.</p>
      <p>The traditional form 飛 makes the picture fuller: it doubles the wing element and adds the body in the center, so the eye reads two wings spread on either side of a rising trunk. The Shuowen Jiezi places 飛 among its own radical and glosses it as a bird beating its wings and rising into the air. Simplification kept only the most economical trace of that image, the three strokes of 飞, which is why the modern character can feel abstract until you set it beside its ancestor and watch the wings reappear.</p>
      <p>From the concrete sense of a bird leaving the ground, 飞 extended outward along two clear lines. One is height and the air: anything airborne flies, so the same character covers insects, kites, planes, and dust lifted by wind. The other is speed: because flight is the fastest motion an early observer could name, 飞 became an intensifier of swiftness, attached to running, growing, and rising prices alike. The bird is still inside every one of these uses.</p>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="speed"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">飞快</span><span class="sh-py">fēikuài</span><span class="sh-en">Flying as Speed</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      <p>Because flight is the fastest motion in the old vocabulary, 飞 detached from birds and became a way to say very fast. Placed in front of a quality or a verb, it adds the sense of moving at flying speed: 飞快 (flying-fast), 飞跑 (to run flat out), 飞涨 (prices shooting up). The bird is gone but its velocity remains.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="飞" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a single sparrow lifting off a courtyard wall at dawn. Its body tilts up along one rising line, the first stroke of 飞, and the two hooked strokes below are the wings caught at the bottom of their downbeat, the tail feathers flung out behind. Hold that frame: three strokes, one bird, the instant before it clears the rooftop.</p>
      <p>Every compound puts a different cargo on that same bird. Bolt an engine to it and you get 飞机, the flying machine. Let it leave the ground and that is 起飞, the takeoff. Strip away the feathers and keep only the speed, and the bird becomes an intensifier: 飞快 is anything moving as fast as that wingbeat, 飞涨 is a price shooting up the same way. Wherever you meet 飞, look for the bird still hidden in the stroke, and the meaning follows it into the air.</p>
