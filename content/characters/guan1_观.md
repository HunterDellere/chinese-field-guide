    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="观">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>观 (traditional 觀) is a pictophonetic compound: 见 jiàn (to see) carries the meaning, and 雚 guàn supplies the sound.</strong> The meaning element 见 is the eye over a kneeling figure, the radical of seeing that anchors the whole character to vision. The sound element 雚, an old name for a heron or egret, lent its reading to the graph and survives in the simplified form only as the abbreviated left side 又. So the eye is original; the bird was only ever borrowed for its pronunciation.</p>
      <p>Note the simplified-traditional pair. The full form 觀 keeps the bird 雚 on the left; the simplified 观 replaces it with the cursive shorthand 又, while 见 stands on the right in both. The two are the same character in different scripts, and you will meet 觀 in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and pre-reform texts.</p>
      <p>From "to look at" the character grows two families of sense that anchor a wide vocabulary:</p>
      <p><strong>1. To observe, to watch, to view.</strong> The eye actively trained on something: 观察 guānchá (to observe closely), 参观 cānguān (to visit, to tour), 观看 guānkàn (to watch), 观众 guānzhòng (audience, the watching crowd).<br>
      <strong>2. A view, an outlook, a way of seeing.</strong> Not the act but the settled lens: 观点 guāndiǎn (viewpoint), 观念 guānniàn (concept, notion), 乐观 lèguān (optimistic), 世界观 shìjièguān (worldview).</p>
      <p>Read as <strong>guàn</strong>, in the falling fourth tone, the same graph names a Daoist temple: 道观 dàoguàn. The thread from looking to a place of contemplation runs through the idea of the lookout tower or watchtower where one gazed out, which in the Daoist tradition became the hall set apart for observing heaven and cultivating the way.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── OUTLOOK ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="outlook"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">观点</span>
      <span class="sh-py">guāndiǎn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Views &amp; Outlook: How One Sees</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="观">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The second family turns 观 from a verb into a lens. Here it is no longer the act of looking but the settled <em>way</em> of looking that shapes what a person sees. 观点 guāndiǎn is the angle from which you view a matter, your viewpoint; 观念 guānniàn is a deeper, more durable notion or concept that you carry, such as 传统观念 (traditional notions) or 时间观念 (a sense of punctuality).</p>
      <p>Bolt 观 onto the end of a noun and you build a whole outlook: 世界观 shìjièguān is your worldview, 人生观 rénshēngguān your outlook on life, 价值观 jiàzhíguān your values. In each, 观 is the framing eye through which the world, a life, or worth is judged. The paired moods 乐观 lèguān (optimistic) and 悲观 bēiguān (pessimistic) work the same way: they describe not what you see but the cast of the eye that does the seeing.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── TEMPLE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="temple"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">道观</span>
      <span class="sh-py">dàoguàn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Daoist Temple: the Reading guàn</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="观">
      <div class="scholar-label">读音洞见 dúyīn dòngjiàn · Reading Insight</div>
      <p>Shift the tone from level guān to falling <strong>guàn</strong> and the same character changes class entirely: it becomes a noun naming a Daoist temple. The compound to know is 道观 dàoguàn, the hall or monastery of the 道 (the Way), the Daoist counterpart to the Buddhist 寺 sì and the more general 庙 miào. A famous example is the 白云观 Báiyúnguàn, the White Cloud Temple in Beijing.</p>
      <p>The two readings are far apart in use but joined at the root. The temple began as a 观, a raised lookout or tower from which one gazed at the heavens; in Daoist practice that place of gazing became the hall set apart for contemplating the Way. So even in the temple sense, the eye that looks is never far off. When you meet 观 in a place name and a temple is in view, read it guàn, not guān.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="观" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Find the eye. The seeing radical 见 stands on the right of 观 in both the simplified and traditional forms, and it is the key to the whole character. Everything 观 means flows from that eye looking.</p>
      <p>First the eye does its work: it observes (观察), tours (参观), watches a show (观看), and gathers into the watching crowd (观众). Then the eye stops moving and becomes a lens: the angle you argue from (观点), the notions you hold (观念), the bright or dark cast of your outlook (乐观, 悲观), the whole frame through which you read the world (世界观). And when the tone falls to guàn, the lookout where one gazed at heaven settles into a Daoist temple (道观). Act of seeing, way of seeing, place of seeing: hold the eye, and every sense of 观 follows.</p>
