    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="队">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>队 = 阝 + 人. On the left stands 阝, the mound radical (also called 左耳旁, the left-ear), which in characters of place and terrain pictures a stepped slope of packed earth. On the right stands 人, a person seen in profile. The simplified glyph compresses an older, busier picture into these two strokes-worth of meaning: a body of people gathered against rising ground.</p>
      <p>The traditional form is 隊, where the right side carries 㒸 over a fuller phonetic-semantic load. Shuōwén glosses 隊 as 从高隊也, a falling from a height, and the graph's first life was the verb 墜 (zhuì), to plummet down a slope. The mound supplied the height; people or things tumbling from it supplied the action. That sense of bodies moving together down and along terrain is the hinge.</p>
      <p>From a mass of people pouring along the foot of a rampart, the meaning settled on the people themselves once they are formed up: a column, a band, a unit of soldiers marching in order. The fall became the formation. By the time the word reaches everyday Mandarin, 队 names any organized group of humans, a 球队 (qiúduì) on the field or a 排队 (páiduì) line at the bank, and works as the measure word for such ranks: 一队士兵 (yī duì shìbīng), one column of soldiers.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps the traditional 隊 in 部隊 (butai), a military unit, and 自衛隊 (jieitai), the Self-Defense Forces, preserving the martial weight that Mandarin has softened into sports clubs and queues.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">队列</span><span class="sh-py">duìliè</span><span class="sh-en">Formed Ranks &amp; Lines</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">队伍</span><span class="card-py">duìwǔ</span><span class="card-en">ranks, procession, contingent</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">队 (formed group) + 伍 (a file of five soldiers, the old squad). The pairing names a body of people standing or moving in order, from a literal military column to a figurative workforce or movement.</div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="队" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>See a high earthen rampart at dawn, the kind that ringed an old Chinese town. The mound radical 阝 is that wall, stepped and packed solid against the left edge of the character. Now a single soldier 人 stands at its foot on the right, and behind him, out of frame, a whole column waits to fall in. One person against a mound: the seed of a team.</p>
      <p>Hold that picture and the compounds line up behind it like ranks. 排队 (páiduì) sets the people single file along the wall to wait. 队长 (duìzhǎng) puts a captain at the head of the column. 球队 (qiúduì) trades the spears for a ball and marches them onto a pitch. The mound never moves; only what gathers against it changes.</p>
      <p>When you reach for the measure word, hear the boots: 一队骆驼 (yí duì luòtuó), one file of camels strung out along the slope. 队 is always a body of bodies in order, the crowd 人 disciplined by the wall 阝 into a line.</p>
