    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="间">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>间 jiān = 门 (mén, gate) enclosing 日 (rì, sun).</strong> The picture is light showing through the crack between a pair of closed doors: the gap itself, the space that lies between. The traditional form 間 sets 月 (yuè, moon) in the gateway instead, moonlight slipping through the door at night; the simplified 间 swaps in 日, the sun. Either way the meaning is the same, the chink of light that marks an opening, an interval, a space-between.</p>
      <p>From that gap, four senses open out:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Space between, interval.</strong> The literal gap: 之间 zhījiān (between), 空间 kōngjiān (space), 间隔 jiàngé (an interval).<br>
      <strong>2. A span of time.</strong> The interval read on the clock and calendar: 时间 shíjiān (time), 期间 qījiān (a period), 中间 (in the middle of a stretch).<br>
      <strong>3. A room.</strong> A bounded space within a building, and the measure word for counting such spaces: 房间 fángjiān (room), 一间屋子 yì jiān wūzi (one room).<br>
      <strong>4. Among, within a domain.</strong> The space inside a sphere of life: 人间 rénjiān (the human world), 民间 mínjiān (among the people, folk), 田间 (in the fields).</p>
      <p>A second reading, jiàn, foregrounds the gap as separation: 间接 jiànjiē (indirect, by way of an intermediary) and 离间 líjiàn (to sow discord, to drive a wedge between). The thread through every sense is the same opening between the doors: a gap in space, in time, or in the fabric of a relationship.</p>

        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">时间</span>
          <span class="card-py">shíjiān</span>
          <span class="card-en">time; a period of time; the time (on the clock)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          时 shí (hour; time) + 间 jiān (interval). Time as a measurable span, the everyday word for it: 没有时间 "no time," 花时间 "to spend time," 什么时间 "at what time." Time understood as the interval between two moments.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我今天没有时间。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ jīntiān méiyǒu shíjiān.</div>

          </div>
          <div class="example">

            <div class="ex-py">Xué Zhōngwén xūyào hěn duō shíjiān.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Learning Chinese takes a lot of time.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 时间 vs. 时候</span>
          <strong>时间</strong> is time as a quantity or span you can have, spend, and run out of: 有时间 "to have time." <strong>时候</strong> shíhou is a point or occasion, the "when" of something: 小时候 "when (one was) little," 什么时候 "when?" One measures duration; the other locates a moment.
        </div>

    <!-- ── ROOM ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="room"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">房间</span>
      <span class="sh-py">fángjiān</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Room, the Measure Word</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="间">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>A room is a bounded space within walls, so the "space-between" character naturally came to name it. 房间 fángjiān is the everyday word for a room, and 间 also serves as the measure word for counting them: 一间房 yì jiān fáng (one room), 三间教室 sān jiān jiàoshì (three classrooms), 一间小店 (a small shop). When you count rooms, shops, or other walled spaces, 间 is the word that does the counting.</p>
      <p>The same logic builds the names of specific rooms: 车间 chējiān (a workshop, the "vehicle space" of a factory floor), 卫生间 wèishēngjiān (bathroom, the "hygiene room"), 洗手间 xǐshǒujiān (washroom). In each, 间 marks off one enclosed space from the rest of the building, the architectural cousin of the gap between the doors.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="间" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a pair of closed doors, 门, with a thin blade of sunlight, 日, showing through the crack where they meet. That sliver of light is the whole character: 间 is the gap between, the space-between made visible.</p>
      <p>Widen the gap and it becomes a span of time (时间), the interval between two moments. Set it in a row of people and it becomes the middle (中间). Wall it off and it becomes a room you can count one by one (一间房). Open it inside a relationship and it becomes either closeness with no gap (亲密无间) or the wedge that drives people apart (离间). Wherever 间 goes, look for the space between two things.</p>
