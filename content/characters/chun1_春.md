    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="春">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>Two stacked elements sit over a single anchor: 春 = the sprout-cluster over 屯 (the upper graph), with 日 (the sun) holding the base. 日 is the semantic anchor, sunlight as the engine of the season. The upper element traces back to a drawing of young shoots pushing up out of the ground, the same growth imagery that survives in 屯 (zhūn/tún), a graph of a seedling straining against hard earth as it breaks the surface.</p>
      <p>Read the picture from the bottom up: the sun shines, and under that warmth the new shoots strain upward. The oracle-bone and bronze forms show plant strokes clustered around the seedling graph with a sun riding alongside or below, the whole composition saying one thing, the moment when light reaches the cold ground and growth begins. By the small-seal stage the components had compacted into the tight three-part stack standard today, the plant strokes fused into the flat top, 日 squared off at the foot.</p>
      <p>The concrete sense (the growing season) extended early into the abstract. If spring is the surge of green after winter, then 春 becomes the surge of life in anything: youth (青春 qīngchūn, "green spring"), vigor, and from there the warmth of erotic feeling. Classical poetry uses 春 for desire so consistently that 春情 (chūnqíng) and 怀春 (huáichūn, "to harbor spring") read as longing without any further gloss. The same logic runs into the calendar, where 春 caps the year's first season and names its greatest festival.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps the graph as 春 (haru / shun), Korean as 춘 (chun), both carrying the season and the same overtone of youth and stirring feeling, a shared East Asian reading of one sun over one field of sprouts.</p>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="season"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">季节</span><span class="sh-py">jìjié</span><span class="sh-en">The Season on the Calendar</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">春天</span><span class="card-py">chūntiān</span><span class="card-en">spring; springtime</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">春 (spring) + 天 (tiān, day/season). The everyday word for the season, the one you reach for in conversation. 天 here marks a stretch of time rather than a single day, the same suffix in 夏天 (summer) and 冬天 (winter).</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">春天来了，树都绿了。</div><div class="ex-py">Chūntiān lái le, shù dōu lǜ le.</div><div class="ex-en">Spring has come, and the trees have all turned green.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">我最喜欢北京的春天。</div><div class="ex-py">Wǒ zuì xǐhuan Běijīng de chūntiān.</div><div class="ex-en">Spring is my favorite season in Beijing.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 春天 vs. 春季</span> 春天 is the warm spoken word; 春季 (chūnjì) is the calendar-quarter term used in weather reports, schedules, and formal writing.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">春节</span><span class="card-py">chūnjié</span><span class="card-en">Spring Festival; Chinese New Year</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">春 (spring) + 节 (jié, festival/node). The lunar new year, the largest holiday in the Chinese world. The name frames the new year as the gate of spring, the festival that opens the growing season rather than just turning the calendar.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">每年春节，我们全家都回老家。</div><div class="ex-py">Měi nián chūnjié, wǒmen quánjiā dōu huí lǎojiā.</div><div class="ex-en">Every Spring Festival, the whole family goes back to our hometown.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 春节 vs. 新年</span> 春节 is specifically the lunar new year and its festival; 新年 (xīnnián) can mean either the lunar or the January-1 new year depending on context.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">立春</span><span class="card-py">lìchūn</span><span class="card-en">Start of Spring (solar term)</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">立 (lì, to stand/establish) + 春 (spring). The first of the twenty-four solar terms (节气 jiéqì), falling around February 4, when the year's farming cycle is reckoned to begin. 立 marks a season being set up, the same verb in 立夏, 立秋, 立冬.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">过了立春，天气慢慢暖和起来。</div><div class="ex-py">Guò le lìchūn, tiānqì mànman nuǎnhuo qǐlái.</div><div class="ex-en">After the Start of Spring, the weather gradually warms up.</div></div>
        </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="vitality"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">生机</span><span class="sh-py">shēngjī</span><span class="sh-en">Vitality, Youth &amp; Feeling</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="青">
      <div class="scholar-label">引申义 yǐnshēnyì · Extended Sense</div>
      <p>Once spring means "the surge of new life," the character travels outward from the calendar. 春 stands in for youth, for vigor, and for the warmth of desire. The shoots breaking ground become the same image used for a young person's energy and for the first stir of love, which is why the cluster below reaches from 青春 (the green years) to 春风 (a spring wind that, by extension, means kindness and good fortune).</p>
    </div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">青春</span><span class="card-py">qīngchūn</span><span class="card-en">youth; the prime of life</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">青 (qīng, green/blue, the color of new growth) + 春 (spring). "Green spring," the years of youth at their freshest. Used for the whole season of being young, often with a note of how briefly it lasts.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">青春一去不复返。</div><div class="ex-py">Qīngchūn yī qù bù fù fǎn.</div><div class="ex-en">Youth, once gone, never returns.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他把青春献给了教育事业。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā bǎ qīngchūn xiàngěi le jiàoyù shìyè.</div><div class="ex-en">He gave his youth to the cause of education.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">春风</span><span class="card-py">chūnfēng</span><span class="card-en">spring breeze; warmth, favor</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">春 (spring) + 风 (fēng, wind). The mild wind of spring, and by extension any warm, reviving influence, a kind teacher, good news, a stroke of fortune. The phrase 春风得意 (chūnfēng déyì) describes riding high on success.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">老师的鼓励像一阵春风。</div><div class="ex-py">Lǎoshī de gǔlì xiàng yī zhèn chūnfēng.</div><div class="ex-en">The teacher's encouragement was like a spring breeze.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · literal vs. figurative</span> Weather reports use 春风 for the literal wind; in praise and poetry it almost always carries the warmth-and-favor sense.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">春意</span><span class="card-py">chūnyì</span><span class="card-en">the feel of spring; signs of spring</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">春 (spring) + 意 (yì, sense/intent). The atmosphere of spring in the air, the green budding and softening light that signal the season has arrived. 春意盎然 (chūnyì àngrán) describes a scene brimming with spring.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">公园里春意盎然，到处是花。</div><div class="ex-py">Gōngyuán lǐ chūnyì àngrán, dàochù shì huā.</div><div class="ex-en">The park is brimming with spring, flowers everywhere.</div></div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="春" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture the morning after the last frost. A low sun (日, the base of the character) clears the horizon and lands on a bare field. Where the light touches, green shoots strain upward through the cold crust, that straining seedling is the 屯 wedged in the middle, and the sprout-strokes fan out across the top. The whole glyph is one frame, sun at the foot pushing growth up through the body of the character.</p>
      <p>Hold that frame and the compounds fall into place. 春天 (chūntiān) is simply that field across a whole season. 青春 (qīngchūn) is the same shoots, now standing for a young life still rising. 春风 (chūnfēng) is the warm wind crossing the field, the breath that coaxes the green up, which is why it also means kindness and good fortune.</p>
      <p>For the festival, see the field at dawn on lunar new year, red couplets (春联) on the doorframe, the household stepping out into the first light of the growing year. Sun under shoots: that single image carries the season, the youth, the warmth, and the holiday all at once.</p>
