    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure: A Path With No Obstruction</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="通">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>通 tōng</strong> is a pictophonetic compound: the walk-movement radical 辶 (chuò, a foot moving along a road, abbreviated from 辵 which shows a foot and a crossroads) carries the meaning, while 甬 yǒng supplies the sound. The 辶 places 通 squarely in the family of motion characters, the same radical that drives 道 (the Way), 过 (to pass through), 近 (near), and 远 (far). Where those characters measure distance or name the path, 通 names a different quality of the path: that it is open, that movement along it meets no obstacle.</p>
      <p>The phonetic 甬 is itself worth noticing. In its own right it named a covered walkway or a lane, a passage with walls on either side that channels movement straight through. So even the sound-bearing half of 通 carries an echo of corridors and through-routes. The combined character means to go through, to pass without being stopped, to get from one side to the other.</p>
      <p>The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 通 as 達也 (dá yě), to reach through, to arrive by passing through. From this root sense of unobstructed passage every later meaning radiates. A road that is 通 is open to traffic. A message that 通 has gotten through to its recipient. A person who is 通 in a subject has passed all the way through it and understands it thoroughly. And something that is 通 across many cases is general, common, the ordinary state that holds throughout. Passage, communication, mastery, and generality are one image seen from four sides: nothing blocks the way.</p>
      <p>通 carries the same range across the Sinosphere. Japanese 通 (tsū / tsūjiru) gives 交通 kōtsū (traffic), 通信 tsūshin (communication), and 通 used alone for a connoisseur, someone who has passed all the way into a field. Korean 통 (tong) appears in 교통 (gyotong, traffic) and 통신 (tongsin, communication). Vietnamese thông survives in giao thông (traffic) and thông tin (information), direct borrowings of the Chinese compounds.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">构词基础 gòucí jīchǔ · 通 as semantic base</span>
      <strong>通</strong> (to go through) + <strong>过 guò</strong> (to cross; to pass) = <strong>通过</strong> (to pass through; by means of)<br>
      <strong>交 jiāo</strong> (to exchange; to cross) + <strong>通</strong> = <strong>交通</strong> (traffic; transport)<br>
      <strong>通</strong> + <strong>知 zhī</strong> (to make known) = <strong>通知</strong> (to notify; a notice)<br>
      <strong>普 pǔ</strong> (widespread; universal) + <strong>通</strong> = <strong>普通</strong> (ordinary; common)<br>
      <strong>精 jīng</strong> (refined; thorough) + <strong>通</strong> = <strong>精通</strong> (to be expert in; to master)<br>
      The pattern: 通 contributes the idea of getting all the way through, whether through a road, a message, a subject, or every case alike.
    </div>

It is equally a function word meaning "by means of" or "via," introducing the channel through which an action happens: 通过努力 (through effort), 通过电话 (by phone). Historically it meant to exchange letters: 我们通了多年信 (we corresponded for many years). In the modern era it has become the technical term for telecommunications: 通信技术 (communications technology), 通信卫星 (a communications satellite).         <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">打通</span>
          <span class="card-py">dǎtōng</span>
          <span class="card-en">to get through (a call); to open up (a passage); to clear a blockage</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">VC 动补</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">打 dǎ (to make; to strike; to do) + 通 (to get through). A resultative verb: to do something until it is through. Most often heard about phone calls (电话打通了, the call got through), but also used for clearing any obstructed passage, physical or figurative: 打通关节 (to clear the way through the right connections). The 通 is the result, the moment the obstruction gives way and the connection completes.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我打了好几次，电话终于打通了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ dǎ le hǎojǐ cì, diànhuà zhōngyú dǎtōng le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I called several times, and the call finally got through.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · the measure word reading tòng</span>
          In the second tone-one reading 通 tòng serves as a measure word for letters, calls, and stretches of talking or scolding: 一通电话 (a phone call), 打了一通 (gave them an earful). This is a separate reading from the verb 打通 (dǎtōng) above, where 通 keeps its first-tone sense of getting through.
        </div>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">普通</span>
          <span class="card-py">pǔtōng</span>
          <span class="card-en">ordinary; common; general; average</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">普 pǔ (widespread; universal) + 通 (general; running throughout). Widespread and holding everywhere: the quality of being ordinary, the common case rather than the special one. 普通人 (an ordinary person), 普通话 (the common spoken standard, Mandarin). 普通 is neutral, neither praise nor complaint; it simply locates something in the broad middle where most things sit.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他只是一个普通的上班族。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā zhǐ shì yīgè pǔtōng de shàngbānzú.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He is just an ordinary office worker.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请用普通话交流。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng yòng pǔtōnghuà jiāoliú.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please communicate in Mandarin.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 普通 vs 一般</span>
          Both mean "ordinary," but they lean differently. 普通 describes a thing as belonging to the common, widespread type: 普通人 (an ordinary person), 普通房间 (a standard room). 一般 (yībān) leans toward "average" or "so-so" as a judgment of degree: 他的成绩一般 (his grades are mediocre). You can call a meal 很普通 (quite ordinary) without criticism, but 很一般 nudges toward "nothing special."
        </div>
      </div>

The case that holds across most instances: usually, as the general rule. 通常 sets up the ordinary expectation against which exceptions are measured: 我通常七点起床 (I usually get up at seven). It reads as slightly more formal than 一般 and is common in writing and careful speech.</div>

        <span class="cy-py">sì tōng bā dá</span>
        <span class="cy-en">"open on four sides, reaching to eight": extending in all directions; well connected</span>
        <span class="cy-note">四 sì (four) + 通 (open through) + 八 bā (eight) + 达 dá (to reach). Roads that are open through the four cardinal directions and reach out to the eight points of the compass. The phrase describes a place so well connected that one can travel from it anywhere: a transport hub, a crossroads city, a network with no dead ends. Used in praise of a city's location or an organization's reach. The four-and-eight pairing is a stock way of saying "every direction without exception."</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">通情达理</span>
        <span class="cy-py">tōng qíng dá lǐ</span>
        <span class="cy-en">"through to feeling, reaching to reason": sensible, reasonable, understanding</span>
        <span class="cy-note">通 (to be through to) + 情 qíng (human feeling; the way people are) + 达 dá (to reach) + 理 lǐ (reason; principle). A person who is 通情达理 understands both how people feel and what reason requires, and weighs the two fairly. It is high praise for someone you can argue with productively: not rigid, not merely emotional, but able to see a situation from all sides and respond justly. Often used of an elder, a boss, or a mediator who can be trusted to be fair.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">融会贯通</span>
        <span class="cy-py">róng huì guàn tōng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">"melt together, thread through": to master a subject by integrating all its parts</span>
        <span class="cy-note">融 róng (to melt; to merge) + 会 huì (to come together) + 贯 guàn (to thread through; to run a string through) + 通 (to penetrate fully). To melt separate pieces of knowledge into one whole and thread a single understanding through all of them, so that the parts connect and the subject opens up completely. The phrase, associated with the learning method discussed by the Neo-Confucian philosopher Zhu Xi, describes the moment study stops being a heap of facts and becomes an integrated grasp. The ideal of deep, connected learning rather than rote accumulation.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">水泄不通</span>
        <span class="cy-py">shuǐ xiè bù tōng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">"not even water can leak through": packed solid; completely blocked or surrounded</span>
        <span class="cy-note">水 shuǐ (water) + 泄 xiè (to leak; to drain out) + 不 bù (not) + 通 (to pass through). So densely packed that not even a drop of water could trickle out. The phrase describes a crowd, a traffic jam, or a siege so tight that nothing can get through: 挤得水泄不通 (jammed so tight not even water could leak through). It is the vivid negative of 通, the image of total obstruction set against the character's core sense of free passage.</span>
      </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="通">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>A foot setting off down a covered corridor that runs straight from one end to the other, with no door bolted across it and no wall built halfway. That is the picture inside 通: the walk-movement radical 辶 is the foot on the move, and the phonetic 甬 was itself a name for a walled passage that channels you straight through. Whatever the corridor leads to, the point is that you reach it. Nothing stops the way.</p>
      <p>Hold that one image and every meaning falls out of it. A road that lets vehicles run from end to end is 通车, open to traffic; the whole crossing-and-running network is 交通. A phone call that finds its way to the person you dialed has 打通; a message that reaches its recipient is a 通知; people who open a channel of understanding between them 沟通. A person who has walked all the way through a subject down to its fine grain is 精通, a master of it. And what holds throughout, the common case running across all instances, is 普通, ordinary, or 通用, good everywhere.</p>
      <p>The negative is just as sharp. When the corridor is jammed so tight that not even water could leak through, you have 水泄不通, the exact opposite of 通. Keep both ends in mind: the open corridor that lets everything pass, and the blockage that lets nothing. 通 is always the version where the way is clear.</p>
