    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="望">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>望 wàng began as a vivid picture of looking far.</strong> The early graph shows a person standing tall on a mound, with a large staring eye lifted to take in the distance; later forms added 月 (the moon), the very thing one strains upward to see across the night sky. The phonetic element 亡 (wáng) lends the sound. In the modern eleven-stroke form the 月 (moon) sits at the lower left and 王 (king) at the lower right, with 亡 riding on top, the components plainly visible. The simplified and traditional forms are identical here; only one glyph exists.</p>
      <p>From that one image of a raised, far-reaching gaze, three senses grow, moving from the eye outward to the heart and finally to the eyes of others:</p>
      <p><strong>1. To gaze, to look into the distance.</strong> The literal act of looking far: 眺望 tiàowàng (to gaze into the distance), 张望 zhāngwàng (to look around), 望远镜 wàngyuǎnjìng (telescope, literally a glass for looking far).<br>
      <strong>2. To hope, to expect.</strong> The inner gaze toward what has not yet come: 希望 xīwàng (to hope), 期望 qīwàng (to expect), 盼望 pànwàng (to look forward to), 渴望 kěwàng (to crave), 失望 shīwàng (to be disappointed, literally to lose the hope).<br>
      <strong>3. Reputation, prestige.</strong> Being looked up to by others: 名望 míngwàng (fame, standing), 威望 wēiwàng (prestige), 声望 shēngwàng (reputation).</p>
      <p>A fourth, grammatical use survives as a coverb meaning <em>toward</em>, much like 朝 cháo or 向 xiàng: 望前看 wàng qián kàn (to look toward the front, to look ahead). The thread through all of them is the lifted, directed gaze: the eye looks toward the horizon, the heart looks toward what it longs for, and the world looks toward the person it esteems.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── TO GAZE & LOOK AFAR ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="gaze"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">眺望</span>
      <span class="sh-py">tiàowàng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">To Gaze &amp; Look Afar</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="望">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The most concrete face of 望 is the literal act of looking into the distance, the gaze that strains beyond what is near at hand. 眺望 tiàowàng is the elevated, deliberate version, gazing out from a high place over a wide view. 张望 zhāngwàng is restless and searching, the eyes darting this way and that to find something. And 望远镜 wàngyuǎnjìng, the telescope, freezes the idea in an object: a tool whose whole purpose is to make the far near. All three keep the original picture of a person on a mound, eye lifted toward the horizon.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── TO HOPE & EXPECT ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="hope"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">希望</span>
      <span class="sh-py">xīwàng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">To Hope &amp; Expect</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="望">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Turn the gaze inward and 望 becomes the look of the heart toward what has not yet come. 希望 xīwàng is the broad, hopeful version, both the verb to hope and the noun hope itself. 期望 qīwàng is firmer, an expectation tied to a set time or a definite outcome, often what one person expects of another. 盼望 pànwàng is warm and longing, a yearning fixed on something one waits eagerly for. 渴望 kěwàng is hotter still, a thirsting craving. And when the awaited thing fails to arrive, the gaze collapses into 失望 shīwàng, literally to lose the hope: disappointment.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 希望 -->
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">

        <div class="card-def">
          希 xī (to wish for) + 望 wàng (to look toward). The everyday word for hoping, as a verb and as a noun: 我希望你早点回来 "I hope you come back soon," 充满希望 "full of hope." Broad and neutral, the default way to express a wish for some future good.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我希望明天天气好。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ xīwàng míngtiān tiānqì hǎo.</div>

            <div class="ex-cn">孩子是一个家庭的希望。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Háizi shì yí ge jiātíng de xīwàng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Children are the hope of a family.</div>
          </div>

        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 希望 vs. 期望</span>
          <strong>希望</strong> xīwàng is a plain wish for some future good, light and very common: I hope it does not rain. <strong>期望</strong> qīwàng is heavier and more demanding, an expectation placed on a person or outcome, often with a standard attached: parents 期望 a great deal of their children. You can 希望 for almost anything, but you 期望 when there is a real claim or anticipation behind the wish.
        </div>
      </div>

    <!-- ── REPUTATION & PRESTIGE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="reputation"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">名望</span>
      <span class="sh-py">míngwàng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Reputation &amp; Prestige</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="望">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The third sense turns the gaze around: now it is others who look toward the person, with respect. 望 here means the standing one holds in the eyes of the world. 名望 míngwàng pairs it with 名 (name) for fame and good repute. 威望 wēiwàng pairs it with 威 (authority, awe) for the prestige that commands deference, the standing of a leader or an elder. 声望 shēngwàng pairs it with 声 (voice, reputation) for general public esteem. All three share the picture of many eyes lifted toward one esteemed figure, the original gaze inverted.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">望子成龙</span>
        <span class="cy-py">wàng zǐ chéng lóng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to hope ones son becomes a dragon: to long for ones child to succeed greatly</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "to hope the son becomes a dragon," the dragon being the emblem of supreme success and distinction. The classic expression of parental ambition: the deep hope that ones child will rise high in life. A close partner phrase, 望女成凤, hopes the daughter becomes a phoenix.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">大失所望</span>
        <span class="cy-py">dà shī suǒ wàng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to be greatly let down: bitterly disappointed in what one had hoped for</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "greatly to lose what one hoped for." Used when reality falls far short of expectation and the disappointment is severe, as when an eagerly awaited result turns out badly. The intensified form of plain 失望.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">望尘莫及</span>
        <span class="cy-py">wàng chén mò jí</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to see only the dust and never catch up: to be hopelessly outdistanced</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "to gaze at the dust and be unable to reach." One can see only the dust kicked up by someone far ahead, with no hope of catching them. Used to admit that another is so far superior or so far in front that one cannot hope to match them.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">望梅止渴</span>
        <span class="cy-py">wàng méi zhǐ kě</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to quench thirst by thinking of plums: to comfort oneself with illusions</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "to look toward plums to stop ones thirst." From a tale of the warlord Cao Cao, who spurred on his parched troops by telling them a plum grove lay just ahead, so the thought of the sour fruit set their mouths watering. Now it means consoling oneself, or others, with hopes that cannot really be fulfilled.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Hold one picture at the center of 望: a figure standing on a mound, eye lifted, gazing toward the moon and the far horizon. That single raised gaze is the seed of every sense.</p>
      <p>Point the gaze at the distance and you get the literal look: 眺望 to gaze afar, 张望 to look around, 望远镜 the telescope that makes the far near, 一望无际 a view that runs to the edge of sight. Turn the gaze inward, toward what the heart longs for, and you get hope: 希望 to hope, 期望 to expect, 盼望 to long for, 渴望 to crave, and its shadow 失望, to lose the hope. Turn the gaze around so that others look toward you with respect, and you get reputation: 名望 standing, 威望 prestige, 声望 esteem. Even the coverb 望前看, to look toward the front, keeps the same arrow of a directed gaze. Whenever you meet 望, ask which way the eyes are pointed: out to the horizon, in toward a longing, or back toward a person the world holds high.</p>
