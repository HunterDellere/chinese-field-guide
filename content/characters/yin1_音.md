    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="音">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>音 yīn is the picture of a mouth with a sound marked inside it.</strong> The early form is the graph for 言 yán (speech, itself a mouth with a tongue) carrying one extra stroke laid across the opening of the mouth. That added mark is the sound being made audible: not the word spoken but the very noise of it, the tone leaving the lips. 音 and 言 are close kin in the old script, and 音 splits off to name the heard quality of speech rather than its content.</p>
      <p>音 is also a radical in its own right, the head of a small family of characters about resonance and rhythm. 韵 yùn (rhyme; resonance) and 韶 sháo (a kind of ancient court music) both carry it, and the dictionary files them under 音.</p>
      <p>Three senses grow from the marked mouth:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Sound, tone, note.</strong> The heard thing itself: 声音 shēngyīn (sound), 音乐 yīnyuè (music), 噪音 zàoyīn (noise).<br>
      <strong>2. Pronunciation, syllable.</strong> The sound shape of speech: 发音 fāyīn (pronunciation), 拼音 pīnyīn (the spelled-out sound), 口音 kǒuyīn (accent).<br>
      <strong>3. News, tidings.</strong> Sound traveling as word from afar: 音信 yīnxìn (news; message), 佳音 jiāyīn (good news).</p>
      <p>The thread is the sound that leaves a mouth and reaches an ear. Heard as melody it is music; shaped into syllables it is pronunciation; carried across distance it becomes news. Wherever 音 appears, listen for the noise itself rather than the meaning behind it.</p>
    </div>

          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 音 vs. 声</span>

    <!-- ── PRONUNCIATION ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="pronunciation"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">发音</span>
      <span class="sh-py">fāyīn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Pronunciation &amp; Syllables</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="音">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The second great field of 音 is the sound shape of language: not what a word means but how it is voiced. A learner meets this 音 on the very first day, in 拼音 pīnyīn, the system that spells Mandarin sounds in the Latin alphabet. 拼 pīn means to piece together, so 拼音 is the sound pieced together from its parts: an initial, a final, and a tone.</p>
      <p>From there the field opens up. 发音 fāyīn is pronunciation, the act of issuing a sound. 口音 kǒuyīn is the accent, the regional flavor a speaker carries. 元音 yuányīn (vowel) and 辅音 fǔyīn (consonant) name the building blocks of the syllable. In every one, 音 is the syllable considered as pure sound, the thing the mouth produces before meaning is attached.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── NEWS ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="news"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">音信</span>
      <span class="sh-py">yīnxìn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">News &amp; Tidings</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="音">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The third sense is the most literary: sound as word carried across distance. When a voice or a letter reaches you from far away, it is 音, the sound of someone heard again. 音信 yīnxìn pairs it with 信 xìn (letter; message) to mean news or word of a person, and the phrase 杳无音信 yǎo wú yīnxìn ("vanished without a sound or a letter") is the standard way to say someone has dropped entirely out of contact.</p>
      <p>The sense leans warm and hopeful in 佳音 jiāyīn, good news, the welcome word one waits for. In a religious register, 福音 fúyīn (literally "fortune-sound") is the term Chinese uses for the Christian gospel, the good tidings. Across all of these, 音 keeps its first meaning intact: it is still a sound reaching an ear, only now the sound has traveled a long way to get there.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="音" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>See a mouth open, and one stroke laid across it to mark the sound coming out. That marked sound is 音. Follow it outward in three directions. Let it ring as melody and it is music (音乐); let it grate and it is noise (噪音). Shape it into syllables and it becomes pronunciation, the sound the mouth makes before meaning (发音, 拼音, 口音). Send it across a distance and it arrives as news, the voice of someone heard again from far away (音信, 佳音).</p>
      <p>Keep 音 apart from its kin 声 shēng. 声 is the sound at its source, the cry or the voice, the unit tones are counted in. 音 is the sound as it reaches the ear: the note, the timbre, the pronunciation. When you meet 音, listen for the heard sound, never the meaning underneath it.</p>
