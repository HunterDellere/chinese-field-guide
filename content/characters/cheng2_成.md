    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="成">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>成 chéng is built on 戈 gē, the halberd.</strong> The dominant element is 戈, the ancient dagger-axe carried on a long shaft, and the early forms add a small marking stroke to it. The reading is one of a task brought to a finish: the weapon set down once the work of building, defending, or settling a thing is done. Where 戈 elsewhere signals cutting and warfare, here it is read as completion, the moment a labor reaches its full form and stands finished. The simplified and traditional shapes are identical, so the same glyph appears in both columns above.</p>
      <p>From that core image of a thing brought to completion, several senses fan out:</p>
      <p><strong>1. To become, to turn into.</strong> One state finished and a new one standing in its place: 成为 chéngwéi (to become), 变成 biànchéng (to turn into).<br>
      <strong>2. To succeed, to accomplish.</strong> A labor carried through to its end: 成功 chénggōng (to succeed), 完成 wánchéng (to complete), 成就 chéngjiù (achievement).<br>
      <strong>3. Alright, that works.</strong> A colloquial yes that settles a matter on the spot: a bare 成 chéng meaning "fine, agreed, it works."<br>
      <strong>4. A tenth, ten percent.</strong> As a fraction word, 成 names one part in ten: 一成 yī chéng (ten percent), 九成 jiǔ chéng (ninety percent).<br>
      <strong>5. Ready-made, established, set.</strong> Already finished and fixed: 成语 chéngyǔ (a set idiom), 成绩 chéngjì (results, grades), 成熟 chéngshú (mature, ripe).</p>
      <p>The thread through every sense is a process carried to its full conclusion. Whether the outcome is a new state, a won success, a settled agreement, a fixed fraction, or a finished and ripened form, 成 marks the point where becoming is done and the result stands complete.</p>
    </div>

        <div class="card-def">

          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 成 vs. 变</span>
          <strong>成</strong> chéng is the finish line: a state completed, a result standing whole. <strong>变</strong> biàn is the shifting itself: change in progress, with no promise of where it lands. They pair in 变成, where 变 supplies the movement and 成 supplies the arrival, so the phrase means the change is done and the new form is set.

    <!-- ── SUCCEED ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="succeed"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">成功</span>
      <span class="sh-py">chénggōng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Succeed &amp; Complete</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="成">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The sense of 成 that learners meet most often is the one of carrying a task all the way through. <strong>成功</strong> chénggōng pairs 成 with 功 gōng (merit, the result of effort) to mean success, the labor brought to its rewarding end. <strong>完成</strong> wánchéng pairs 完 wán (whole, finished) with 成 to mean completion, the job done with nothing left over. And <strong>成就</strong> chéngjiù names the achievement itself, the lasting thing that effort produces.</p>
      <p>Notice how often 成 sits in the second slot, where it acts almost like a result-marking ending: 做成 zuòchéng (to get something done), 写成 xiěchéng (to finish writing), 完成 wánchéng (to complete). In these the first character names the action and 成 reports that it reached completion. The same finishing 成 turns up in 达成 dáchéng (to reach, as in reaching an agreement), where what is finished is a meeting of minds.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── MATURE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="mature"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">成熟</span>
      <span class="sh-py">chéngshú</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Results &amp; Maturity: the Ripened 成</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="成">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>A thing that has finished becoming is a thing that is ready, set, fully grown. From this sense 成 reaches into the language of results and ripeness. <strong>成绩</strong> chéngjì names the finished record of how one did, school grades or any measured outcome of effort. <strong>成熟</strong> chéngshú means mature or ripe, said of fruit on the branch, of a plan ready to act on, and of a person grown into steadiness.</p>
      <p>The same fixed-and-finished 成 stands at the head of 成语 chéngyǔ, the set idioms of the language. A 成语 is a phrase already cast in a settled four-character form, ready-made and passed down whole, not assembled fresh each time. Here 成 means established rather than newly succeeded: the wording is finished and waits, complete, for use.</p>
    </div>

      <p>See the halberd, 戈, set down at the end of a day's labor: the wall is built, the work is defended, the task stands finished. That weapon resting over a completed job is the whole character. Everything 成 means is the moment a process reaches its full conclusion.</p>
      <p>On a person, that finish is becoming, one self left behind and a new one standing whole (成为). On a labor, it is success, the effort carried through to its reward (成功). On a job, it is completion, nothing left undone (完成). On fruit or on character, it is ripeness, fully grown and ready (成熟). And on a phrase, it is the set idiom, cast once and handed down unchanged (成语). Whenever you meet 成, ask what has just been finished, and you will find the result standing complete.</p>
