    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="阳">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>阳 yáng pictures a sunlit mound.</strong> On the left stands 阝, the mound or hill radical (the so-called "left ear," from 阜 fù, a slope or earthwork); on the right sits 日 rì, the sun. The image is a hill with the sun on it, and the original sense is concrete and geographic: the sunny side of a slope, the south face that catches the light all day while the far side stays in shade. From that bright slope the meaning opened outward into light itself, into brightness, and into the whole cosmic idea of the active, warming, masculine force.</p>
      <p>The character carries a simplified-traditional pair worth noticing. The simplified form 阳 is the clean mound-plus-sun you see today; the traditional form <strong>陽</strong> keeps the same mound radical but replaces the simple 日 with a fuller phonetic element 昜 yáng (sun-rays streaming down), which is why the traditional glyph looks so much more elaborate. Both forms read yáng and mean the same thing; the simplification swapped the busy right side for a plain sun.</p>
      <p>Three senses branch from the sunlit hill:</p>
      <p><strong>1. The sun, sunlight.</strong> The light of day made into a thing: 太阳 tàiyáng (the sun), 阳光 yángguāng (sunlight).<br>
      <strong>2. The yang principle.</strong> In cosmology 阳 is the bright, warm, active, masculine half of the universe, forever paired with 阴 yīn, its dark, cool, receptive opposite: 阴阳 yīnyáng.<br>
      <strong>3. The sunny side, in place names.</strong> A town on the sunlit (south) face of a hill or the north bank of a river often takes 阳 in its name: 洛阳 Luòyáng, 沈阳 Shěnyáng. The same sense gives 阳台 yángtái, the balcony that faces the sun.</p>
      <p>The thread is the south slope in full light. Sun, brightness, warmth, the active force, the side that faces the day: every sense of 阳 leans toward the light, just as its partner 阴 leans into the shade.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── YIN-YANG ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="yinyang"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">阴阳</span>
      <span class="sh-py">yīnyáng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Yang &amp; the Pair: the Active Principle</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="阳">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Beyond the sun in the sky, 阳 names one half of the oldest pair in Chinese thought. 阳 is the bright, warm, active, expanding, masculine force; 阴 yīn is its dark, cool, still, receptive, feminine complement. The geography is right there in the two characters: 阳 is the sunlit south slope, 阴 is the shaded north slope, and the same hill holds both. They are not good and evil but two faces of one whole, each defined by the other and each carrying a seed of its opposite.</p>
      <p>Together they form <strong>阴阳</strong> yīnyáng, the framework that underlies traditional cosmology, the <em>Book of Changes</em>, and Chinese medicine alike. In the body, health is read as a balance of 阴 and 阳; a fever or restlessness may be called an excess of 阳气 yángqì, the warm active energy. Note the order in speech: the pair is almost always said 阴阳, dark before light, even though 阳 leads in the place names. To go deeper on the shaded counterpart, the 阴 entry traces the same logic from the other side.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="bright"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">向阳</span>
      <span class="sh-py">xiàngyáng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Sunny Side &amp; Place Names</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="阳">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The oldest, most concrete sense of 阳 survives in geography. A spot on the sunny (south) face of a hill, or on the north bank of a river where the water reflects light back, was the 阳, and countless Chinese towns carry that brightness in their names: 洛阳 Luòyáng on the sun-facing bank of the Luo, 沈阳 Shěnyáng, 贵阳 Guìyáng. Read a map of China and you are reading a record of which slopes caught the light.</p>
      <p>The same logic gives ordinary words for the sunward side of things. 向阳 xiàngyáng is "facing the sun," said of a south-facing room or a slope that gets full light; 阳台 yángtái is the balcony, the platform built to take in the sun. Wherever 阳 sits in a name, it points at the warm, lit side of the world.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="阳" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
