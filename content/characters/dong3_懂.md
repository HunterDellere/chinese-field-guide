    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="懂">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>懂 splits cleanly down the middle: 懂 = 忄 + 董. The left side is 忄, the compressed standing form of 心 (xīn, heart), the radical that supplies meaning. The right side is 董 (dǒng), which carries the sound. This is a textbook pictophonetic, and the two halves do exactly the work their position assigns them. 忄 places the word in the territory of the heart-mind, where thought and feeling are the same organ; 董 lends its pronunciation almost unchanged.</p>
      <p>The choice of 忄 is the whole argument of the character. Classical Chinese did not separate intellect from emotion. The heart (心) was the seat of cognition, so a verb for comprehension naturally grows a heart radical rather than an eye or an ear. To 懂 something is to let it settle inward and become part of you, not merely to register it on the surface.</p>
      <p>懂 is a comparatively late graph; it does not appear in the oracle bones or the Shuōwén Jiězì, and it rose to its modern dominance in vernacular Mandarin, where it became the everyday verb for understanding. The phonetic 董 itself originally named a kind of plant and the surname it later became, but in 懂 its botanical sense drops away entirely and only the sound remains.</p>
      <p>In modern usage 懂 leans toward grasped, lived comprehension: you 懂 a language because you can move inside it, you 懂 a person because you sense what they mean. The colder, more clinical 理解 (lǐjiě, to comprehend analytically) covers the same ground from the outside; 懂 covers it from within the heart radical it was built on.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">听看</span><span class="sh-py">tīng kàn</span><span class="sh-en">Understanding Through the Senses</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      <p>懂 is the favorite resultative complement for the verbs of perception. 动词 + 懂 says that the act of hearing or seeing landed: the sound or the sight passed through the sense and arrived at comprehension. Negate it with 不 for failure (听不懂, can't make it out) and 得 for capability (看得懂, can manage to read it).</p>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="懂" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a small upright heart standing at a doorway, leaning in close to listen. That is the left side of the character, 忄, the standing form of 心 pressed thin against the frame. On the right, 董, a dense tangle of strokes, is the muffled noise coming from the room: foreign words, a complicated argument, a film with no subtitles. The heart presses nearer and nearer until the tangle suddenly resolves into sense. That click of resolution is 懂.</p>
      <p>The picture explains why this verb, not 理解, fills the resultative slot after the senses. 听懂 is the heart catching what the ear let in; 看懂 is the heart catching what the eye let in. In each case the eye or ear opens the door, but it is the little standing heart on the left that does the final grasping. When the noise stays a tangle, you reach for 半懂不懂, half in, half out, the heart still pressed to the door.</p>
