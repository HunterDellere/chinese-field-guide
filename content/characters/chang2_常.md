    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>

      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="常">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>常 cháng = 尚 (shàng, the phonetic) + 巾 (jīn, cloth).</strong> A pictophonetic compound: 巾, the cloth radical at the base, supplies the meaning, while 尚 on top supplies the sound (shàng → cháng). The original referent was a long lower garment, a length of cloth that hung full and steady. From "the long, full-hanging cloth" the character generalized to its lasting modern senses, and the concrete garment meaning was later written with the separate character 裳.</p>
      <p>Three senses grow from the idea of what hangs steady and endures:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Constant, lasting, unchanging.</strong> What persists rather than passing: 经常 jīngcháng (regularly), 常常 chángcháng (often), and the philosophical 无常 wúcháng (impermanence).<br>
      <strong>2. Ordinary, common, normal.</strong> What is usual rather than special: 平常 píngcháng (ordinary), 正常 zhèngcháng (normal), 通常 tōngcháng (usually).<br>
      <strong>3. The constant principle.</strong> The standing rule or shared ground: 常识 chángshí (common sense), and the classical 纲常 gāngcháng, the constant bonds of moral order.</p>
      <p>The thread is endurance. Whether the subject is how often something happens, how ordinary it is, or what truth holds firm over time, 常 names what does not change: the steady cloth of the everyday.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">经常</span>
          <span class="card-py">jīngcháng</span>
          <span class="card-en">often; frequently; regularly</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          经 jīng (to pass through; constant) + 常 cháng (constant). The standard adverb for an action that recurs as a regular habit: 我经常去那家店 "I go to that shop often." Stresses regularity, not just repetition.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他经常迟到。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā jīngcháng chídào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He is often late.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们经常一起吃饭。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen jīngcháng yìqǐ chī fàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We often eat together.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 经常 vs. 常常</span>
          <strong>经常</strong> leans on settled regularity and reads slightly more written: 经常锻炼 "exercises regularly." <strong>常常</strong> is warmer and more colloquial, simply "often, time and again": 他常常忘事 "he often forgets things." In most everyday sentences they are interchangeable.
        </div>
      </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="constant"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">常识</span>
      <span class="sh-py">chángshí</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Constant &amp; Common</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="常">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Beneath frequency and ordinariness lies the oldest sense of 常: the constant, the principle that holds firm. 常识 chángshí, "constant knowledge," is the common sense everyone is assumed to share. In classical thought the 五常 wǔcháng are the five constant virtues, and 纲常 gāngcháng names the enduring bonds of the moral order. Against this stands the Buddhist 无常 wúcháng, impermanence: the teaching that nothing in fact stays constant, that all things rise and pass.</p>
      <p>This is why 常 can feel reassuring and unremarkable at once. What is 常 is what you can count on, the steady backdrop that does not surprise you. To say a thing is 反常 fǎncháng, against the constant, is to mark it as strange or alarming precisely because the 常 is what we expect the world to keep doing.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a long banner of cloth, 巾, hanging full and steady in the same place day after day. That steadiness is the whole character. What is 常 is what stays: it hangs there always, unchanging, ordinary precisely because it is always there.</p>
      <p>From that steady cloth, three readings follow. An action that recurs as steadily as the banner hangs is done 经常, often. A thing as unremarkable as the everyday backdrop is 平常, ordinary. And a truth that holds firm over time is the 常 of 常识, common sense. When something breaks the pattern, it is 反常, against the constant, and that is exactly why it startles.</p>
