    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="话">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>话 huà = 讠 (yán, speech) + 舌 (shé, tongue).</strong> The traditional form 話 writes the speech radical in full as 言; the simplified 话 compresses it to 讠. Either way the logic is the same: speech put together with the tongue, the organ that shapes it. Where the bare speech radical 讠 marks anything done through the mouth, the added 舌 narrows it to the living, spoken word, the thing the tongue actually produces.</p>
      <p>From spoken words, the meaning spreads along three lines:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Words, speech, what one says.</strong> The literal sense: 说话 shuōhuà (to speak), 一句话 yí jù huà (a single remark), 听话 tīnghuà (to obey, literally "to heed words").<br>
      <strong>2. A language or dialect.</strong> A whole spoken variety: 普通话 pǔtōnghuà (Mandarin, the "common speech"), 广东话 Guǎngdōnghuà (Cantonese), 中国话 (the Chinese language as spoken).<br>
      <strong>3. A topic or saying.</strong> A unit of talk passed between people: 话题 huàtí (topic), 笑话 xiàohua (joke), 童话 tónghuà (fairy tale).</p>
      <p>The thread is the spoken word as a living thing that leaves the tongue and travels: said aloud, carried down a wire, shaped into a dialect, or handed around as a story. 话 is talk in motion, distinct from 字 (the written character) and 语 (language as a system).</p>

    <div class="section-head">

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 说话 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">说话</span>
          <span class="card-py">shuōhuà</span>
          <span class="card-en">to speak; to talk; to say something</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          说 shuō (to say) + 话 huà (words). The everyday verb for speaking, formed as verb-plus-object: literally "to say words." Because 话 is already the object, you do not say 说话一件事; to speak about something you use 说 alone: 说一件事.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请大声说话。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng dàshēng shuōhuà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please speak loudly.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他在跟朋友说话。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā zài gēn péngyou shuōhuà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He is talking with a friend.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 听话 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">听话</span>
          <span class="card-py">tīnghuà</span>
          <span class="card-en">to be obedient; to heed what one is told</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          听 tīng (to listen) + 话 huà (words). To listen to the words, hence to do as told. Said above all of children and, gently, of anyone who follows guidance: 这孩子很听话 "this child is very obedient."
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个孩子很听话。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège háizi hěn tīnghuà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This child is very obedient.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 实话 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">实话</span>
          <span class="card-py">shíhuà</span>
          <span class="card-en">the truth; honest words</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          实 shí (real; true) + 话 huà (words). Words that match the facts. The set phrase 说实话 shuō shíhuà, "to tell the truth," also works as an aside meaning "honestly speaking."
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">说实话，我不太喜欢。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Shuō shíhuà, wǒ bú tài xǐhuan.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Honestly, I don't really like it.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 话 vs. 字 vs. 语</span>
          <strong>话</strong> huà is the spoken word, talk as it leaves the mouth. <strong>字</strong> zì is the written character on the page. <strong>语</strong> yǔ is language as a system (语言 yǔyán, 汉语 Hànyǔ). So 普通话 is the spoken standard you hear, while 汉语 names the Chinese language overall.
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>See the speech radical 讠 standing beside a tongue, 舌. That is the whole character: speech shaped by the tongue, the living spoken word. Everything 话 means is talk in motion as it leaves the mouth.</p>
      <p>Said plainly to another person, it is speaking (说话) and being heeded (听话). Carried down a wire, it is the telephone (电话). Standardized across a country, it is a spoken language (普通话). Handed around for amusement or wonder, it is a joke or a tale (笑话, 童话). Keep 话 apart from 字, the written character, and from 语, language as a system, and you will always know you are dealing with words that are spoken.</p>
