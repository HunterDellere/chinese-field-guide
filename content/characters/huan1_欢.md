    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="欢">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>欢 huān = 又 (yòu, hand) + 欠 (qiàn, open mouth).</strong> The simplified form pairs the hand element 又 on the left with the radical 欠 on the right. 欠 is the ancient pictograph of a person with the mouth thrown open, the same element that drives a family of words about breath and open-mouthed action: 吹 (to blow), 歌 (to sing), 叹 (to sigh). Here the open mouth is the laugh, and the whole glyph reads as the body breaking into open, audible delight.</p>
      <p>The traditional form <strong>歡</strong> is a phonetic compound: the same meaning-element 欠 sits on the right, and on the left the bird-and-eyes element <strong>雚</strong> (guàn) supplies the sound. The simplification kept 欠 for sense and swapped the bulky 雚 for the compact 又, giving the six-stroke 欢 used today. The 欢 / 歡 split is one of the cleaner cases where the radical that carries the meaning stayed put while the phonetic half was traded for a simpler shape.</p>
      <p>Three senses branch from the open-mouthed laugh:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Joy, merriment.</strong> Delight as a felt state and as shared revelry: 欢乐 huānlè (joy), 欢喜 huānxǐ (delighted), 狂欢 kuánghuān (carnival; revelry).<br>
      <strong>2. To like, to welcome.</strong> Pleasure directed at a person or thing: 喜欢 xǐhuan (to like), 欢迎 huānyíng (to welcome).<br>
      <strong>3. Lively, high-spirited.</strong> The brisk, animated quality of a thing in full swing: 欢快 huānkuài (lively), 欢腾 huānténg (jubilant).</p>
      <p>The thread is delight that shows: not a quiet inner contentment but joy that opens the mouth, lifts the voice, and spills outward into welcome and celebration.</p>
    </div>

            <div class="ex-en">The children sang merrily.</div>

        <div class="card-def">
          欢 huān (joy) + 喜 xǐ (glad; to like). Bright, beaming delight, often at good news or a happy event. 满心欢喜 "filled with delight." The 喜 here is the same 喜 of double-happiness at weddings.

            <div class="ex-en">She was overjoyed to hear the news.</div>

      <!-- 狂欢 -->
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">狂欢</span>
          <span class="card-py">kuánghuān</span>
          <span class="card-en">to revel; carnival; wild celebration</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          狂 kuáng (wild; unrestrained) + 欢 huān (joy). Joy turned up to its loudest: unrestrained revelry. 狂欢节 kuánghuānjié is the standard word for Carnival, and 狂欢 names any wild, all-out celebration.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">人们在广场上彻夜狂欢。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Rénmen zài guǎngchǎng shàng chèyè kuánghuān.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">People reveled in the square all night long.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 欢乐 vs. 狂欢</span>
          <strong>欢乐</strong> is warm, festive joy that can fill any happy gathering. <strong>狂欢</strong> adds 狂 (wild) and pushes the same joy into unrestrained, often nighttime revelry: a party that loses all reserve, not just a cheerful afternoon.

    <!-- ── WELCOME ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="welcome"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">喜欢</span>
      <span class="sh-py">xǐhuan</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Like &amp; Welcome</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="欢">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>When 欢 turns outward toward a person or thing, it becomes the joy of welcome. <strong>喜欢</strong> xǐhuan, pairing 欢 with <a href="../../pages/characters/xi3_喜.html">喜</a> (glad), is the everyday word for "to like," the very first verb of preference a learner meets: 我喜欢茶 "I like tea." And <strong>欢迎</strong> huānyíng, "joy" plus 迎 (to greet), is the warmth offered to a newcomer: to receive someone gladly. The 欢 supplies the gladness; the second character points it at its object.</p>
      <p>The same root opens the door and closes it kindly. 欢迎 greets the arriving guest; 欢送 huānsòng gives the departing one a warm send-off. Both frame coming and going as occasions of shared good feeling, the host meeting the guest with an open, glad face.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 喜欢 -->
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">喜欢</span>
          <span class="card-py">xǐhuan</span>
          <span class="card-en">to like; to be fond of; to enjoy</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          喜 xǐ (glad) + 欢 huan (here toneless). The core verb for liking, used of things, activities, and people: 喜欢音乐 "to like music," 喜欢你 "to like you." Stronger than mild approval, lighter than 爱 ài (to love).
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我很喜欢中国茶。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ hěn xǐhuan Zhōngguó chá.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I really like Chinese tea.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你喜欢做什么？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ xǐhuan zuò shénme?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">What do you like to do?</div>
          </div>
        </div>

      <!-- 欢迎 -->
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">欢迎</span>
          <span class="card-py">huānyíng</span>
          <span class="card-en">to welcome; welcome!</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          欢 huān (joy) + 迎 yíng (to greet; to meet). To receive someone gladly. Also the set greeting 欢迎 "welcome!" and the shop sign 欢迎光临 "welcome, please come in." 受欢迎 shòu huānyíng means "popular; well-liked."
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">欢迎你来我家。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Huānyíng nǐ lái wǒ jiā.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Welcome to my home.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">用法 yòngfǎ · using 欢迎</span>
          <strong>欢迎</strong> works both as a verb and as a standalone greeting. As a verb it takes a guest or an action as its object: 欢迎你 "we welcome you," 欢迎参观 "visitors welcome." On its own it is the bright "welcome!" called out to anyone arriving. The phrase 受欢迎 flips it to a passive sense, "to be welcomed," that is, popular.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 欢送 -->
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">欢送</span>
          <span class="card-py">huānsòng</span>
          <span class="card-en">to see off (warmly); to give a send-off</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          欢 huān (joy) + 送 sòng (to see off). The warm counterpart of 欢迎: to send a departing person off with good feeling, as at a farewell party. 欢送会 huānsònghuì is a send-off gathering.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">大家欢送毕业生离校。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Dàjiā huānsòng bìyèshēng lí xiào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Everyone gave the graduates a warm send-off as they left the school.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
