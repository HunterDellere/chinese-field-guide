    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="离">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>离 is the simplified form of 離, and the simplification kept the louder half of the old picture: a creature snared. The traditional graph carried 隹 (zhuī), the short-tailed bird, on its right. The left side, preserved whole in 离, drew a long-tailed bird with a net dropping over its head. The 禸 component at the foot is the catalogued radical, the same track-and-snare element that runs through 禽 (qín, fowl) and 禹. So the glyph began as a hunter's snapshot: feathers, a beak, and the closing mesh.</p>
      <p>The Shuōwén Jiězì reads 離 as the name of a particular bird, a kind of oriole or mythical pheasant called 离黄. That bird sense is the literal one, and it has almost vanished from living speech. What survived is a phonetic loan. Speakers of Old Chinese reached for the sound of 离 to write the verb meaning to part, to come away, and that borrowed reading buried the bird.</p>
      <p>From to part the meaning spread outward in two directions. One is physical departure, leaving a room or a city, which gives 离开 (líkāi). The other is measured separation, the gap between two fixed points, which gives 距离 (jùlí). Both keep the original tension of the net closing: something that was whole is now held apart.</p>
      <p>The form also names a hexagram, 離 (Lí) in the Yìjīng, the trigram of fire and clinging brightness. Japanese borrowed the character as 離 (ri), reading it in 離婚 (rikon, divorce) and 距離 (kyori, distance), the same two senses that anchor the modern Mandarin word stock.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">离去</span><span class="sh-py">líqù</span><span class="sh-en">Leaving &amp; Departure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">离开</span><span class="card-py">líkāi</span><span class="card-en">to leave, to depart from</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">离 (to part) plus 开 (kāi, to open, to move away). The most common verb for leaving a place or a person. It takes a direct object: you leave <em>something</em>.</div>

        </div>

      </div>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">距离</span><span class="sh-py">jùlí</span><span class="sh-en">Distance &amp; Measuring Apart</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      <p>As a preposition, 离 marks the gap between two points: <strong>A 离 B + 远/近/distance</strong>, A is far from / near / a measured distance from B. The reference point follows 离, not precedes it.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">离</span><span class="card-py">lí</span><span class="card-en">(prep.) away from, from</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">prep. 介词 jiècí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">Standing alone, 离 is the preposition of separation in space or time. It states how far one thing sits from another.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">我家离地铁站很近。</div><div class="ex-py">Wǒ jiā lí dìtiězhàn hěn jìn.</div><div class="ex-en">My home is very close to the subway station.</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">离春节还有两个星期。</div><div class="ex-py">Lí chūnjié hái yǒu liǎng ge xīngqī.</div><div class="ex-en">There are still two weeks until Spring Festival.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 离 vs. 从</span> 从 (cóng) marks a starting point of motion (from here to there); 离 marks a static gap (how far apart two points are).</div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="离" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a bright bird mid-flight, and a net dropping over it from above. The bird strains upward, the mesh pulls it back, and for one frozen instant the two pull in opposite directions. That tension is 离: not a clean break, but a thing held away from where it wants to be. The 禸 track at the foot of the glyph is the snare that catches the feet.</p>
      <p>Carry that pulling-apart through the compounds. To leave a house is 离开 (líkāi), the net opening just enough to let the bird out the door. The space the bird cannot cross is 距离 (jùlí), the measured gap. When two birds that flew together are pulled into separate nets, that is 分离 (fēnlí), and when the parting carries grief it becomes 离别 (líbié). Every one of these words is the same caught bird, straining against the distance the snare has opened.</p>
