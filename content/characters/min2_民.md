    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="民">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>民</strong> mín is a pictograph and serves as its own radical: it does not decompose into a meaning-part plus a sound-part the way most characters do. The simplified and traditional forms are identical, five strokes that have stayed remarkably stable across the script's history. In the standard dictionary system 民 heads its own small radical group, so a learner meets it not as a component assembled from others but as a primitive sign in its own right.</p>
      <p>The early graph is sobering. On bronze and seal forms 民 is usually read as a figure pierced or marked at the eye, an image many paleographers connect to the branding or blinding of captives reduced to servitude. From that harsh origin the word generalised: from the subjugated, to the governed, to the ordinary mass of people set below the ruler, and finally to <em>the people</em> as such. The trajectory matters, because the political weight of 民 in later Chinese thought, the idea that a ruler holds power only so long as the people consent, sits on top of a word that once meant the lowliest of subjects.</p>
      <p>Three semantic lives grow out of this core of <em>the governed mass</em>: 民 as the populace and the people (人民, 民众); 民 as the individual member of a polity, the citizen or resident (公民, 居民, 农民); and 民 as folk and nation, the people considered as a culture and a stock (民族, 民间, 民俗). A fourth, narrower sense pairs 民 against the military: 民用 civilian-use, 民航 civil aviation. And in modern coinage the word anchors two great political abstractions, 民主 mínzhǔ (democracy, the people as master) and 民生 mínshēng (the people's livelihood).</p>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="people"></span>
    <div class="section-head">

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 人民 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">人民</span>
          <span class="card-py">rénmín</span>
          <span class="card-en">the people (the political collective)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          人 rén (person) + 民. The people understood as a single political subject, not merely a head-count of individuals. The term carries strong institutional weight in modern China, saturating the names of state organs and the language of governance. It points to the people as the source and beneficiary of legitimate power.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">政府应该为人民服务。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhèngfǔ yīnggāi wèi rénmín fúwù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The government should serve the people.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">人民币是中国的法定货币。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Rénmínbì shì Zhōngguó de fǎdìng huòbì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The renminbi is China's official currency. (lit. the people's currency)</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">文化 wénhuà · Key Compounds</span>
          人民日报 Rénmín Rìbào (People's Daily) · 人民币 Rénmínbì (the currency, lit. the people's currency) · 人民法院 rénmín fǎyuàn (People's Court) · 人民大会堂 Rénmín Dàhuìtáng (the Great Hall of the People). The word threads through official and civic discourse in a way no neutral synonym does.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 民众 -->
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">民众</span>
          <span class="card-py">mínzhòng</span>
          <span class="card-en">the masses; the general public</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          民 + 众 zhòng (multitude, crowd). The ordinary public viewed as a large body, common in news writing and formal description: how the public reacts, what the public expects. More descriptive and less ideologically charged than 人民, and broader and more neutral than the warmer, address-style 大家.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这项政策受到广大民众的支持。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè xiàng zhèngcè shòudào guǎngdà mínzhòng de zhīchí.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This policy enjoys the support of the broad public.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">消息一出，民众十分关注。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Xiāoxi yī chū, mínzhòng shífēn guānzhù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">As soon as the news broke, the public paid close attention.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 平民 -->
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">平民</span>
          <span class="card-py">píngmín</span>
          <span class="card-en">commoner; an ordinary person (vs the elite)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          平 píng (level, ordinary, common) + 民. An ordinary person without rank, wealth, or official position: the commoner as opposed to nobles, officials, or celebrities. 平民百姓 píngmín bǎixìng is a fixed pairing meaning the common folk, the man and woman in the street.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他出身平民，靠努力改变了命运。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā chūshēn píngmín, kào nǔlì gǎibiàn le mìngyùn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He came from a common background and changed his fortunes through hard work.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">战争中受苦最多的是平民。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhànzhēng zhōng shòukǔ zuì duō de shì píngmín.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">It is civilians who suffer the most in war.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 平民 vs. 公民</span>
          <strong>平民</strong> píngmín contrasts a person with the elite: ordinary, untitled, without privilege. <strong>公民</strong> gōngmín is a legal status: a member of a state with rights and duties. A king's subject can be a 平民 without being a 公民 in the modern legal sense; a wealthy executive is a 公民 but no longer a 平民. The first axis is social rank, the second is legal membership.
        </div>
      </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="citizens"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">公民</span>

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 公民 -->
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">公民</span>
          <span class="card-py">gōngmín</span>
          <span class="card-en">citizen (a legal member of a state)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          公 gōng (public, common) + 民. The legal-political term for a citizen: a person who holds the nationality of a state and bears its rights and obligations. The standard word in constitutional and legal contexts, paired with 权利 quánlì (rights) and 义务 yìwù (duties).
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">每个公民都有受教育的权利。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Měi gè gōngmín dōu yǒu shòu jiàoyù de quánlì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Every citizen has the right to an education.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">纳税是公民的义务。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nàshuì shì gōngmín de yìwù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Paying taxes is a duty of citizens.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 人民 vs. 公民</span>
          <strong>人民</strong> rénmín is the people as a collective political subject, a mass with a shared will; you cannot count it or hold a single 人民. <strong>公民</strong> gōngmín is the individual legal member, countable and rights-bearing: one citizen, every citizen. The collective and the member are two faces of 民, the political body and its legal atom.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 居民 -->
      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">居民</span>
          <span class="card-py">jūmín</span>
          <span class="card-en">resident; inhabitant (of a place)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          居 jū (to dwell, to reside) + 民. A person defined by where they live rather than by their nationality: the residents of a building, a neighbourhood, a city, a region. The everyday administrative word, as in 居民区 jūmínqū (a residential district) and 城市居民 (urban residents).
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">小区居民对噪音很有意见。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Xiǎoqū jūmín duì zàoyīn hěn yǒu yìjiàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The residents of the complex have many complaints about the noise.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这座城市的居民超过一千万。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè zuò chéngshì de jūmín chāoguò yīqiān wàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This city has more than ten million inhabitants.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 农民 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">农民</span>
          <span class="card-py">nóngmín</span>
          <span class="card-en">farmer; peasant (a person of the land)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          农 nóng (agriculture, farming) + 民. A person whose livelihood is the land. The word covers both the neutral modern sense farmer and, in historical and social contexts, the peasantry as a class. For most of Chinese history the 农民 were the overwhelming majority of the population and the bedrock of the agrarian economy.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">农民在田里辛勤劳作。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nóngmín zài tián lǐ xīnqín láozuò.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The farmers toil diligently in the fields.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">很多农民进城打工。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Hěn duō nóngmín jìn chéng dǎgōng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Many farmers go to the cities to find work.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">构词 gòucí · The X + 民 role pattern</span>
          A field or place set before 民 names a person by role or status: 农民 farmer, 渔民 yúmín (fisherman), 牧民 mùmín (herder), 网民 wǎngmín (netizen), 移民 yímín (immigrant, emigrant), 难民 nànmín (refugee). The pattern is highly productive: once you know 民 names a member of a group, each new compound reads itself.
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">民族</span>
          <span class="card-py">mínzú</span>
          <span class="card-en">ethnic group; nationality; a people</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          民 + 族 zú (clan, lineage, group). A people considered as a cultural and ethnic stock: an ethnic group, a nationality, a nation in the cultural sense. China officially recognises 56 民族, the 汉族 Hànzú (Han) and 55 少数民族 shǎoshù mínzú (ethnic minorities). The word also carries the larger sense of a whole nation's identity, as in 中华民族.

          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">中国是一个多民族的国家。</div>

            <div class="ex-en">China is a multi-ethnic country.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">每个民族都有自己的语言和文化。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Měi gè mínzú dōu yǒu zìjǐ de yǔyán hé wénhuà.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Every ethnic group has its own language and culture.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 民间 -->
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">民间</span>
          <span class="card-py">mínjiān</span>
          <span class="card-en">among the people; folk; non-governmental</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          民 + 间 jiān (among, within). The realm of ordinary life as opposed to the official, the courtly, or the state: that which exists among the people. As a modifier it marks something as folk or grassroots: 民间故事 a folk tale, 民间艺术 folk art, 民间组织 a non-governmental organisation, 民间借贷 informal private lending.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是一个流传很广的民间故事。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì yī gè liúchuán hěn guǎng de mínjiān gùshi.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This is a widely circulated folk tale.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">很多传统手艺保存在民间。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Hěn duō chuántǒng shǒuyì bǎocún zài mínjiān.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Many traditional crafts are preserved among the common people.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 民间 vs. 官方</span>
          <strong>民间</strong> mínjiān (among the people, folk, unofficial) stands opposite <strong>官方</strong> guānfāng (official, governmental). A 民间传说 is a folk legend passed mouth to mouth; an 官方声明 is an official statement. The pair maps the whole divide between grassroots life and the apparatus of the state.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 民歌 -->
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">

            <div class="ex-en">This folk song from northern Shaanxi is very beautiful.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">奶奶会唱很多老民歌。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǎinai huì chàng hěn duō lǎo míngē.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Grandma can sing many old folk songs.</div>

    <!-- ── CHENGYU ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="chengyu"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">成语</span>
      <span class="sh-py">chéngyǔ</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Idioms &amp; Set Phrases</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="chengyu-grid">

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">国泰民安</span>
        <span class="cy-py">guó tài mín ān</span>
        <span class="cy-en">the country prosperous and the people at peace: an era of stability and good order</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "the state secure, the people at peace." A classic blessing for an age of good governance: when the country is stable and well-run, the people live in peace and security. A staple of New Year couplets and official well-wishing, it pairs the fate of the state with the welfare of the people as two halves of one ideal.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">民以食为天</span>
        <span class="cy-py">mín yǐ shí wéi tiān</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to the people, food is heaven: nothing matters more than feeding the people</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "the people take food as heaven." An ancient maxim of statecraft: the first and highest concern of any ruler is that the people have enough to eat. Still quoted constantly in everyday life, often half-jokingly, to defend the seriousness of a good meal. It makes feeding the people the foundation of all governance.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">民不聊生</span>
        <span class="cy-py">mín bù liáo shēng</span>

        <span class="cy-note">Literally "the people have no means to make a living." A grim phrase for times of misrule, war, or disaster when ordinary people cannot even keep themselves alive. 聊 liáo here is the classical sense "to rely on, to depend on for," so 不聊生 means having nothing to depend on for life.       <p>Hold 民 mín as <strong>the people set below the ruler</strong>, the broad mass on whom every state finally rests. The graph's dark beginning, a captive marked at the eye, became over the centuries the most weighted political word in the language: not a number, but a subject with a will. Whoever holds power holds it on loan from the 民.</p>
      <p>From that one idea the whole vocabulary unfolds. The collective is 人民 the people and 民众 the masses; the individual member is the 公民 citizen, the 居民 resident, the 农民 farmer; the people as a culture and a stock is the 民族 nation, living in the 民间 folk realm and singing its 民歌. And the great modern coinages keep the same root: 民主, the people as master, and 民生, the people's livelihood. Remember the proverbs and the politics follow: 国泰民安 when the state is sound the people are at peace, and 民以食为天, that to the people, food itself is heaven.</p>
