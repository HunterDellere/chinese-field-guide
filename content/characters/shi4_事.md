    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="事">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>事 shì began as a picture of a hand holding the implements of office.</strong> The oracle-bone form shows a hand gripping an upright object, read variously as a hunting standard, a writing brush, or a banner of office. Whatever the exact tool, the image is of someone holding the instrument of a duty. From that single picture three closely related characters fanned out: 事 shì (the affair, the matter handled), 吏 lì (the official who handles it), and 史 shǐ (the scribe who records it). All three trace back to the same hand-and-implement source, and in the earliest texts they were barely distinguished.</p>
      <p>The modern shape is opaque, a tangle of strokes ending in the hook 亅 (jué) that gives the character its radical. There is no clean "this part plus that part" breakdown to recover; what survives is the ending hook of the old upright implement. So 事 is best learned not by pulling it apart but by holding onto its idea: <em>the thing one is occupied with</em>.</p>
      <p>That idea is wide. 事 covers the whole field of abstract matters that fill a life:</p>
      <p><strong>1. A matter, an affair.</strong> Anything that happens or must be dealt with: 事情 shìqing (matter), 没事 méishì (it's nothing), 出事 chūshì (something went wrong).<br>
      <strong>2. Work, an undertaking.</strong> A job or a life's project: 事业 shìyè (career, enterprise), 做事 zuòshì (to work, to handle things).<br>
      <strong>3. An event or incident, often a bad one.</strong> 事故 shìgù (accident), 事件 shìjiàn (incident, event).</p>
      <p>Crucially, 事 is abstract. It names a matter or happening, never a physical object. The physical "thing" is 东西 dōngxi or 物 wù. You can 有事 (have a matter to attend to) but you cannot 有事 to mean "own an object." That split, affair versus object, is the single most useful thing to fix about 事.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">事情</span>
          <span class="card-py">shìqing</span>
          <span class="card-en">matter; affair; thing (to be dealt with)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          事 shì + 情 qíng (situation; circumstance). The everyday noun for a matter or affair, anything happening that you talk about or handle. The most common way to say "thing" when you mean a situation rather than an object.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我有一件事情想跟你说。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ yǒu yī jiàn shìqing xiǎng gēn nǐ shuō.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">There's something I want to tell you.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这件事情很重要。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn shìqing hěn zhòngyào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This matter is very important.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 事情 vs. 东西</span>
          <strong>事情</strong> is an abstract matter or affair: a situation, a task, something that happens. <strong>东西</strong> dōngxi is a physical object you can hold. 这件事情 "this matter"; 这个东西 "this object." Mixing them is the classic beginner slip.
        </div>
      </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">同事</span>
      <span class="sh-py">tóngshì</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Stories &amp; the People in Them</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="事">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Once 事 means "the thing one is occupied with," the words that grow from it sort neatly. A 事 shared with others at work makes a <strong>同事</strong> tóngshì, literally "same-affair person," a colleague. A 事 strung into a telling becomes a <strong>故事</strong> gùshi, an "old matter" retold as a story. And a 事 taken up as a life's purpose becomes 事业 shìyè, a career or enterprise. The character keeps its abstract core throughout: in every case it is a matter, an undertaking, never an object.</p>
    </div>

          <span class="card-en">career; undertaking; enterprise; cause</span>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          事 shì + 业 yè (occupation; trade). A matter taken up as a life's work. Larger and more purposeful than a mere job (工作 gōngzuò): a 事业 is something you build, a calling or enterprise.

            <div class="ex-en">He devoted his whole life to the cause of education.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 事业 vs. 工作</span>
          <strong>工作</strong> gōngzuò is a job: the work you do, day to day. <strong>事业</strong> shìyè is a career or calling: the larger thing you build over a lifetime. You can have a 工作 without a 事业, and a 事业 is praised in a way a 工作 is not.

    <!-- ── TROUBLE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="trouble"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">没事</span>
      <span class="sh-py">méishì</span>
      <span class="sh-en">No Trouble: the Colloquial 事</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="事">
      <div class="scholar-label">口语洞见 kǒuyǔ dòngjiàn · Colloquial Insight</div>
      <p>In everyday speech 事 becomes shorthand for "a problem" or "something going on," and the two phrases <strong>没事</strong> méishì and <strong>有事</strong> yǒushì carry an enormous load. 没事, literally "no matter," is the all-purpose Chinese "it's nothing, no problem, never mind, are you okay." 有事, "have a matter," means you are busy, something has come up, or there is a problem to deal with. In the north you will hear them with the 儿 ending: 没事儿 méishìr, 有事儿 yǒushìr.</p>
      <p>The same colloquial 事 drives 出事 chūshì, "something went wrong" (an accident, a crisis), and 闹事 nàoshì, "to make trouble." When a Chinese speaker asks 你怎么了，没事吧？ they are asking, with one small character, whether anything is the matter.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="chengyu-grid">

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">实事求是</span>
        <span class="cy-py">shí shì qiú shì</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to seek truth from facts: to judge by the real state of things, not by wishes</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: real-matters-seek-truth. From the Book of Han, describing a prince devoted to learning. It became a watchword for pragmatic, evidence-based thinking, weighing matters as they actually are rather than as doctrine prescribes.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">事半功倍</span>
        <span class="cy-py">shì bàn gōng bèi</span>
        <span class="cy-en">half the effort, double the result: great gains from the right method</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: matter-half-merit-double. To accomplish twice as much with half the work. Its opposite is 事倍功半, double the effort for half the result. The phrase praises efficiency and the wisdom of finding the right approach.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">平安无事</span>
        <span class="cy-py">píng ān wú shì</span>
        <span class="cy-en">safe and sound, nothing amiss: all is calm and well</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: peaceful-safe-no-matter. A reassuring summary that nothing has gone wrong: a journey passed without incident, a household at peace. The 无事 here is the formal cousin of colloquial 没事.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">多事之秋</span>
        <span class="cy-py">duō shì zhī qiū</span>
        <span class="cy-en">an eventful autumn: a troubled time, full of upheavals</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: many-matters' autumn. Autumn was the season of harvest reckoning and of war, so a "many-matters autumn" came to mean a period crowded with crises and disturbances, a turbulent, eventful time for a state or a person.</span>
