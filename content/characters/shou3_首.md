    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="首">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>首 shǒu is a pictograph of a head.</strong> The oracle-bone and bronze forms draw a head seen in profile, with strands of hair above and an eye below. The modern glyph still preserves both: the strokes across the top stand for the hair (in some early forms a tuft or a horn-like crest), and the 目 (mù, eye) sitting underneath is the unmistakable face beneath that hair. Where 头 tóu is the everyday spoken word for a head, 首 is the older, more literary one, and it is this glyph that the dictionaries enshrine as its own radical.</p>
      <p>As a radical 首 is rare, but it gives 道 dào (the way, a road one walks with the head leading) its head-element, and it heads its own short section in the dictionary. Far more often 首 lives inside two-character words, where its senses fan out from the single idea of <em>the head, the part that goes first and leads</em>.</p>
      <p>From the head, the meaning radiates outward:</p>
      <p><strong>1. The head (literary).</strong> 昂首 ángshǒu (to hold the head high), 俯首 fǔshǒu (to bow the head).<br>
      <strong>2. First, foremost.</strong> 首先 shǒuxiān (first of all), 首要 shǒuyào (of prime importance), 首次 shǒucì (the first time), 首都 shǒudū (capital city).<br>
      <strong>3. Chief, leader, the one at the head.</strong> 首领 shǒulǐng (chieftain), 元首 yuánshǒu (head of state), 首相 shǒuxiàng (prime minister), 部首 bùshǒu (the heading radical of a character).<br>
      <strong>4. To confess, to give oneself up.</strong> 自首 zìshǒu (to turn oneself in to the authorities), where one bows the head before the law.<br>
      <strong>5. The measure word for songs and poems.</strong> 一首歌 yī shǒu gē (a song), 一首诗 yī shǒu shī (a poem).</p>
      <p>The thread running through all of them is the head as the leading, foremost part: of a body, of a sequence, of a group, even the head-word that names a category.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="first"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">首先</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shǒuxiān</span>
      <span class="sh-en">First &amp; Foremost</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="首">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Once 首 names the head, it naturally names the head of a sequence: the thing that comes first and stands foremost. This is the most productive modern sense. 首先 shǒuxiān opens a list (first of all); 首要 shǒuyào marks the matter of highest priority; 首次 shǒucì labels the very first occurrence of something; 首都 shǒudū is the head-city, the capital that leads the nation.</p>
      <p>This sense reaches into news and ranking too. 首位 shǒuwèi is first place or the leading position; 首选 shǒuxuǎn is the first choice, the top pick; 榜首 bǎngshǒu is the top of the chart or list. In each, 首 supplies the meaning "at the front, ahead of all the rest."</p>
    </div>

          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 首先 vs. 起初</span>
          <strong>首先</strong> orders points in a sequence: it means "the first item," and it sets up the next ones (其次, 然后). <strong>起初</strong> qǐchū orders time: it means "at first, in the beginning," describing how things were at the start before they changed, and it pairs with 后来 hòulái (later on). You list reasons 首先; you recall that 起初 you did not understand, but later you did.

    <!-- ── CHIEF ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="chief"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">首领</span>
      <span class="sh-py">shǒulǐng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Chief, Leader &amp; the Measure Word</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="首">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The head of a group is its leader. 首领 shǒulǐng is a chieftain or boss of a band; 元首 yuánshǒu is the head of a whole state; 首相 shǒuxiàng is a prime minister, the chief minister. The same logic names the head-character of a writing system: 部首 bùshǒu, the radical, is literally the "section head" under which a character is filed in the dictionary.</p>
      <p>Separately, 首 serves as the measure word, the classifier, for songs and poems: 一首歌 yī shǒu gē (one song), 一首诗 yī shǒu shī (one poem), 几首曲子 jǐ shǒu qǔzi (a few tunes). A poem or song is counted by its 首, much as 头 counts cattle, each piece a self-contained "head." This is the sense an HSK learner meets first and uses most.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>See the glyph as the drawing it is: hair along the top, an eye (目) looking out from below. That is a head, 首, the literary cousin of everyday 头. Now let the head lead. Hold it high and you are proud (昂首); bow it down and you submit, even before the law (自首). Put it at the front of a sequence and it becomes first (首先), foremost (首要), the head-city itself (首都).</p>
      <p>Set the head over a group and it becomes the chief: the chieftain (首领), the head of state (元首), even the section head that files a character in the dictionary (部首). Last, count by it: a song, a poem, each a self-contained head, one 首. From hair-over-an-eye to the first of all things, every sense of 首 is the head out in front, leading.</p>
