    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="做">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>做 is built from two parts: the person radical 亻 (rén) on the left, and 故 gù on the right. 做 = 亻 + 故. The right element does double duty: it supplies the sound (故 gù and 做 zuò are close in many historical readings) and it nudges the meaning, since 故 carries the sense of cause, reason, and the bringing-about of a result. The composite reads almost transparently: a person 亻 who causes something to happen 故. That is exactly what doing is.</p>
      <p>做 is a relatively late character. For most of literary history the verb "to do, to make" was written 作 zuò, which traces back to oracle bone and bronze forms. 做 emerged during the Song dynasty as a vernacular variant, coined to carry the colloquial, hands-on senses of the verb while the older 作 drifted toward formal and abstract uses. The two are etymological cousins reading the same syllable, and the division of labor between them, settled only gradually, is still one of the trickiest fine distinctions in everyday Chinese.</p>
      <p>Because 做 was born from speech rather than the classical canon, it sits at the practical center of daily life: 做饭 (cook), 做事 (handle affairs), 做生意 (do business), 做梦 (dream). Where 作 keeps company with composition and abstraction, 做 stays close to the kitchen, the workshop, and the office. The person radical is no accident: 做 is the verb of human agency, the doing that fills an ordinary day.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── 做 vs 作 ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="zuo-zuo"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">做作之别</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zuò zuò zhī bié</span>
      <span class="sh-en">做 vs 作 — Dividing the Labor</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="作">
      <div class="scholar-label">辨析 biànxī · The Core Distinction</div>
      <p>做 and 作 are the same word historically, both read zuò, and the line between them is drawn by register and object, not by meaning. The working rule: 做 takes concrete, everyday objects and produces a tangible result (做饭, 做衣服, 做作业); 作 takes abstract, formal, or literary objects (作文 write an essay, 作曲 compose music, 作画 paint, 工作 work). When the object is a real thing made by hand, reach for 做. When the action is composition, creation in the artistic or abstract sense, or part of a fixed formal compound, reach for 作.</p>
      <p>The boundary is genuinely blurry, and native usage varies by region and by individual. A few compounds are simply fixed and must be memorized: 工作 (work, always 作), 作业 (homework, always 作), 做饭 (cook, always 做), 做梦 (dream, always 做). For the open cases, the heuristic above is reliable enough for a learner: concrete and colloquial leans 做, abstract and formal leans 作. The two characters also pair into 做作 zuòzuo, an adjective meaning affected or pretentious, behaving in a way that is "made" rather than natural.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">分工速查 fēngōng sùchá · Quick Reference</span>
      <strong>做 zuò</strong> , concrete, colloquial, tangible result: 做饭 · 做事 · 做生意 · 做朋友 · 做作业<br>
      <strong>作 zuò</strong> , abstract, formal, creative or compositional: 作文 · 作曲 · 作品 · 工作 · 作为<br>
      <strong>叠用</strong> 做作 zuòzuo (adj.) , affected, pretentious; behavior that is contrived rather than natural<br>
      <strong>记法</strong> if you can hold it in your hands, it is usually 做; if it lives on paper or in the mind, often 作
    </div>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">做饭</span>
          <span class="card-py">zuòfàn</span>
          <span class="card-en">to cook (a meal)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag-vn">verb-object</span></div>
        <p class="card-def">做 (to make) + 饭 fàn (cooked rice; a meal). The standard way to say "cook." Always 做, never 作, because the result is a concrete, edible thing. A verb-object compound: the 饭 can be separated and modified, as in 做了一顿饭 (cooked a meal). One of the first useful phrases a learner needs at home.</p>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">我妈妈每天都做饭。</span><span class="ex-py">Wǒ māma měitiān dōu zuòfàn.</span> — My mom cooks every day.</div>
          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">今天晚上谁做饭？</span><span class="ex-py">Jīntiān wǎnshang shéi zuòfàn?</span> — Who is cooking tonight?</div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">做事</span>
          <span class="card-py">zuòshì</span>
          <span class="card-en">to handle matters; to work; to conduct oneself in action</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag-vn">verb-object</span></div>
        <p class="card-def">做 (to do) + 事 shì (matter, affair, task). To get things done, to handle one's affairs. Often carries a sense of character: 做事认真 (conscientious in one's work), 做事靠谱 (reliable). 会做人会做事 (knowing how to deal with people and how to get things done) is a high compliment in Chinese social life.</p>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">他做事很认真。</span><span class="ex-py">Tā zuòshì hěn rènzhēn.</span> — He is very conscientious in how he works.</div>
          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">做事要有计划。</span><span class="ex-py">Zuòshì yào yǒu jìhuà.</span> — One should have a plan when handling things.</div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">做人</span>
          <span class="card-py">zuòrén</span>
          <span class="card-en">to conduct oneself; to be a (decent) person</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag-vn">verb-object</span></div>
        <p class="card-def">做 (to do, to act as) + 人 rén (person). Not "to make a person" but "to be a person," meaning to conduct oneself ethically in society. A deeply Confucian phrase: 学会做人 (learn how to be a proper person) is held to matter more than learning a skill. The pairing 做人做事 captures the whole of social competence , character toward people, capability in tasks.</p>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">做人要诚实。</span><span class="ex-py">Zuòrén yào chéngshí.</span> — In conducting oneself, one must be honest.</div>
          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">他很会做人。</span><span class="ex-py">Tā hěn huì zuòrén.</span> — He really knows how to handle people well.</div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">做梦</span>
          <span class="card-py">zuòmèng</span>
          <span class="card-en">to dream; (figuratively) to fantasize</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag-vn">verb-object</span></div>
        <p class="card-def">做 (to do, to have) + 梦 mèng (dream). The verb for dreaming, both the literal nighttime kind and the figurative "you're dreaming" used to dismiss an unrealistic hope: 做白日梦 (to daydream, literally "to dream a white-day dream"). Always 做. A reminder that 做 covers not only making things but undergoing experiences.</p>

          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">我昨天做了一个奇怪的梦。</span><span class="ex-py">Wǒ zuótiān zuòle yí gè qíguài de mèng.</span> — I had a strange dream yesterday.</div>
          <div class="ex"><span class="ex-cn">别做白日梦了。</span><span class="ex-py">Bié zuò báirìmèng le.</span> — Stop daydreaming.</div>
        </div>
      </div>

          <span class="card-cn">做生意</span>
          <span class="card-py">zuò shēngyi</span>

        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag-vn">verb-object</span></div>
        <p class="card-def">做 (to do) + 生意 shēngyi (business, trade). The everyday phrase for being in business or running a trade. Concrete and colloquial, so 做 is correct; the more formal abstract noun is 经营 jīngyíng (to manage, to operate). 做买卖 zuò mǎimai is a folksier synonym.</p>

    <!-- ── RESULTATIVE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="resultative"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">做完做好</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zuòwán zuòhǎo</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Completing the Doing</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="做">
      <div class="scholar-label">语法 yǔfǎ · 做 with Result Complements</div>
      <p>做 is a workhorse base for the resultative complement, the construction where a second verb or adjective attaches to say how an action turned out. 做 names the doing; the complement names the result. 做完 (zuòwán) means finished doing, with 完 wán marking completion. 做好 (zuòhǎo) means done well or done and ready, with 好 hǎo marking successful completion or a good state. The difference is subtle but real: 做完作业 is "finished the homework" (it is over); 做好准备 is "got fully prepared" (it is in good order).</p>
      <p>Other common results stack onto 做 the same way: 做对 (did it right), 做错 (did it wrong), 做到 (managed to do, achieved). The negative uses 不 between the verb and complement for a potential reading: 做不完 (cannot finish), 做不好 (cannot do well), 做不到 (cannot manage it). This potential form, splitting the verb and result with 得 or 不, is one of the most distinctive features of Chinese grammar, and 做 is among the best verbs to learn it on because the doing is so general.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">结果补语 jiéguǒ bǔyǔ · 做 + Result</span>
      <strong>做完 zuòwán</strong> , finished doing (完 = completion): 作业做完了 the homework is done<br>
      <strong>做好 zuòhǎo</strong> , done well / ready (好 = good state): 准备做好了 preparations are in order<br>
      <strong>做对 / 做错</strong> , did it right / wrong: 这道题做错了 got this problem wrong<br>
      <strong>做到 zuòdào</strong> , managed to do, achieved: 说到做到 say it and do it (keep one's word)<br>
      <strong>潜能式</strong> 做得完 / 做不完 (can / can't finish) · 做得好 / 做不好 (can / can't do well)
    </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">说到做到</span>
        <span class="cy-py">shuō dào zuò dào</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to do what one says — to be as good as one's word</span>
        <span class="cy-note">说 shuō (to say) + 到 dào (reach the point of) + 做 + 到. Literally "say it to the point, do it to the point." A high compliment: this is a person whose words and deeds match, who does not promise idly. The structure leans on 做到 (to achieve, to follow through). It echoes the Confucian value placed on 信 xìn (trustworthiness) and the older maxim 言必信，行必果 (be true to your word, see your deeds through).</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">敢做敢当</span>
        <span class="cy-py">gǎn zuò gǎn dāng</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to dare to act and dare to answer for it — to own one's actions</span>
        <span class="cy-note">敢 gǎn (to dare) + 做 + 敢 + 当 dāng (to bear, to take responsibility). The mark of a person of integrity: bold enough to act, and equally willing to shoulder the consequences. The phrase praises moral courage paired with accountability, the refusal to act and then duck the blame. A near variant, 敢作敢为 (with 作), stresses boldness in action itself; 敢做敢当 stresses owning the result.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">做贼心虚</span>
        <span class="cy-py">zuò zéi xīn xū</span>
        <span class="cy-en">having done the theft, the heart is uneasy — a guilty conscience betrays itself</span>
        <span class="cy-note">做 + 贼 zéi (thief) + 心 xīn (heart) + 虚 xū (empty, weak, unsteady). One who has done wrong cannot keep an easy heart; the inner unease leaks out as nervousness and over-caution. The phrase observes that guilt is self-announcing, that the wrongdoer's own jumpiness gives the game away. Used to describe someone whose anxious behavior reveals what they are hiding.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">小题大做</span>
        <span class="cy-py">xiǎo tí dà zuò</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to make a big production of a small topic — to overreact</span>
        <span class="cy-note">小 xiǎo (small) + 题 tí (topic, subject) + 大 dà (big) + 做 (to make, to treat). Originally from the examination tradition, where a trivial topic was expanded into a grand essay; now it means blowing a minor matter out of proportion. The 做 here is "to make into, to treat as," showing the verb's reach beyond physical making into the handling of a situation. The opposite is 大事化小 (scale a big problem down).</span>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">记忆</span>

      <span class="sh-rule"></span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="故">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆钩子 jìyì gōuzi · Memory Hook</div>
      <p>Read 做 left to right as a tiny sentence: a person 亻 brings about a cause 故. The 故 on the right is the same character found in 故事 (a story, literally a "told cause") and 故意 (on purpose, "with intent"). Doing, in this glyph, is a person making a cause happen, the human hand behind every result. That image keeps 做 anchored to its everyday, hands-on territory: cooking, working, handling affairs, conducting oneself.</p>
      <p>To keep 做 and 作 apart, picture where the action lives. If it lives in your hands or in the world , a meal, a deal, a chore , it is 做. If it lives on the page or in the abstract , an essay, a melody, a body of work , it is 作. The fixed compounds 工作 (work) and 做饭 (cook) are the two anchors to memorize first; almost everything else follows the concrete-versus-abstract split. And when someone behaves in a way that feels manufactured rather than real, the doubled 做作 names it exactly: behavior that was "made" instead of lived.</p>
