    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="朋">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>朋 sets two 月 side by side: 朋 = 月 + 月.</strong> The modern glyph reads as a doubled moon component, but the picture underneath is older and more concrete. In the oracle-bone and bronze forms 朋 draws two strings of cowrie shells hung together, the strands dangling in parallel. Cowries were money before coins, and a 朋 was a fixed unit of them, a pair of strings counted as one bundle.</p>
      <p>Two strands of equal length, hung as a matched pair, gave the character its lasting idea: things of the same kind kept close together. From shells of equal value the sense slid to people of equal standing, companions who keep company on a level footing. By the time of the classical texts 朋 already means a friend, a fellow of one's own group.</p>
      <p>The opening line of the <em>Analects</em> fixes the word in every learner's ear: 有朋自远方来，不亦乐乎 "to have friends come from afar, is that not a joy?" Here 朋 is the companion who shares your path. In later prose 朋 rarely stands alone; it pairs into 朋友 péngyou for the everyday friend, and into bound compounds like 亲朋 and 良朋 for the wider circle.</p>
      <p>Read the glyph as two matched strands and the whole field opens: equal value, equal standing, company kept side by side. The radical 月 here is a shape inherited from the shell-strings, not the moon in the sky, but it is what you count strokes by today.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="friends"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">朋友</span>
      <span class="sh-py">péngyou</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Friends: the Everyday Word</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="朋">
      <div class="scholar-label">用法洞见 yòngfǎ dòngjiàn · Usage Insight</div>
      <p>In modern Mandarin 朋 almost never walks alone; it leans on a second syllable. The workhorse is 朋友 péngyou, the plain word for a friend, light enough to cover a schoolmate, a colleague you like, or a lifelong companion. It stretches into set phrases too: 男朋友 nán péngyou is a boyfriend, 女朋友 nǚ péngyou a girlfriend, and 交朋友 jiāo péngyou is the verb-object "to make friends." The single 朋 keeps its old weight only inside compounds and idioms.</p>
    </div>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="circle"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">亲朋</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qīnpéng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Kin &amp; Company: the Wider Circle</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="朋">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>Outside 朋友, the bare 朋 lives on in bound compounds with a more literary ring. 亲朋 qīnpéng folds kin and friends into one phrase, the whole circle of people close to you; it is the heart of 亲朋好友 "relatives and friends," the set greeting for any gathering. 良朋 liángpéng is a good friend in the older, weightier sense, a worthy companion. These keep the older standing of 朋, the equal whose company is worth keeping, that the casual 朋友 has softened.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="朋" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture two strings of cowrie shells hung side by side, each strand the same length, the polished shells clicking together as they sway. They are matched: equal value, equal weight, counted as one bundle. That paired bundle is 朋, two 月 components standing shoulder to shoulder on the page.</p>
      <p>Now let the shells become people. Two friends of equal standing, walking side by side, neither above the other, that is the friend (朋友) at the heart of the character. Widen the strands and the circle grows: kin and friends together (亲朋), a worthy companion worth keeping (良朋), a hall where honored guests fill every seat (高朋满座). Each time you see 朋, hear the two strands click together: company on a level footing.</p>
