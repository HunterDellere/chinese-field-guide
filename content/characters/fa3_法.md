    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="法">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>法 fǎ = 氵 (shuǐ, water) + 去 (qù, to go).</strong> The simplified and traditional forms are identical here; the modern shape is the streamlined version of a longer archaic graph. The water radical on the left is the key to the old meaning. A water surface always settles dead level, and that level is the natural emblem of fairness: the law as the line everyone is measured against, with no side standing higher than another. The right element 去 carries the sense of going forth, the law flowing out evenly to all. So the character pairs water as a standard of levelness with 去 as the going-out of that standard.</p>
      <p>From this image of level, flowing measure, four senses spread across thousands of words:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Law, statute.</strong> The rule that binds everyone equally: 法律 fǎlǜ (law), 法院 fǎyuàn (court of law), 合法 héfǎ (lawful), 违法 wéifǎ (to break the law).<br>
      <strong>2. Method, way of doing.</strong> The settled procedure for a task: 方法 fāngfǎ (method), 办法 bànfǎ (a way; a solution), 用法 yòngfǎ (usage; how a thing is used).<br>
      <strong>3. The Buddhist Dharma.</strong> The teaching and the truth it points to: 佛法 Fófǎ (the Buddhist teaching), 法师 fǎshī (a Dharma master).<br>
      <strong>4. France, in proper names.</strong> Chosen for its sound: 法国 Fǎguó (France), 法语 Fǎyǔ (the French language).</p>
      <p>The thread running through every sense is a fixed standard you measure against. Whether the standard is the statute, the proven method, or the Dharma, 法 is the level line that water finds on its own, the rule that holds true wherever it is laid.</p>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="法">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>When Buddhism crossed into China, translators reached for 法 to render the Sanskrit <em>dharma</em>, and the fit was natural. Dharma means at once the teaching of the Buddha and the underlying truth or law of how things are, so the Chinese 法 with its sense of a fixed, impartial standard carried the idea cleanly. 佛法 Fófǎ is the body of Buddhist teaching; 说法 shuōfǎ is to expound the Dharma (and in everyday speech, a way of putting something); 法师 fǎshī is a master of the Dharma, a respectful title for a learned monk.</p>
      <p>So the single glyph 法 spans a remarkable range. In a courtroom it is the statute. In a workshop it is the proven method. In a temple it is the cosmic law the Buddha awakened to. The common thread is the same standard that the water radical first set down: a truth that holds level and even, whether written in a legal code or realized in meditation.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="法" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a still pool of water finding its own level, flat and even from edge to edge. That level surface is the heart of 法: a standard that holds true the same for everyone, with no side raised above another. The water radical 氵 on the left is the pool; the 去 on the right is that levelness going forth into the world.</p>
      <p>From the one image, follow the senses out. Lay the level over a society and it becomes the law (法律), enforced in the court (法院). Lay it over a task and it becomes the proven method (方法), the way that reliably works. Lay it over the cosmos and it becomes the Dharma (佛法), the truth a Buddha awakens to. Whenever you meet 法, look for the standard being measured against, and you will find the level water beneath it.</p>
