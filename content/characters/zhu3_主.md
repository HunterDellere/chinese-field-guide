    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>

      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="主">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>主 zhǔ is a pictograph of a flame burning on a lamp-stand.</strong> The oracle-bone and bronze forms show a tall stand with a single point of fire resting on top. That top dot, classed under the radical 丶 (zhǔ, "dot"), is the flame itself; the strokes below are the body of the lamp. The lamp was the steady central light of a room, and from that fixed, presiding point came the idea of the one in charge: the master of the house, the host who keeps the hearth.</p>
      <p>From the central light four senses radiate outward:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Master, owner, host.</strong> The one who presides: 主人 zhǔrén (master; host), 房主 fángzhǔ (homeowner), 主席 zhǔxí (chairman; the one at the head of the table).<br>
      <strong>2. Main, primary, principal.</strong> The central thing among many: 主要 zhǔyào (main), 主题 zhǔtí (theme), 主意 zhǔyi (idea).<br>
      <strong>3. To be in charge, to decide, to advocate.</strong> The master's act of deciding: 做主 zuòzhǔ (to decide), 主持 zhǔchí (to host; to preside), 主张 zhǔzhāng (to advocate).<br>
      <strong>4. The Lord, a deity.</strong> The supreme master in religion: 主 zhǔ is how Chinese Christians address God, and 天主 Tiānzhǔ ("Lord of Heaven") names the Catholic God.</p>
      <p>The thread through every sense is the presiding center. Whether the field is a household, a meeting, an argument, or the heavens, 主 is the one fixed point that holds authority, the flame at the middle of the room that everything else arranges itself around.</p>

        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 主要 vs. 重要</span>
          <strong>主要</strong> ranks one thing as the chief among several, the principal one in a set: the main reason, the leading cause. <strong>重要</strong> zhòngyào simply says a thing matters a lot, with no ranking implied. Many things can be 重要 at once, but only the foremost is 主要.

    <!-- ── DECIDE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="decide"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">主张</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zhǔzhāng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Advocate &amp; Be in Charge</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="主">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The master's defining act is to decide. 做主 zuòzhǔ is to "act as master," to be the one who settles a matter: 这件事你做主 "you make the call on this." From the same root comes <strong>主持</strong> zhǔchí, to preside over or host an event, the verb behind every emcee and chair. And <strong>主张</strong> zhǔzhāng is to advocate, to put forward and stand by a position: as a verb it means "to maintain," as a noun it means "a stance," the view one has decided to hold.</p>
      <p>The same character anchors the political word <strong>民主</strong> mínzhǔ, democracy, literally "the people as master." Where a 君主 jūnzhǔ (monarch) is the single presiding master of a state, 民主 hands that mastering role to the people themselves. The flame that once meant the one in charge of a household scales all the way up to who holds authority over a country.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>See the single flame resting on its stand: the top dot is the fire, the strokes below are the lamp. That steady central light is the whole character. Everything 主 means is the one presiding point that the room arranges itself around.</p>
      <p>In a household, that point is the master and host (主人). Among many things, it is the main one (主要). In a meeting, it is the one who presides (主持). In an argument, it is the position one holds and stands by (主张). In a state, it is who holds authority, whether a single monarch (君主) or the people themselves (民主). Whenever you meet 主, look for the presiding center, and you will find the flame on the stand.</p>
