    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="救">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>救 splits cleanly down the middle: 救 = 求 + 攵. On the left sits 求 (qiú), the phonetic; on the right sits 攵, a hand gripping a stick, the action radical that drives so many verbs of forceful doing (改 to alter, 攻 to attack, 放 to release). The right side carries the meaning, an arm in motion; the left side carries the sound and, by happy accident, a fitting echo.</p>
      <p>That echo runs deep. 求 in its oldest forms pictures a fur garment, and very early it was borrowed to write the verb "to seek, to beg, to plead." So the two halves of 救 read almost like a small drama: a plea on the left, a striking hand on the right. Someone cries out; a hand reaches in and acts. The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 救 as 止也, "to stop, to halt," the act of arresting a disaster before it finishes its work.</p>
      <p>From that physical core, to halt a calamity by force of hand, the sense fanned outward. First the literal: pull a drowning child from the river, beat out a fire. Then the social: relieve a famine, deliver a kingdom, salvage a sinking reputation. The hand never disappears from the word; even in abstract uses like 救国 (jiùguó, to save the nation), the verb still implies decisive intervention, not mere wishing.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps the character as 救 (kyū / suku-u), the second reading meaning "to rescue, to save," as in 救う. Korean reads it 구 (gu), surfacing in 구조 (救助, rescue). Across all three the image holds steady: an arm thrust into danger to drag a life back out.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">救命</span><span class="sh-py">jiùmìng</span><span class="sh-en">Saving Life · The Cry for Help</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">救命</span><span class="card-py">jiùmìng</span><span class="card-en">help! / to save a life</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">VO 动宾 dòngbīn</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">救 (rescue) + 命 (mìng, life). Literally "save a life." As a shout it is the standard Chinese cry of distress, the equivalent of "Help!" As a verb-object phrase it describes the act of saving someone from death.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">救命！有人落水了！</div><div class="ex-py">Jiùmìng! Yǒu rén luòshuǐ le!</div><div class="ex-en">Help! Someone has fallen into the water!</div></div>
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">是这位医生救了我的命。</div><div class="ex-py">Shì zhè wèi yīshēng jiùle wǒ de mìng.</div><div class="ex-en">It was this doctor who saved my life.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">救人</span><span class="card-py">jiùrén</span><span class="card-en">to save / rescue someone</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">VO 动宾 dòngbīn</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">救 (rescue) + 人 (rén, person). The general act of pulling a person out of danger, whether from water, fire, illness, or ruin.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他跳进河里救人，自己却没能上岸。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā tiào jìn hé lǐ jiùrén, zìjǐ què méi néng shàng'àn.</div><div class="ex-en">He jumped into the river to save someone, but could not get back to shore himself.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 救人 vs. 帮人</span> 帮人 (bāng rén) is everyday help with any task; 救人 implies the person is in real danger and the stakes are life or ruin.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">救火</span><span class="card-py">jiùhuǒ</span><span class="card-en">to fight a fire</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">VO 动宾 dòngbīn</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">救 (rescue) + 火 (huǒ, fire). Counterintuitively this means "to put out a fire," not to save the fire; here 救 keeps its Shuōwén sense of 止, halting the disaster. The same logic gives 救灾 (jiùzāi, disaster relief).</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">消防员连夜赶去救火。</div><div class="ex-py">Xiāofángyuán liányè gǎn qù jiùhuǒ.</div><div class="ex-en">The firefighters rushed through the night to fight the blaze.</div></div>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">急救</span><span class="sh-py">jíjiù</span><span class="sh-en">Emergency Aid · Rescue in Crisis</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      <p>救 anchors the vocabulary of urgent intervention. Pair it with 急 (jí, urgent) and you get first aid; pair it with 援 (yuán, to assist) and you get organized rescue; pair it with 护 (hù, to protect) and 车 (chē, vehicle) and you get the ambulance, 救护车.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">

        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 救援 vs. 救助</span> 救援 stresses the active operation of reaching the endangered; 救助 (jiùzhù) leans toward sustained relief and material support for the vulnerable.</div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="救" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a person waist-deep in a flooding river, one arm flung up out of the water, mouth open in a plea. That raised, pleading arm is 求 on the left of the character, the cry for help frozen into the phonetic. On the bank stands a rescuer, and in their hand a long pole reaching out across the current. That is 攵 on the right, the striking hand, the action that does the saving.</p>
      <p>Hold those two halves together and the whole word becomes a single gesture: plea meets reaching hand. 救 = 求 + 攵. The Shuōwén gloss 止, "to halt," is exactly what the pole does, it stops the drowning before the water finishes its work.</p>
      <p>Run the rescuer's pole through every compound and the field opens up. Reach it toward a sinking 命 (life) and you get 救命; toward a burning 火 (fire) and you get 救火; toward a wounded stranger in 急救; toward a whole nation in 拯救. Wherever the pole reaches, 救 follows: a hand thrust into danger to drag a life back to the bank.</p>
