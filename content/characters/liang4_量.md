    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure · a vessel for measuring grain</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="量">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>量 is among the oldest words in the script for the human act of quantifying. The earliest forms picture a vessel or a tied bag with a marker over the opening, the kind of standardized container used to scoop and level off a measure of grain. The character is best read as a pictograph of a measuring instrument rather than as a tidy sum of independent parts: it shows the tool, and from the tool comes both the act of measuring and the amount that results.</p>
      <p>This double payload is built into the word from the start, and it survives in the modern character as a tone split. Read <strong>liáng</strong> (second tone), 量 is the verb: to measure, to gauge, to weigh out. Read <strong>liàng</strong> (fourth tone), 量 is the noun: the quantity, the amount, the capacity that the measuring produces. The relationship is the same one English keeps between "to measure" and "a measure," compressed into a single glyph that changes only its tone.</p>
      <p>In the modern Kangxi system 量 is filed under the radical 里 (lǐ, village; a unit of distance; inside), which sits at the foot of the character. The radical assignment is a convenience of the stroke arrangement rather than a statement about meaning. What carries the sense is the whole figure of the calibrated container, the thing you fill to find out how much. The simplified and traditional forms are identical; 量 was never simplified, so a reader of either script meets exactly the same twelve strokes.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── TWO READINGS / PATTERNS ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="patterns"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">两读</span>
      <span class="sh-py">liǎng dú</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Two Readings · liàng the amount, liáng the act</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <p>The split is reliable enough to use as a rule of thumb: when 量 names a <strong>thing</strong> (an amount, a capacity, a count), it is <strong>liàng</strong>; when 量 names an <strong>action</strong> (the measuring itself), it is <strong>liáng</strong>.</p>
      <p><strong>liàng (noun, fourth tone)</strong> appears in the everyday vocabulary of "how much": 数量 (shùliàng, quantity), 大量 (dàliàng, a large amount), 质量 (zhìliàng, quality), 容量 (róngliàng, capacity), 酒量 (jiǔliàng, drinking capacity), and the grammatical term 量词 (liàngcí, measure word). If you can put a number or a "how much" question to it, you are in liàng territory.</p>
      <p><strong>liáng (verb, second tone)</strong> appears wherever someone is actively gauging: 测量 (cèliáng, to survey, to measure), 量体温 (liáng tǐwēn, to take a temperature), 量身 (liángshēn, to take body measurements). One high-frequency word sits between the two, neutralized: 商量 (shāngliang, to discuss) takes the verb reading but with the second syllable toneless, fossilizing the old image of two people measuring a matter out together before they decide.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">数量</span>

    <div class="cards">

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">数量</span>
          <span class="card-py">shùliàng</span>
          <span class="card-en">quantity: the amount or number of something</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          数 shù (number) + 量 liàng (amount). The neutral, countable "how many / how much" of a thing. 数量 answers questions of scale and stock: how many units, how large a supply. It pairs naturally with verbs of increase and decrease (增加数量, 减少数量) and is the standard word in commerce, logistics, and statistics. Hold it apart from 质量 below: 数量 is how much, 质量 is how good.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这批货的数量不够，还要再订一些。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè pī huò de shùliàng bú gòu, hái yào zài dìng yìxiē.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The quantity of this shipment is not enough; we need to order some more.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">大量</span>
          <span class="card-py">dàliàng</span>
          <span class="card-en">a large amount: a great quantity of</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj/Adv 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          大 dà (big) + 量 liàng (amount). Functions as a quantifier before mass and plural nouns: 大量资金 (a large amount of capital), 大量时间 (a great deal of time), 大量生产 (to mass-produce). It modifies the noun rather than counting discrete items, so it sits comfortably with both countables in bulk and uncountables. The mirror term is 少量 (shǎoliàng, a small amount).
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这项研究需要大量的数据支持。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè xiàng yánjiū xūyào dàliàng de shùjù zhīchí.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This research needs a large amount of data to support it.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">质量</span>
          <span class="card-py">zhìliàng</span>
          <span class="card-en">quality: how good something is (also physical mass)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          质 zhì (substance, nature) + 量 liàng (measure). In everyday speech 质量 is quality, the standard or grade of a product or piece of work: 质量好 (good quality), 提高质量 (to raise quality). In physics the same word is the technical term for mass. Context separates the two cleanly. Note the contrast that anchors this page: 数量 (quantity, how much) versus 质量 (quality, how good); Chinese business writing constantly pairs them, 重数量更要重质量 (value quantity, but value quality even more).
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这家工厂的产品质量很可靠。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiā gōngchǎng de chǎnpǐn zhìliàng hěn kěkào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This factory's product quality is very reliable.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">力量</span>
          <span class="card-py">lìliàng</span>
          <span class="card-en">strength: force, power, capability</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          力 lì (force, strength) + 量 liàng (measure, amount). The measurable quantity of force, whether physical, social, or moral: 体力的力量 (bodily strength), 团结的力量 (the power of unity), 知识就是力量 (knowledge is power). Where 力 alone is the raw faculty, 力量 frames strength as something you can gauge, gather, and bring to bear, the amount of power available.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他用尽全身的力量推开了那扇门。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā yòngjìn quánshēn de lìliàng tuīkāi le nà shàn mén.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He used all his body's strength to push that door open.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="card-def">

The maximum a vessel, device, or system can take in: a bottle's volume, a battery's charge, a hard drive's storage, a hall's seating. 电池容量 (battery capacity), 硬盘容量 (disk capacity), 存储容量 (storage capacity). It is the original measuring-vessel image made abstract: how much fits before the container is full.

          <div class="example">

            <div class="ex-en">This phone's battery capacity is much larger than the previous generation's.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">酒量</span>
          <span class="card-py">jiǔliàng</span>
          <span class="card-en">drinking capacity: how much alcohol one can hold</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          酒 jiǔ (alcohol) + 量 liàng (capacity). A person's tolerance for drink, the amount they can take before they are affected. A culturally loaded word at the banquet table, where 酒量好 (a good capacity) is treated as a social asset: 他酒量很大 (he can really hold his drink). The 量 here applies the container metaphor to the body itself, framing the drinker as a vessel with a known limit.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我酒量不好，喝一杯就脸红。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ jiǔliàng bù hǎo, hē yì bēi jiù liǎnhóng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I cannot hold my drink; my face turns red after just one glass.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">量词</span>
          <span class="card-py">liàngcí</span>
          <span class="card-en">measure word: the classifier between a number and a noun</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          量 liàng (measure) + 词 cí (word). The grammatical class that Chinese inserts between a number (or 这/那) and a noun: 一<strong>本</strong>书 (one volume of book), 三<strong>个</strong>人 (three people), 一<strong>杯</strong>茶 (one cup of tea). Every countable noun pairs with a conventional 量词, and choosing the right one is a core task of learning Chinese. The name is literal: these are the "measuring words," the verbal descendants of the measuring vessel, naming the unit by which a noun is counted.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">汉语里每个名词都要配一个合适的量词。</div>

            <div class="ex-en">In Chinese every noun has to be paired with a suitable measure word.</div>

    <div class="cards">

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">测量</span>
          <span class="card-py">cèliáng</span>
          <span class="card-en">to measure: to survey, to gauge with an instrument</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          测 cè (to gauge, to fathom) + 量 liáng (to measure). The formal verb for taking precise measurements: surveying land, gauging distance, recording temperature or pressure with an instrument. 测量土地 (to survey land), 测量距离 (to measure distance). Note the reading: as the act of measuring, 量 is second tone liáng, not liàng. This is the clearest place to feel the tone split in action.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">工程师正在测量这座桥的长度。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Gōngchéngshī zhèngzài cèliáng zhè zuò qiáo de chángdù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The engineer is measuring the length of this bridge.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">量体温</span>
          <span class="card-py">liáng tǐwēn</span>
          <span class="card-en">to take a temperature: to measure body heat</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">V-O 动宾 dòngbīn</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          量 liáng (to measure) + 体温 tǐwēn (body temperature). A high-frequency everyday verb-object phrase: to take someone's temperature with a thermometer. The same 量 measures other body data in parallel phrases, 量血压 (liáng xuèyā, to take blood pressure), 量身高 (liáng shēngāo, to measure height). All take the second-tone liáng, since each names the action of gauging.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">护士每天早上给病人量体温。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Hùshi měitiān zǎoshang gěi bìngrén liáng tǐwēn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The nurse takes the patients' temperature every morning.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">商量</span>
          <span class="card-py">shāngliang</span>
          <span class="card-en">to discuss: to talk something over, to consult</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          商 shāng (to consult, to confer) + 量 liang (measure, toneless here). To talk a matter over with someone before deciding: to weigh it between two minds. The literal image is measuring something out together, and the modern word keeps that cooperative flavor: 商量 is collaborative, not a one-sided announcement. The second syllable is neutralized to the toneless liang in speech, a fossil of the verb reading.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这件事我们再商量商量再决定。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn shì wǒmen zài shāngliang shāngliang zài juédìng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Let's talk this matter over a bit more before we decide.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          商量 is the one place where 量 is fully toneless in standard speech. The reduplicated form 商量商量 softens the request further, turning "discuss" into "let's just kick it around a little."

The phrase leans on 量 in its core sense, the limit of what measuring can reach.</span>

In modern usage it has also taken on a data-age sense, 海量信息 (a vast sea of information), 海量数据 (massive amounts of data), where 海量 simply means an enormous quantity.</span>

      <p>Hold the picture of the standardized grain measure: a vessel you fill, then level off at the rim. Everything 量 means radiates from that single tool. Fill the vessel and you have the act, 量 liáng (to measure): 测量 the land, 量体温 the patient, 商量 a matter out together between two minds. Read off the level and you have the result, 量 liàng (the amount): the 数量 you counted, the 容量 the vessel holds, the 力量 you can bring to bear, the 酒量 you can take before the night gets away from you.</p>
      <p>Let the tone carry the difference and you will rarely guess wrong. Action is second tone liáng, the rising effort of the act; amount is fourth tone liàng, the settled fall of the result. The grammarians named their classifiers 量词, "the measuring words," because every Chinese count still passes through that ancient vessel: 一本书, 三个人, 一杯茶, each noun met at the rim by the unit that measures it. And the moral idioms keep the same discipline, 量力而行 and 量入为出, both asking you to gauge the size of one quantity before you commit to another. To measure first, then act: the whole character in four strokes of advice.</p>
