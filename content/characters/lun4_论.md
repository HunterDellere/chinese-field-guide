    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="论">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>论</strong> is a pictophonetic compound: the 讠 radical (yán, speech) carries the meaning, and 仑 (lún) supplies the sound. The traditional form 論 pairs the full 言 (speech) with 侖 (lún) on the same logic. The phonetic 仑 / 侖 still matches the modern reading closely (lún → lùn), which makes the sound-side of this character unusually transparent: the right-hand element really does tell you how to say it.</p>
      <p>The 讠 radical anchors 论 firmly in the speech-family of characters (说 shuō, 话 huà, 语 yǔ, 词 cí, 议 yì, 谈 tán). But where many speech-characters cover talking in general, 论 carries a sharper sense from the start: speech that is <em>ordered, reasoned, weighed</em>. The phonetic 仑 also writes the idea of arranging things in sequence, and that flavour bleeds into 论: to 论 is not just to talk, but to set arguments in order, to reason a matter through.</p>
      <p>Two semantic lives fall out of this reasoned-speech core, plus a grammatical extension:</p>
      <p><strong>1. The verb: to discuss, debate, comment.</strong> 讨论 tǎolùn "to discuss," 议论 yìlùn "to comment on," 辩论 biànlùn "to debate," 评论 pínglùn "to review."<br>
      <strong>2. The noun: a theory, a view, a treatise.</strong> 理论 lǐlùn "theory," 结论 jiélùn "conclusion," 论文 lùnwén "a paper, a thesis," 舆论 yúlùn "public opinion."<br>
      <strong>3. The grammatical sense: regardless, no matter.</strong> 无论 wúlùn and 不论 bùlùn "no matter (what, who, how)," where the idea of "weighing, distinguishing" is negated: <em>without distinguishing the cases</em>.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── THEORY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="theory"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">理论</span>
      <span class="sh-py">lǐlùn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Theory, Conclusion &amp; Paper: The Noun</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="论">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>When 论 sits at the end of a compound and behaves as a noun, it names <em>a body of reasoned discourse</em>: a theory, a doctrine, a treatise, a settled view. This is the 论 of academic and intellectual life. A field of knowledge organised into principles is a 理论 lǐlùn; the worked-out endpoint of an argument is a 结论 jiélùn; the formal written argument a student or scholar produces is a 论文 lùnwén. Classical Chinese already used 论 this way as a genre label: a free-standing essay arguing a thesis was a 论, as in Jia Yi's famous essay on the faults of Qin, 过秦论.</p>
      <p>The pattern is productive. Attach 论 to an "-ism" or a stance and you name the doctrine itself: 进化论 jìnhuàlùn "the theory of evolution," 无神论 wúshénlùn "atheism," 相对论 xiāngduìlùn "the theory of relativity." The 论 turns a position into a named, arguable body of thought.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── REGARDLESS ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="regardless"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">无论</span>
      <span class="sh-py">wúlùn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Regardless: The No-Matter Construction</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="论">
      <div class="scholar-label">语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight</div>
      <p>A third life of 论 is purely grammatical. Negate the verb (to weigh, to distinguish) and you get the meaning <em>without distinguishing the cases</em>, that is, <strong>regardless, no matter</strong>. The two common forms are <strong>无论</strong> wúlùn (more formal, written) and <strong>不论</strong> bùlùn (slightly more colloquial); a third, 不管 bùguǎn, is the most casual of the set.</p>
      <p>The construction has a fixed shape. After 无论 / 不论 comes an open-ended element, a question word (谁 who, 什么 what, 怎么 how), an A-不-A choice, or an "X 还是 Y" alternative, and the main clause then takes 都 dōu or 也 yě to mark that the outcome holds across <em>every</em> case: 无论遇到什么困难，他都不放弃 "No matter what difficulties he meets, he never gives up." The logic is exactly the negated verb: the result does not get sorted by case; it applies to all of them alike.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── LUN ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="lun"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">论 Lún</span>
      <span class="sh-py">Lún</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Second Reading: The Analects Sense</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="论">
      <div class="scholar-label">异读 yìdú · Alternate Reading</div>
      <p>The same character has a second reading, the rising tone <strong>Lún</strong>, which in modern Chinese survives in essentially one place: the title of the Confucian classic <strong>论语</strong> <em>Lúnyǔ</em>, the Analects. The title is conventionally parsed as "edited sayings" or "selected discourses": 论 here carries the older sense of <em>arranging, compiling in order</em> (the same flavour the phonetic 仑 contributes), and 语 yǔ means "sayings." So 论语 is the ordered, edited record of Confucius and his disciples in conversation.</p>
      <p>Outside this fixed title the Lún reading does not appear in ordinary modern words, so learners only need to remember the single exception: the book is <em>Lúnyǔ</em>, not "Lùnyǔ." Everywhere else, the character is read lùn. The split is worth knowing precisely because 论语 is one of the most-cited titles in the language, and mispronouncing it is a giveaway.</p>
    </div>

        <span class="cy-cn">高谈阔论</span>

        <span class="cy-en">to talk loftily and expansively: to hold forth grandly, often emptily</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "lofty talk, broad discoursing." Describes someone holding forth at length on grand themes, frequently with the implication that the talk is more impressive than substantial. Can be neutral (spirited, wide-ranging conversation) but often carries a faint edge of "all talk."</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">长篇大论</span>
        <span class="cy-py">cháng piān dà lùn</span>
        <span class="cy-en">a long-winded discourse: a lengthy, overdrawn speech or piece of writing</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "long chapters, big discoursing." A speech or text that goes on at excessive length. Usually mildly critical: the listener or reader feels the argument could have been made in a fraction of the words.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">争论不休</span>
        <span class="cy-py">zhēng lùn bù xiū</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to argue without end: an endless, unresolved dispute</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "dispute and discuss without ceasing." Describes a debate that drags on with no side yielding and no resolution in sight. 不休 bù xiū "without stopping" stresses the wearisome, going-nowhere quality of the argument.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">一概而论</span>
        <span class="cy-py">yī gài ér lùn</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to lump together and judge as one: to treat distinct cases as if identical</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Literally "to discuss everything by one measure." Almost always negated: 不能一概而论 bùnéng yī gài ér lùn "you cannot lump them all together," a standard caution against treating different cases with a single blanket judgement. 概 gài is the level-bar that strikes off grain in a measure, hence "by one uniform standard."</span>
      </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="论" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture 论 as <strong>speech put in order</strong>: the 讠 radical (speech) on the left, the phonetic 仑 on the right carrying its own quiet sense of <em>arranging things in sequence</em>. To 论 is never just to talk; it is to lay arguments out in order and reason a matter through. Hold that single image and all three lives of the character follow from it.</p>
      <p>As a verb, ordered speech among people is discussion and debate: 讨论 pooling views, 辩论 contesting them, 评论 passing judgement. As a noun, ordered speech written down and settled is a body of doctrine: a 理论 theory, a 结论 conclusion, a 论文 thesis, and the classical essay-genre 论 itself. And negate the underlying idea of distinguishing case from case and you get the grammatical 无论, no matter, regardless: the outcome that refuses to be sorted. Even the lone exception, the rising-tone Lún of 论语 the Analects, keeps faith with the core: it names the <em>edited, ordered</em> record of a master's sayings.</p>
