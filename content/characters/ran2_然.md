    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="然">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>然 rán originally meant to burn.</strong> It is built from the fire radical 灬 (huǒ, the four dots that stand for flames along the bottom of a character) together with elements above it that early seal-script analysts read as meat over a fire. The picture is of something set alight and cooking. The 灬 along the foot of 然 is the same fire that sits under 热 (rè, hot), 烈 (liè, fierce), and 煮 (zhǔ, to boil): four short strokes that are not water drops but tongues of flame.</p>
      <p>So 然 began life as the word for kindling and combustion. Then it was borrowed, as so many concrete characters were, to write an abstract grammatical word that happened to sound the same: 然 meaning "so, like this, correct, that is how it is." The borrowing won out so completely that the burn sense had to be rescued by adding a second fire on the left, giving 燃 rán (to burn, to ignite), which carries the original meaning today. The bare 然 you meet on this page is almost always the grammatical one.</p>
      <p>Three jobs branch from the abstract "so":</p>
      <p><strong>1. Bound morpheme "so / thus / this way."</strong> The glue of written and spoken logic: 然后 ránhòu (afterward, and then), 然而 rán'ér (however, yet), 当然 dāngrán (of course), 虽然 suīrán (although).<br>
      <strong>2. The -ly manner suffix.</strong> Attached to a root it forms a two-syllable adverb meaning "in a ... manner": 忽然 hūrán (suddenly), 突然 tūrán (abruptly), 显然 xiǎnrán (obviously), 自然 zìrán (naturally).<br>
      <strong>3. The standalone "so / right."</strong> The literary sense surviving in 不然 bùrán (otherwise, if not so) and the agreeing 然也 of classical texts.</p>
      <p>The thread is sameness of state: 然 points back and says "thus, in this way, so it stands." Whether it links two clauses, turns an adjective into an adverb of manner, or affirms that a thing is the case, it is always saying "like this."</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 然后 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">然后</span>
          <span class="card-py">ránhòu</span>
          <span class="card-en">afterward; and then; after that</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adv">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          然 rán (so) + 后 hòu (after). The everyday word for sequencing events: do one thing, 然后 do the next. The backbone of any "first ... then ..." narration: 先 ... 然后 ....
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我们先吃饭，然后去看电影。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒmen xiān chīfàn, ránhòu qù kàn diànyǐng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">We'll eat first, and then go see a movie.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他洗了手，然后坐下来。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā xǐ le shǒu, ránhòu zuò xiàlái.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He washed his hands, then sat down.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 然后 vs. 后来</span>
          <strong>然后</strong> chains the next step in a sequence and can point at the future or a plan: A, then B. <strong>后来</strong> hòulái means "later on, afterward" only about the past, marking what eventually happened after a gap of time. You can say 我们以后然后做什么 only with 然后 for the next step; 后来 cannot set up a future plan.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 然而 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">然而</span>
          <span class="card-py">rán'ér</span>
          <span class="card-en">however; yet; nevertheless</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adv">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          然 rán (so) + 而 ér (but, and yet). A written, slightly formal "however," used to pivot against what was just said: things stood thus, 然而 the opposite turned out true. More literary than the spoken 但是 dànshì.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他很努力，然而没有成功。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā hěn nǔlì, rán'ér méiyǒu chénggōng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He worked hard; however, he did not succeed.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 仍然 -->
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">仍然</span>
          <span class="card-py">réngrán</span>
          <span class="card-en">still; as before; yet remaining</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adv">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          仍 réng (still, as before) + 然 rán (so). Marks that a state holds unchanged despite what might have altered it: 仍然 here, the situation is still thus. Close to 还是 háishì but more written.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">下了雨，他仍然去了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Xià le yǔ, tā réngrán qù le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">It rained, but he still went.</div>
          </div>
        </div>

    <!-- ── CONJUNCTIONS ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="conjunctions"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">当然</span>
      <span class="sh-py">dāngrán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Of Course &amp; Although: the Conjunctions</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="然">
      <div class="scholar-label">语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight</div>
      <p>A cluster of high-frequency conjunctions are built by capping a one-syllable logical word with 然. The 然 contributes the sense "it is so," and the front syllable colors how. <strong>当然</strong> dāngrán is 当 (ought, fitting) plus 然 (so): "as it ought to be," hence "of course, naturally." <strong>虽然</strong> suīrán is 虽 (although) plus 然: "granted that it is so," the opener of a concession that 但是 dànshì or 可是 kěshì later answers. <strong>既然</strong> jìrán is 既 (given that, since) plus 然: "since it is already the case," setting up a conclusion drawn from a fact.</p>
      <p>Watch the pairings, because Chinese loves matched connectors. 虽然 ... 但是 ... runs "although ... yet ..."; 既然 ... 就 ... runs "since ... then ...". The 然 word opens the clause and its partner closes the thought. Learn the pairs as units and the logic of a long sentence falls into place at once.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <!-- 当然 -->
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">当然</span>
          <span class="card-py">dāngrán</span>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adv">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>

          当 dāng (ought, fitting) + 然 rán (so). "As it should be," and so "of course." Stands alone as a full answer (当然！"of course!") or leads a clause: 当然可以 "certainly you may."
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">你会帮我吗？当然会。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Nǐ huì bāng wǒ ma? Dāngrán huì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Will you help me? Of course I will.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 虽然 -->
      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">虽然</span>
          <span class="card-py">suīrán</span>
          <span class="card-en">although; even though</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adv">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          虽 suī (although) + 然 rán (so). Opens a concession and almost always pairs with 但是 dànshì or 可是 kěshì in the second clause: 虽然 ... 但是 ... "although ..., yet ...".
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">虽然很累，但是我很开心。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Suīrán hěn lèi, dànshì wǒ hěn kāixīn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Although I'm tired, I'm very happy.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 不然 -->
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">不然</span>
          <span class="card-py">bùrán</span>
          <span class="card-en">otherwise; or else; if not so</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adv">ADV 副词 fùcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          不 bù (not) + 然 rán (so). Literally "if it is not so," hence "otherwise." Warns of the alternative outcome: do this, 不然 the bad thing happens. Close to 否则 fǒuzé but more colloquial.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">快走吧，不然就迟到了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Kuài zǒu ba, bùrán jiù chídào le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Let's go quickly, otherwise we'll be late.</div>
          </div>
        </div>

    <!-- ── SUFFIX ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="suffix"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">忽然</span>
      <span class="sh-py">hūrán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">The Manner Suffix: the -rán Adverbs</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="然">
      <div class="scholar-label">语法洞见 yǔfǎ dòngjiàn · Grammatical Insight</div>
      <p>The second great job of 然 is as a manner suffix, the rough equivalent of English -ly. Attach 然 to a one-syllable root that describes a quality and you get a two-syllable adverb or adjective meaning "in a ... way." <strong>忽然</strong> hūrán is 忽 (sudden) made into "suddenly"; <strong>突然</strong> tūrán is 突 (to dash out) made into "abruptly"; <strong>显然</strong> xiǎnrán is 显 (visible) made into "obviously"; <strong>自然</strong> zìrán is 自 (self) made into "naturally, of itself." The pattern is everywhere: 居然 jūrán and 竟然 jìngrán (to one's surprise), 偶然 ǒurán (by chance), 必然 bìrán (inevitably).</p>
      <p>Here 然 echoes a function it had in classical Chinese, where it turned a stative root into "having that quality, in that state." That is the same instinct as English -ly turning an adjective into an adverb. So when you meet an unfamiliar two-syllable word ending in 然, your first guess should be "in a ... manner," and the front character tells you which manner.</p>
    </div>

          <div class="example">

      <p>Start with the four dots of fire at the foot of 然, 灬, and remember that this character was born meaning "to burn." The flame was so useful for its sound that the language drafted it to write the little logical word "so, thus, like this," and had to light a second fire (燃) to keep the original meaning. Picture that first flame handing its body over to abstraction.</p>
      <p>From "so" come the two patterns you will use daily. As a connective it stitches clauses: 然后 (and so, after), 然而 (so, yet). As a suffix it turns a quality into a manner, the Chinese -ly: 忽然 (sudden becomes suddenly), 显然 (visible becomes obviously), 自然 (self becomes naturally). Whenever you meet a word ending in 然, ask "in what manner?", and the syllable in front will answer.</p>
