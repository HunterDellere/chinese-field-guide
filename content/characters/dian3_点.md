    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="点">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>The traditional form 點 = 黑 (hēi, black) + 占 (zhān, the phonetic).</strong> It is a pictophonetic compound: 黑 supplies the meaning, a small black mark, a speck of soot or ink; 占 supplies the sound. The original sense is a black dot, the smallest possible mark on a surface. The simplified form 点 keeps the phonetic 占 on top and replaces the bulky 黑 with 灬, the four-dot "fire" radical at the bottom, which here reads as the dot itself.</p>
      <p>From the single dot, the meanings fan out by analogy:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Dot and point.</strong> The literal mark: 点 a dot, 小数点 xiǎoshùdiǎn (decimal point), 地点 dìdiǎn (a spot, a location).<br>
      <strong>2. A little, some.</strong> A dot is the smallest amount, so 一点儿 yìdiǎnr means "a little bit," the everyday softener of quantity.<br>
      <strong>3. O'clock.</strong> The clock face is read off in points: 三点 sān diǎn (three o'clock), 几点 jǐ diǎn (what time).<br>
      <strong>4. To touch a point, hence to select or ignite.</strong> To put your finger on a point: 点菜 diǎn cài (to order dishes), 点头 diǎntóu (to nod), 点火 diǎnhuǒ (to light a fire).</p>
      <p>The unifying image is the touch of a single point: the brush-tip on paper, the fingertip on a menu, the spark on a wick, the smallest measurable amount. Everywhere 点 goes, something is being pinpointed.</p>

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">一点儿</span>
          <span class="card-py">yìdiǎnr</span>
          <span class="card-en">a little; a bit; some (small amount)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">PHR 短语 duǎnyǔ</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          一 yī (one) + 点 diǎn (dot) + 儿 (suffix). One dot's worth, the everyday way to say "a little." Placed after an adjective it softens it: 大一点儿 "a bit bigger." With a negative it means "not in the least": 一点儿都不累 "not tired at all."
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请给我一点儿水。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng gěi wǒ yìdiǎnr shuǐ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please give me a little water.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这件衣服大一点儿。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn yīfu dà yìdiǎnr.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This piece of clothing is a bit bigger.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 一点儿 vs. 有点儿</span>
          <strong>一点儿</strong> goes after an adjective to mark a small degree or comparison: 好一点儿 "a bit better." <strong>有点儿</strong> yǒudiǎnr goes before an adjective and carries a faint complaint: 有点儿贵 "a bit (too) expensive." The first measures; the second grumbles.
        </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">点菜</span>
      <span class="sh-py">diǎn cài</span>
      <span class="sh-en">To Order &amp; To Light — the Verb 点</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="点">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>As a verb, 点 means to touch a single point with intent, and that one gesture covers a surprising range. You touch an item on a menu to select it: 点菜 diǎn cài (to order food), 点歌 diǎn gē (to request a song). You touch a wick to ignite it: 点火 diǎnhuǒ (to light a fire), 点灯 diǎndēng (to light a lamp), 点蜡烛 (to light a candle). You touch each name on a list to count attendance: 点名 diǎnmíng (to call the roll). And you touch your chin down toward your chest to agree: 点头 diǎntóu (to nod).</p>
      <p>In every case the verb is precise and momentary. It is not a sweep or a push but a single contact at one point. This is why ordering, lighting, nodding, and roll-calling all share the same word: each is one deliberate touch landing exactly where it is aimed.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="点" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Hold one dot of ink at the tip of a brush. Press it to paper and you have a 点, a point: a spot on a map (地点), the part that matters most (重点), the decimal that fixes a number. Make it the smallest possible amount and it becomes 一点儿, a little. Read it off a clock face and it becomes the hour, 三点.</p>
      <p>Now make the brush a finger. Touch a dish on the menu and you have ordered it (点菜); touch a wick and you have lit it (点火); dip your head once and you have agreed (点头). Every meaning of 点 is one deliberate touch at a single point, whether the point is on paper, on a clock, on a menu, or on a candle.</p>
