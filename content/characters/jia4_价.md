    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="价">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>价 splits cleanly into two parts: 价 = 亻 + 介. The left side 亻 is the standing-person radical, the form 人 (rén) takes when it leans against another component. The right side 介 (jiè) is the phonetic, carrying the sound that anchored the older reading. The structure says it plainly: worth is something a person sets, not a property the object holds on its own.</p>
      <p>The character you see is a modern simplification of the fuller traditional form 價. There the phonetic was 賈 (jiǎ / gǔ), built on 貝 (bèi), the cowrie shell that served as China's earliest money and that sits inside nearly every word about commerce, debt, and trade. Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 價 under the merchant sense: the figure paid, the count of shells laid out across a table. The simplification dropped that heavy 賈 and reused 介, an older same-sound borrowing, leaving a lean six-stroke glyph.</p>
      <p>Meaning extended outward from the marketplace. First the concrete number: the shells, the coins, the price spoken aloud over a stall. Then the abstraction: 价值 jiàzhí, the value of a person's labor, a sacrifice, an idea, things no one ever paid in cowries. The same falling-tone syllable that once meant the count on the counter now carries the worth of a human life.</p>
      <p>Japanese kept the traditional skeleton in 価 (ka), seen in 価格 (kakaku, price) and 評価 (hyōka, appraisal), the very compounds Chinese builds with 价. Reading across the two scripts, the shared logic stays visible: a person, and a worth assigned in exchange.</p>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="价" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a single person, 亻, standing at a market stall, one hand resting on a wooden price-board: that board is 介, the phonetic, propped upright beside them. Nothing on the table has a worth until that person speaks. The vegetable, the bolt of cloth, the jade: each waits in silence until a human voice attaches a number. 价 is that moment, the person and the figure they name, frozen into six strokes.</p>
      <p>Hold the split steady, 价 = 亻 + 介, and the whole word-family falls into place around it. Add 格, the slot, and you get the 价格 on the tag. Add 值, to be worth, and the number lifts off the table into 价值, the value of things money never bought. Stretch the haggle out and the same person calls 讨价还价 across the stall, price out, price back, until two voices meet.</p>
