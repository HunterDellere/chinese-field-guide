    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="进">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>The traditional form 進 = 辶 (chuò, the walk radical) + 隹 (zhuī, a short-tailed bird).</strong> The 辶 element on the left is the ancient sign for movement along a road, a foot on a path. The 隹 inside it is a bird, and the old reading turns on a fact of nature: a bird can only ever walk forward, never backward. So the picture is movement that goes ahead and never reverses, which is exactly the sense of the word: to advance, to go forward, to enter.</p>
      <p>The simplified form 进 swaps the bird 隹 for 井 jǐng (a well), a tidier shape that is easier to write. The walk radical 辶 stays put, so the modern character still reads as movement on the road, even though the inner element no longer carries the bird image.</p>
      <p>Three senses branch from the foot on the forward path:</p>
      <p><strong>1. To enter, to go in.</strong> Movement across a threshold into a space: 进来 jìnlái (come in), 进去 jìnqù (go in), 进入 jìnrù (to enter).<br>
      <strong>2. To advance, to make progress.</strong> Movement ahead in space or in skill: 前进 qiánjìn (to advance), 进步 jìnbù (progress), 进展 jìnzhǎn (to make headway).<br>
      <strong>3. To take in, to carry out.</strong> Movement of goods or action inward: 进口 jìnkǒu (to import), 进行 jìnxíng (to carry out), 进食 jìnshí (to take food).</p>
      <p>The thread through every sense is forward and inward. Whether a person crosses a doorway, a student climbs in skill, or a country brings goods across its border, 进 is the bird that only walks ahead, the foot that moves in and never back. Its standing opposite is 退 tuì, to retreat.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── ADVANCE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="advance"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">进步</span>
      <span class="sh-py">jìnbù</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Advance &amp; Progress</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="进">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The forward sense of 进 is where the character earns its everyday weight. Step forward in space and you have 前进 qiánjìn, to advance; step forward in skill or condition and you have 进步 jìnbù, progress. The same root powers 上进 shàngjìn, the drive to better oneself, and 进展 jìnzhǎn, the headway a project makes over time. In each the picture is the same forward-walking bird, now climbing rather than merely crossing a threshold.</p>
      <p>Its constant foil is <strong>退</strong> tuì, to retreat. The two are so tightly bound that they fuse into single words: 进退 jìntuì (advance and retreat, i.e. one's room to maneuver) and the idiom 进退两难, caught with no good move in either direction. To know 进 well is to feel 退 standing right behind it.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a short-tailed bird on a road, the 隹 inside the walk radical 辶 of the traditional 進. A bird hops only forward, never back, so the whole character is forward motion that cannot reverse. Hold that single image and every sense follows.</p>
      <p>Walk that bird across a threshold and you have entering (进来, 进去, 进入). Walk it ahead in skill and you have progress (进步, 前进). Walk goods in across a border and you have importing (进口, 引进). And set 退, retreat, behind it, and you feel the tension that powers 进退两难: the bird that can only go forward, caught at a spot where forward is blocked too.</p>
