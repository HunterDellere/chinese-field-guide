    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="意">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>意 yì = 音 (yīn, sound) over 心 (xīn, heart).</strong> The Unihan decomposition reads 意 = ⿱ 音 心: the upper element is 音, a sound or voice, and the lower element is 心, the heart-mind. Read it as the sound of the heart, what the mind utters or holds before it is fully spoken. The traditional and simplified forms are identical, so the same glyph stands in both script columns.</p>
      <p>From the sound of the heart branch three senses that anchor a large family of everyday words:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Meaning, significance.</strong> What a thing means or signifies: 意思 yìsi (meaning), 意义 yìyì (significance), 含意 hányì (implied sense).<br>
      <strong>2. Intention, wish, idea.</strong> What the heart wants or proposes: 愿意 yuànyì (be willing), 同意 tóngyì (agree), 主意 zhǔyi (an idea), 故意 gùyì (deliberately).<br>
      <strong>3. Feeling, notion, the mind turned outward.</strong> The heart noticing or responding: 注意 zhùyì (pay attention), 满意 mǎnyì (satisfied), 意外 yìwài (unexpected).</p>
      <p>The thread through every sense is the mind reaching past itself. Whether 意 names what words signify, what a person intends, or what the attention settles on, it is the heart pressing outward into the world: meant, willed, or noticed.</p>
    </div>

          </div>

      </div>

      <!-- 意义 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">意义</span>
          <span class="card-py">yìyì</span>
          <span class="card-en">significance; meaning (of importance or value)</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          意 yì (meaning) + 义 yì (significance; principle). The deeper meaning that gives something worth or purpose: 人生的意义 "the meaning of life," 有意义的工作 "meaningful work."
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这件事很有意义。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn shì hěn yǒu yìyì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This matter is very meaningful.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 意思 vs. 意义</span>
          <strong>意思</strong> is the plain sense of a word or sentence, what it conveys: ask 什么意思 when you do not understand. <strong>意义</strong> is significance or worth, the value something carries: a gesture, a sacrifice, or a life can have 意义. You look up 意思 in a dictionary, but you weigh 意义 in the heart.
        </div>
      </div>

    <!-- ── INTENTION ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="intention"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">愿意</span>
      <span class="sh-py">yuànyì</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Intention &amp; Willingness</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="意">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The second great field of 意 is the will: what the heart wants and chooses. Here 意 names intention rather than meaning. <strong>愿意</strong> yuànyì pairs 意 with 愿 (yuàn, to wish) for "to be willing," the readiness of the heart to act. <strong>同意</strong> tóngyì sets 意 under 同 (tóng, same) for "to agree," that is, to share the same intent. And <strong>故意</strong> gùyì, "on purpose," marks an act done with the heart fully behind it, as opposed to 无意 wúyì, by accident.</p>
      <p>Notice how the same root flips between noun and verb-complement. As a bare noun, 主意 zhǔyi is "an idea," the proposal the mind forms. Inside 愿意 and 同意 it works as a verbal element, the leaning of the will toward a course of action. The constant is the heart settling on something it means to do.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="意" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Stack the character the way it is built: 音 sound resting on 心 heart. Picture a sound rising up out of the heart, the unspoken note the mind holds before it reaches the tongue. That note is 意, what you mean.</p>
      <p>Then follow it three ways. Aim the sound at a word and it becomes meaning (意思, 意义). Aim it at a deed and it becomes intention, the heart willing to act (愿意, 同意, 故意). Aim it outward at the world and it becomes attention and feeling, the mind noticing and being filled (注意, 满意, 意外). Hear the sound of the heart, and every 意 word follows from it.</p>
