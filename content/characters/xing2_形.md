    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure: Pattern Strokes</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="形">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>形 is a pictophonetic compound: the right-hand element <strong>彡 shān</strong> carries the meaning, and the left-hand element 开 supplies the sound. The radical 彡 is the cluster of three drawn-in strokes that runs through characters about visible markings and texture: 彩 cǎi (color), 彩饰 (ornament), 影 yǐng (shadow), 须 xū (whiskers). Those three slanting strokes stand for lines, sheen, hair, anything that gives a surface its visible pattern. So the meaning side of 形 says, in effect: this is a character about <em>what something looks like</em>.</p>
      <p>The Shuōwén Jiězì 说文解字 glosses 形 as 象形也 in the broad sense of an outward image, the look that a thing presents to the eye. From that root the meaning fans out cleanly. First the literal sense: the <em>shape</em> or <em>outline</em> of a thing, what you would trace if you drew its edge. Then the bodily sense: 形 as the physical body, the figure, the material form a person or thing has. Then a verbal turn: <em>to take shape</em>, <em>to appear</em>, <em>to become visible</em>, and from there <em>to describe</em>, to put a thing's look into words. Finally the abstract sense: the <em>form</em> or <em>configuration</em> of a situation, the shape that circumstances have taken.</p>
      <p>One image holds all of these together. 彡 is the visible pattern; 形 is the moment a thing presents that pattern to the eye, whether the thing is a triangle, a human body, a forming cloud, or the lay of a battlefield. When you meet 形 in a new compound, ask what is being made visible, and the sense usually falls out.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── PATTERNS / BIANXI ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="patterns"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">辨析</span>
      <span class="sh-py">biànxī</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Form vs the Lay of Things: 形式 vs 形势</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">辨析 biànxī · One syllable apart</span>
      <strong>形式 xíngshì</strong> (form 式 = pattern, format): the outward <em>form</em> or <em>format</em> of something, as opposed to its content. 内容 nèiróng (content) is its natural opposite. 形式主义 = formalism, going through the motions.<br>
      <strong>形势 xíngshì</strong> (势 = momentum, power): the <em>situation</em>, the configuration of forces, the way circumstances are shaped at a given moment. 国际形势 = the international situation; 形势严峻 = the situation is grim.<br>
      Same pinyin, same tones, one character apart. 形式 is about the <em>shape of a thing</em> (form vs. content); 形势 is about the <em>shape of events</em> (the lay of the land). Hear 势 "momentum" and you are in the world of unfolding circumstances.
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">速记 sùjì · Quick test</span>
      <strong>Is it form against content?</strong> → 形式 xíngshì. (改变形式, change the format; 形式上 = nominally, in form only.)<br>
      <strong>Is it how the situation stands?</strong> → 形势 xíngshì. (形势大好 = the situation is excellent; 看清形势 = read the situation clearly.)
    </div>

    <!-- ── FORM & DESCRIBE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="form-describe"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">形成</span>
      <span class="sh-py">xíngchéng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">To Take Shape &amp; To Describe</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="形">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>When 形 turns verbal, two senses dominate. The first is <strong>to take shape</strong>: 形成 xíngchéng, the standard verb for something coming into existence by a gradual process, a habit forming, a system forming, clouds forming. The second is <strong>to describe</strong>: 形容 xíngróng, literally to give a thing a face and a figure in words. To describe something is, in this metaphor, to draw its form so that a listener can see it. The two senses share one logic: 形 is the moment a shape becomes visible, whether to the eye (it takes shape) or to the imagination (you describe it into view).</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── SITUATION & TERRAIN ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="situation"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">情形</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qíngxíng</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Situation, Circumstance &amp; Terrain</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="形">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>The most abstract branch of 形 treats circumstances as something with a shape. A situation has a 形: a configuration you can read, like the outline of a landscape. 情形 qíngxíng is the everyday "situation" or "state of affairs." 形势 xíngshì raises the stakes: the lay of forces, the momentum of events, the strategic picture. And 地形 dìxíng brings the metaphor back to literal ground: the <em>shape of the land</em>, terrain, the contour map a general or a hiker reads. All three say the same thing in different registers: to understand a situation is to see its form.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="chengyu-grid">

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">形影不离</span>
        <span class="cy-py">xíng yǐng bù lí</span>
        <span class="cy-en">form and shadow never part: inseparable</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: form and shadow do not separate. A shadow follows its form everywhere; two people who are 形影不离 are constant companions, always together. Warm and affectionate, used for close friends, devoted couples, or a child who will not leave a parent's side.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">形形色色</span>
        <span class="cy-py">xíng xíng sè sè</span>
        <span class="cy-en">all shapes and colors: of every kind, all sorts</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: shape-shape, color-color. The reduplication piles up variety: people, goods, schemes, opinions of every imaginable kind. 形形色色的人 = all sorts of people. Often carries a faint edge of "a mixed bag," not all of it admirable.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">形单影只</span>
        <span class="cy-py">xíng dān yǐng zhī</span>
        <span class="cy-en">a lone form, a single shadow: solitary and without companions</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: the form is alone, the shadow is single. The mirror image of 形影不离: here there is only one form and its one shadow, no one alongside. A literary, somewhat melancholy phrase for a person who is utterly on their own.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">喜形于色</span>
        <span class="cy-py">xǐ xíng yú sè</span>
        <span class="cy-en">joy takes shape in the face: visibly delighted</span>
        <span class="cy-note">Lit: joy 喜 takes form 形 upon 于 the complexion 色. Happiness that cannot be hidden and shows plainly on the face. Here 形 is verbal, "to take shape, to appear," a clear window onto the verb sense of the character.</span>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="形" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Read the three strokes on the right, 彡, as the visible markings on a surface: lines, sheen, the strokes of a brush. 形 is the moment a thing shows those markings, the moment a shape becomes visible to the eye. Hold that one image and the whole vocabulary unfolds: a thing's outline (形状, 图形, 三角形), a body's figure (形体), the act of taking shape or being put into words (形成, 形容), and the shape that circumstances have taken (情形, 形势, 地形).</p>
      <p>The single trap to lock in: <strong>形式 xíngshì</strong> is form against content, while <strong>形势 xíngshì</strong> is the lay of the situation. Same sound, one character apart. When you hear 势 "momentum," you are reading events, not formats.</p>
