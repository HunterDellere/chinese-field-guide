    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="件">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>件 jiàn</strong> joins 亻 (the standing-person radical, a compressed form of 人 rén) with 牛 niú (ox). The traditional reading sees a person dividing an ox: cutting the whole animal into separate portions. From that act of portioning comes the core idea that runs through every modern use of the character: a single, separable unit broken off from a larger whole.</p>
      <p>That image explains why 件 became the natural measure word for discrete, countable things. An ox can be parcelled into joints; a matter can be parcelled into individual affairs; a wardrobe can be parcelled into separate garments. Each portion is 一件: one piece, set apart and counted on its own. The character never strayed far from that founding sense of a thing made individual.</p>
      <p>From the concrete portion, the meaning fanned out in three directions. A piece of writing became a 文件 (document); a piece of machinery became a 零件 (part); a discrete affair brought before officials became an 案件 (case). In each, 件 still marks one separable unit, whether of cloth, of paper, of metal, or of legal business.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">构词规律 gòucí guīlǜ · Pattern</span>
      <strong>Number + 件 + Noun = a counted item</strong><br>
      件 is the classifier for matters, garments, gifts, luggage, and other discrete objects. It sits between a number (or 这/那/几) and the noun.<br>
      一件事 (one matter) · 两件衣服 (two garments) · 这件礼物 (this gift) · 几件行李 (a few pieces of luggage)
    </div>

      <!-- 一件事 -->
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">一件事</span>
          <span class="card-py">yī jiàn shì</span>
          <span class="card-en">a matter: one affair; one thing to deal with</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          一 yī (one) + 件 jiàn (measure word) + 事 shì (matter; affair). 件 is the standard classifier for an affair, an errand, or a piece of business, anything that can be treated as a single, separable concern. You do not say 一个事 in careful speech; the matter takes 件.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我有一件事想跟你说。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ yǒu yī jiàn shì xiǎng gēn nǐ shuō.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">There is a matter I would like to tell you about.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这件事很重要。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn shì hěn zhòngyào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This matter is very important.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他每天要处理很多件事。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā měitiān yào chǔlǐ hěn duō jiàn shì.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He has to handle many matters every day.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 件 vs. 个</span>
          For an affair, choose 件: 一件事 is standard, 一个事 sounds careless. For a person or a generic object with no dedicated classifier, use 个: 一个人, 一个问题. As a rule, 件 counts discrete matters, garments, and parcelled objects; 个 is the catch-all when no specific measure word applies.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 一件衣服 -->
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">一件衣服</span>
          <span class="card-py">yī jiàn yīfu</span>
          <span class="card-en">an item of clothing: one garment</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          一 yī (one) + 件 jiàn (measure word) + 衣服 yīfu (clothing). 件 counts upper-body and one-piece garments: shirts, coats, dresses, sweaters. Lower-body garments take 条 tiáo instead (一条裤子 a pair of trousers, 一条裙子 a skirt), so the measure word itself signals which part of the body is meant.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我想买一件衣服。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ xiǎng mǎi yī jiàn yīfu.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I want to buy a piece of clothing.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这件外套很暖和。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn wàitào hěn nuǎnhuo.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This coat is very warm.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">她买了三件毛衣。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā mǎi le sān jiàn máoyī.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">She bought three sweaters.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">语法 yǔfǎ · Grammar</span>
          件 for tops and coats; 条 tiáo for trousers and skirts; 双 shuāng for paired items such as shoes and socks (一双鞋). Choosing the wrong classifier is one of the most common learner slips, so the garment type and its measure word are best memorized together.
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">一件礼物</span>
          <span class="card-py">yī jiàn lǐwù</span>
          <span class="card-en">a gift: one present</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          一 yī (one) + 件 jiàn (measure word) + 礼物 lǐwù (gift; present). A present counts as a discrete object, so it takes 件. The same classifier extends to other countable belongings and pieces of luggage: 一件行李 (one piece of luggage), 一件作品 (one work of art).
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是给你的一件礼物。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì gěi nǐ de yī jiàn lǐwù.</div>

            <div class="ex-py">Tā shōudào le hěn duō jiàn shēngrì lǐwù.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He received many birthday gifts.</div>
          </div>

    <!-- ── DOCUMENTS AND MAIL ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="documents"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">文件/邮件/信件</span>
      <span class="sh-py">wénjiàn / yóujiàn / xìnjiàn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Documents &amp; Mail</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="文">
      <div class="scholar-label">学者洞见 xuézhě dòngjiàn · Scholar Note</div>
      <p>When 件 attaches as a suffix to a noun, it turns that noun into a class of discrete documents or pieces. A 文 (writing) becomes a 文件 (a document, a file); a 信 (letter) becomes 信件 (letters as a category, mail). In the digital age the same suffix produced 邮件 (email), where the old word for postal delivery, 邮, was simply joined to 件. The character carries its founding sense of one separable unit straight into the inbox.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">文件</span>
          <span class="card-py">wénjiàn</span>
          <span class="card-en">document; file; paperwork</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          文 wén (writing; text) + 件 jiàn (a piece). A written document, an official paper, or a computer file. The word covers both physical paperwork and digital files: on a screen, 文件 is exactly the word for a file, and 文件夹 wénjiànjiā is a folder.
        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请把这份文件打印出来。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng bǎ zhè fèn wénjiàn dǎyìn chūlái.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please print out this document.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">我把文件保存在电脑里了。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Wǒ bǎ wénjiàn bǎocún zài diànnǎo lǐ le.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">I saved the file on the computer.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这是一份重要的法律文件。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè shì yī fèn zhòngyào de fǎlǜ wénjiàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This is an important legal document.</div>
          </div>

            <div class="ex-en">I sent you an email.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请查收邮件。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng cháshōu yóujiàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please check and receive the mail.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这件邮件还没寄到。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhè jiàn yóujiàn hái méi jì dào.</div>

        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">公司每天都收到大量信件。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Gōngsī měitiān dōu shōudào dàliàng xìnjiàn.</div>

            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng bǎ xìnjiàn fàngjìn xìnxiāng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please put the letters into the mailbox.</div>

    <!-- ── PARTS AND CASES ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="parts"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">零件/软件/案件</span>
      <span class="sh-py">língjiàn / ruǎnjiàn / ànjiàn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Parts &amp; Cases</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="零">
      <div class="scholar-label">学者洞见 xuézhě dòngjiàn · Scholar Note</div>
      <p>The portion sense of 件 reaches into machines and into law. A machine is the sum of its 零件 (parts) and 部件 (components); the things you can touch are the 硬件 (hardware) and the things you cannot are the 软件 (software). A discrete affair brought to a court or an investigator is an 案件 (a case), and an event that breaks out as a single incident is a 事件. In every one, 件 marks a unit cut from a larger whole.</p>
    </div>

        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">这个软件很好用。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Zhège ruǎnjiàn hěn hǎoyòng.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">This software is very easy to use.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请先安装这个软件。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng xiān ānzhuāng zhège ruǎnjiàn.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please install this software first.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">硬件和软件都很重要。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Yìngjiàn hé ruǎnjiàn dōu hěn zhòngyào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Both hardware and software are important.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 软件 vs. 硬件</span>
          软件 ruǎnjiàn = software (the soft, intangible programs). 硬件 yìngjiàn = hardware (the hard, physical machinery). The pair turns the abstract soft and hard distinction into two nouns that share 件 as their unit.
        </div>
      </div>

      <!-- 案件 -->
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">案件</span>
          <span class="card-py">ànjiàn</span>
          <span class="card-en">a legal case; an incident under investigation</span>
        </div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">
          案 àn (case; record; desk on which records were kept) + 件 jiàn (a piece). A discrete legal or police matter, treated as a single file of business. 案 alone meant the official desk and then the file of papers on it; with 件 it becomes one such case, counted and pursued on its own.

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Hold the founding picture: a person standing beside an ox, knife in hand, dividing the whole animal into separate portions. That single act of portioning is the whole character. Every modern use is just another thing cut into countable pieces.</p>
      <p>So when you count, you are portioning. 一件事 is one affair sliced off from the day's business. 一件衣服 is one garment lifted out of the wardrobe. 一件礼物 is one present set apart from the rest. The number plus 件 plus the noun is you, holding the knife, deciding where one piece ends and the next begins.</p>
      <p>And when 件 trails a noun as a suffix, the portion has hardened into a thing: a 文件 is a piece of writing, a 零件 a piece of machine, an 案件 a piece of legal business. Person and ox, again and again, parcelling the world into units small enough to count, file, repair, and try.</p>
