    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="船">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>船 splits cleanly down the middle: 船 = 舟 + 㕣. On the left stands 舟 (zhōu), the boat radical, a narrow hull seen from the side with its planks lashed across. On the right sits the phonetic 㕣 (yǎn), stacked from 几 over 口. The hull tells you what the word is about; the right side once cued how it sounded. This is a textbook pictophonetic, the most common way Chinese built its working vocabulary.</p>
      <p>舟 is the older, more elemental word for a boat: in oracle-bone script it is a curved dugout, a single hollowed log riding the current. As watercraft grew from a paddled log into framed, multi-plank ships, the spoken language needed a fresh word, and writing answered by pinning the new sound onto the trusted hull. The seal form already shows the two halves side by side exactly as the modern glyph does, the boat firmly on the left where radicals like to live.</p>
      <p>From the literal hull the sense never wandered far. 船 stayed anchored in the physical: anything that floats and carries, from a flat river barge to a steel ocean liner. Where 舟 drifted toward the poetic and the antique (think of a lone fisherman's skiff in a Tang quatrain), 船 became the plain, everyday word a modern speaker reaches for at the ferry terminal or the shipyard.</p>
      <p>Japanese borrowed the graph as 船 (fune, sen) and Korean as 선 (seon), both keeping the boat radical and the maritime meaning intact across the Sinosphere.</p>

    <span class="section-anchor" id="vessels"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">船舶</span><span class="sh-py">chuánbó</span><span class="sh-en">Vessels &amp; Craft</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">船 sits at the head or tail of a compound and names the kind of craft. The modifier in front says what powers it (帆 sail, 轮 wheel/engine) or what it does (渔 fishing); 船只 stays generic, the umbrella term for shipping of every sort.</div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">船只</span><span class="card-py">chuánzhī</span><span class="card-en">vessels, shipping (collective)</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">船 (boat) + 只, here a classifier turned collective suffix. The pair names boats as a category rather than one hull, the way 车辆 names vehicles in bulk. Standard in port notices, news, and maritime law.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">港口里停着各种船只。</div><div class="ex-py">Gǎngkǒu lǐ tíngzhe gè zhǒng chuánzhī.</div><div class="ex-en">All kinds of vessels are moored in the harbor.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>

        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">帆船</span><span class="card-py">fānchuán</span><span class="card-en">sailboat, sailing ship</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">帆 (sail) + 船 (boat): a boat driven by canvas. Covers everything from a small racing dinghy to the old treasure ships, and names the Olympic sport of sailing.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">远处有一艘白色的帆船。</div><div class="ex-py">Yuǎnchù yǒu yì sōu báisè de fānchuán.</div><div class="ex-en">There is a white sailboat in the distance.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">轮船</span><span class="card-py">lúnchuán</span><span class="card-en">steamship, large powered ship</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">轮 (wheel) + 船 (boat): the wheel is the paddle wheel of the first steamers, so the word came to mean any large engine-driven ship. Today it is the everyday term for a big passenger or cargo liner.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">我们坐轮船去上海。</div><div class="ex-py">Wǒmen zuò lúnchuán qù Shànghǎi.</div><div class="ex-en">We are taking a steamship to Shanghai.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 轮船 vs. 帆船</span> 轮船 runs on an engine and is large; 帆船 runs on wind and is usually small. Both are 船.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-violet">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">渔船</span><span class="card-py">yúchuán</span><span class="card-en">fishing boat</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">渔 (to fish) + 船 (boat): a boat worked for the catch. 渔 carries the water radical 氵, marking the act of fishing, distinct from 鱼 the fish itself.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">天还没亮，渔船就出海了。</div><div class="ex-py">Tiān hái méi liàng, yúchuán jiù chūhǎi le.</div><div class="ex-en">Before dawn, the fishing boats had already put out to sea.</div></div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="船" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a wooden hull cutting across grey water at dawn, the planks of 舟 lashed tight on the left of the glyph, taking the spray. Crammed against the cabin on the right is a small mouth 口 under a low table 几, the phonetic 㕣 riding along like a passenger who paid only for the sound of the word, not the work of carrying it. The hull does the meaning; the passenger does the noise.</p>
      <p>Hold that split and the compounds fall into place. Hang a sail in front and you get 帆船; bolt on an engine wheel and you get 轮船; send it out for the catch and it is a 渔船. The boat radical never moves, so every craft on the page is still recognizably a 船 the moment you spot 舟 on the left.</p>
