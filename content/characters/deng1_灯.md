    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="灯">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>灯 splits cleanly into two parts: 灯 = 火 + 丁. On the left sits 火 (huǒ), the fire radical, three tongues of flame compressed into four strokes; on the right sits 丁 (dīng), a blunt nail-head shape that here carries no meaning at all. 火 tells you the character is about burning light; 丁 only lends its sound. This is a textbook pictophonetic build, semantic element plus phonetic element, the most common way Chinese characters are made.</p>
      <p>The pairing of fire and 丁 is the modern shorthand. The older, fuller form is 燈, where 火 joins 登 (dēng, to ascend, to mount). That 登 supplies the rhyme and hints at a lamp raised up on its stand to throw light across a room. When the script was simplified, the bulky 登 was swapped for the small, same-sounding 丁, and the radical migrated to the left. The flame stayed; the phonetic shrank.</p>
      <p>The earliest 燈 named the oil dish on a pedestal, a saucer of fat with a floating wick that you literally climbed a stand to reach and trim. From that physical object the word stretched outward: to the paper lantern (灯笼), to the gas lamp, to the electric bulb (电灯), and finally to pure signal light with no flame inside it at all, like the 红灯 (hóngdēng) that halts traffic. The fire on the left is now a memory of how the light used to be made.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps both shapes, writing 灯 and 燈 for tō or akari, and Korean reads the same graph as deung in words for lamp and lantern. Across the region 灯 stays anchored to the same idea: a contained, deliberate flame meant to be seen.</p>

    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">电灯</span><span class="card-py">diàndēng</span><span class="card-en">electric light, lamp</span></div>

        <div class="examples">

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="灯" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture an old oil lamp on a wooden stand: a shallow dish of fat, a wick floating in it, and one steady flame leaning in the draft. That flame is 火, the fire radical clinging to the left side of 灯. Reach over and tap the iron nail-head holding the stand together, a small blunt 丁: it makes a clear ringing dēng. Fire on the left, the dēng-sound on the right, and the lamp is built.</p>
      <p>Now follow that single flame as it changes costume. Drop it into a paper cage and it becomes a 灯笼 swaying at the New Year. Run a wire to it and the flame turns into a glowing 电灯 bulb. Raise it on a pole over the road and it is a 路灯. Strip away the fire entirely and leave only its color, and you have the 红灯 that stops your car at the crossing.</p>
      <p>Every one of those words keeps the same nail tapping out dēng on the right, while the left-hand fire quietly remembers that all of this light, even the cold red signal at the intersection, began as one wick burning in a dish.</p>
