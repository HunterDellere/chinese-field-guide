    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="农">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>The modern 农 is a drastic shorthand for the traditional 農, and the simplified six strokes no longer divide cleanly into recognizable parts. Read it as a meaning-built graph that has been smoothed down, not as a sum you can take apart. The top sits under the 冖 cover that the dictionary files it under; the lower strokes are what survived of a much busier ancient picture.</p>
      <p>That ancient picture is where the sense lives. The seal and bronze forms of 農 combined the field, 田, with a clearing tool and the element 辰 (chén), which named both a shell-edged hoe and the rising of certain stars that told a farmer when to plant. So the graph fused three things a peasant lived by: the ground worked, the blade that worked it, and the season read off the night sky. 农 means the labor of growing crops, timed to the turning year.</p>
      <p>From that core the word fans out by association. The activity becomes the sector (农业, agriculture), the activity becomes the person who does it (农民, a farmer), and it becomes the world that activity builds (农村, the village; 农场, the farm). The same syllable that names dirt under fingernails also names a fifth of a national economy.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps the full traditional 農 and reads it <em>nō</em>, as in 農業 (nōgyō, agriculture); Korean reads the same graph <em>nong</em> (농). The simplified 农 is a mainland reform, so a sign reading 农 anywhere instantly marks the text as written for readers of simplified Chinese.</p>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="农" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a farmer straightening up in a dark field just before dawn. The crops are still under cover, a sheet of mist lying flat over them like the 冖 lid sitting across the top of the character. He has not started by reading a clock; he started by reading the sky, watching for the star 辰 to lift over the ridge, the old signal that the soil is ready to break.</p>
      <p>That single figure carries the whole word family. Give him a profession and he is 农民, a farmer. Give him a patch of houses and lanes and he is the 农村, the village. Give him an enterprise of fields and fences and you have a 农场, a farm. Hand the whole nation his labor and you have 农业, agriculture. Every one of those compounds is the same person seen from a different distance, all of them anchored to the one who reads the stars and works the ground.</p>
