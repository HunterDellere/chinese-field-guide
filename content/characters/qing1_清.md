    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="清">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · water and the color qīng</div>
      <p>清 is a pictophonetic compound: the water radical 氵 (the three-dot form of 水, shuǐ) carries the meaning, and 青 (qīng), the color of new growth and clear sky, carries the sound. The phonetic is not chosen at random. 青 names the blue-green of young leaves, of jade, of distant hills, of a sky washed clean after rain, and that field of fresh, unmuddied color leans toward the meaning the whole character settles on. 清 is what 青-colored water looks like: bright, limpid, with nothing stirred up in it.</p>
      <p>The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 清 as 朖也，澄水之皃 (lǎng yě, chéng shuǐ zhī mào), "brightness; the look of settled, transparent water." The base image is physical and exact: a pool that has stood long enough for the sediment to drop, so that you can see straight to the bottom. Everything 清 means afterward is an extension of that single picture. Speech you can follow to the bottom is 清楚 (qīngchu, clear). A reputation with nothing stirred into it is 清白 (qīngbái, clean, innocent). An evening with the day's heat settled out of it is 清凉 (qīngliáng, cool).</p>
      <p>From limpid water the senses fan out along four lines. First, clarity of the senses: water you can see through, sound you can hear distinctly, an image that is sharp rather than blurred, hence 清楚 and 清晰 (qīngxī, clear, well-defined). Second, cleanness and purity, whether physical or moral: 清洁 (qīngjié, clean) and 清白 (qīngbái, of unstained character). Third, the act of making something clear by sorting and settling it: 清理 (qīnglǐ, to clean up, to sort out), 清点 (qīngdiǎn, to check by counting), 结清 (jiéqīng, to settle an account in full). Fourth, the calm and coolness of clear conditions: 清静 (qīngjìng, quiet) and 清凉 (qīngliáng, cool).</p>
      <p>The character also names a dynasty: 清 was the last imperial house of China, the Manchu state that ruled from Beijing until the early twentieth century. The choice of so favorable a character, clear and pure, was a deliberate dynastic claim. Across East Asia 清 travels widely: Japanese reads it <em>sei</em> and <em>kiyoshi</em>, a common given name; Korean keeps 청 (cheong); Vietnamese reads <em>thanh</em>. The character is also one half of 清明 (Qīngmíng), the tomb-sweeping festival whose name means "clear and bright," the spring days when families clean ancestral graves under a washed sky.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">用法</span>
      <span class="sh-py">yòngfǎ</span>

      <span class="sh-rule"></span>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">one image, four directions</span>
      <p>Almost every 清 compound traces back to settled, transparent water. Keeping the four directions in view makes the large compound family legible:</p>
      <ul>
        <li><strong>Clear to the senses.</strong> 清水 (qīngshuǐ): clear water. 清楚 (qīngchu): clear, distinct, easy to follow. 清晰 (qīngxī): sharp, well-defined. The thing can be perceived all the way down.</li>
        <li><strong>Clean and pure.</strong> 清洁 (qīngjié): clean. 清白 (qīngbái): morally clean, innocent. 清新 (qīngxīn): fresh. Nothing soiled, nothing stirred in.</li>
        <li><strong>Settled and sorted.</strong> 清理 (qīnglǐ): to clean up, sort out. 清点 (qīngdiǎn): to check by counting. 结清 (jiéqīng): to settle in full. The verb of bringing something to a clear, finished state.</li>
        <li><strong>Quiet and cool.</strong> 清静 (qīngjìng): quiet, still. 清凉 (qīngliáng): cool, refreshing. The calm that comes when conditions are clear.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>One reading note: in 清楚 the second syllable is toneless in standard speech, <em>qīngchu</em>, not <em>qīngchǔ</em>. The bare character 清 keeps its first tone, flat and level, in every compound on this page.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── CLEAR ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="clear"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">清楚</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qīngchu</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Clear &amp; Distinct · seen and heard plainly</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="楚">
      <div class="scholar-label">清楚 vs. 明白 · two ways of being clear</div>
      <p>清楚 and 明白 are the two everyday words for "clear," and the difference between them is worth holding precisely. 清楚 (qīngchu) describes the clarity of the thing itself: it is distinct, sharp, well-articulated, easy to perceive. A photograph is 清楚, handwriting is 清楚, an explanation laid out in order is 清楚. The clarity sits in the object. 明白 (míngbai), built on 明 (bright) and 白 (white), describes the clarity inside the listener's mind: it means to understand, to grasp, to get it. The clarity sits in the comprehender.</p>
      <p>So the two pair naturally and are not interchangeable. 你说清楚 (nǐ shuō qīngchu), "say it clearly," asks the speaker to make the words distinct. 你明白吗 (nǐ míngbai ma), "do you understand," asks the listener whether the meaning has landed. A teacher can speak very 清楚 and a student still fail to 明白. When something is both well-stated and well-grasped, Mandarin often stacks them: 我听清楚了，也明白了 (wǒ tīng qīngchu le, yě míngbai le), "I heard it clearly, and I understood it too."</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── CLEAN ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="clean"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">清洁</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qīngjié</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Clean &amp; Pure · nothing soiled, nothing hidden</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="潔">
      <div class="scholar-label">from physical clean to moral clean</div>
      <p>The second sense of 清 slides smoothly from the body to the character. 清洁 (qīngjié) is physical cleanliness: a clean room, clean water, a sanitation worker is a 清洁工 (qīngjiégōng). But the same root of unstirred purity reaches into morality, where 清 becomes the word for a person or an institution with nothing soiled in its record. 清白 (qīngbái), "clear and white," is innocence and integrity together; a defendant cleared of charges has had their 清白 restored, and an official of unimpeachable honesty is 清官 (qīngguān), "a clean official."</p>
      <p>The freshness sense rides alongside. 清新 (qīngxīn), "clear and new," is the word for air after rain, a forest morning, a style of music or design that feels uncluttered and bright. Advertising leans on it heavily: 清新的口气 (qīngxīn de kǒuqì), "fresh breath," 清新自然 (qīngxīn zìrán), "fresh and natural." All three words, 清洁, 清白, 清新, share the same logic as the settled pool: what is 清 has had the impurity allowed to drop out of it, whether the impurity is dirt, corruption, or staleness.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── SETTLE ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="settle"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">清理</span>
      <span class="sh-py">qīnglǐ</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Settle &amp; Sort · clearing and counting</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">清 as verb · bringing things to a clear state</span>
      <p>The third sense turns 清 into an active verb: to make something clear by clearing it out, sorting it, or settling it to the bottom. This is the 清 of housework, inventory, and accounts:</p>
      <ul>
        <li>清理 (qīnglǐ): to clean up, clear out, sort through. 清理房间 (qīnglǐ fángjiān), "tidy the room"; 清理垃圾 (qīnglǐ lājī), "clear out the rubbish."</li>
        <li>清点 (qīngdiǎn): to check by counting, to take stock. 清点库存 (qīngdiǎn kùcún), "do a stock count."</li>
        <li>结清 (jiéqīng): to settle an account in full, to pay off completely. 结清欠款 (jiéqīng qiànkuǎn), "clear the outstanding debt."</li>
        <li>清账 (qīngzhàng): to settle accounts, literally to make the books clear.</li>
        <li>清除 (qīngchú): to eliminate, purge, get rid of entirely.</li>
      </ul>
      <p>The unifying idea is completion: to 清 a thing is to bring it to a finished, settled state with nothing left murky or outstanding. A room is 清 when nothing is out of place; an account is 清 when nothing is owed; a list is 清 when every item has been counted.</p>
    </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <div class="cy-cn">一清二楚</div>
        <div class="cy-py">yī qīng èr chǔ</div>
        <div class="cy-en">"first clear, second distinct": perfectly clear, in complete detail</div>
        <div class="cy-note">Stacks 清 and 楚, the two halves of 清楚, into a numbered formula for total clarity. Used when every part of a matter is understood without the slightest ambiguity: 这件事我看得一清二楚, "I see this whole matter perfectly clearly." Often used of accounts, sequences of events, or someone's motives that the speaker claims to grasp in full.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="cy">
        <div class="cy-cn">山清水秀</div>
        <div class="cy-py">shān qīng shuǐ xiù</div>
        <div class="cy-en">"hills clear, waters lovely": beautiful natural scenery</div>
        <div class="cy-note">The stock phrase of praise for landscape: clear hills and graceful waters. 这里山清水秀, "the scenery here is beautiful." A staple of travel writing and tourism slogans, it pairs 清 (the clarity of the green hills) with 秀 (the elegance of the streams), and is one of the most common four-character compliments a place can receive.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="cy">
        <div class="cy-cn">两袖清风</div>
        <div class="cy-py">liǎng xiù qīng fēng</div>
        <div class="cy-en">"clear wind in both sleeves": an incorruptible, honest official</div>
        <div class="cy-note">The image is of an official whose wide sleeves, the traditional place to tuck away a bribe, hold nothing but a clear breeze. It praises a public servant who leaves office as poor as they entered it, having taken not a single illicit coin. 他为官两袖清风, "as an official he was completely incorruptible." The phrase carries the whole Confucian ideal of the honest magistrate.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="cy">
        <div class="cy-cn">清心寡欲</div>
        <div class="cy-py">qīng xīn guǎ yù</div>
        <div class="cy-en">"a clear heart, few desires": free of craving, ascetic in temper</div>
        <div class="cy-note">Describes a state of mind cleared of greed and appetite, the temper of a hermit, a monk, or a contented recluse. 清 settles the heart as it settles water; 寡欲 means having few desires. The phrase is rooted in Daoist and Buddhist ideals of detachment: 他过着清心寡欲的生活, "he lives a life free of craving."</div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="清">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · the settled pool</div>
      <p>Hold one picture and the whole family follows: a pool of water that has stood long enough for everything stirred into it to drop to the bottom, so that you can see straight through to the floor. That is 清, water plus the fresh blue-green color 青 that lends it both its sound and its brightness. Everything else is that picture pointed in a different direction.</p>
      <p>Point it at the senses and you get 清楚 and 清晰: speech and sight you can follow to the bottom. Point it at character and you get 清洁 and 清白: a record with nothing soiled stirred into it. Point it at action and you get 清理 and 清点 and 结清: clearing a room, counting an inventory, settling a debt down to zero. Point it at the air of an evening and you get 清静 and 清凉: the calm and coolness of conditions that have settled clear. The same still water names a sharp photo, an honest official, a tidied desk, and a quiet cool night. When you meet 清 in a new word, ask which way the clarity is pointing, and the meaning resolves on its own.</p>
