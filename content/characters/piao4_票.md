    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>

      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="票">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>票 stacks two meaning parts: 覀 sits on top and 示 shì (altar; sign) sits beneath. The 示 element is the same ritual altar that anchors 神 shén (spirit), 祭 jì (to sacrifice), and 福 fú (blessing), a stone table where offerings are made to the dead and the gods. The 覀 on top stands for flames. The original picture is fire rising over an altar: an offering going up in smoke. The Shuōwén reads the seal form as 火飞 huǒ fēi, fire flying upward, the bright sparks that lift off a burning thing.</p>
      <p>The concrete scene behind the character is the Chinese custom of burning paper offerings for the dead. Mock money, 纸钱 zhǐqián (spirit money) and joss paper printed to look like banknotes, is set alight at a grave or a household altar so the smoke carries wealth across to the ancestors. 票 began here, in the lightness and flight of things rising in flame, and the earliest sense was light, swift, fluttering. A scrap of burning paper lifts and darts on the heat.</p>
      <p>From that fluttering slip of paper the modern senses branch out, and they cohere around one object: a small, light piece of paper that stands for value. The spirit money burned for the dead became, by extension, any paper slip that carries worth or grants a right. A banknote is a slip of paper standing for money (钞票 chāopiào). A ticket is a slip of paper standing for admission or a seat (车票 chēpiào, 门票 ménpiào). A receipt is a slip standing for a completed payment (发票 fāpiào). A ballot is a slip standing for a choice (选票 xuǎnpiào). Every one of them is a piece of paper that represents something larger than itself, the same way the burned offering stood in for real gold.</p>
      <p>The falling fourth tone piào is the everyday reading for note, ticket, and vote. The character also carries a rare reading piāo in the literary compound 票姚 piāoyáo (nimble; valiant), preserving the old fluttering, swift sense from the flames. In daily Mandarin you will only meet piào, and you can let the smoke of the altar stand quietly behind every ticket and banknote.</p>
    </div>

      <strong>入 (admission)</strong> 车票 chēpiào, a travel ticket; 门票 ménpiào, an entrance ticket; 机票 jīpiào, a plane ticket; 票房 piàofáng, box office<br>
      <strong>钱 (money)</strong> 钞票 chāopiào, a banknote; 发票 fāpiào, an official receipt; 支票 zhīpiào, a cheque; 邮票 yóupiào, a postage stamp<br>
      <strong>选 (choice)</strong> 投票 tóupiào, to cast a vote; 选票 xuǎnpiào, a ballot; 票数 piàoshù, the number of votes; 一票 yí piào, a single vote<br>

      Each branch is the same thing in the hand: a small printed slip that stands for a seat, a sum, or a choice. The paper is light; what it represents is not.

          <span class="card-cn">车票</span>

        </div>

          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">车票多少钱一张？</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Chēpiào duōshǎo qián yì zhāng?</div>
            <div class="ex-en">How much is one ticket?</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">量词 liàngcí · Measure Word</span>
          Tickets are counted with 张 zhāng (the measure word for flat, sheet-like things): 一张票 (one ticket), 三张电影票 (three movie tickets). The flatness of 张 fits the slip of paper exactly.

            <div class="ex-py">Bówùguǎn de ménpiào yào èrshí kuài qián.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">The museum's entrance ticket costs twenty yuan.</div>

            <div class="ex-py">Dàjiā qǐng wèi nǐ xǐhuān de zuòpǐn tóupiào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Everyone, please vote for the work you like.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">语法 yǔfǎ · Separable Verb</span>
          投票 splits like 见面 and 帮忙: the count or target slots in between. 投一票 (cast one vote), 投反对票 (cast a vote against), 给他投票 / 投他的票 (vote for him).

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head">
          <span class="card-cn">选票</span>
          <span class="card-py">xuǎnpiào</span>
          <span class="card-en">ballot: a ballot paper; a vote (as a counted unit)</span>

        </div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">他得到了最多的选票。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Tā dédào le zuìduō de xuǎnpiào.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">He received the most votes.</div>
          </div>
          <div class="example">
            <div class="ex-cn">请把选票投进箱子里。</div>
            <div class="ex-py">Qǐng bǎ xuǎnpiào tóu jìn xiāngzi lǐ.</div>
            <div class="ex-en">Please drop the ballot into the box.</div>
          </div>
        </div>
        <div class="note">
          <span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 选票 vs 投票</span>
          选票 is the thing, the ballot paper or a counted vote. 投票 is the act, the casting of it. You 投票 (perform the voting) by dropping a 选票 (the ballot) into the box.

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">一票否决</span>
        <span class="cy-py">yí piào fǒu jué</span>
        <span class="cy-en">a single vote vetoes it: one negative vote overrides the rest; absolute veto power</span>
        <span class="cy-note">一票 yí piào = a single vote. 否决 fǒujué = to vote down; to veto. A decision rule in which one opposing vote is enough to block a proposal outright, the power a permanent member holds on the UN Security Council. In wider use it describes any single factor that can disqualify everything else: 安全问题在工厂里是一票否决的。(In the factory, a safety problem is an automatic disqualifier.) Modern, common in committee and policy talk.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">投桃报李</span>
        <span class="cy-py">tóu táo bào lǐ</span>
        <span class="cy-en">throw a peach, return a plum: to repay a kindness in kind; mutual gift-giving</span>
        <span class="cy-note">This idiom shares the verb 投 tóu (to throw; to give) that drives 投票, though it does not contain 票 itself, so it sits here as a near neighbor on the 投 thread rather than a 票 idiom. 投桃报李 comes from the Shījīng (Book of Songs): you toss me a peach, I send back a plum. It captures the etiquette of reciprocal generosity. The genuine 票 idiom in daily speech is 一票否决 above; classical set phrases built on 票 are rare, because the character's everyday senses (note, ticket, ballot) are largely modern.</span>
      </div>

      <div class="cy">
        <span class="cy-cn">绑票勒索</span>
        <span class="cy-py">bǎng piào lè suǒ</span>
        <span class="cy-en">to kidnap and extort: to seize a hostage and demand ransom</span>
        <span class="cy-note">绑 bǎng = to tie up; to bind. 票 piào = here, a kidnapped person held for ransom (the slang 肉票 ròupiào, a human "ticket" to be cashed in). 勒索 lèsuǒ = to extort; to blackmail. A four-character description, not a classical chéngyǔ, but a fixed legal and news expression for kidnapping for ransom. It preserves a vivid underworld extension of 票: the captive treated as a slip to be exchanged for money. 警方破获了一起绑票勒索案。(The police cracked a kidnapping-for-ransom case.)</span>

      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Stand at a grave on the Qingming festival and watch a sheet of joss paper catch fire on a stone altar. The flames (覀 across the top) leap and the burning slip lifts off the altar (示 underneath), fluttering upward as smoke, carrying spirit money to the ancestors. That is 票, fire over an altar, a slip of paper rising in flame. Fix the picture and the whole character holds: a light printed slip that stands for something worth far more than the paper.</p>
      <p>From that burning offering, follow the slip into the everyday world. It lands at a station turnstile as a 车票 (train ticket), at a museum gate as a 门票 (entrance ticket), in your wallet as a 钞票 (banknote), at the checkout as a 发票 (official receipt), and in the ballot box as a 选票 (a vote you 投, you throw in). Each is the same object the ancestors received: a piece of paper that represents a seat, a sum, or a choice. The smoke of the offering still drifts behind every ticket you tear and every ballot you cast.</p>
      <p>When 票 appears in a new word, ask what the slip stands for. A way in (车票, 门票)? An amount of money (钞票, 发票, 邮票)? A choice to be counted (投票, 选票)? The answer is always some version of the first image: light paper carrying heavy value, lifted off the altar two and a half thousand years ago and still in your hand today.</p>
