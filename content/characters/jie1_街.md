    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="街">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>街 = 行 + 圭. The frame is 行 (xíng), the walk radical, which in oracle-bone script is a pictograph of two roads crossing: a four-way intersection drawn as paths meeting at a hub. The radical splits down the middle and the rest of the character is set inside its open center, so the whole glyph reads as something held within the crossing of roads.</p>
      <p>Nested in that opening is 圭 (guī), the phonetic. 圭 is a tapered jade tablet, two 土 (earth) stacked, an emblem a noble held at court. It lends 街 its sound (圭 guī steering toward jiē) and contributes nothing to the meaning; the sense comes entirely from 行. This is a textbook pictophonetic build: 行 semantic, 圭 phonetic.</p>
      <p>Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 街 as 四通道也, "a road open in all four directions," which is exactly the picture 行 supplies. A 街 is not a lane or a back alley but a public way wide enough to carry traffic both ways, the artery of a walled city laid out on a grid. From that concrete sense of a paved cross-route the word spread to mean the urban street in general, then by extension the shops and crowds that line it, so 逛街 (guàngjiē) came to mean strolling and shopping rather than merely walking a road.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps the character as 街 (kai, machi) for a town quarter or district, and Korean reads it 가 (ga) in compounds; in both, the original sense of a built-up public way survives intact.</p>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="街" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Stand at the center of an old walled city at dusk. The 行 radical is the intersection itself: two roads crossing under your feet, the left and right halves of the character splitting apart like the four arms of the junction. Cart wheels groove the stone in both directions, and you can hear footsteps coming from every side.</p>
      <p>Set into the heart of the crossing, between the two halves, is the jade tablet 圭, two blocks of earth (土) stacked one on the other. Picture a paving stone marker dropped at the exact center of the intersection, a small jade boundary post that says: here is where the public ways meet. It carries the sound, jiē, but it sits there silent, a mute marker letting the crossroads 行 do all the talking.</p>
      <p>Once the crossroads is fixed in mind, every compound radiates out from it. Walk one arm and you are on the 大街 (dàjiē), the big street; turn a corner to the 街头 (jiētóu); drift along the shopfronts and you are 逛街 (guàngjiē), strolling; greet the people who live along it, your 街坊 (jiēfang). Every word is just another step taken from that one stone crossing.</p>
