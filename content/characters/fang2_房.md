    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="房">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>房 sits in the half-enclosure frame ⿸: the door radical 户 (hù) leans over the top-left like a single hinged leaf, and the phonetic 方 (fāng) tucks beneath it. The decomposition is 房 = 户 + 方. 户 carries the meaning, a one-leaf door cut into a wall, and 方 carries the sound, lending the rising fáng reading. This makes 房 a textbook pictophonetic: one component points at the thing, the other points at how to say it.</p>
      <p>The radical 户 is the key to the original sense. A full gate of two leaves is 門 (mén); a single leaf, the kind hung on an inner room rather than a courtyard entrance, is 户. So 房 named the chamber you reach through a single inner door: the side-room, the wing flanking the main hall, the lesser room off the central space. The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses it as 室在旁也, the room that lies to the side. You can still feel that geometry in a traditional courtyard house, where the 正房 (zhèngfáng) faces south across the yard and the 厢房 (xiāngfáng) line the flanks.</p>
      <p>From that side-chamber the sense spread two ways. Outward it grew to mean any built room and then the whole building, the house, until 房子 became the everyday word for a dwelling. Inward and metaphorically, because each branch of a large family took its own wing of the compound, 房 came to mean a branch or line of descent: 大房 the senior branch, 二房 the second branch. The honeycomb logic of partitioned space also lets 房 label a cell or chamber in other systems, as in 心房 (xīnfáng), the atrium of the heart.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">居所</span><span class="sh-py">jūsuǒ</span><span class="sh-en">House &amp; Room: the Building Itself</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="cards">
      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">房子</span><span class="card-py">fángzi</span><span class="card-en">house, building</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">房 (building) plus the neutral-tone noun suffix 子 (zi). The default everyday word for a house or any standalone building you live in. Concrete and countable: you buy one, rent one, or build one.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他们在城里买了一套房子。</div><div class="ex-py">Tāmen zài chéng lǐ mǎi le yí tào fángzi.</div><div class="ex-en">They bought a house in the city.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 房子 vs. 家</span> 房子 is the physical building; 家 (jiā) is the home as a household and emotional center. You can own three 房子 but have only one 家.</div>
      </div>
      <div class="card c-ochre">

        <div class="examples">

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">分间</span><span class="sh-py">fēnjiān</span><span class="sh-en">Rooms by Function: Named Chambers</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>
    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">X + 房 · the room where X happens</span>
      房 attaches to a function word to name a room by what is done in it. 厨 (kitchen) → 厨房, 书 (books) → 书房 (study), 病 (illness) → 病房 (hospital ward), 茶 (tea) → 茶房 (old word for a tea-serving steward or pantry). The pattern reaches back to the courtyard wing: each 房 is a chamber assigned a purpose.
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="房" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a courtyard house at dusk. The great two-leaf gate 門 of the compound stands shut for the night, but along the inner walls a row of single doors 户 hangs open, each one a single hinged leaf swinging into a small lit chamber. Step through one of those single doors and you are inside a 房: a side-room with a lamp, a bed, the smell of cooking drifting from the next 房 down the row.</p>
      <p>The glyph holds that scene in its frame. The 户 leans across the top-left like the single open door you just pushed through, and 方 sits squarely beneath it, giving you the sound fáng to say as you cross the threshold. Hear it: the rising tone lifts like the door swinging up and open.</p>
      <p>Once the image is set, the compounds slot in as rooms along the corridor: cooking smells make 厨房 (the kitchen), books and lamplight make 书房 (the study), a bed makes 卧房 (the bedroom). Step out of the building entirely and the same word zooms out to mean the whole house, 房子, with a 房东 (landlord) holding the key and 房租 (rent) due at month's end.</p>
