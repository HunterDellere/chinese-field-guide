    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">字源</span><span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span><span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="笑">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p>笑 stacks two parts: ⺮ (bamboo) sits on top of 夭 yāo (a person with the head tilted, the body bent at the waist). 笑 = ⺮ + 夭. The shape is a figure bowed over beneath a crown of bamboo leaves, and that double picture is exactly why the character has provoked argument for a thousand years.</p>
      <p>The Shuōwén Jiězì does not give 笑 a clean entry, and the gap opened a debate that the Song scholar Xú Xuàn made famous: does the bamboo bend because a person doubles over laughing, or does a person bend the way bamboo bends when wind rakes through a grove? The Tang annotator Lǐ Yángbīng argued for the rustling reeds; later commentators preferred the body convulsed with mirth. Neither reading was ever settled, which is rare for so common a glyph.</p>
      <p>What is firm is the lived image. To laugh hard is to lose the upright spine: the shoulders drop, the head pitches forward, the whole frame folds the way a green bamboo stalk folds and springs back. 夭 already carries the sense of a supple, bending body, and the bamboo above lends both the sound and the swaying. The sense never had to travel far from concrete to abstract, because the posture of laughing and the posture of a wind-bent reed are the same posture.</p>
      <p>Japanese keeps the character as 笑 (read <em>warau</em>, "to laugh") and Korean reads it <em>so</em> (소), both holding the bamboo-over-bending-person shape unchanged across the writing systems.</p>

    <div class="section-head"><span class="sh-cn">笑声</span><span class="sh-py">xiàoshēng</span><span class="sh-en">Kinds of Laughter: Warm vs. Mocking</span><span class="sh-rule"></span></div>

    <div class="pattern">
      <span class="label">语气梯度 yǔqì tīdù · From a Quiet Smile to a Sneer</span>
      <strong>微笑 wēixiào</strong> → a faint, gentle smile; no sound, lips only<br>
      <strong>笑 xiào</strong> → to laugh or smile, the neutral base word<br>
      <strong>大笑 dàxiào</strong> → to laugh loudly, the whole body in it<br>
      <strong>嘲笑 cháoxiào</strong> → to laugh <em>at</em> someone; mockery, contempt
    </div>

    <div class="cards">

      <div class="card c-red">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">微笑</span><span class="card-py">wēixiào</span><span class="card-en">to smile; a smile</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">V/N 动名词 dòngmíngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">微 wēi (faint; slight; minute) + 笑 xiào (laugh/smile). A small, soundless laugh: the smile. The word that names the warm, closed-lipped expression, distinct from the open-mouthed 大笑.</div>

          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">她对我微微一笑。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā duì wǒ wēiwēi yí xiào.</div><div class="ex-en">She gave me a faint little smile.</div></div>

        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-ochre">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">可笑</span><span class="card-py">kěxiào</span><span class="card-en">laughable; ridiculous; ludicrous</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-adj">Adj 形容词 xíngróngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">可 kě (worthy of; -able) + 笑 xiào (laugh). Worthy of being laughed at: ridiculous, absurd. Built on the same 可 + verb pattern as 可爱 (worthy of love → cute) and 可怕 (worthy of fear → frightening). Often carries a sting.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">他的借口很可笑。</div><div class="ex-py">Tā de jièkǒu hěn kěxiào.</div><div class="ex-en">His excuse is laughable.</div></div>
        </div>
      </div>

      <div class="card c-teal">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">嘲笑</span><span class="card-py">cháoxiào</span><span class="card-en">to mock; to ridicule; to jeer at</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-v">V 动词 dòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">嘲 cháo (to taunt; to deride) + 笑 xiào (laugh). To laugh at someone with scorn. This is the cruel edge of 笑: the same sound aimed downward at a target. Takes a direct object, the person mocked.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">同学们嘲笑他的口音。</div><div class="ex-py">Tóngxuémen cháoxiào tā de kǒuyīn.</div><div class="ex-en">His classmates mocked his accent.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 嘲笑 vs. 微笑</span> 微笑 is warmth turned outward; 嘲笑 is contempt turned downward. The shared 笑 is why a smile and a sneer can look so alike, and why "他笑了" alone leaves you guessing which one it was.</div>

        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-vn">N/V 名动词 míngdòngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">笑 xiào (laugh) + 话 huà (speech; words). Laugh-words: a joke, a funny story. As a verb it flips to "to laugh at, to make a fool of." 讲笑话 = to tell a joke; 闹笑话 = to make an embarrassing blunder that others laugh at.</div>

      <div class="card c-sienna">
        <div class="card-head"><span class="card-cn">玩笑</span><span class="card-py">wánxiào</span><span class="card-en">a joke; a prank; banter</span></div>
        <div class="tags"><span class="tag tag-n">N 名词 míngcí</span></div>
        <div class="card-def">玩 wán (to play) + 笑 xiào (laugh). Play-laughter: a joke made between people who are at ease together. 开玩笑 kāi wánxiào (to crack a joke, to be kidding) is one of the highest-frequency phrases in spoken Mandarin.</div>
        <div class="examples">
          <div class="example"><div class="ex-cn">我只是开个玩笑，别生气。</div><div class="ex-py">Wǒ zhǐshì kāi ge wánxiào, bié shēngqì.</div><div class="ex-en">I was just joking, don't be angry.</div></div>
        </div>
        <div class="note"><span class="note-label">辨析 biànxī · 玩笑 vs. 笑话</span> 玩笑 is the shared, friendly kind, banter you make <em>with</em> someone; 笑话 is a packaged joke you tell, or, as a verb, laughter aimed <em>at</em> someone. Open the first, but try not to become the second.</div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="笑" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>Picture a stand of green bamboo on a hillside. A gust comes through and the whole grove bows in one motion, every slim stalk folding at the waist and whipping back up, leaves hissing. Now picture a person who has just heard something irresistibly funny: the head drops forward, the shoulders shake, the spine gives way at exactly the same hinge. That is the glyph. ⺮ (bamboo) rides on top, 夭 (the bent body) underneath. The whole character is one body folding over.</p>
      <p>Hang the compounds off that bend. A 微 (faint) fold is a 微笑, a smile barely creasing the lips. A 大 (big) fold is 大笑, doubled over and loud. Add 话 (words) and you get 笑话, the joke that triggers the fold; add 玩 (play) and you get 玩笑, the friendly banter that makes a whole table fold together. Once you see the wind moving through the bamboo, every word in the family is just a different strength of gust.</p>
