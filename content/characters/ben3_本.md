    <!-- ── ETYMOLOGY ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="etymology"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">字源</span>
      <span class="sh-py">zìyuán</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Etymology &amp; Structure</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="本">
      <div class="scholar-label">字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight</div>
      <p><strong>本 běn = 木 (mù, tree) + 一 (a marking stroke at the base).</strong> It is an indicative character (指事字): a tree, 木, with a short stroke drawn across its lower trunk to point at the roots. The mark does not depict an object; it indicates a location, the part of the tree below ground. So 本 means, from the start, the <em>root</em>. Its mirror image is 末 mò, the same tree with the stroke at the <em>top</em>, indicating the tip of the branches, the far end.</p>
      <p>From root, the meanings climb outward:</p>
      <p><strong>1. Root, origin, source.</strong> The literal base: 本来 běnlái (originally), 根本 gēnběn (fundamental; root), 本质 běnzhì (essence).<br>
      <strong>2. The fundamental, the basic.</strong> What everything else rests on: 基本 jīběn (basic), 本能 běnnéng (instinct), 本事 běnshì (ability, what one is built on).<br>
      <strong>3. This, one's own.</strong> The thing at hand, originating here: 本人 běnrén (oneself; the person in question), 本国 běnguó (one's own country), 本月 běnyuè (this month).<br>
      <strong>4. Principal, capital, and the bound volume.</strong> The original sum that earns interest, 本金 běnjīn; and the book, since a volume is a "base" of written leaves: 课本 kèběn (textbook), 一本书 yì běn shū (one book).</p>
      <p>The thread is foundation. Whatever is original, underlying, one's own, or principal traces back to the marked root of the tree: the place a thing starts from and stands on.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- ── BOOK ── -->
    <span class="section-anchor" id="book"></span>
    <div class="section-head">
      <span class="sh-cn">课本</span>
      <span class="sh-py">kèběn</span>
      <span class="sh-en">Book &amp; Volume — the Measure Word</span>
      <span class="sh-rule"></span>
    </div>

    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="本">
      <div class="scholar-label">语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight</div>
      <p>A book is a "base," a foundation of bound leaves, and so 本 became both the word for certain kinds of volume and the measure word for counting them. To count books you say 一本书 yì běn shū (one book), 两本词典 (two dictionaries), 几本杂志 (a few magazines). The same 本 names the bound thing itself: 课本 kèběn (textbook), 笔记本 bǐjìběn (notebook, and by extension a laptop), and the plain 本子 běnzi (an exercise book or pad).</p>
      <p>The measure word 本 belongs to anything bound between covers: books, notebooks, ledgers, magazines, dictionaries. It is one of the first measure words a learner meets, and it stays useful for a lifetime, because the bound volume never stops being something you reach for and count.</p>
    </div>

    <!-- RETENTION IMAGE -->
    <div class="scholar" data-glyph="本" style="margin-top:2.5rem;">
      <div class="scholar-label">记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image</div>
      <p>See a tree, 木, and watch a single short stroke land across its lower trunk, pointing down at the roots. That stroke is the whole character: 本 is the tree's root, the place it starts from. Its mirror 末, the same tree with the stroke up at the branch-tips, is the far end. Keep root and tip straight and you will never reverse 本末.</p>
      <p>From the root grows everything 本 means: what was there originally (本来), what underlies all the rest (基本), the thing or person at hand (本人), and the bound volume you build knowledge on and count one by one (一本书). Whatever is foundational traces back to that marked root.</p>
