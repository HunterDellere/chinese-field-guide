字源 zìyuán Etymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

候 hòu is built on 亻 (rén, person) plus a watching element. The left side is the standing-person radical 亻, the form 人 takes when it leans against another component. The right side carries an old sense of peering out and keeping watch, the same family as 侯 hóu, the lord who held a frontier post. Put together, 候 pictures a person posted to look out and stand by: a watcher, a sentry, someone waiting for a sign on the horizon.

From that figure of one who waits and watches, three senses fan out:

1. The bound word for time and the weather. 候 rarely stands alone today; its commonest life is inside two-syllable words. It supplies the "when" of 时候 shíhou (time, moment) and the long-term weather of 气候 qìhòu (climate).
2. To wait, to await. The direct verb sense: 等候 děnghòu (to await), 候车 hòu chē (to wait for a bus or train), 候诊 hòuzhěn (to wait to see a doctor). Here 候 is a more formal cousin of 等 děng.
3. Season, period, the unit of climate time. In the old calendar a 候 was a five-day span, three of them to a 节气 jiéqì (solar term). This is the 候 that ties weather to the turning year.

The thread through every sense is waiting measured against time. The sentry waits for what the day brings; 时候 is the moment you wait to arrive; 气候 is the slow weather you wait out across a season. 候 is the person on watch, and the time being watched.

时候 shíhou When & Time
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

For most learners the first and most important 候 lives inside one word: 时候 shíhou. It is the everyday "time" in the sense of "when," the moment or stretch at which something happens. 什么时候 shénme shíhou is "when?", the single most useful time-question in the language. 小时候 xiǎo shíhou is "when (I was) little," childhood. The pattern ...的时候 ...de shíhou turns any clause into "when ...": 吃饭的时候 "while eating," 我来的时候 "when I came." Note the second syllable goes neutral (shíhou, not shíhòu) in this everyday word.

Keep 时候 apart from 时间 shíjiān. 时候 answers "at what point" (a moment on the timeline). 时间 answers "how much" (a duration, a quantity of time you can have, spend, or run out of). 现在几点钟 asks the clock; 你有时间吗 asks whether you have duration to spare. The two are not interchangeable.

时候 shíhou time; moment; (the point) when
N 名词 míngcí
时 shí (time) + 候 hou (suffix). The everyday word for the point in time at which something happens. Anchors the question 什么时候 "when?" and the clause pattern ...的时候 "while / when ...".
你什么时候来？
Nǐ shénme shíhou lái?
When are you coming?
吃饭的时候不要看手机。
Chīfàn de shíhou bùyào kàn shǒujī.
Don't look at your phone while eating.
辨析 biànxī · 时候 vs. 时间 时候 is a point or occasion: the "when" something happens (什么时候来 "when are you coming"). 时间 shíjiān is a quantity of time, a duration you can have or spend (有时间 "to have time," 浪费时间 "to waste time"). Ask "at what point" and use 时候; ask "how much" and use 时间.
小时候 xiǎo shíhou in (one's) childhood; when (one was) little
N 名词 míngcí
小 xiǎo (small) + 时候 (the time when). The fixed phrase for "when I was little," the standard way to open a memory of childhood.
我小时候住在乡下。
Wǒ xiǎo shíhou zhù zài xiāngxià.
When I was little I lived in the countryside.
等候 děnghòu To Wait & Await
等候 děnghòu to wait; to await
V 动词 dòngcí
等 děng (to wait) + 候 hòu (to await). A more formal, written-leaning word for waiting than plain 等. Common on signs and announcements: 请耐心等候 "please wait patiently."
请在门外等候。
Qǐng zài mén wài děnghòu.
Please wait outside the door.
辨析 biànxī · 等候 vs. 等 等 děng is the plain, everyday verb for waiting, used in any register: 等一下 "wait a moment." 等候 děnghòu is its formal twin, at home in announcements, notices, and polite requests rather than casual speech.
候车 hòu chē to wait for a bus or train
V/N 动宾 dòngbīn
候 hòu (to wait) + 车 chē (vehicle). To wait for transport. Seen everywhere on station signage: 候车室 hòuchēshì "waiting room," 候车厅 "waiting hall."
乘客在候车室里休息。
Chéngkè zài hòuchēshì lǐ xiūxi.
The passengers are resting in the waiting room.
候诊 hòuzhěn to wait to see a doctor
V 动词 dòngcí
候 hòu (to wait) + 诊 zhěn (to examine medically). To wait for a medical consultation. The room where patients sit is the 候诊室 hòuzhěnshì.
病人在候诊室等医生。
Bìngrén zài hòuzhěnshì děng yīshēng.
The patients wait for the doctor in the waiting room.
气候 qìhòu Climate & Season
语义洞见 yǔyì dòngjiàn · Semantic Insight

The other major life of 候 is the slow time of the weather. 气候 qìhòu joins 气 qì (air, vapor, the breath of the atmosphere) to 候 (the season) and names the climate: the long-run pattern of a place, not the day-to-day. This is the 候 of the old farmer's calendar, where a 候 was a five-day unit and weather, crops, and ritual all turned on the season's rhythm. From the same root come 候鸟 hòuniǎo (migratory bird, the creature that times its flight to the season) and 火候 huǒhou (literally "fire's season," the cook's exact heat-and-timing).

Distinguish 气候 from 天气 tiānqì. 天气 is today's weather, the sun or rain you step out into this morning. 气候 is the standing climate of a region, what you would tell someone packing for a year abroad. 北京的气候 is "Beijing's climate"; 今天的天气 is "today's weather."

气候 qìhòu climate
N 名词 míngcí
气 qì (air; atmosphere) + 候 hòu (season). The long-term weather pattern of a place. Also figurative: 政治气候 "political climate," the prevailing conditions of a situation.
这里的气候很温和。
Zhèlǐ de qìhòu hěn wēnhé.
The climate here is very mild.
辨析 biànxī · 气候 vs. 天气 气候 is climate: the long-run pattern of a region (这里的气候 "the climate here"). 天气 tiānqì is the weather of a particular day (今天的天气 "today's weather"). One is the standing backdrop, the other is what is happening outside right now.
候鸟 hòuniǎo migratory bird
N 名词 míngcí
候 hòu (season) + 鸟 niǎo (bird). The bird that moves with the season, contrasted with 留鸟 liúniǎo, the resident bird that stays year-round.
秋天到了，候鸟开始南飞。
Qiūtiān dào le, hòuniǎo kāishǐ nán fēi.
Autumn has come, and the migratory birds begin flying south.
火候 huǒhou the right heat and timing; (by extension) the right moment or level of skill
N 名词 míngcí
火 huǒ (fire) + 候 hou (the timing of). In cooking, the exact control of heat and duration that makes or ruins a dish. Figuratively, the ripeness of a moment or the maturity of a skill: 火候到了 "the moment is ripe."
炒菜最讲究火候。
Chǎo cài zuì jiǎngjiu huǒhou.
Stir-frying depends above all on getting the heat and timing right.
成语 chéngyǔ Idioms & Set Phrases
守候多时 shǒu hòu duō shí to have kept watch for a long while: to wait faithfully and at length Lit: keep-watch-much-time. Said of someone who has been standing by patiently for a long stretch, often awaiting a person or an awaited moment. 守候 pairs the vigil of 守 with the waiting of 候.
时候不早 shíhou bù zǎo the hour is not early: it is getting late A common set expression rather than a classical chengyu. Used to signal politely that time has slipped on, often as a cue to wrap up or take one's leave: 时候不早了，我该走了 "it is getting late, I should go."
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a sentry, 亻 the person, posted at the edge of a road, peering out and waiting for a sign. That figure of one who watches and waits is the whole of 候. Hold the image and the senses follow.

What the sentry waits for is a moment: that is 时候, the "when" of 什么时候 and 小时候. The act of standing by is 等候, the formal waiting of a station sign. And the slow weather the watcher endures across a turning season is 气候, the climate. One person on watch, three kinds of time. When you meet 候, ask which time is being awaited, and the meaning falls into place.
