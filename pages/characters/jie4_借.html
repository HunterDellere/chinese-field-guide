字源zìyuánEtymology & Structure
字源洞见 zìyuán dòngjiàn · Etymological Insight

借 = 亻 + 昔. The left side is 亻, the standing form of 人 (rén), a person seen from the side. The right side is 昔 (xī), which supplies the sound. This is a textbook pictophonetic build: a human radical fixes the meaning in the world of people and exchange, and 昔 carries the pronunciation, drifting from xī to jiè over the centuries the way phonetic components often do.

昔 itself once pictured layered, sun-dried strips of meat above a sun, the old graph for "former days, of old." That ancient sense leaves a faint residue in 借: a thing handed over now is owed back later, a transaction stretched across time. The person stands beside the marker of elapsed days, holding something that is theirs only on loan.

The Shuōwén Jiězì glosses 借 as 假 (jiǎ), "to provisionally have," catching the heart of it. What you 借 is never permanently yours; you hold it on credit against a future return. From that single root the word swings both directions: the borrower 借 the thing in, the lender 借 the thing out, and only the surrounding words tell you which way the object moves.

Japanese inherits the character as 借 (read shaku or ka-riru, "to borrow"), keeping the same two-sided ledger logic in compounds like 借金 (debt). The Chinese glyph never split borrow from lend into separate characters, so the doubled meaning is original, not a modern blur.

借贷jièdàiBorrowing & Lending

One verb, two directions. 我借你的书 means "I borrow your book," while 我借你一本书 means "I lend you a book." The grammar, not the verb, sorts out who gives and who receives: 向/跟 marks the source you borrow from, while 给 or a direct recipient marks who you lend to.

借钱jiè qiánto borrow / lend money
VO 动宾 dòngbīn
借 (borrow/lend) + 钱 (money). A verb-object pair whose direction depends on the prepositions around it: 向他借钱 is borrowing from him, 借钱给他 is lending to him.
我想跟你借点儿钱。
Wǒ xiǎng gēn nǐ jiè diǎnr qián.
I'd like to borrow a little money from you.
他借了一笔钱给弟弟开店。
Tā jiè le yì bǐ qián gěi dìdi kāi diàn.
He lent a sum of money to his younger brother to open a shop.
租借zū jièto lease, to rent on loan
V 动词 dòngcí
租 (to rent) + 借 (to borrow). A bound compound for taking something for temporary use, often with a fee: leasing land, chartering equipment, the historical "leased territories" (租借地) of treaty-port China.
公司向政府租借了这片土地。
Gōngsī xiàng zhèngfǔ zūjiè le zhè piàn tǔdì.
The company leased this tract of land from the government.
辨析 biànxī · 租 vs. 借租 always carries payment and a formal term; plain 借 can be a free favor between friends with no money changing hands.
借给jiè gěito lend (to someone)
V 动词 dòngcí
借 (lend) + 给 (give to). Pinning 给 after 借 fixes the direction outward: the subject is the one handing the object over.
能不能把你的伞借给我？
Néng bu néng bǎ nǐ de sǎn jiè gěi wǒ?
Could you lend me your umbrella?
凭借píngjièUsing & Pretext
语义延伸 yǔyì yánshēn · Semantic Extension

Stretch "to take on loan" one step and it becomes "to make use of," to lean on something that is not your own. You 借 the wind to fill a sail, 借 an occasion to make a point, 借 someone else's strength to lift your own weight. From there it slides once more into 借口, the borrowed mouth: an excuse you put on like a coat that does not belong to you.

借口jiè kǒuexcuse, pretext
N 名词 míngcíV 动词 dòngcí
借 (to borrow) + 口 (mouth). A "borrowed mouth," words you reach for that are not the true reason. As a noun it is the excuse itself; as a verb, "to use as a pretext."
他总是找借口不来上班。
Tā zǒngshì zhǎo jièkǒu bù lái shàngbān.
He's always finding excuses not to come to work.
别拿天气当借口。
Bié ná tiānqì dàng jièkǒu.
Don't use the weather as an excuse.
辨析 biànxī · 借口 vs. 理由理由 (lǐyóu) is a reason, neutral and possibly genuine; 借口 implies the reason is hollow, a cover for the real motive.
借助jiè zhùto draw on, with the help of
V 动词 dòngcí
借 (to make use of) + 助 (aid). To accomplish something by leaning on an external tool, force, or person. Formal register, common in writing.
借助望远镜，我们看清了远处的山。
Jièzhù wàngyuǎnjìng, wǒmen kàn qīng le yuǎnchù de shān.
With the help of a telescope, we saw the distant mountains clearly.
借鉴jiè jiànto learn from, draw on as a model
V 动词 dòngcíN 名词 míngcí
借 (to borrow) + 鉴 (mirror, to reflect on). To borrow another's experience as a mirror for your own, taking what is useful from a model or precedent.
我们可以借鉴别国的经验。
Wǒmen kěyǐ jièjiàn bié guó de jīngyàn.
We can draw on the experience of other countries.
成语chéngyǔIdioms & Set Phrases
借刀杀人jiè dāo shā rénborrow a knife to kill a man: destroy a rival by another's handThe third of the Thirty-Six Stratagems (三十六计). You stay clean while a third party does the deed; still vivid in business and political commentary today.
借题发挥jiè tí fā huīseize on a topic to make a wider point: use a pretext to ventLiterally "borrow the subject to elaborate." Common for someone who turns a small matter into a platform for a bigger grievance.
借花献佛jiè huā xiàn fóborrow flowers to offer the Buddha: give a gift that cost you nothingA self-deprecating phrase at banquets when you re-gift or pour someone else's wine as if it were your own offering. Buddhist in origin, warm in tone.
借古讽今jiè gǔ fěng jīnuse the past to satirize the presentA classic mode of Chinese writing: cloak criticism of present rulers in a story about an ancient dynasty. Long the safest way to speak truth to power.
记忆法 jìyìfǎ · Master Retention Image

Picture a lone figure (亻) standing at a counter at dusk, the day's sun (the bottom of 昔) already low behind a stack of dried, layered slips (the strips piled in 昔). On the counter sits something not theirs: a book, a coin, an umbrella. They slide it across. Whether it travels toward them or away depends on which way they are facing, and that ambiguity is the character.

Hold the picture of elapsed time baked into 昔, "former days," and 借 always means a thing held against the clock. The umbrella borrowed at dusk must come back; the wind borrowed for the sail blows on only a while; the borrowed mouth of 借口 lasts exactly as long as the lie holds.

Run the compounds off that single image and they line up: 借钱 slides money over the counter, 借助 reaches across it for a tool, 借鉴 props a mirror on it to study, and 借刀杀人 hands someone else the knife from your side of the table while your own hands stay empty.
